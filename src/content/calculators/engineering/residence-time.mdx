## How to use the residence time calculator

Enter the **working (liquid) volume** and the **flow rate** through the vessel. The calculator returns the residence time and the space velocity. To size a vessel, clear the volume and enter the residence time you need.

## Residence time formula

<p class="formula">τ = V ÷ Q &nbsp;·&nbsp; SV = 1 ÷ τ</p>

## Worked example

A 10 m³ buffer tank with 5 m³/h flowing through it:

- τ = 10 ÷ 5 = **2 h** (120 min)
- Space velocity = 1 ÷ 2 h = **0.5 h⁻¹**

## How fluid really leaves a tank

The formula gives the average. In a well-mixed tank (CSTR) the fraction of the inlet fluid that has already left after a time t is 1 − e<sup>−t/τ</sup>. Splitting the same volume into two equal tanks in series narrows the spread towards plug flow. For the 10 m³ tank at 5 m³/h (τ = 2 h):

| Time after entry | One mixed tank | Two tanks in series | Ideal plug flow |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 τ (12 min) | 9.5% | 1.8% | 0% |
| 0.25 τ (30 min) | 22.1% | 9.0% | 0% |
| 0.5 τ (60 min) | 39.3% | 26.4% | 0% |
| 1 τ (120 min) | 63.2% | 59.4% | 100% |
| 2 τ (240 min) | 86.5% | 90.8% | 100% |
| 3 τ (360 min) | 95.0% | 98.3% | 100% |

This is why disinfection contact tanks are baffled: a mixed tank lets about 10% of the water out in a tenth of the nominal time.

## Residence time in a pipe

For a pipe, τ = L ÷ v. A 100 mm line 300 m long at 1.5 m/s holds the fluid for 200 s (3.3 min), carrying 42.4 m³/h. The same result follows from τ = V ÷ Q with the pipe's internal volume. This sets the transport delay seen by an analyser at the end of a sample line.

## Measuring residence time

The actual residence time distribution is found with a tracer test: inject a pulse or step of salt, dye or a conductivity tracer at the inlet and log the outlet concentration. The mean of the response curve is the real residence time; if it is much shorter than V ÷ Q, part of the tank is a dead zone and flow is short-circuiting.

## How long a change at the inlet takes to show

When the feed composition steps to a new value, a mixed tank's outlet follows 1 − e<sup>−t/τ</sup>. For the 10 m³ tank at 5 m³/h the outlet is 90% of the way to the new value after τ ln 10 = **4.61 h** and 99% after 9.21 h. Splitting the volume into two 5 m³ tanks in series gives a short initial delay but reaches 90% at 3.89 h. These times decide how long to wait before sampling after a recipe change, and how far a buffer tank smooths a spike in an upstream stream.

## Sizing a chlorine contact tank with a baffle factor

Drinking water disinfection rules credit the time by which only 10% of the water has passed, T<sub>10</sub>, not the average. T<sub>10</sub> = baffle factor × τ, with tracer-based factors from the US EPA guidance manual. For a required T<sub>10</sub> of 30 min at a peak flow of 120 m³/h:

| Tank design | Baffle factor | Residence time needed | Working volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unbaffled, mixed | 0.1 | 300 min | 600 m³ |
| Poor (single inlet baffle) | 0.3 | 100 min | 200 m³ |
| Average (some intra basin baffles) | 0.5 | 60 min | 120 m³ |
| Superior (serpentine baffles) | 0.7 | 43 min | 86 m³ |
| Perfect plug flow (pipeline) | 1.0 | 30 min | 60 m³ |

An average baffled tank needs 120 m³, five times less than a single unbaffled tank. Size on peak hourly flow and the minimum operating level, because both shorten the real contact time.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the total vessel volume.** Use the liquid volume at operating level, minus the space taken by internals, agitators and settled solids.
- **Using the wrong flow.** Include recycle streams that pass through the vessel; a 100% recycle halves the residence time per pass.
- **Assuming every particle stays τ.** For contact, kill or reaction time, design on the short end of the distribution, not the average.
- **Mixing units.** m³ divided by m³/h gives hours; convert to minutes or seconds before comparing with a specification.
- **Ignoring density change in reactors.** For gas reactions with a change in moles, space time based on the inlet flow differs from the actual mean residence time.

## Notes

- Use the liquid volume at normal operating level, not the vessel's total volume — get it from the [vertical tank](/engineering/vertical-tank-volume/) or [horizontal tank](/engineering/horizontal-tank-volume/) calculator.
- For a pipe, residence time = length ÷ velocity.
