## How to use this calculator

Enter your **monthly investment**, **expected annual return** and **period**. You see the future value, the total you put in and the returns earned. To plan for a goal, press × on Monthly investment and type the target amount.

## The formula

<p class="formula">FV = M × [((1 + i)<sup>n</sup> − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)</p>

- **i** = annual return ÷ 12
- **n** = years × 12
- The extra (1 + i) assumes you invest at the start of each month, as with most SIPs.

## Worked example

5,000 per month at 12% a year for 10 years:

- Total invested = 5,000 × 120 = **600,000**
- Future value = **1,161,695**
- Returns earned = **561,695**

## The power of time

At 5,000 per month and 12%:

| Years | Invested | Future value |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 300,000 | 412,432 |
| 10 | 600,000 | 1,161,695 |
| 15 | 900,000 | 2,522,880 |
| 20 | 1,200,000 | 4,995,740 |
| 25 | 1,500,000 | 9,488,175 |

Doubling the time from 10 to 20 years doubles what you put in but more than **quadruples** the result. Starting early matters more than investing a lot.

## SIP vs lump sum

| | SIP | Lump sum |
|---|---|---|
| Invests | A fixed amount every month | Everything at once |
| Best when | You invest from monthly income | You already have the money |
| Market timing risk | Low — buys at many prices | High — one entry price |
| Rupee cost averaging | Yes | No |

Use the [lumpsum calculator](/finance/lumpsum/) to compare, or combine both: invest a lump sum and run a SIP alongside it.

## Rupee cost averaging

A SIP buys more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high. Over time your average cost per unit is lower than the average price over the same period. It does not guarantee a profit, but it removes the stress of timing the market.

## Raise your SIP every year

Increasing your SIP by 10% every year can nearly double the final corpus over 20 years. See the [step-up SIP calculator](/finance/step-up-sip/).

## When to stop or pause

- Do **not** stop a SIP just because markets fall — that is when it buys the most units.
- Review the fund once a year against its benchmark.
- Shift to safer funds 2–3 years before you need the money.

## Keep in mind

- Returns are not guaranteed and markets can fall.
- Adjust the target for inflation with the [inflation calculator](/finance/inflation/).
- Fund expenses and taxes reduce the final amount.
