    Heart rate zones divide exercise intensity into ranges based on your maximum heart rate. Training in the right zone targets endurance, fat burning or performance.

    How to use the heart rate zone calculator

    Enter your age, resting heart rate and the intensity you want to train at. The calculator gives your estimated maximum heart rate and the target heart rate for that intensity.

    Heart rate zone formulas

    Max HR = 208 − 0.7 × age (Tanaka, 2001)
    Target HR = Resting HR + intensity × (Max HR − Resting HR)

    Worked example

    Age 40, resting heart rate 60 bpm, 70% intensity:

    • Max HR = 208 − 28 = 180 bpm
    • Target = 60 + 0.7 × (180 − 60) = 144 bpm

    Training zones (for this example)

    Zone Intensity Target HR Feels like
    1 — Very light 50–60% 120–132 Easy, recovery
    2 — Light 60–70% 132–144 Comfortable, can talk
    3 — Moderate 70–80% 144–156 Breathing harder
    4 — Hard 80–90% 156–168 Short sentences only
    5 — Maximum 90–100% 168–180 All-out effort

    Moderate and vigorous ranges use the percentage-of-max method from the American College of Sports Medicine. The Karvonen method in the calculator adjusts these for your resting heart rate.

    The five-zone model

    Zone % of heart rate reserve Feels like Used for
    1 50–60% Very easy, can chat freely Warm-up, recovery
    2 60–70% Easy, can hold a conversation Endurance base, fat use
    3 70–80% Moderate, short sentences Aerobic fitness
    4 80–90% Hard, a few words at a time Threshold training
    5 90–100% Maximum effort Short intervals

    Most endurance plans spend about 80% of training time in zones 1–2.

    The talk test

    No monitor? Use your breathing:

    • Moderate: you can talk but not sing.
    • Vigorous: you can only say a few words before pausing for breath.

    Guidelines

    The WHO recommends adults do at least 150–300 minutes of moderate or 75–150 minutes of vigorous aerobic activity each week, plus muscle-strengthening on two or more days.

    Safety

    Formulas are estimates — individual maximum heart rates can differ by 10–15 bpm. Some medicines (such as beta-blockers) lower heart rate. If you have a heart condition, ask your doctor before starting intense exercise.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is maximum heart rate estimated?

    This calculator uses the Tanaka formula, 208 − 0.7 × age, which is more accurate for adults than the older 220 − age rule.

    What is the Karvonen method?

    It sets training zones using heart rate reserve (max − resting), so it adapts to your fitness level. Target = resting + intensity × (max − resting).

    How do I measure resting heart rate?

    Count your pulse for 60 seconds after waking, before getting out of bed. Average several mornings.

    Why is my real max heart rate different?

    Age formulas are averages with a spread of about ±10 beats per minute. A supervised exercise test gives your true maximum.

    Do beta-blockers affect heart rate zones?

    Yes. They lower heart rate, so zones based on age will be too high. Use perceived effort and ask your doctor.
