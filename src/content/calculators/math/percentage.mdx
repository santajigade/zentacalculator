## How to use the percentage calculator

This one calculator answers all three common percentage questions. Fill in any **two** fields and the third is calculated:

| You know | You get | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Percentage and whole | Part | 15% of 240 = **36** |
| Part and whole | Percentage | 36 of 240 = **15%** |
| Part and percentage | Whole | 36 is 15% of **240** |

To switch question, press **×** on the field you want calculated, then type the other values.

## Percentage formula

<p class="formula">part = percentage × whole</p>

A percentage is a fraction of 100, so 15% means 15 ÷ 100 = 0.15. Rearranging the same formula gives the other two forms:

- percentage = part ÷ whole
- whole = part ÷ percentage

## Quick mental tricks

- **10%** — move the decimal point one place left (10% of 240 = 24).
- **5%** — half of 10% (12).
- **15%** — 10% + 5% (24 + 12 = 36).
- **x% of y = y% of x** — 8% of 50 is the same as 50% of 8, which is 4.

## Three types of percentage question

| Question | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What is P% of X? | X × P ÷ 100 | 15% of 240 = 36 |
| Y is what % of X? | Y ÷ X × 100 | 36 is 15% of 240 |
| Y is P% of what? | Y ÷ P × 100 | 36 is 15% of 240 |

The calculator handles all three — fill in any two fields.

## Percent vs percentage points

If an interest rate rises from 5% to 6%, it went up by **1 percentage point** but by **20 percent**. News reports often mix these up.

## Common mistakes

- **Percentage points vs percent.** A rate rising from 4% to 5% is a rise of 1 *percentage point* but 25 *percent*. Use the [percentage change calculator](/math/percentage-change/) for the second kind.
- **Adding percentages in sequence.** A 20% rise followed by a 20% fall does not return to the start: 100 → 120 → 96.
