## How to use the wavelength & frequency calculator

Choose the **type of wave**, then enter the **frequency** (in Hz) or the **wavelength**. The other is calculated.

## Wavelength & frequency formula

<p class="formula">v = f × λ</p>

## Worked examples

- **FM radio at 100 MHz:** λ = 299,792,458 ÷ 100,000,000 = **3.00 m**
- **Wi-Fi at 2.4 GHz:** λ = **12.5 cm**
- **Sound at 440 Hz in air:** λ = 343 ÷ 440 = **78 cm**

## Radio and wireless frequencies

| Use | Frequency | Wavelength |
|---|---|---|
| AM radio | 1 MHz | 300 m |
| FM radio | 100 MHz | 3 m |
| Mobile (4G band) | 1.8 GHz | 0.167 m |
| Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz | 2.4 GHz | 0.125 m |
| Microwave oven | 2.45 GHz | 0.122 m |
| Wi-Fi 5 GHz | 5 GHz | 0.06 m |
| 5G mmWave | 28 GHz | 0.0107 m |

A quarter-wave antenna for 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi is about 3.1 cm long — a quarter of the 12.5 cm wavelength.

## Sound waves

For sound, use the speed of sound instead of light:

| Medium | Speed of sound |
|---|---|
| Air, 20 °C | 343 m/s |
| Water | about 1,480 m/s |
| Steel | about 5,900 m/s |

Middle A (440 Hz) in air has a wavelength of 343 ÷ 440 = **0.78 m**.

## Photon energy

The energy of a photon is E = h × f, where h = 6.626 × 10<sup>−34</sup> J·s. Higher frequency means more energy — which is why ultraviolet can damage skin and X-rays can pass through tissue.
