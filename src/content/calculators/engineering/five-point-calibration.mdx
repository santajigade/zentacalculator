## How to use the 5-point calibration calculator

Enter the **calibrated input range** (LRV and URV) and the **output range** (4 and 20 for a 4–20 mA transmitter, 1 and 5 for a 1–5 V signal). The calculator gives the input to apply and the output you should read at 25, 50 and 75%; the 0% and 100% points are the range ends you entered. Record the measured values and check each one with the [calibration error calculator](/engineering/calibration-error/).

## Calibration point formula

<p class="formula">Input<sub>p</sub> = LRV + p × (URV − LRV) &nbsp;·&nbsp; Output<sub>p</sub> = O<sub>low</sub> + p × (O<sub>high</sub> − O<sub>low</sub>)</p>

where p = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1.

## Example: 0–10 bar pressure transmitter

| Point | Input | 4–20 mA output | 1–5 V output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 0 bar | 4.00 mA | 1.00 V |
| 25% | 2.5 bar | 8.00 mA | 2.00 V |
| 50% | 5 bar | 12.00 mA | 3.00 V |
| 75% | 7.5 bar | 16.00 mA | 4.00 V |
| 100% | 10 bar | 20.00 mA | 5.00 V |

## Calibration procedure

1. Isolate the transmitter, connect the pressure source and a reference gauge, and connect a multimeter or calibrator in the loop.
2. Apply each point **upscale** (0 → 100%) and record the output, then repeat **downscale** (100 → 0%).
3. Calculate the error at each point as % of span. If any point is out of tolerance, adjust zero and span and repeat to record as-left data.
4. For DP flow transmitters with square-root extraction, the output at 25% DP is 50% flow — see the [DP flow square-root calculator](/engineering/dp-flow-square-root/).

## Worked example: temperature transmitter with as-found errors

A temperature transmitter is ranged **50 to 250 °C** with a 4 to 20 mA output. Entering LRV 50, URV 250, 4 and 20 gives the 25, 50 and 75% inputs of **100, 150 and 200 °C**, with expected outputs of 8, 12 and 16 mA. The technician applies each point from a dry block, upscale and then downscale. Error is (measured − expected) ÷ 16 mA × 100:

| Point | Input | Expected | Upscale | Downscale | Error up | Error down | Hysteresis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 50 °C | 4.000 mA | 4.006 mA | 4.010 mA | +0.038% | +0.062% | 0.025% |
| 25% | 100 °C | 8.000 mA | 8.012 mA | 8.022 mA | +0.075% | +0.138% | 0.062% |
| 50% | 150 °C | 12.000 mA | 12.018 mA | 12.026 mA | +0.113% | +0.162% | 0.050% |
| 75% | 200 °C | 16.000 mA | 16.014 mA | 16.020 mA | +0.087% | +0.125% | 0.038% |
| 100% | 250 °C | 20.000 mA | 19.994 mA | 19.994 mA | -0.038% | -0.038% | 0.000% |

The worst error is **0.162% of span** and the largest hysteresis is at 25%. Against a ±0.25% tolerance the transmitter passes as found; against ±0.1% it fails and needs a zero and span trim, followed by a fresh as-left record.

| ±0.25% | ±0.040 mA | ±0.010 V | ±0.025 V |
| ±0.5% | ±0.080 mA | ±0.020 V | ±0.050 V |

## Good practice

- Choose a reference standard at least **4 times** more accurate than the tolerance being checked (a 4:1 test accuracy ratio).
- Let each point settle before reading, and approach it from one direction only. If you overshoot on the way up, go back below the point and approach it again, otherwise you hide hysteresis.
- Exercise pressure transmitters to full range two or three times before recording, so the sensor and seals settle.
- Record the as-found data before you touch anything, then the as-left data after adjustment. Trend the as-found error from one calibration to the next to set the interval.

## Common mistakes

- **Adjusting before recording as-found data.** The as-found result is what tells you whether the loop was within tolerance in service.
- **Ignoring damping.** A transmitter with several seconds of damping needs time to settle; reading too early looks like a span error.
- **Using a gauge reference on an absolute transmitter.** Atmospheric pressure then appears as a zero error of about 1.013 bar.
- **Calibrating a DP flow transmitter in flow units.** The table here is linear in DP; the square-root output is a separate check.
- **Skipping the downscale run.** Hysteresis only shows when you compare upscale and downscale readings at the same point.
