## How to use the factor of safety calculator

Enter the **material strength** (yield strength for ductile metals, ultimate strength for brittle ones) and the **working stress** in the part. The calculator returns the factor of safety and the margin of safety. Enter the **required factor of safety** to get the allowable working stress for design.

## Factor of safety formula

<p class="formula">FoS = S ÷ σ<sub>working</sub> &nbsp;·&nbsp; MS = FoS − 1 &nbsp;·&nbsp; σ<sub>allow</sub> = S ÷ FoS<sub>required</sub></p>

## Example

A steel part with 250 MPa yield strength is stressed to 100 MPa; the design standard asks for FoS = 2:

- FoS = 250 ÷ 100 = **2.50**
- Margin of safety = 2.50 − 1 = **150%**
- Allowable stress at FoS 2 = 250 ÷ 2 = **125 MPa** — the part passes

## Notes

- For fatigue (fluctuating) loading, compare the stress amplitude with the endurance limit, not the static strength.
- Find working stresses with the [bending stress](/engineering/bending-stress/), [bolt torque](/engineering/bolt-torque/) or [weld size](/engineering/weld-size/) calculators.

## Worked example: a rod in tension

A 20 mm diameter S235 steel rod (yield 235 MPa) hangs a load of **30 kN**. The required factor of safety is 2:

- Area = π × 20² ÷ 4 = **314.2 mm²**
- Working stress = 30,000 N ÷ 314.2 mm² = **95.5 MPa**
- Factor of safety = 235 ÷ 95.5 = **2.46**, margin of safety **146%**
- Smallest rod for FoS 2: allowable stress 117.5 MPa, so d = √(4F ÷ (π σ<sub>allow</sub>)) = **18.0 mm**

## Typical design factors by application

| Application | Typical factor | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Aircraft structure | 1.5 | Ultimate load ÷ limit load |
| Structural steel (allowable stress design) | about 1.67 | Yield |
| Pressure vessels, ASME VIII Div. 1 | 3.5 on ultimate, 1.5 on yield | Lower allowable governs |
| General machine parts, known loads | 2 to 3 | Yield |
| Chain slings, grade 80 | 4 | Breaking load ÷ working load limit |
| Wire rope slings | 5 | Breaking load ÷ working load limit |
| Brittle materials, shock loads | 4 to 8 | Ultimate |

For example, a pressure vessel steel with 485 MPa ultimate and 260 MPa yield gets an allowable of min(485 ÷ 3.5, 260 × 2/3) = **138.6 MPa**.

## Partial factors in limit state design

Modern structural codes split the safety factor between loads and materials. In Eurocode the design load is 1.35 × permanent + 1.5 × variable. For 10 kN permanent and 20 kN variable load, the design load is 1.35 × 10 + 1.5 × 20 = **43.5 kN**, an overall factor of **1.45** on the actual load, while steel uses a material factor of 1.0 on yield. The effective factor of safety therefore depends on the mix of loads.

## Common mistakes

- **Applying the factor twice.** If the allowable stress from a code already includes a safety factor, comparing it with the working stress again with another factor of 2 is overly conservative.
- **Ignoring stress concentrations.** Holes, threads and sharp corners raise the local stress by 2 to 3 times; use the peak stress, not the nominal one.
- **Using static strength for fatigue loads.** A part with FoS 3 on yield can still fail by fatigue under millions of cycles.
- **Mixing yield and ultimate.** State which strength you used; FoS 2 on ultimate can be close to FoS 1.2 on yield for some steels.
- **Forgetting dynamic and shock loads.** A load dropped or started suddenly can double the stress compared with the same load applied slowly.
