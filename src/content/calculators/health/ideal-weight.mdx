## How to use the ideal weight calculator

Choose your **sex** and enter your **height**. You get the Devine ideal weight and the healthy weight range based on BMI 18.5–24.9. Height can be entered in cm, m, inches or feet; weights display in kg or lb.

## Ideal weight formulas

<p class="formula">Men: 50 kg + 2.3 kg × (inches over 60)<br/>Women: 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg × (inches over 60)</p>

The healthy range comes from the BMI formula, weight = BMI × height (m)².

## Worked example

A man 180 cm tall (70.87 inches):

- Devine ideal weight = 50 + 2.3 × 10.87 = **75.0 kg** (165 lb)
- Healthy BMI range = 18.5 × 1.8² to 24.9 × 1.8² = **59.9 – 80.7 kg**

A woman 165 cm tall: Devine ideal weight = **56.9 kg**.

## Other formulas

| Formula (year) | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Devine (1974) | 50 + 2.3/in | 45.5 + 2.3/in |
| Robinson (1983) | 52 + 1.9/in | 49 + 1.7/in |
| Miller (1983) | 56.2 + 1.41/in | 53.1 + 1.36/in |
| Hamwi (1964) | 48 + 2.7/in | 45.5 + 2.2/in |

"/in" means kilograms per inch over 5 feet. The formulas differ by several kilograms, which shows these are rough guides, not targets.

## Which formula to trust?

All four classic formulas were designed for drug dosing, not to define a perfect body weight. They give a single number, whereas real health spans a range. For personal goals, the **healthy BMI range** in the last column is a better guide, ideally combined with waist size.

## Ideal vs adjusted body weight

Doctors sometimes use **adjusted body weight** for drug dosing in people with obesity:

<p class="formula">Adjusted weight = Ideal weight + 0.4 × (Actual weight − Ideal weight)</p>

For a man 175 cm (ideal 70.5 kg) weighing 100 kg, adjusted weight = 82.3 kg.

## Frame size

Some charts adjust ideal weight for small, medium or large frames, often by about ±10%. Wrist circumference is a simple way to estimate frame: a smaller wrist for your height suggests a smaller frame.

## Important

These formulas do not consider muscle mass, frame size, age or ethnicity. For Asian adults, health risks rise at a lower BMI, so the upper end of the range may be lower. Talk to a doctor before setting a weight goal.
