## How to use the EMI calculator

Enter the **loan amount**, the **interest rate** your bank quotes and the **tenure** in years. The calculator shows your monthly EMI, the total interest and the total amount you will repay. Change any value and the results update instantly.

It works in every direction. To find the loan you can afford, press × on Loan amount and type the EMI you are comfortable with. To check what rate a lender is really charging, enter the loan, EMI and tenure and press × on the rate.

## The EMI formula

<p class="formula">EMI = P × r × (1 + r)<sup>n</sup> ÷ ((1 + r)<sup>n</sup> − 1)</p>

- **P** = loan amount (principal)
- **r** = monthly interest rate = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
- **n** = number of monthly instalments = years × 12

Each EMI has two parts: interest on the outstanding balance and a repayment of principal. In the early years most of the EMI goes to interest. As the balance falls, more of each EMI repays the loan.

## Worked example

A home loan of **₹30,00,000** at **8.5%** for **20 years**:

- Monthly rate r = 8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.007083
- Number of EMIs n = 20 × 12 = 240
- EMI = **₹26,035**
- Total payment = ₹26,035 × 240 = **₹62,48,327**
- Total interest = **₹32,48,327**

The interest is more than the loan itself, which is normal for a 20-year home loan.

A longer tenure lowers the EMI but the total interest climbs steeply. Choose the shortest tenure whose EMI fits comfortably in your budget.

## How to reduce your loan cost

- **Prepay early.** A prepayment in the first few years saves far more interest than the same amount later. Floating-rate home loans for individuals have no prepayment penalty in India.
- **Increase the EMI with your salary.** Raising the EMI by 5–10% each year can cut a 20-year loan by several years.
- **Compare the rate, not just the EMI.** A small difference of 0.5% on ₹30 lakh over 20 years is worth roughly ₹2,29,700.
- **Check the reset clause.** Floating rates move with the repo rate, so your EMI or tenure can change.

## Keep in mind

- Most lenders want total EMIs below about 40–50% of your take-home pay.
- Interest on a home loan may be deductible under the old tax regime. Check current rules with a tax adviser.
- This is an estimate. Your bank's schedule may differ slightly because of rounding and the date the loan starts.

## Where each EMI goes: interest first, principal later

For the default ₹30,00,000 loan at 8.5% over 20 years the EMI is **₹26,035**. In the first month the interest is ₹30,00,000 × 8.5% ÷ 12 = ₹21,250, so only ₹4,785 of the EMI reduces the loan. The principal part overtakes the interest part only in month 143, more than 11 years in.

| Loan year | Interest paid | Principal repaid | Interest share | Balance at year end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | ₹2,52,709 | ₹59,707 | 81% | ₹29,40,293 |
| Year 5 | ₹2,28,632 | ₹83,785 | 73% | ₹26,43,815 |
| Year 10 | ₹1,84,452 | ₹1,27,964 | 59% | ₹20,99,815 |
| Year 15 | ₹1,16,977 | ₹1,95,440 | 37% | ₹12,68,962 |
| Year 20 | ₹13,921 | ₹2,98,495 | 4% | ₹0 |

This is why a home loan statement after five years can still show most of the original amount outstanding, and why the interest certificate your bank issues each April is largest in the early years.

## When the repo rate moves: higher EMI or longer tenure

Most Indian home loans are floating and linked to an external benchmark such as the RBI repo rate, so the rate is reset when the benchmark changes. Suppose the rate rises from 8.5% to 9.0% after 36 EMIs, when the balance is ₹28,04,580. The bank can do one of two things:

- **Raise the EMI** to ₹26,890 for the remaining 204 months. Extra interest over the rest of the loan: ₹1,74,568.
- **Keep the EMI** at ₹26,035 and stretch the loan by 17 months. Extra interest: ₹4,37,739.

Stretching the tenure costs more in total, yet many lenders default to it. RBI rules on resetting floating rate EMI loans require the lender to tell you about the reset and let you choose a higher EMI, a longer tenure or a mix of both, and to offer a switch to a fixed rate.

## A small prepayment early on

Floating rate loans taken by individuals carry no foreclosure or part prepayment charges. Paying ₹2,00,000 once, just after the 12th EMI, while keeping the EMI unchanged, closes the loan in 206 months instead of 240, about 2.8 years sooner, and saves **₹6,87,516** of interest. The same amount paid in year 15 saves far less because little interest is left to avoid.

## How banks check what EMI you can afford

Lenders look at the fixed obligation to income ratio (FOIR): all EMIs, including the new one, as a share of net monthly income. Many cap it around 50% for salaried borrowers. With a take home pay of ₹1,00,000 and an existing car EMI of ₹10,000, a 50% FOIR leaves ₹40,000 for the new EMI, which at 8.5% over 20 years supports a loan of about **₹46,09,234**. Your CIBIL score, age and employer category then decide the rate and the final sanction.

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing EMIs at different tenures.** A lower EMI over 25 years can cost lakhs more than a slightly higher EMI over 20; compare total interest instead.
- **Ignoring pre-EMI on an under-construction flat.** Until full disbursement you pay interest only on the amount released, and none of it reduces the principal.
- **Using the flat rate an NBFC or dealer quotes.** A "flat" 7% on a car loan charges interest on the original amount throughout and works out to about 12.5% on the reducing balance this calculator uses.
- **Forgetting loan insurance bundled into the amount.** A single premium policy added to the loan raises the EMI and is charged interest for the full term.
- **Not checking the reset after a rate hike.** If the bank silently extends the tenure, a loan can run past your retirement age; ask for the revised schedule.
