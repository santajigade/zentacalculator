## How to use the battery bank size calculator

Enter the **load power**, the **backup time**, the **bank voltage**, the **inverter efficiency**, the **allowed depth of discharge**, a **design margin** and the **voltage of one battery**. The calculator returns the energy the load uses, the amp-hour capacity the bank must have and how many batteries to connect in series. Choose the next standard Ah size above the result.

## Battery sizing formula

<p class="formula">Ah = (P × t) ÷ (V<sub>bank</sub> × η × DoD) × k &nbsp;·&nbsp; N<sub>series</sub> = V<sub>bank</sub> ÷ V<sub>battery</sub></p>

## Example

A 500 W load must run for 4 hours from a 48 V lead-acid bank through a 90% efficient inverter, discharging to 80% with a 1.25 ageing margin:

- Energy = 500 × 4 = **2,000 Wh**
- Ah = 2000 ÷ (48 × 0.9 × 0.8) × 1.25 = **72.3 Ah**
- Series batteries = 48 ÷ 12 = **4** → four 12 V, 75 Ah (or larger) batteries

## Capacity needed on a 48 V bank

90% inverter efficiency, 80% depth of discharge, 1.25 margin:

| Load | 1 h | 2 h | 4 h | 8 h |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 250 W | 9 Ah | 18 Ah | 36 Ah | 72 Ah |
| 500 W | 18 Ah | 36 Ah | 72 Ah | 145 Ah |
| 1,000 W | 36 Ah | 72 Ah | 145 Ah | 289 Ah |
| 2,000 W | 72 Ah | 145 Ah | 289 Ah | 579 Ah |
| 3,000 W | 109 Ah | 217 Ah | 434 Ah | 868 Ah |
| 5,000 W | 181 Ah | 362 Ah | 723 Ah | 1,447 Ah |

## Practical notes

- Lead-acid batteries last much longer when discharged to only 50%; LiFePO₄ tolerates 80–90% routinely.
- At 0–10 °C, lead-acid capacity falls by 10–20% — add a temperature correction factor.
- The charger must be able to recharge the bank in a reasonable time; see the [battery charge time calculator](/electronics/battery-charge-time/).
- For the UPS kVA rating itself, use the [UPS sizing calculator](/electrical/ups-sizing/).

## Building the bank from real batteries

The default case needs 72.3 Ah at 48 V, which is 4 batteries of 12 V in series. Two common ways to meet it:

- **One string of 100 Ah batteries:** 4 batteries. Backup = 48 × 100 × 0.9 × 0.8 ÷ 1.25 ÷ 500 = **5.53 h**, comfortably above the 4 h target.
- **50 Ah batteries:** 72.3 ÷ 50 rounds up to 2 parallel strings, so 8 batteries in total.

Fewer, larger cells are better. Each extra parallel string adds connections and lets current share unevenly between strings, so most makers advise no more than three or four.

## Same load on different bank voltages

The energy stays at 2,000 Wh, but the Ah and the battery current change with voltage:

| Bank voltage | 12 V batteries in series | Battery current | Capacity needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 V | 1 | 46.3 A | 289.4 Ah |
| 24 V | 2 | 23.1 A | 144.7 Ah |
| 48 V | 4 | 11.6 A | 72.3 Ah |

On a 12 V bank the cables carry four times the current of a 48 V bank, which is why anything above a few hundred watts is better built at 24 or 48 V.

## Effect of battery chemistry

Depth of discharge has the largest effect after the load itself. For the default 500 W, 4 h load on 48 V:

| Battery type | Typical DoD | Capacity needed |
|---|---|---|
| Flooded lead-acid, long life | 50% | 115.7 Ah |
| AGM or gel | 60% | 96.5 Ah |
| Lead-acid, occasional use | 80% | 72.3 Ah |
| LiFePO₄ | 90% | 64.3 Ah |

A long-life flooded bank needs 116 Ah where LiFePO₄ needs 64 Ah, which narrows the price gap between the two.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving out the inverter's own consumption.** A 25 W idle draw adds 100 Wh over 4 h and raises the requirement to 76.0 Ah.
- **Adding Ah across a series string.** Four 12 V, 100 Ah batteries in series make 48 V, 100 Ah, not 400 Ah.
- **Mixing old and new batteries.** The weakest unit sets the string capacity and is driven into deep discharge first.
- **Sizing for the rated load instead of the actual load.** Measure the real average power; a nominal 500 W inverter rarely runs at 500 W for hours.
- **Skipping the margin for lithium.** LiFePO₄ also loses capacity with cycles, so keep a factor of at least 1.1 to 1.25.
