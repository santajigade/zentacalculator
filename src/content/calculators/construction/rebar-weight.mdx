## How to use the rebar weight calculator

Choose the **bar diameter**, then enter the **length** and **number of bars**. The weight per metre and total weight are calculated.

## Rebar weight formula

<p class="formula">kg/m = d² ÷ 162.2</p>

## Example

10 bars of 12 mm, each 12 m long:

- Weight per metre = 144 ÷ 162.2 = **0.888 kg/m**
- Total = 0.888 × 12 × 10 = **106.5 kg**

## Where the formula comes from

Steel density is 7,850 kg/m³. For a 1 m bar of diameter d (mm):

<p class="formula">Weight = π × (d ÷ 2000)² × 1 × 7,850 = d² ÷ 162.2</p>

## Estimating steel for a slab

Typical residential RCC uses roughly 80–120 kg of steel per m³ of concrete for slabs and 150–250 kg/m³ for columns and beams. Use these only for rough budgets — the structural design gives exact quantities.

## Laps and wastage

Add for lap lengths (often 40–50 × bar diameter), bends and offcuts. Allow 3–5% wastage for cutting.

## Worked example: steel for a slab mesh

A 4 m × 5 m slab has 10 mm bars at 150 mm spacing in both directions with 25 mm end cover. Bars along the 4 m side are spaced over the 5 m width: (5 − 0.05) ÷ 0.15 + 1 = **34 bars** of 3.95 m. Bars along the 5 m side number (4 − 0.05) ÷ 0.15 + 1 = **27 bars** of 4.95 m. The total length is 34 × 3.95 + 27 × 4.95 = 267.95 m, and at 0.617 kg/m the mesh weighs **165.2 kg**. Adding 5% for bends, chairs and offcuts brings the order to about 173 kg. Laps add more where bars must be joined: a 50 d lap on a 16 mm bar is 0.80 m, or 1.26 kg of extra steel at every joint.

## Checking delivered bars by weight

Mills roll bars to a nominal mass, and IS 1786 allows a batch tolerance on mass per metre. A quick site check is to cut a 1 m piece, weigh it and compare:

| Bar | Nominal kg/m | Batch tolerance | Acceptable kg/m |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 mm | 0.395 | ±7% | 0.367 to 0.422 |
| 10 mm | 0.617 | ±7% | 0.573 to 0.660 |
| 12 mm | 0.888 | ±5% | 0.843 to 0.932 |
| 16 mm | 1.578 | ±5% | 1.499 to 1.657 |
| 20 mm | 2.466 | ±3% | 2.392 to 2.540 |
| 25 mm | 3.853 | ±3% | 3.738 to 3.969 |
| 32 mm | 6.313 | ±3% | 6.124 to 6.503 |

For example, a 1 m piece of 12 mm bar weighing 0.842 kg is -5.2% below the nominal 0.888 kg/m, outside the 5% band, so the batch should be rejected or billed by actual weight. Underweight bars also mean less steel area in the structure.

## Weight by the bundle

The default inputs, 10 bars of 12 mm at 12 m each, give **106.5 kg**. Steel is often priced per tonne, so divide by 1,000 and multiply by the rate. Suppliers may bill on theoretical weight (this formula) or on weighbridge weight; agree which in the order.

## Common mistakes

- **Putting the diameter in cm or inches.** The 162.2 divisor needs millimetres; 1.2 entered for 12 mm gives a weight 100 times too small.
- **Using 162 for big orders.** The rounded divisor overstates the weight by about 0.1%, which is around 1 kg per tonne; small, but worth noting on large bills.
- **Ignoring laps and hooks.** Standard hooks and bends add length beyond the clear span; take cutting lengths from the bar bending schedule.
- **Counting bars as span ÷ spacing.** You need one more bar than the number of gaps.
- **Measuring over the ribs.** TMT bars are sold by nominal diameter; calipers over the ribs read larger and overestimate weight.
