## How to use the SIP calculator

Enter your **monthly investment**, **expected annual return** and **period**. You see the future value, the total you put in and the returns earned. To plan for a goal, press × on Monthly investment and type the target amount.

## SIP formula

<p class="formula">FV = M × [((1 + i)<sup>n</sup> − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)</p>

- **i** = annual return ÷ 12
- **n** = years × 12
- The extra (1 + i) assumes you invest at the start of each month, as with most SIPs.

## Worked example

5,000 per month at 12% a year for 10 years:

- Total invested = 5,000 × 120 = **600,000**
- Future value = **1,161,695**
- Returns earned = **561,695**

## The power of time

At 5,000 per month and 12%:

| Years | Invested | Future value |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 300,000 | 412,432 |
| 10 | 600,000 | 1,161,695 |
| 15 | 900,000 | 2,522,880 |
| 20 | 1,200,000 | 4,995,740 |
| 25 | 1,500,000 | 9,488,175 |

Doubling the time from 10 to 20 years doubles what you put in but more than **quadruples** the result. Starting early matters more than investing a lot.

## SIP vs lump sum

| | SIP | Lump sum |
|---|---|---|
| Invests | A fixed amount every month | Everything at once |
| Best when | You invest from monthly income | You already have the money |
| Market timing risk | Low — buys at many prices | High — one entry price |
| Rupee cost averaging | Yes | No |

Use the [lumpsum calculator](/finance/lumpsum/) to compare, or combine both: invest a lump sum and run a SIP alongside it.

## Rupee cost averaging

A SIP buys more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high. Over time your average cost per unit is lower than the average price over the same period. It does not guarantee a profit, but it removes the stress of timing the market.

## Raise your SIP every year

Increasing your SIP by 10% every year can nearly double the final corpus over 20 years. See the [step-up SIP calculator](/finance/step-up-sip/).

## When to stop or pause

- Do **not** stop a SIP just because markets fall — that is when it buys the most units.
- Review the fund once a year against its benchmark.
- Shift to safer funds 2–3 years before you need the money.

## Keep in mind

- Returns are not guaranteed and markets can fall.
- Adjust the target for inflation with the [inflation calculator](/finance/inflation/).
- Fund expenses and taxes reduce the final amount.

## How sensitive the result is to the return

The default plan (5,000 a month for 10 years at 12%) grows to **1,161,695** from 600,000 invested, a gain of 561,695. The return is the input you control least, so it is worth seeing the same plan at other rates:

| Annual return | Future value | Gain | Multiple of money invested |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6% | 823,494 | 223,494 | 1.37× |
| 8% | 920,828 | 320,828 | 1.53× |
| 10% | 1,032,760 | 432,760 | 1.72× |
| 12% | 1,161,695 | 561,695 | 1.94× |
| 14% | 1,310,457 | 710,457 | 2.18× |

Four percentage points of return separate a plan that grows modestly from one that nearly doubles your money. Use a conservative figure for planning and treat anything above it as a bonus.

## What the corpus is worth in today's money

At 6% inflation, 1,161,695 in 10 years buys what **648,685** buys today, only a little more than the 600,000 you put in. Real growth is what matters for a goal, so inflate the goal amount first with the [inflation calculator](/finance/inflation/), then solve for the monthly SIP.

## Costs and tax in India

- **Expense ratio.** A regular plan often costs about 1% a year more than the direct plan of the same fund. At 11% instead of 12% the default plan ends at 1,094,936, 66,759 less.
- **Tax on equity funds.** Under the rates in force since July 2024, units held over 12 months are long term, with gains above 1.25 lakh a year taxed at 12.5%; units held 12 months or less are taxed at 20%. Each instalment has its own holding period, so in a redemption the most recent 12 instalments are still short term. If the whole gain of 561,695 were long term, the tax would be about 56,770 including 4% cess.
- **Debt funds** bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at your slab rate whatever the holding period.

Check the current Budget for any change before redeeming a large amount.

## Common mistakes

- **Projecting a recent bull market return.** Trailing 3 year returns after a rally can be far above the long run average.
- **Reading the rate as an annual effective return.** The formula compounds 12% ÷ 12 each month, which is an effective 12.68% a year, so a fund's annual CAGR of 12% corresponds to a slightly lower monthly figure.
- **Judging a SIP by absolute return.** Money invested in the last few months has had little time to grow; compare SIPs by XIRR, which weights each instalment by its time in the market.
- **Forgetting the exit load.** Many equity funds charge about 1% on units redeemed within a year of purchase, which applies to each recent instalment.
