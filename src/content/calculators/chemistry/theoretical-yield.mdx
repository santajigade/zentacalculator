## How to use the theoretical yield calculator

Enter the **mass of the limiting reagent**, its **molar mass**, the **coefficients** of the reagent and the product from the balanced equation, and the **molar mass of the product**. The calculator returns the moles and grams of product. Enter your **actual yield** to see the percent yield.

## Theoretical yield formula

<p class="formula">m<sub>theoretical</sub> = (m<sub>reagent</sub> / M<sub>reagent</sub>) × (p / r) × M<sub>product</sub></p>

<p class="formula">% yield = m<sub>actual</sub> / m<sub>theoretical</sub> × 100%</p>

## Worked example: aspirin synthesis

2.00 g salicylic acid (138.12 g/mol) reacts 1 : 1 to give aspirin (180.16 g/mol); 2.10 g of aspirin is recovered:

- Moles = 2.00 ÷ 138.12 = **0.01448 mol**
- Theoretical yield = 0.01448 × 180.16 = **2.609 g**
- Percent yield = 2.10 ÷ 2.609 = **80.5%**

## Theoretical yield from 1 kg of reagent

| Reaction | Product : reagent | Theoretical yield |
|---|---|---|
| Salicylic acid → aspirin | 1:1 | 1,304.4 g aspirin |
| Glucose → ethanol (fermentation) | 2:1 | 511.4 g ethanol |
| CaCO₃ → CaO (calcination) | 1:1 | 560.3 g CaO |
| Fe₂O₃ → Fe (reduction) | 2:1 | 699.4 g Fe |
| CH₄ → CO₂ (combustion) | 1:1 | 2,743.2 g CO₂ |

## Why actual yield is lower

- Incomplete reaction or an equilibrium that does not go to completion
- Side reactions forming other products
- Losses in filtration, transfer, washing and recrystallisation

## Checking which reagent is limiting

In the aspirin synthesis the salicylic acid is reacted with acetic anhydride (C₄H₆O₃, 102.09 g/mol). A typical recipe adds 5.0 mL of anhydride, which at 1.08 g/mL is 5.40 g or 0.0529 mol. That is 3.65 times the 0.01448 mol of salicylic acid, so the anhydride is in excess and the salicylic acid really is the limiting reagent. Basing the yield on the anhydride would overstate the theoretical yield by the same factor.

## Second example: a 1 : 2 mole ratio

Fermentation turns one glucose into two ethanol: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2 C₂H₅OH + 2 CO₂. For 50 g of glucose (180.156 g/mol), set r = 1, p = 2 and the product molar mass to 46.069 g/mol:

- Moles of glucose = 50 ÷ 180.156 = 0.27754 mol
- Moles of ethanol = 0.27754 × 2 ÷ 1 = **0.55507 mol**
- Theoretical yield = 0.55507 × 46.069 = **25.57 g** of ethanol

So the theoretical ethanol yield is 51.1% of the sugar mass; the rest leaves as CO₂. Yeast also burns some sugar for growth, so real fermentations reach only part of this figure.

## Theoretical yield, atom economy and overall yield

Theoretical yield says how much product the limiting reagent can make. **Atom economy** says how much of all the reactant mass can end up in the product. For aspirin it is 180.16 ÷ (138.12 + 102.09) = **75.0%**, because one acetic acid is made as a by-product for every aspirin. A reaction can have a 100% yield and still a poor atom economy.

In a multistep synthesis the overall yield is the product of the step yields. Three steps at 80%, 85% and 90% give 61.2% overall:

| Steps | 90% per step | 80% per step | 70% per step |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 90.0% | 80.0% | 70.0% |
| 2 | 81.0% | 64.0% | 49.0% |
| 3 | 72.9% | 51.2% | 34.3% |
| 5 | 59.0% | 32.8% | 16.8% |
| 8 | 43.0% | 16.8% | 5.8% |

This is why process chemists fight for every percent in a long route.

## Common mistakes

- **Inverting the mole ratio.** Using r ÷ p instead of p ÷ r in the fermentation example gives 6.39 g instead of 25.57 g, a factor of four too low.
- **Ignoring reagent purity.** With 98% pure salicylic acid only 1.96 g is reactant, so the theoretical yield drops to 2.557 g and the same 2.10 g gives 82.1%, not 80.5%.
- **Using the wrong molar mass form.** A hydrate or salt (for example sodium salicylate instead of salicylic acid) has a different molar mass; use the form you actually weighed.
- **Weighing a damp product.** Solvent left in the crystals inflates the actual yield, sometimes above 100%. Dry to constant mass before weighing.
- **Basing the calculation on an unbalanced equation.** The coefficients r and p must come from the balanced equation, or the mole ratio is meaningless.
