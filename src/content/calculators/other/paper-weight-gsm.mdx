## How to use the paper weight calculator

Choose the **paper size**, enter the **GSM** and the **number of sheets**. The weight per sheet and total weight are calculated — useful for postage and shipping.

## Paper weight formula

<p class="formula">weight = GSM × sheet area × sheets</p>

## Example

A ream of A4 at 80 GSM: 80 × 0.06237 = 4.99 g per sheet → **2.49 kg** per 500 sheets.

## GSM guide

| GSM | Typical use |
|---|---|
| 60–70 | Newsprint, notebooks |
| 75–80 | Everyday office printer paper |
| 90–120 | Letterheads, quality documents |
| 130–170 | Flyers, brochures, posters |
| 200–250 | Brochure covers, light card |
| 300–350 | Business cards, postcards, invitations |
| 400+ | Premium business cards, packaging |

## Paper sizes and areas

| Size | Dimensions | Area | Weight of 1 sheet at 80 gsm |
|---|---|---|---|
| A3 | 297 × 420 mm | 0.1247 m² | 9.98 g |
| A4 | 210 × 297 mm | 0.0624 m² | 4.99 g |
| A5 | 148 × 210 mm | 0.0311 m² | 2.49 g |
| US Letter | 216 × 279 mm | 0.0603 m² | 4.82 g |
| US Legal | 216 × 356 mm | 0.0769 m² | 6.15 g |

An A0 sheet is exactly 1 m², so a sheet of A0 at 80 gsm weighs 80 g. Each smaller A size is half the previous one.

## GSM vs US paper weight (lb)

US paper is labelled by the weight of 500 sheets in its basic size, which differs by paper type:

| US weight | Approx. GSM |
|---|---|
| 20 lb bond | 75 |
| 24 lb bond | 90 |
| 80 lb text | 120 |
| 80 lb cover | 216 |
| 100 lb cover | 270 |

## Postage

Postage often depends on weight. Ten A4 sheets at 80 gsm weigh about 50 g, plus about 5–10 g for a C4 envelope.

## Worked example: a ream and a carton

The default inputs are a ream of 500 A4 sheets (0.06237 m² each) at 80 GSM:

- One sheet: 80 × 0.06237 = **4.99 g**
- One ream: 4.9896 × 500 ÷ 1000 = **2.495 kg**
- A carton of 5 reams: 12.47 kg of paper, plus about 0.5 kg for the wrappers and box

## Worked example: weight of a printed booklet

A 24 page A4 saddle stitched booklet is printed on A3 sheets folded in half. Each A3 sheet gives 4 pages, so it needs 6 sheets: 5 inner sheets on 100 GSM and 1 cover sheet on 250 GSM.

- Inner: 5 × 0.12474 × 100 = 62.4 g
- Cover: 1 × 0.12474 × 250 = 31.2 g
- One booklet: **93.6 g**, so a run of 200 weighs 18.7 kg before packing

Count leaves (sheets), not pages: one A4 leaf printed both sides carries 2 pages.

## Finding the GSM of an unknown paper

Weigh 10 sheets on a kitchen or postal scale and divide by the total area. If 10 A4 sheets weigh 62.4 g, the paper is 62.4 ÷ (10 × 0.06237) = **100 GSM**. Weighing 10 or more sheets keeps the 1 g resolution of a cheap scale from spoiling the result.

GSM is not thickness. Thickness (caliper) also depends on how dense the paper is: uncoated office paper is around 0.8 g/cm³, so 80 GSM is roughly 100 µm thick and a ream stands about 50 mm tall, while a glossy coated paper of the same GSM is thinner and feels lighter in the hand.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating US "lb" as GSM.** 20 lb bond and 80 lb text are basis weights of a ream in different base sizes; 20 lb bond is about 75 GSM, not 20.
- **Counting pages instead of sheets.** A 100 page document printed double sided uses 50 sheets.
- **Forgetting the cover stock.** Cards and covers at 250 to 350 GSM can weigh as much as several inner sheets.
- **Ignoring packaging.** Envelopes, wrappers and cartons often push a parcel into the next postage band.
- **Expecting exact figures.** Mills allow a GSM tolerance of a few percent, and paper gains weight in humid storage.
