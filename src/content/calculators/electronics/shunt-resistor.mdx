## How to use the shunt resistor calculator

Enter the shunt's **rated current** and **full-scale millivolt drop** (from the nameplate, for example 50 A / 75 mV), and the **current** you are measuring. The calculator returns the shunt resistance, its power at full scale, and the voltage drop and power at your current. To design a current-sense resistor, enter the current and the drop your amplifier or ADC needs.

## Shunt resistor formulas

<p class="formula">R = V<sub>FS</sub> ÷ I<sub>FS</sub> &nbsp;·&nbsp; V = I × R &nbsp;·&nbsp; P = I² × R</p>

## Example

A 50 A / 75 mV DC shunt carrying 30 A:

- R = 0.075 ÷ 50 = **1.50 mΩ**
- Voltage drop = 30 × 1.50 mΩ = **45 mV**
- Power = 30² × 0.0015 = **1.35 W** (full scale: **3.75 W**)

## Notes

- Low-side shunts are easy to read but lift the load's ground; high-side shunts need a current-sense amplifier with enough common-mode range.
- Manganin shunts have a very low temperature coefficient (about 15 ppm/°C), which keeps the reading stable as they warm.
- Check the ADC resolution against the small shunt signal with the [ADC resolution calculator](/electronics/adc-resolution/).

| 120% | 60.0 A | 90.0 mV | 5.40 W |

Most manufacturers recommend running at no more than about two-thirds of the rating continuously. For this shunt that is 33.3 A, where it dissipates 1.67 W instead of the 3.75 W at full scale, keeping its temperature rise and drift low.

## Reading the shunt with an ADC

At the default 30 A the shunt gives 45 mV, too small to feed an ADC directly. A current-sense amplifier with a gain of 20 raises it to **0.90 V**, and full scale becomes 1.5 V. A 12-bit ADC with a 3.3 V reference has steps of 0.806 mV, which corresponds to 0.806 mV ÷ 20 ÷ 1.5 mΩ = **26.9 mA** per count, so 30 A is resolved to better than 0.1%.

## Error sources in practice

- **Contact resistance.** Measuring across the main lugs with a bolted joint of 0.5 mΩ reads 33% high; the sense screws avoid this.
- **Thermal EMF.** Copper to manganin junctions produce a few microvolts per degree. A 10 µV offset at 1 A, where the signal is only 1.5 mV, is a 0.67% error, so read low currents with care.
- **Temperature coefficient.** A 50 °C rise changes a manganin shunt by about 0.075%, whereas a length of copper used as a shunt would change by 19.5%.

For AC above a few kilohertz, the shunt's small self-inductance adds a reactive voltage, so use a coaxial or low-inductance shunt for fast current waveforms.

## Common mistakes

- **Mismatching meter and shunt ratings.** A panel meter scaled for 50 A / 50 mV on a 75 mV shunt reads 45 A when 30 A flows, 50% high.
- **Connecting sense leads under the current bolts.** Take the voltage only from the dedicated sense terminals.
- **Running at 100% continuously.** Heat raises drift and can soften solder joints; derate to about two-thirds.
- **Placing a low-side shunt in a system with other ground paths.** Current bypassing the shunt through chassis or communication grounds is not measured.
- **Using an amplifier without enough common-mode range** on a high-side shunt in a 48 V or higher system.
