## How to use the PPM calculator

Enter the **mass of the substance** and the **mass of the sample**. You get ppm, percent and ppb. For water, 1 kg ≈ 1 L, so mg/kg ≈ mg/L ≈ ppm.

## PPM formula

<p class="formula">ppm = m<sub>substance</sub> ÷ m<sub>sample</sub> × 10⁶</p>

## Worked example

50 mg of chlorine in 1 kg (1 L) of water:

- Substance mass: 50 mg = 0.050 g = 0.000050 kg
- ppm = 0.000050 kg ÷ 1 kg × 10⁶ = **50 ppm**
- Percent = 50 ÷ 10,000 = **0.005%**
- Parts per billion = 50 × 1,000 = **50,000 ppb**

## Typical values

| Example | Concentration |
|---|---|
| Drinking-water chlorine (typical) | 0.2–1 ppm |
| WHO drinking-water fluoride limit | 1.5 ppm |
| CO₂ in air | ~420 ppm |
| Sea-water salt | ~35,000 ppm |

## ppm, mg/L and percent

| ppm | mg/L (in water) | Percent |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 0.0001% |
| 10 | 10 | 0.001% |
| 100 | 100 | 0.01% |
| 500 | 500 | 0.05% |
| 1,000 | 1,000 | 0.1% |
| 5,000 | 5,000 | 0.5% |
| 10,000 | 10,000 | 1% |

For dilute water solutions, 1 ppm ≈ 1 mg/L because 1 L of water weighs about 1 kg.

## Drinking water guidelines (WHO)

| Substance | Guideline value |
|---|---|
| Fluoride | 1.5 mg/L |
| Nitrate (as NO₃⁻) | 50 mg/L |
| Arsenic | 0.01 mg/L (10 ppb) |
| Lead | 0.01 mg/L (10 ppb) |

Local standards may be stricter. Many countries also set limits for total dissolved solids (TDS) for taste, often around 500–1,000 mg/L.

## ppm in gases

In air, ppm is usually by **volume** (ppmv). Carbon dioxide in outdoor air is now above 420 ppm. Indoor CO₂ above about 1,000 ppm suggests poor ventilation.

## Dosing chlorine

To dose a 10,000 L tank to 2 ppm free chlorine with 10% sodium hypochlorite: chlorine needed = 10,000 × 2 = 20,000 mg = 20 g, so about **200 mL** of 10% solution (assuming a density close to 1 g/mL; real bleach is slightly denser).

## Conversions

1% = 10,000 ppm · 1 ppm = 1,000 ppb · 1 ppm = 1 mg/kg

## Working backwards to a mass

To hit a target ppm, multiply the target by the sample mass in kilograms to get milligrams. A 20 kg batch of water at 250 ppm sodium needs 250 × 20 = **5,000 mg (5 g) of sodium**. Sodium is only 39.34% of the mass of sodium chloride (22.990 ÷ 58.443), so the salt to weigh out is 5 ÷ 0.3934 = **12.71 g of NaCl**. Always check whether a limit is stated for the ion or for the whole compound.

## When ppm and mg/L are not the same

ppm by mass is milligrams per kilogram; mg/L is milligrams per litre. They match only when a litre of the sample weighs 1 kg. Seawater at 35,000 mg/kg with a density of 1.025 kg/L holds 35,000 × 1.025 = **35,875 mg/L**, a 2.5% difference. For brines, acids, oils and soils, report mg/kg and mg/L separately.

## Gas ppm (by volume) to mg/m³

For gases ppm usually means ppmv, a mole fraction. To convert to a mass concentration at 25 °C and 1 atm, use mg/m³ = ppmv × M ÷ 24.47, where M is the molar mass in g/mol and 24.47 L/mol is the molar volume at those conditions.

| Gas | M (g/mol) | mg/m³ per 1 ppmv | mg/m³ at 50 ppmv |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon monoxide, CO | 28.01 | 1.145 | 57.2 |
| Ammonia, NH₃ | 17.03 | 0.696 | 34.8 |
| Hydrogen sulfide, H₂S | 34.08 | 1.393 | 69.6 |
| Carbon dioxide, CO₂ | 44.01 | 1.799 | 89.9 |
| Sulfur dioxide, SO₂ | 64.06 | 2.618 | 130.9 |
| Nitrogen dioxide, NO₂ | 46.01 | 1.880 | 94.0 |

The same 50 ppm is almost four times heavier for sulfur dioxide than for ammonia, so a gas detector set in ppm and an emission limit set in mg/m³ have to be converted per gas. This calculator itself works by mass; use the table for gas readings.

## How ppm is measured in practice

- **Dissolved solids:** a TDS meter measures electrical conductivity and multiplies by a factor of roughly 0.5 to 0.8 that depends on which salts are present. A reading of 800 µS/cm with a factor of 0.65 is reported as about 520 mg/L.
- **Chlorine:** handheld kits use the DPD colour reaction, and online analyzers in treatment plants use amperometric or colorimetric cells that read free chlorine continuously in mg/L.
- **Trace metals:** ICP-OES and ICP-MS measure elements such as lead and arsenic down to ppb and below after acid digestion of the sample.
- **Gases:** NDIR sensors read CO₂ in ppm, and electrochemical cells read CO and H₂S for personal gas detectors.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing units in the ratio.** Grams over kilograms is a factor of 1,000 off; the calculator converts, but hand calculations must put both masses in the same unit before multiplying by 10⁶.
- **Confusing ppm by mass with ppmv.** 50 ppmv of CO₂ in air is not 50 mg/kg; convert with the molar mass.
- **Assuming mg/L equals ppm in dense liquids** such as seawater or concentrated brine.
- **Dividing by 100 instead of 10,000 for percent.** 50 ppm is 0.005%, not 0.5%.
- **Reporting the compound when the limit is for the ion**, for example nitrate as NO₃⁻ versus nitrate nitrogen (NO₃-N), which differ by a factor of about 4.43.
