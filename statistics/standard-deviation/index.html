    Standard deviation measures how far values typically sit from their mean; a small value means the data are tightly clustered. This standard deviation calculator takes a pasted list and returns the sample and population results, variance, mean and count together.

    Students check homework with it, analysts use it to describe spread in survey and sales data, and engineers rely on it to judge the repeatability of measurements and the capability of a process.

    How to use the standard deviation calculator

    Paste or type your numbers, separated by commas, spaces or new lines — you can paste a column from Excel. All results update instantly.

    Standard deviation formulas

    s = √( Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ (n − 1) ) σ = √( Σ(x − μ)² ÷ n )

    Worked example

    Data: 4, 8, 6, 5, 3, 7, 9, 5 (n = 8)

    • Mean = 47 ÷ 8 = 5.875
    • Sum of squared deviations = 28.875
    • Sample variance = 4.1250 → s = 2.0310
    • Population variance = 3.6094 → σ = 1.8998

    Interpreting standard deviation

    For roughly bell-shaped data, about 68% of values fall within 1 SD of the mean and 95% within 2 SD. See the empirical rule calculator.

    Step-by-step example

    Data: 4, 8, 6, 5, 7.

    x x − mean (6) (x − mean)²
    4 −2 4
    8 2 4
    6 0 0
    5 −1 1
    7 1 1
    Sum 10
    • Sample variance = 10 ÷ (5 − 1) = 2.5, sample SD = 1.581
    • Population variance = 10 ÷ 5 = 2, population SD = 1.414

    Sample or population?

    • Use sample SD (n − 1) when your data are a sample from a larger group — the usual case.
    • Use population SD (n) when you have every member of the group.

    Dividing by n − 1 (Bessel’s correction) makes the sample variance an unbiased estimate of the population variance.

    Standard deviation vs standard error

    The SD describes the spread of individual values. The standard error (SD ÷ √n) describes how precisely the mean is estimated. See the standard error calculator.

    Units

    The SD has the same units as the data; the variance has squared units. That is why SD is easier to interpret: “heights vary by about 7 cm” rather than “49 cm²”.

    In Excel

    Sample: =STDEV.S(range) · Population: =STDEV.P(range)

    Standard deviation in quality control

    In manufacturing and measurement, standard deviation shows whether a process can stay within its limits. The process capability index compares the tolerance with the spread:

    Cp = (USL − LSL) ÷ 6σ

    A shaft specified at 10.0 ± 0.3 mm with σ = 0.08 mm gives Cp = 0.6 ÷ 0.48 = 1.25. Many industries ask for at least 1.33.

    Combining uncertainties

    Independent random errors combine as the root sum of squares, not by simple addition. A sensor with σ = 0.3 and a transmitter with σ = 0.4 give a combined σ of √(0.3² + 0.4²) = 0.5, not 0.7.

    Reading the default results

    For the default list the calculator shows s = 2.0310, σ = 1.8998 and a mean of 5.875. Two more useful figures follow directly. The coefficient of variation is 2.0310 ÷ 5.875 = 34.6%, so the values vary by about a third of their average. The standard error of the mean is 2.0310 ÷ √8 = 0.718, which tells you how uncertain the mean of 5.875 is, rather than how scattered the individual values are.

    Worked example: repeatability of a pressure gauge

    A technician applies a steady 6.000 bar from a dead-weight tester and records ten readings: 6.02, 5.98, 6.01, 6.03, 5.99, 6.00, 6.04, 5.97, 6.01, 6.02 bar.

    • Mean = 6.007 bar, so the average error is +7 mbar
    • Sample standard deviation s = 22.1 mbar, the repeatability of the gauge
    • Standard error of the mean = 22.1 ÷ √10 = 7.0 mbar
    • About 95% of single readings fall within ±2s = ±44 mbar of the mean

    If the gauge is specified as ±0.5% of a 10 bar span (±50 mbar), the ±44 mbar scatter plus the +7 mbar bias already reaches about 51 mbar, right at the edge of the tolerance, so the gauge is a candidate for recalibration or replacement.

    Quick estimate from the range

    On a shop floor, σ is often estimated from the range of a small subgroup: σ ≈ R ÷ d₂. The d₂ constants below are computed as the expected range of n values from a standard normal distribution.

    Subgroup size n d₂
    2 1.128
    3 1.693
    4 2.059
    5 2.326
    6 2.534
    8 2.847
    10 3.078

    For the ten gauge readings, R = 70 mbar gives σ ≈ 70 ÷ 3.078 = 22.7 mbar, close to the 22.1 mbar from the full calculation. For the default list, R = 6 and n = 8 give 2.11, against s = 2.03. The range method wastes information, so use it only for quick checks and control charts with small subgroups.

    Common mistakes

    • Using the population formula on a sample. It underestimates the spread, especially with fewer than about 30 values.
    • Adding standard deviations. Add variances, then take the square root.
    • Applying the 68 to 95 to 99.7 rule to skewed data. It only holds for roughly normal data. For incomes, waiting times or particle sizes, use percentiles instead.
    • Letting one outlier decide the result. Check the data for typing errors before you report it.
    • Using the shortcut Σx² − n x̄² with rounded numbers. For large values close together, such as 1,000.12 and 1,000.15, the two terms nearly cancel and small rounding wipes out the answer; work from deviations about the mean and keep full precision.

    Frequently asked questions

    Should I use sample or population standard deviation?

    Use the sample formula (divide by n − 1) when your data is a sample from a larger group — the usual case. Use the population formula (divide by n) only when you have every member of the group.

    What does standard deviation tell me?

    How spread out the values are around the mean. A small value means the data is tightly clustered.

    How is variance related?

    Variance is the square of the standard deviation.

    What is a good standard deviation?

    There is no universal value — it depends on the data. Compare it with the mean (see coefficient of variation) or with a target tolerance.

    Why divide by n − 1?

    Because the mean is estimated from the same data, which makes deviations slightly smaller. Dividing by n − 1 corrects this bias.

    What is the standard deviation of 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9?

    The population standard deviation is 2 and the sample standard deviation is 2.14. The mean is 5.

    Can standard deviation be negative?

    No. It is a square root of an average squared distance, so it is always zero or positive.

    How do I combine two standard deviations?

    For independent sources, add the variances and take the square root: √(0.3² + 0.4²) = 0.5. Never add the standard deviations directly.

    How can I estimate standard deviation from the range?

    Divide the range by the d₂ constant for your sample size: 1.128 for 2 values, 2.326 for 5 and 3.078 for 10. It is a quick estimate used on control charts; the full calculation is more precise.

    What is the coefficient of variation of my data?

    Divide the standard deviation by the mean and multiply by 100. For the default data it is 2.0310 ÷ 5.875 = 34.6%. It only makes sense for data measured from a true zero, such as length or weight.
