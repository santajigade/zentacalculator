## How to use the pregnancy due date calculator

Choose the **first day of your last period** and your average **cycle length**. Your estimated due date, how many weeks pregnant you are today, and the days remaining are calculated.

## Formula (Naegele's rule)

<p class="formula">Due date = LMP + 280 days + (cycle length − 28)</p>

## Pregnancy milestones

| Weeks | Milestone (typical) |
|---|---|
| 4–6 | Positive pregnancy test, heartbeat may be seen on ultrasound |
| 8–14 | First-trimester screening and dating scan in many countries |
| 18–22 | Detailed anatomy scan |
| 24 | Viability milestone often referenced in care |
| 28 | Start of third trimester |
| 37–42 | Term; most babies are born in this window |

Schedules differ by country and provider.

## How accurate is the due date?

Only about **4–5% of babies** arrive on their exact due date. Most are born within a week either side. An early ultrasound (before about 14 weeks) is usually more accurate than the date of the last period, and your care provider may adjust the due date after it.

## IVF due dates

For IVF pregnancies, the due date is based on the embryo transfer date: 266 days after egg retrieval, or 261 days after a 5-day embryo transfer.

## Irregular cycles

Naegele's rule assumes ovulation about 14 days before the next period. If cycles are irregular, the estimate is less reliable — an early scan gives a better date.

This calculator gives an estimate for planning. Always follow advice from your doctor or midwife.

The due date is 280 days (40 weeks) after the first day of your last period. Only about 4% of babies arrive on the exact day; most are born within two weeks either side.

## Due date from the conception date

If you know the conception date (for example from ovulation tracking), add **266 days**, which is 38 weeks. This gives the same result as counting 280 days from a period that started two weeks earlier. Conception on 15 January gives a due date of about **8 October**.

## Adjusting for cycle length

Naegele's rule assumes a 28-day cycle. If your cycle is longer or shorter, ovulation shifts by the same number of days, so the due date does too. With a 32-day cycle, add 4 days. With a 25-day cycle, take away 3 days. The calculator does this when you enter your cycle length.

## What the default values show

The calculator opens with a last period that started 70 days before today and a 28-day cycle. Seventy days is exactly **10.0 weeks**, so the result reads 10.0 weeks pregnant, and the due date falls 280 − 70 = **210 days** from today. Change the date to your own and the three results update together.

## Worked example with a longer cycle

Suppose the last period began on **3 Mar 2026** and cycles are usually **31 days**. Ovulation is then expected about 3 days later than in a 28-day cycle, so:

- Due date = 3 Mar 2026 + 280 + (31 − 28) days = **11 Dec 2026**
- Likely conception, about 14 days before the next period: **20 Mar 2026**, which is 266 days before the due date
- On 1 Jun 2026: (90 − 3) days ÷ 7 = **12.4 weeks**, or 12 weeks 3 days, with **193 days** to go
- 37 weeks (early term) is reached on 20 Nov 2026, three weeks before the due date

## How the date is confirmed in practice

At the first scan, usually between about 8 and 14 weeks, the sonographer measures the crown to rump length of the embryo. In the first trimester this length tracks age closely, to within about 5 to 7 days, which is better than a period date that relies on memory and a regular cycle. Professional guidance such as that from ACOG suggests keeping the period-based date unless the scan differs by more than a set margin (about 5 to 7 days in the first trimester), so a small difference usually leaves the date unchanged. After that point the agreed date is not normally moved again, even if later scans measure the baby larger or smaller.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting from the date of the positive test** or from the end of the period. Use the **first day** of the last period.
- **Treating the due date as a deadline.** Only about 1 in 25 babies arrive on the day. Most are born within two weeks either side.
- **Keeping the period-based date after a dating scan.** If an early ultrasound gives a different date, your doctor or midwife will usually use the scan date. Always follow their advice.
- **Taking a light bleed in early pregnancy for a period.** Spotting around the time a period was due can make the last period look 4 weeks later than it was, so the scan then measures much larger than expected.
- **Adding the cycle correction to an IVF pregnancy.** With IVF the transfer date fixes the embryo age, so the cycle length you enter here does not apply.
