## How to use this calculator

Enter the **base radius** and **height** to get the slant height, volume and surface areas. You can also solve for the height from a volume, or the radius from the slant height.

## Formulas

<p class="formula">s = √(r² + h²)   V = ⅓πr²h<br/>Lateral area = πrs   Total = πrs + πr²</p>

## Worked example

A cone with a 3 cm radius and 4 cm height:

- Slant height = √(9 + 16) = **5 cm**
- Volume = ⅓ × π × 9 × 4 = **37.7 cm³**
- Lateral area = π × 3 × 5 = **47.1 cm²**
- Total surface = 47.1 + 28.3 = **75.4 cm²**

## Cone vs cylinder

A cone holds exactly **one-third** of a cylinder with the same base and height. Fill a cone with water three times and it fills the cylinder.

## Frustum (cut-off cone)

Buckets, lampshades and many hoppers are frustums — cones with the top sliced off:

<p class="formula">V = π × h ÷ 3 × (R² + R·r + r²)</p>

A bucket 30 cm tall with a 15 cm top radius and 10 cm bottom radius holds π × 30 ÷ 3 × (225 + 150 + 100) = **14.92 litres**.

## Making a cone from paper

To roll a cone from a flat sheet, cut a sector with:

- **Radius** = the cone's slant height s
- **Angle** = 360° × r ÷ s

For a cone with r = 3 and s = 5, cut a sector of 216° from a circle of radius 5.

## Piles of sand and gravel

A loose pile forms a cone. Measure the base circumference and height, find r = C ÷ 2π, then use the volume formula. Multiply by the material density to get its weight.

## Practical uses

- **Silos and hoppers** have conical bottoms — add the cone volume to the cylinder above.
- **Piles of sand or gravel** form cones. Measure the base circumference and height to estimate the volume.
- **Funnels and paper cups** use the lateral area to work out how much material is needed.
