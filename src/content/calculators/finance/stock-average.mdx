## How to use the stock average calculator

Enter the **number of shares** and **price** for each purchase (up to three). The calculator shows total shares, total invested and the average price per share. Leave the third purchase at zero if you only bought twice.

To plan a purchase, type your **target average** in Average price and press × on the second purchase shares — the calculator finds how many to buy at the new price.

## Stock average formula

<p class="formula">Average price = (q₁p₁ + q₂p₂ + q₃p₃) ÷ (q₁ + q₂ + q₃)</p>

## Example: averaging down

| Purchase | Shares | Price | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | 100 | 250 | 25,000 |
| Second | 50 | 200 | 10,000 |
| **Total** | **150** | **233.33** | **35,000** |

The share now needs to rise only to 233.33 to break even, instead of 250.

## Shares needed to reach a target average

Starting with 100 shares at 250, buying at 200:

| Target average | Extra shares needed |
|---|---|
| 240 | 25 |
| 235 | 43 |
| 230 | 67 |
| 225 | 100 |
| 220 | 150 |
| 210 | 400 |

The closer the target is to the new price, the more shares you need — the cost rises steeply.

## Averaging down vs averaging up

- **Averaging down**: buying more as the price falls, lowering your average.
- **Averaging up**: buying more as the price rises, often used by momentum investors.

Both increase your position size. Set a maximum allocation per stock before you start.

## Tax lots

For capital gains tax, many systems use **first in, first out** (FIFO) to decide which shares were sold. Your average cost and your tax cost basis may differ — check local rules.

## Worked example with the default purchases

- First buy: 100 × 250 = 25,000
- Second buy: 50 × 200 = 10,000
- Total shares = 150; total invested = **35,000.00**
- Average = 35,000 ÷ 150 = **233.33** per share

The average fell by only 16.67 even though the second price was 50 lower, because the second lot was half the size of the first. If brokerage and charges of 0.1% are added to each buy, the true cost basis is 35,035.00 and the average becomes **233.57**.

## Average cost against FIFO when you sell part

Selling 60 shares at 240 shows a gain of 400.00 against the average cost, but under FIFO those shares come from the first lot at 250, giving a tax loss of -600.00. Brokers' portfolio screens usually show the average, while contract notes and tax statements may follow FIFO, so keep both views.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving out charges.** Brokerage, exchange fees, stamp duty and tax change the real break-even price.
- **Averaging across a split or bonus.** Adjust old quantities and prices for the split ratio before adding new buys.
- **Treating the average as a target price.** The market does not know your cost; judge the stock on its prospects.
- **Ignoring position size.** Each average down raises the share of money in one stock; set a cap before you start.
- **Mixing currencies or exchanges.** Convert purchases made in different currencies at the rate on each trade date.
