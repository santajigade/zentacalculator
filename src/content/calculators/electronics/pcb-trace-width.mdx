## How to use the PCB trace width calculator

Enter the **current**, the allowed **temperature rise** above ambient, the **copper weight** and whether the trace is on an **external or internal layer**. You get the required cross-section and minimum width in millimetres and mils.

For more background, read [Controlled Impedance PCB Traces](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/controlled-impedance-pcb-traces/).

## The IPC-2221 formula

<p class="formula">I = k × ΔT<sup>0.44</sup> × A<sup>0.725</sup></p>

with A in square mils, k = 0.048 for external layers and 0.024 for internal layers. Width = A ÷ (1.378 × copper oz).

## Trace width for 1 oz copper, 10 °C rise

| Current | External layer | Internal layer |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 A | 0.12 mm (5 mil) | 0.30 mm (12 mil) |
| 1 A | 0.30 mm (12 mil) | 0.78 mm (31 mil) |
| 2 A | 0.78 mm (31 mil) | 2.03 mm (80 mil) |
| 3 A | 1.37 mm (54 mil) | 3.56 mm (140 mil) |
| 5 A | 2.77 mm (109 mil) | 7.19 mm (283 mil) |
| 10 A | 7.19 mm (283 mil) | 18.71 mm (737 mil) |

## Copper weights

| Copper | Thickness |
|---|---|
| 0.5 oz | 17.5 µm |
| 1 oz | 35 µm |
| 2 oz | 70 µm |
| 3 oz | 105 µm |

Doubling copper weight roughly halves the width needed.

## Other checks

- **Voltage drop**: long, thin traces drop voltage. 1 oz copper has about 0.5 mΩ per square.
- **Vias**: a standard 0.3 mm via carries roughly 1–2 A; use several vias in parallel for high currents.
- **Spacing**: high-voltage traces need clearance and creepage per IPC-2221 or IEC 60664.

## Worked example with the default values

For 3 A on an outer layer of 1 oz copper with a 10 °C rise:

1. ΔT<sup>0.44</sup> = 10<sup>0.44</sup> = 2.754
2. A = (3 ÷ (0.048 × 2.754))<sup>1/0.725</sup> = 22.69<sup>1.379</sup> = **74.2 mil²**
3. Width = 74.2 ÷ (1.378 × 1) = **53.8 mil**, which is **1.37 mm**

The same current on an inner layer needs 140 mil (3.56 mm), 2.6 times wider. Halving k does not just double the area, because area appears with the exponent 0.725.

## Voltage drop along the trace

A 50 mm run of that 1.37 mm, 35 µm trace is 37 squares long. Using copper resistivity of 1.72 × 10<sup>−8</sup> Ω·m at 20 °C, R = ρL ÷ (w × t) = **18.0 mΩ**, so 3 A drops 54 mV and dissipates 162 mW. On a 3.3 V rail feeding a processor that drop may matter more than the heating, so check both.

## Width against allowed temperature rise at 3 A

| Rise | External, 1 oz | External, 2 oz | Internal, 1 oz |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 °C | 2.08 mm | 1.04 mm | 5.42 mm |
| 10 °C | 1.37 mm | 0.68 mm | 3.56 mm |
| 20 °C | 0.90 mm | 0.45 mm | 2.33 mm |
| 30 °C | 0.70 mm | 0.35 mm | 1.83 mm |
| 45 °C | 0.55 mm | 0.27 mm | 1.43 mm |

Allowing 20 °C instead of 10 °C shrinks the outer 1 oz trace to 0.90 mm. Add the rise to the worst case ambient inside the enclosure and keep the total well below the laminate rating, typically 130 °C for standard FR-4.

## Checking a built board

Run the board at full current and measure the trace with a thermal camera or a thermocouple taped to the copper, after it settles for 10 to 15 minutes. A four wire milliohm measurement across the trace also confirms the copper weight the fabricator delivered.

## Common mistakes

- **Sizing on the wide part and forgetting neck downs.** A trace that narrows to 0.3 mm at a connector or IC pad heats at that point; keep the full width as close to the pad as possible.
- **Assuming outer layer plating.** Fabricators quote base copper; 1 oz base usually finishes thicker on outer layers after plating, but inner layers stay at base thickness.
- **Using IPC-2221 outside its range.** The chart fits currents up to about 35 A, widths up to 400 mil and 10 to 100 °C rise; beyond that use IPC-2152 or thermal simulation.
- **Ignoring thermal reliefs and vias.** Spoke connections to a plane and a single via can carry less than the trace feeding them.
- **Putting parallel high current traces side by side.** Neighbouring hot traces heat each other, so the real rise is higher than the single trace figure.
