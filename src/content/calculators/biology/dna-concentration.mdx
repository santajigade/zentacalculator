## How to use the DNA / RNA concentration calculator

Choose the **nucleic acid type**, enter the **A260** and **A280** readings and the **dilution factor**. The concentration and purity ratio are calculated.

## DNA / RNA concentration formula

<p class="formula">c (µg/mL) = A260 × conversion factor × dilution</p>

## Example

dsDNA diluted 1:10 with A260 = 0.45 and A280 = 0.24:

- Concentration = 0.45 × 50 × 10 = **225 µg/mL**
- A260/A280 = **1.88** → acceptable purity

## Conversion factors

| Nucleic acid | 1 A260 unit equals |
|---|---|
| Double-stranded DNA | 50 µg/mL |
| Single-stranded DNA | 33 µg/mL |
| RNA | 40 µg/mL |
| Oligonucleotides | about 20–33 µg/mL (sequence-dependent) |

## Purity ratios

| Ratio | Pure DNA | Pure RNA | Low values suggest |
|---|---|---|---|
| A260/A280 | about 1.8 | about 2.0 | Protein or phenol contamination |
| A260/A230 | 2.0–2.2 | 2.0–2.2 | Salts, guanidine, carbohydrates |

## From concentration to copies

Once you know the mass, convert to copy number with the [DNA copy number calculator](/biology/dna-copy-number/) — essential for qPCR standards and cloning.

## Other quantification methods

| Method | Advantage |
|---|---|
| UV absorbance (A260) | Fast, no reagents |
| Fluorescent dyes (e.g. dsDNA-specific) | More sensitive, ignores RNA and free nucleotides |
| Gel electrophoresis | Shows size and integrity |

Fluorometric assays are preferred for low concentrations and for next-generation sequencing library preparation.

## Micro-volume spectrophotometers

Instruments that measure 1–2 µL drops use short path lengths and automatically normalise to 1 cm. Enter the reading as shown and set the dilution factor to 1 if you did not dilute.

## Reliable readings

Spectrophotometers are most accurate for A260 between about 0.1 and 1.0. Dilute concentrated samples and multiply back with the dilution factor.

| 0.05 | 2.5 | 1.7 | 2 |

If you diluted the sample before reading, multiply by the dilution factor. A 1:10 dilution reading A260 = 0.5 means dsDNA at 25 × 10 = **250 ng/µL**.

## How much DNA do you have?

Total yield = concentration × volume. 120 ng/µL in 50 µL is **6 µg** of DNA.

## What your purity ratios are telling you

| Reading | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 260/280 below 1.7 | Protein or phenol carry-over | Repeat the clean-up or add a chloroform step |
| 260/280 above 2.0 (DNA) | RNA contamination | RNase treatment |
| 260/230 below 1.8 | Guanidine salts, EDTA, carbohydrate or phenol | Extra wash or ethanol precipitation |
| A260 below 0.1 | Too dilute for a reliable reading | Use less dilution or a dye-based assay |
| A260 above 1.0 to 1.5 | Outside the linear range of many instruments | Dilute and measure again |

## Common mistakes

- **Blanking with the wrong liquid.** Blank with exactly the buffer the sample is dissolved in.
- **Measuring in unbuffered water.** Slightly acidic water lowers the 260/280 ratio by about 0.2 to 0.3. A slightly alkaline buffer such as TE (pH 8) gives more consistent ratios.
- **Using the dsDNA factor for RNA or oligos.** Use 50 for dsDNA, 40 for RNA and 33 for ssDNA, or the exact extinction coefficient for short oligos.
- **Forgetting the dilution factor** when the sample was diluted for the reading.
- **Not mixing before sampling.** Genomic DNA is viscous and settles. Mix gently and take the reading twice.

## From concentration to pipetting volume

At the default result of **225 ng/µL**, a 50 µL elution holds 225 × 50 ÷ 1,000 = **11.25 µg**. The volume to pipette for a set mass is mass ÷ concentration:

| DNA needed | Volume of 225 ng/µL stock |
|---|---|
| 50 ng | 0.22 µL |
| 100 ng | 0.44 µL |
| 250 ng | 1.11 µL |
| 500 ng | 2.22 µL |
| 1,000 ng | 4.44 µL |
| 2,000 ng | 8.89 µL |

Volumes below about 1 µL are hard to pipette accurately, so dilute the stock first when you need small amounts.

## Second example: RNA for cDNA synthesis

An RNA prep measured undiluted on a micro-volume instrument reads A260 = 0.62 and A280 = 0.3:

- Concentration = 0.62 × 40 × 1 = **24.8 ng/µL** (choose the RNA factor)
- A260/A280 = 0.62 ÷ 0.3 = **2.07**, normal for clean RNA

A reverse transcription that takes 1 µg of RNA would need 1,000 ÷ 24.8 = 40.3 µL, more than most 20 µL reactions allow. The choice is to use less RNA, which is fine for many kits, or to concentrate the sample by precipitation or a column with a small elution volume.

## Readings from a short path length

The factors on this page assume a 1 cm path. A reading of 0.3 taken through a 1 mm path, if the instrument does not normalise it, corresponds to 0.3 × 10 = 3 at 1 cm, which is 150 ng/µL of dsDNA. Enter the 1 cm equivalent as the A260, or put 10 in the dilution box.
