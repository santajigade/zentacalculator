## How to use the gas flow correction calculator

Enter the **actual flow** at line conditions, the **gauge pressure** and the **temperature** in the pipe, and choose the **reference conditions**. You get the flow at reference conditions and the correction factor. To go from Nm³/h to actual flow, press × on Actual flow.

For more background, read [What Is MMSCFD? Natural Gas Flow Rate](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/what-is-mmscfd-natural-gas-flow-rate/).

## Gas flow correction formula

<p class="formula">Q<sub>N</sub> = Q<sub>A</sub> × (P<sub>A</sub> ÷ P<sub>N</sub>) × (T<sub>N</sub> ÷ T<sub>A</sub>)</p>

Pressures must be **absolute** (gauge + atmospheric) and temperatures in **kelvin**.

## Which reference?

| Term | Typical reference |
|---|---|
| Nm³ (normal) | 0 °C, 1.01325 bar(a) — common in Europe and Asia |
| Sm³ (standard, ISO 13443) | 15 °C, 1.01325 bar(a) — natural gas |
| SCF (US) | 60 °F (15.6 °C), 14.696 psia |
| Compressed air (ISO 1217 FAD) | Inlet conditions, often 20 °C, 1 bar(a) |

Always state the reference conditions with a standard flow figure.

## Flow meter compensation

Volumetric gas meters (vortex, turbine, ultrasonic) measure actual flow. Flow computers or multivariable transmitters add pressure and temperature inputs to report normal flow or mass flow in real time.

## Worked example

A vortex meter on a compressed gas line reads **100 m³/h** at **5 bar(g)** and **30 °C**. Converting to normal conditions (0 °C, 1.01325 bar(a)):

- Absolute pressure = 5 + 1.01325 = **6.01325 bar(a)**
- Absolute temperature = 30 + 273.15 = **303.15 K**
- Correction factor = (6.01325 ÷ 1.01325) × (273.15 ÷ 303.15) = **5.3473**
- Normal flow = 100 × 5.3473 = **534.7 Nm³/h**

If the gas compressibility at line conditions is Z = 0.985 (and 1 at reference conditions), divide by Z: 534.7 ÷ 0.985 = **542.9 Nm³/h**, a 1.5% correction that matters for billing.

## Effect of site altitude on the correction

The calculator adds standard atmospheric pressure to the gauge reading. At altitude the real atmosphere is lower, so the true absolute pressure is lower too. For the example (5 bar(g), 30 °C, 100 m³/h actual):

| Site altitude | Local atmosphere | True factor | True normal flow | Error using 1.01325 bar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 m | 1.0132 bar(a) | 5.3473 | 534.7 Nm³/h | +0.00% |
| 500 m | 0.9546 bar(a) | 5.2952 | 529.5 Nm³/h | +0.98% |
| 1,000 m | 0.8987 bar(a) | 5.2455 | 524.5 Nm³/h | +1.94% |
| 1,500 m | 0.8456 bar(a) | 5.1982 | 519.8 Nm³/h | +2.87% |
| 2,000 m | 0.7950 bar(a) | 5.1532 | 515.3 Nm³/h | +3.77% |
| 3,000 m | 0.7011 bar(a) | 5.0697 | 507.0 Nm³/h | +5.48% |

At high altitude, enter the gauge pressure plus the local atmosphere minus 1.01325 bar, or use an absolute pressure transmitter for compensation.

## How compensation is done in practice

A flow computer or DCS block takes the meter's actual flow, a pressure transmitter (preferably absolute) and a temperature sensor, and calculates normal flow every scan with the formula above, often with a Z factor from AGA 8 or a fixed value for air. Pressure and temperature should be measured close to the meter: pressure at the meter body and temperature a few diameters downstream, so the sensor does not disturb the flow profile.

## Common mistakes

- **Using gauge pressure in the ratio.** The formula needs absolute pressures; 5 bar(g) is about 6.01 bar(a).
- **Using °C instead of kelvin.** 30 °C ÷ 0 °C has no meaning; 303.15 K ÷ 273.15 K does.
- **Mixing normal and standard.** One Nm³ (0 °C) equals 1.0549 Sm³ at 15 °C, a 5.5% difference that often appears in contract disputes.
- **Applying the correction to a mass flow meter.** Coriolis and thermal mass meters already measure mass or normal flow; correcting again doubles the effect.
- **Leaving out Z at high pressure.** Natural gas at 50 bar can deviate from ideal behaviour by several percent.
