## How to use the earth fault loop impedance calculator

Enter the **voltage to earth**, the **external loop impedance Ze** (measured at the origin or from the supplier), the circuit's **R1 + R2** (line plus protective conductor resistance), the **MCB type** and its **rating**. You get Zs, the prospective fault current, the maximum Zs for instantaneous tripping, and the margin.

## Earth fault loop impedance formula

<p class="formula">Z<sub>s</sub> = Z<sub>e</sub> + (R₁ + R₂) &nbsp;·&nbsp; I<sub>f</sub> = C<sub>min</sub> × U₀ ÷ Z<sub>s</sub></p>

<p class="formula">Z<sub>s,max</sub> = C<sub>min</sub> × U₀ ÷ I<sub>a</sub></p>

with C<sub>min</sub> = 0.95 and I<sub>a</sub> the instantaneous trip current of the device.

For measured values at room temperature, compare against about 80% of these figures.

## Reducing Zs

- Use larger line and protective conductors (lower R1 + R2).
- Shorten the circuit or move the distribution board closer.
- Use RCD protection where Zs cannot be reduced enough — in TT systems this is the usual solution.

## Measuring R1 + R2

Link line and protective conductor at the distribution board and measure between them at each outlet with a low-resistance ohmmeter. Copper 2.5/1.5 mm² cable has about 19.5 mΩ/m for R1 + R2 at 20 °C.

## Worked example with the default inputs

A 32 A Type B radial circuit on a PME supply with Z<sub>e</sub> = 0.35 Ω and R<sub>1</sub> + R<sub>2</sub> = 0.4 Ω:

- Z<sub>s</sub> = 0.35 + 0.4 = **0.75 Ω**
- I<sub>f</sub> = 0.95 × 230 ÷ 0.75 = **291.3 A**
- Z<sub>s,max</sub> = 0.95 × 230 ÷ (5 × 32) = **1.3656 Ω**, so the margin is **0.6156 Ω** and the MCB trips instantly

In 2.5/1.5 mm² twin and earth (19.51 mΩ/m at 20 °C), 0.4 Ω is about 20.5 m of cable. At its 70 °C operating temperature the conductors are about 20% more resistive, so R<sub>1</sub> + R<sub>2</sub> becomes 0.48 Ω and Z<sub>s</sub> 0.83 Ω, still inside the limit. A measured cold reading should be no more than 80% of 1.366 Ω, that is **1.09 Ω**, and 0.75 Ω passes.

The same circuit on a 32 A **Type C** breaker fails: its limit is 0.683 Ω, so 291 A would not reach the 320 A needed for instantaneous tripping.

## Maximum circuit length for a 32 A MCB

Z<sub>e</sub> = 0.35 Ω, copper conductors corrected to 70 °C, 0.4 s disconnection:

| Line / CPC size | R<sub>1</sub> + R<sub>2</sub> at 20 °C | Type B 32 A | Type C 32 A |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5 / 1.5 mm² | 19.51 mΩ/m | 43 m | 14 m |
| 4 / 1.5 mm² | 16.71 mΩ/m | 51 m | 17 m |
| 6 / 2.5 mm² | 10.49 mΩ/m | 81 m | 26 m |
| 10 / 4 mm² | 6.44 mΩ/m | 131 m | 43 m |

Voltage drop often limits the length before Zs does, so check both.

## How Zs is tested on site

Z<sub>e</sub> is measured at the origin with the main earthing conductor disconnected, which is only safe with the installation isolated, and reconnected straight after. Z<sub>s</sub> is measured at the furthest point of each circuit with a loop tester, using its no-trip mode on RCD protected circuits. Parallel earth paths through metal pipework can make a live Zs reading lower than Z<sub>e</sub> + R<sub>1</sub> + R<sub>2</sub>, so a reading far below the calculated value is worth questioning rather than celebrating.

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing a cold reading with the full table value.** Conductors heat up in service, which is why the 80% rule exists.
- **Using the Type B column for a Type C or D breaker.** The limit halves each time the trip multiple doubles.
- **Assuming the supplier's declared Ze.** The declared 0.35 Ω is a maximum; measure the real value, but design with the declared one.
- **Adding R1 + R2 for a ring as if it were radial.** On a ring final circuit the end-to-end values are combined as (r1 + r2) ÷ 4.
- **Forgetting the RCD on TT systems.** With a Zs of tens of ohms an MCB can never trip on earth faults and an RCD is the protection.
