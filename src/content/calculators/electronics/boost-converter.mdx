## How to use the boost converter calculator

Enter the **input** and **output voltage**, **output current**, expected **efficiency**, **switching frequency**, inductor **ripple** and allowed **output ripple**. You get the duty cycle, input current, inductance, peak switch current and minimum output capacitance.

For more background, read [PFC Circuit: Boost Power Factor Correction](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/pfc-circuit-boost-power-factor-correction/).

## The formulas (continuous conduction)

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Duty cycle | D = 1 − V<sub>i</sub>η ÷ V<sub>o</sub> |
| Input current | I<sub>i</sub> = V<sub>o</sub>I<sub>o</sub> ÷ (V<sub>i</sub>η) |
| Inductance | L = V<sub>i</sub> D ÷ (f ΔI) |
| Output capacitance | C = I<sub>o</sub> D ÷ (f ΔV) |

## Example

5 V to 12 V at 0.5 A, 90% efficiency, 500 kHz, 30% ripple:

- D = 1 − 4.5 ÷ 12 = **62.5%**
- Input current = 6 ÷ 4.5 = **1.33 A**
- ΔI = 0.4 A, L = 5 × 0.625 ÷ (500,000 × 0.4) = **15.6 µH** → use 15 to 22 µH
- Peak switch current = **1.53 A**
- For 50 mV ripple, C ≥ 0.5 × 0.625 ÷ (500,000 × 0.05) = **12.5 µF**

## Design checks

- The switch and diode must handle the full output voltage plus spikes.
- Inductor saturation current must exceed the peak current.
- A boost converter has no short-circuit protection by itself: current flows straight from input to output through the inductor and diode.
- At very light loads the converter enters discontinuous mode, and the formulas above no longer apply.

## Related topologies

- **Buck-boost and SEPIC** give an output above or below the input.
- **PFC boost stages** in power supplies shape input current to follow the mains voltage.

## Worked example with the default values

The defaults boost **5 V to 12 V at 0.5 A**, with 90% efficiency, 500 kHz switching, 30% inductor ripple and 50 mV allowed output ripple:

1. Duty cycle D = 1 − (5 × 0.9) ÷ 12 = **62.5%**
2. Input current Ii = (12 × 0.5) ÷ (5 × 0.9) = **1.333 A**
3. Ripple ΔI = 0.3 × 1.333 = 0.40 A, so L = (5 × 0.625) ÷ (500,000 × 0.40) = **15.63 µH**
4. Peak switch current = 1.333 × (1 + 0.3 ÷ 2) = **1.533 A**
5. Minimum output capacitance C = (0.5 × 0.625) ÷ (500,000 × 0.05) = **12.5 µF**

The input power is 6.67 W for 6 W out, so 0.67 W is lost as heat in the switch, diode and inductor. A capacitor with 10 mΩ ESR adds about 1.53 × 0.01 = 15 mV of ripple on top of the capacitive part, which is why low ESR ceramics are preferred at the output.

## Design values for different output voltages

Same 5 V input, 0.5 A load, 90% efficiency, 500 kHz and 30% ripple:

| Output | Duty cycle | Input current | Inductance | Peak current | Min. C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 V | 25.0% | 0.667 A | 12.5 µH | 0.767 A | 5.0 µF |
| 9 V | 50.0% | 1.000 A | 16.7 µH | 1.150 A | 10.0 µF |
| 12 V | 62.5% | 1.333 A | 15.6 µH | 1.533 A | 12.5 µF |
| 15 V | 70.0% | 1.667 A | 14.0 µH | 1.917 A | 14.0 µF |
| 24 V | 81.2% | 2.667 A | 10.2 µH | 3.067 A | 16.2 µF |

The higher the step-up, the larger the duty cycle and the input current. At 24 V the input current is already 2.67 A for a 12 W load, and the peak current, not the average, sets the switch and inductor ratings.

## Practical example: a Li-ion cell to 5 V

Boosting a 3.7 V cell to 5 V at 1 A with 92% efficiency at 1 MHz and 40% ripple gives D = 31.9%, an input current of 1.47 A, L = 2.01 µH and a peak of 1.76 A. Design for the end of discharge, not the nominal voltage: at 3 V the duty cycle rises to 44.8% and the input current to 1.81 A, so the inductor saturation rating should cover about 2.2 A.

## Choosing the parts

- **Inductor:** pick the next standard value at or above the result and check the saturation current against the peak at the lowest input voltage.
- **Output capacitor:** ceramic capacitors lose much of their capacitance under DC bias; a 22 µF 25 V X5R part may give only 8 µF to 10 µF at 12 V, so check the derating curve.
- **Diode or synchronous switch:** a Schottky diode rated above the output voltage with low forward drop; above a few amps a synchronous MOSFET saves more power.
- **Switch:** rated for the output voltage plus ringing, typically at least 1.3 times Vo.

## Common mistakes

- **Taking the ripple percentage of the output current.** The inductor carries the input current, so the ripple is a percentage of Ii, which is much larger than Io.
- **Designing at nominal input voltage only.** The worst case for current is the lowest input voltage; the worst case for output voltage stress is the highest.
- **Expecting short-circuit protection.** The input is connected to the output through the inductor and diode even with the switch off, so an output short pulls current straight from the source.
- **Ignoring discontinuous mode.** At light load the inductor current falls to zero each cycle and the duty cycle formula above no longer holds.
- **Skipping the input capacitor.** The input current ripple still needs a local ceramic capacitor close to the inductor and switch.
