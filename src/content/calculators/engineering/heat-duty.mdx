## How to use this calculator

Enter the **mass flow**, **specific heat** and the **temperature difference** between inlet and outlet. The heat duty is calculated in kW (or hp). You can also find the flow needed to carry a given load, or the temperature rise for a known heater power.

## The formula

<p class="formula">Q̇ = ṁ × c<sub>p</sub> × ΔT</p>

## Worked example

2 kg/s of water cooled by 10 °C in a heat exchanger:

Q̇ = 2 × 4.186 × 10 = **83.7 kW**

## Typical specific heats

| Fluid | c<sub>p</sub> (kJ/kg·K) |
|---|---|
| Water | 4.186 |
| Glycol 30% | ~3.7 |
| Thermal oil | ~2.0 – 2.5 |
| Air | 1.005 |
| Steel | 0.49 |

## Practical uses

- **Chiller and boiler sizing** — enter design flow and ΔT.
- **Energy metering** — BTU/energy meters calculate exactly this using a flow meter and two temperature sensors (often matched Pt100 or Pt500 pairs).
- **Checking performance** — compare measured duty with the design value to spot fouling.
