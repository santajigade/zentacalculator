## How to use the cell doubling time calculator

Enter the **initial** and **final cell counts** and the **time** between them. The number of doublings and the doubling time are calculated.

## Cell doubling time formulas

<p class="formula">doublings = log₂(N ÷ N₀)   T<sub>d</sub> = t ÷ doublings</p>

## Example

Cells grow from 100,000 to 800,000 in 72 hours:

- Doublings = log₂(8) = **3**
- Doubling time = 72 ÷ 3 = **24 hours**

## Typical doubling times of cell lines

| Cell line | Approximate doubling time |
|---|---|
| HEK293 | 24–36 h |
| HeLa | about 24 h |
| CHO | 14–24 h |
| Jurkat | 25–50 h |
| Primary human fibroblasts | 24–72 h, slows with passages |
| Mouse embryonic stem cells | 12–16 h |

Doubling time depends on medium, serum, confluence and passage number.

## Population doubling level (PDL)

For primary cells, track the cumulative population doublings across passages. Normal human fibroblasts typically stop dividing after about 40–60 doublings (the Hayflick limit).

## Planning experiments

To have 5 million cells from 500,000 with a 24-hour doubling time: log₂(10) = 3.32 doublings, so about **80 hours**. Add time for the lag phase after seeding.

## Tips for accurate counts

- Count in the exponential phase, not when cells are confluent.
- Use the same counting method each time — see the [hemocytometer calculator](/biology/hemocytometer/).
- Exclude dead cells with trypan blue.

## Planning how many cells to seed

Work backwards from the number you need:

<p class="formula">N<sub>seed</sub> = N<sub>target</sub> ÷ 2<sup>t ÷ T<sub>d</sub></sup></p>

To have 2 × 10⁶ cells in 48 hours with a 24 hour doubling time, seed 2 × 10⁶ ÷ 2² = **5 × 10⁵ cells**. Most lines show a lag of 12 to 24 hours after passaging, so add that to the time or seed a little more.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting during the lag phase.** Counts in the first day after seeding make the doubling time look too long. Start timing once growth is steady.
- **Including dead cells.** Count only viable cells, for example with trypan blue, or the growth rate will be wrong.
- **Letting cultures reach confluence.** Growth slows as cells fill the surface, so the last count gives a falsely long doubling time.
- **Using only two time points.** Count at three or more times and fit a line to ln(count) against time for a more reliable result.
