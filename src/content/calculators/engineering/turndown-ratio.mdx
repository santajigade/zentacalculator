## How to use the turndown ratio calculator

Enter the **maximum** and **minimum flow**. You get the turndown ratio and, for differential pressure meters, the DP at minimum flow as a percentage of the DP at maximum flow. Enter a turndown and press × on Minimum flow to find the lowest flow a meter can handle.

## Turndown ratio formula

<p class="formula">Turndown = Q<sub>max</sub> ÷ Q<sub>min</sub></p>

<p class="formula">DP meters: ΔP<sub>min</sub> ÷ ΔP<sub>max</sub> = (Q<sub>min</sub> ÷ Q<sub>max</sub>)²</p>

## Worked example

A process runs between 10 and 100 m³/h:

- Turndown = 100 ÷ 10 = **10.0 : 1**
- For an orifice meter, DP at minimum flow = (10 ÷ 100)² = **1%** of the DP at maximum flow

If the transmitter is ranged 0 to 250 mbar, minimum flow produces only 2.5 mbar, close to the noise level of the reading. A vortex, magnetic or Coriolis meter covers this 10:1 range comfortably; an orifice plate with one transmitter does not.

## Accuracy across the range

Meters specified in **% of reading** keep the same relative accuracy across their turndown; meters specified in **% of full scale** become much less accurate at low flow. See the [instrument accuracy calculator](/engineering/instrument-accuracy/).

## Getting more turndown in practice

- **Stacked DP transmitters:** a second transmitter ranged to 10% of the maximum DP takes over below about 32% of maximum flow, extending an orifice installation to roughly 10:1.
- **Two meters in parallel:** a small and a large meter with automatic switching, common in custody transfer and boiler fuel lines.
- **Split-range control valves:** a small valve opens first and a large valve takes over, raising the overall rangeability of the control loop.
- **Choose the meter for the range:** if the turndown needed is above about 10:1, a linear meter (magnetic, Coriolis, ultrasonic) is usually simpler than tricks with DP meters.

## Common mistakes

- **Quoting the catalogue turndown for the application.** A Coriolis meter's 100:1 is for its own maximum flow; if the meter is sized for twice your maximum, half of that range is wasted.
- **Forgetting the square law.** A 10:1 flow range on a DP meter means a 100:1 DP range for the transmitter.
- **Mixing accuracy bases.** Compare meters on the same basis: % of reading and % of span give very different errors at low flow.
- **Ignoring the low-flow cutoff.** Many transmitters force readings below a cutoff to zero, which sets the real minimum flow.
- **Confusing valve rangeability with installed rangeability.** The installed value is lower because the valve's pressure drop changes with flow.
