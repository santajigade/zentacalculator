## How to use the NTC thermistor calculator

Enter the thermistor's **R25** and **beta value** from the datasheet, then a **temperature** to get its resistance — or enter a measured **resistance** and press × on Temperature to read the temperature. The sensitivity shows how much resistance changes per °C at that point.

## NTC beta equation

<p class="formula">R<sub>T</sub> = R₂₅ × e<sup>B (1/T − 1/298.15)</sup> &nbsp;·&nbsp; T = 1 ÷ [1/298.15 + ln(R/R₂₅) ÷ B]</p>

T is in kelvin (°C + 273.15).

## Reading a thermistor with an ADC

Place the thermistor in a [voltage divider](/electronics/voltage-divider/) with a fixed resistor equal to R25. The divider output is most linear and sensitive around 25 °C. Keep the measuring current low (under about 100 µA for 10 kΩ) to avoid self-heating.

## Worked example: 10 kΩ, B 3950 thermistor at 60 °C

With the default inputs (R25 = 10 kΩ, B = 3,950 K, T = 60 °C):

- T = 60 + 273.15 = **333.15 K**
- 1/T − 1/298.15 = 0.003002 − 0.003354 = -0.000352 K⁻¹
- R = 10 × e^(3,950 × -0.000352) = 10 × 0.2486 = **2.49 kΩ**
- Sensitivity = −3,950 ÷ 333.15² × 100 = **-3.56 %/°C**

Going the other way, a reading of 4 kΩ gives T = 1 ÷ (1/298.15 + ln(4/10) ÷ 3,950) − 273.15 = **47.2 °C**.

## Divider output with a 10 kΩ fixed resistor and a 12 bit ADC

With the thermistor from 3.3 V to the ADC pin and a 10 kΩ resistor from the pin to ground, V = 3.3 × 10 ÷ (10 + R<sub>T</sub>):

| Temperature | R<sub>T</sub> | ADC pin voltage | 12 bit count |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 °C | 33.62 kΩ | 0.757 V | 939 |
| 25 °C | 10.00 kΩ | 1.650 V | 2,048 |
| 40 °C | 5.30 kΩ | 2.157 V | 2,676 |
| 60 °C | 2.49 kΩ | 2.643 V | 3,280 |
| 80 °C | 1.27 kΩ | 2.928 V | 3,633 |
| 100 °C | 0.70 kΩ | 3.085 V | 3,828 |

The counts change fastest near 25 °C, where the two resistors are equal. If the range of interest is 60 to 100 °C, a smaller fixed resistor (around 2.2 kΩ) puts the steepest part of the curve where it is needed.

## Self-heating in practice

At 60 °C the example divider passes 264 µA and the thermistor dissipates 0.174 mW. With a typical bead dissipation constant of 1.5 mW/°C, that warms the sensor by about **0.12 °C** above the air it is measuring. In still air or with a lower value thermistor this error grows quickly, so firmware often powers the divider only while sampling.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the wrong B value.** B25/50 and B25/85 differ by 1 to 2%. Reading a 3,435 K part as 3,950 K turns a true 60 °C into an indicated 55.0 °C.
- **Forgetting kelvin.** The exponent needs absolute temperature; plugging in °C gives nonsense.
- **Trusting the beta model far from 25 °C.** Beyond about 0 to 100 °C, fit Steinhart-Hart coefficients or use the maker's R-T table.
- **Ignoring lead and connector resistance.** It is small next to 10 kΩ at room temperature, but at 125 °C the thermistor is only a few hundred ohms.
- **Mounting without thermal contact.** A thermistor taped loosely to a heatsink reads the air gap, not the metal. Use thermal paste or a lug.
