## How to use the age calculator

Pick the **date of birth**. "Age on this date" starts at today — change it to find your age on any other date. You get completed years and months, plus total weeks and days.

## How it works

The calculator counts full calendar months between the two dates and divides by 12 for years. A birthday has to be reached before the year counts — just as people normally count age.

## Example

Born 15 March 1990, on 14 March 2026 → **35 years** (the 36th birthday is the next day).

## Fun facts

- You turn 10,000 days old at about 27 years and 5 months.
- One billion seconds is about 31 years and 8 months.
- The number of days is the most precise measure — try it for anniversaries too.

## Birthdays on 29 February

People born on 29 February have a real birthday only in leap years. In common years most countries treat **1 March** as the legal birthday for age-based rules, though some use 28 February. The calculator counts a completed year on 1 March in non-leap years.

## Age for forms and eligibility

Many rules depend on age on a specific date, not today:

- **School admission**: age on a cut-off date, such as 1 April or 31 March.
- **Exams and government jobs**: age limits on a notified date, often 1 January or 1 August.
- **Retirement and pensions**: the month you reach the retirement age.
- **Insurance**: age last birthday or age next birthday, depending on the insurer.

Change the “Age on this date” field to the cut-off date to check.

## Age difference between two people

Enter the older person's date of birth and set the second date to the younger person's date of birth. The result is the exact age gap in years, months and days.

## Ages around the world

Most countries count age from zero at birth. Traditional East Asian age reckoning added a year at birth and at each New Year, but South Korea officially switched to the international method for legal purposes in 2023.

## What the defaults show

When the page opens, the date of birth is set 12,784 days before today and the second date is today, so the days field always reads **12,784 days** and the weeks field **1,826.3 weeks**. That is about 35 years; the completed years and months depend on how many 29 Februarys fall inside the window, so they can shift by one around the boundary.

## Worked example on fixed dates

Take a birth date of **15 March 1990** and an "age on" date of **20 November 2026**. These dates are fixed so the example does not change with today's date:

- Completed years: the 2026 birthday (15 March) has passed, so **36 years**
- Completed months: 36 × 12 + 8 = **440 months**, the last monthly anniversary being 15 November 2026
- Days since that anniversary: **5 days**, so the age reads 36 years, 8 months and 5 days
- Total days: **13,399**, which is 1,914.1 weeks; 9 leap days fall in the period
- Days to the next birthday on 15 March 2027: **115**

## Age on admission and recruitment cut-off dates

The same child, born on 20 September 2020, can fall either side of an age rule depending on the cut-off the school or board uses:

| Cut-off date | Age on that date | Days since birth |
|---|---|---|
| 31 March 2026 | 5 years 6 months | 2,018 |
| 1 April 2026 | 5 years 6 months | 2,019 |
| 1 June 2026 | 5 years 8 months | 2,080 |
| 30 September 2026 | 6 years 0 months | 2,201 |
| 31 December 2026 | 6 years 3 months | 2,293 |

A rule of "6 years on 31 March 2026" excludes this child, while a 31 December cut-off includes them. Always read the age limit together with its date.

## How the counting works in practice

Completed years and months are counted the way people and most laws count them: a year is complete when the birthday is reached, and a month is complete when the same day number comes round. When the birth day number does not exist in a shorter month, such as the 31st in April, this calculator completes that month on the 1st of the following month, the same rule it uses for 29 February birthdays. The day total is the plain calendar difference and does not include the birth day itself; counting both end dates, as some leave and contract rules do, adds one, giving 13,400 days in the example above.

## Common mistakes

- **Subtracting birth year from current year.** On 14 March 2026, 2026 − 1990 gives 36, but someone born on 15 March 1990 is still 35 until the next day; the month and day must be compared too.
- **Using 365 days a year.** 13,399 ÷ 365 gives 36.71, which overstates age slightly because of the 9 leap days; use calendar dates, not division.
- **Checking age on the application date.** Exam and job notices fix age on a stated date, often 1 January or 1 August; entering today can put you on the wrong side of the limit.
- **Mixing day and month order.** 04/05/2010 is 4 May in India and the UK but 5 April in the US. Pick the date from the calendar rather than typing it.
- **Forgetting time zones for very recent births.** A baby born late at night in one country may have a different birth date abroad; use the date on the birth certificate.
