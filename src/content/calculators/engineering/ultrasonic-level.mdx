## How to use the ultrasonic level calculator

Enter the **round-trip echo time**, the **air temperature** in the tank and the **empty distance** from the sensor face to the bottom (0% level). You get the speed of sound, the distance to the surface and the liquid level.

## Ultrasonic level formula

<p class="formula">c = 331.3 × √(1 + T/273.15) m/s &nbsp;·&nbsp; d = c × t ÷ 2 &nbsp;·&nbsp; Level = E − d</p>

## Speed of sound in air

| Temperature | Speed of sound | Distance error if 20 °C assumed |
|---|---|---|
| -20 °C | 318.9 m/s | -7.1% |
| 0 °C | 331.3 m/s | -3.5% |
| 10 °C | 337.3 m/s | -1.7% |
| 20 °C | 343.2 m/s | +0.0% |
| 30 °C | 349.0 m/s | +1.7% |
| 40 °C | 354.7 m/s | +3.4% |
| 60 °C | 365.9 m/s | +6.6% |
| 80 °C | 376.7 m/s | +9.8% |

## Example

Echo time 20 ms at 30 °C, empty distance 6 m:

- c = 349.0 m/s, distance = 349.0 × 0.020 ÷ 2 = **3.49 m**
- Level = 6 − 3.49 = **2.51 m**

## What a temperature error costs

Take the same echo of 20 ms with an empty distance of 6 m, but suppose the transmitter's temperature compensation fails and it assumes 20 °C while the air in the tank is really 30 °C:

- Assumed speed of sound at 20 °C = 331.3 × √(1 + 20/273.15) = **343.2 m/s**
- Distance it reports = 343.2 × 0.02 ÷ 2 = **3.432 m**, so level = 6 − 3.432 = **2.568 m**
- True level at 30 °C = **2.510 m**, so the reading is **58 mm** too high

The error grows with distance, so it is worst when the tank is nearly empty, which is exactly when a pump may be running dry.

## Echo time by distance and temperature

Round-trip echo time t = 2d ÷ c:

| Distance to surface | 0 °C | 20 °C | 40 °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 m | 3.02 ms | 2.91 ms | 2.82 ms |
| 1 m | 6.04 ms | 5.83 ms | 5.64 ms |
| 2 m | 12.07 ms | 11.65 ms | 11.28 ms |
| 4 m | 24.15 ms | 23.31 ms | 22.55 ms |
| 6 m | 36.22 ms | 34.96 ms | 33.83 ms |
| 8 m | 48.29 ms | 46.62 ms | 45.10 ms |
| 10 m | 60.37 ms | 58.27 ms | 56.38 ms |
| 15 m | 90.55 ms | 87.41 ms | 84.57 ms |

A transmitter that measures to 15 m must wait about 90 ms for each echo, which is why long-range units update more slowly and average several pulses.

## Setting up the transmitter in practice

The transmitter does not know where the bottom of the tank is: you enter the **empty distance** (sensor face to the 0% point) and the **span** (0% to 100%), and it scales the 4 to 20 mA output between them. The 100% point must stay below the **blanking distance**, so a sensor with 0.3 m blanking cannot read the last 0.3 m under its face. During commissioning, run the false echo suppression (echo mapping) with the tank empty or at low level, so the unit learns and ignores reflections from ladders, welds and agitator blades. Then compare the reading with a manual dip at two levels at least.

## Common mistakes

- **Measuring the empty distance to the nozzle flange** instead of the transducer face. The face sits some way inside or above the flange, and the whole offset ends up in the level.
- **Mounting in a long, narrow nozzle.** The pulse rings off the nozzle walls and the unit locks onto that echo. Keep the transducer face at or just below the end of the nozzle.
- **Trusting the built-in temperature probe in a stratified tank.** The probe reads the air at the sensor, but a warm vapour layer near the liquid has a different speed of sound.
- **Using ultrasonic over foam.** Foam absorbs the pulse, so the echo is weak or comes from the top of the foam rather than the liquid.
- **Ignoring the gas in the tank.** In carbon dioxide sound travels at about 267 m/s at 20 °C, roughly 22% slower than in air, so an air-calibrated unit reads badly wrong.

## Installation tips

- Keep the liquid surface out of the blanking distance at maximum level.
- Mount away from the tank wall, inlet stream and internal ladders to avoid false echoes.
- Use the sensor's built-in temperature probe; vapour layers with a different gas composition still cause errors.
- For foam, vapour or pressure, consider [radar level measurement](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/radar-level-selection-non-contact-vs-guided-wave/).
