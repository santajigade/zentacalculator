## How to use the protein concentration calculator

Enter the **A280** reading, the protein's **extinction coefficient** and **molecular weight**, and the **path length**. The concentration is given in µM and mg/mL.

## Protein concentration formula

<p class="formula">c = A280 ÷ (ε × l)</p>

## Example: BSA

A280 = 0.8, ε = 43,824 M⁻¹cm⁻¹, 1 cm:

- c = 0.8 ÷ 43,824 = **18.25 µM**
- × 66,463 g/mol = **1.213 mg/mL**

## Extinction coefficients of common proteins

| Protein | MW (Da) | ε₂₈₀ (M⁻¹cm⁻¹) | A280 of 1 mg/mL |
|---|---|---|---|
| BSA | 66,463 | 43,824 | 0.66 |
| Lysozyme (hen egg) | 14,313 | 37,970 | 2.65 |
| IgG (typical) | 150,000 | 210,000 | 1.40 |
| GFP | 26,900 | 21,890 | 0.81 |
| Insulin | 5,808 | 6,335 | 1.09 |

## Calculating ε from sequence

ε₂₈₀ can be estimated from the amino acid composition:

<p class="formula">ε = (number of Trp × 5,500) + (number of Tyr × 1,490) + (number of cystines × 125)</p>

Proteins without tryptophan or tyrosine absorb very little at 280 nm and need another method.

## Other protein assays

| Assay | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A280 | about 0.1 to 100 mg/mL | Fast, non-destructive; needs pure protein |
| Bradford | 1 to 1,500 µg/mL | Simple; sensitive to detergents |
| BCA | 20 to 2,000 µg/mL | Tolerates detergents; sensitive to reducing agents |

## Nucleic acid contamination

Nucleic acids absorb strongly at 260 nm and also at 280 nm. An A260/A280 ratio around 0.57 suggests pure protein; higher values indicate nucleic acid contamination.

## Worked example with the default values

The defaults are BSA read at **A280 = 0.8** in a **1 cm** cuvette, with ε = 43,824 M⁻¹cm⁻¹ and MW = 66,463 Da:

1. Molar concentration c = 0.8 ÷ (43,824 × 1) = 1.8255e-05 M = **18.25 µM**
2. Mass concentration = 18.25 µM × 66,463 g/mol ÷ 10⁶ = **1.213 mg/mL**

The absorbance of a 1 mg/mL solution, often written A₂₈₀(0.1%), is ε ÷ MW = 0.659, so dividing the reading by 0.659 gives the same 1.213 mg/mL directly.

## Diluted and microvolume readings

If that reading of 0.8 came from a 1 in 10 dilution, the stock is 1.213 × 10 = **12.13 mg/mL**. Microvolume spectrophotometers avoid the dilution by using a short path: the same stock on a 0.1 mm (0.01 cm) pedestal reads about 0.080, and on a 1 mm path 0.80. Always enter the true path length, or a normalised 1 cm value if the instrument already converts it.

|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 2.28 µM | 0.152 mg/mL | 0.038 mg/mL |
| 0.2 | 4.56 µM | 0.303 mg/mL | 0.075 mg/mL |
| 0.4 | 9.13 µM | 0.607 mg/mL | 0.151 mg/mL |
| 0.8 | 18.25 µM | 1.213 mg/mL | 0.302 mg/mL |
| 1 | 22.82 µM | 1.517 mg/mL | 0.377 mg/mL |
| 1.5 | 34.23 µM | 2.275 mg/mL | 0.565 mg/mL |

The same absorbance means very different amounts of protein: lysozyme is rich in tryptophan, so it absorbs about 4.0 times more per milligram than BSA. Using a generic factor of 1 A per mg/mL would overstate BSA by about 52% here.

The sequence formula above reproduces the table values: lysozyme has 6 Trp, 3 Tyr and 4 cystines, giving 6 × 5,500 + 3 × 1,490 + 4 × 125 = 37,970 M⁻¹cm⁻¹, and insulin has 4 Tyr and 3 cystines, giving 6,335.

## Practical notes

- Blank the instrument with the exact buffer the protein is in; imidazole, some detergents and reducing agents absorb near 280 nm.
- Keep readings between about 0.1 and 1.0 for a standard spectrophotometer; dilute stronger samples.
- Read A320 or A340 as well. Absorbance there comes from light scattered by aggregates, and subtracting it gives a better A280.
- Use the ε for the protein state you have: values computed with all cysteines reduced are slightly lower than those that count disulfide bonds.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the dilution factor.** The calculator gives the concentration in the cuvette; multiply by the dilution to get the stock.
- **Using the extinction coefficient of the wrong construct.** His tags, GFP fusions or missing domains change ε and MW, so compute them from the actual sequence.
- **Reading cloudy samples.** Aggregates scatter light and raise A280, so spin or filter the sample first.
- **Ignoring nucleic acid contamination.** DNA and RNA absorb at 280 nm too; an A260/A280 ratio well above 0.6 means the A280 result is too high.
- **Mixing up M⁻¹cm⁻¹ and mL·mg⁻¹cm⁻¹.** A mass extinction coefficient near 1 entered in the molar box gives a concentration tens of thousands of times too high.
