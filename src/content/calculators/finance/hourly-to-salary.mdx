## How to use the hourly to salary calculator

Enter your **hourly rate**, **hours per week** and **weeks per year**. You see weekly, monthly and annual pay. Type a salary into any of those fields to find the equivalent hourly rate.

## Hourly to salary formulas

<p class="formula">Weekly = hourly × hours per week<br/>Annual = weekly × weeks per year   Monthly = annual ÷ 12</p>

## Worked example

25 per hour, 40 hours a week, 52 weeks:

- Weekly = 25 × 40 = **1,000**
- Annual = 1,000 × 52 = **52,000**
- Monthly = **4,333.33**

A quick mental rule: **annual ≈ hourly × 2,000**.

## Salary to hourly for contractors

If you switch from a salaried job to contracting, do not just divide your old salary by 2,080 hours. A salary comes with paid leave, holidays and benefits that a contractor must cover:

- **Unpaid time off**: holidays and sick days, often 4–6 weeks a year.
- **Non-billable hours**: admin, sales and training, often 20–30% of working time.
- **Benefits and taxes**: health insurance, pension contributions and employer-side taxes.

A common rule of thumb is that a contract rate needs to be **1.5 to 2 times** the equivalent salaried hourly rate to give the same income.

## Overtime

Many jobs pay overtime at 1.5× the normal rate above 40 hours a week. To include it, work out the overtime pay separately:

<p class="formula">Weekly pay = rate × 40 + rate × 1.5 × overtime hours</p>

At 25 an hour with 5 hours of overtime, that is 1,000 + 187.50 = **1,187.50** a week.

## Gross vs take-home pay

The results are **gross** pay, before income tax, social security and other deductions. Take-home pay is usually 15–35% lower depending on your country, income and tax choices.

## Remember

These are gross (before-tax) figures. Overtime, bonuses and benefits are not included.

## Pay per period at the default 40 hours and 52 weeks

The defaults (25 an hour, 40 hours, 52 weeks) give **1,000** a week, **52,000** a year and **4,333.33** a month, over 2,080 paid hours. Payroll rarely runs monthly everywhere, so the same salary splits differently by pay schedule:

| Hourly | Daily (8 h) | Weekly | Biweekly (26 pays) | Semi-monthly (24 pays) | Monthly |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | 120 | 600 | 1,200.00 | 1,300.00 | 2,600.00 |
| 20 | 160 | 800 | 1,600.00 | 1,733.33 | 3,466.67 |
| 25 | 200 | 1,000 | 2,000.00 | 2,166.67 | 4,333.33 |
| 35 | 280 | 1,400 | 2,800.00 | 3,033.33 | 6,066.67 |
| 50 | 400 | 2,000 | 4,000.00 | 4,333.33 | 8,666.67 |

At 25 an hour, a biweekly cheque is 2,000.00 and a semi-monthly one is 2,166.67. Both add up to 52,000 over the year; the difference is only the number of pay days.

## Real hourly pay from a salary

A salary hides the hours behind it. A salary of 60,000 looks like 28.85 an hour on a 40-hour week, but if the job really takes 45 hours every week it pays **25.64** an hour. To check, type the salary into Annual salary, set hours per week to what you actually work, and press × on Hourly rate.

Unpaid leave works the same way. If 52,000 is earned in 48 working weeks, set weeks to 48 and the hourly equivalent rises to 27.08; if the 4 weeks off are unpaid, keep the hourly rate and the annual pay falls to 48,000.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating a month as four weeks.** A year has about 4.33 weeks per month, so 25 an hour for 40 hours is 4,333.33 a month, not 4,000.
- **Comparing gross salary with take-home hourly pay.** Convert both offers before tax, or both after tax, never one of each.
- **Using contracted hours when you work longer.** Regular unpaid overtime lowers the real hourly rate of a salaried job.
- **Forgetting paid holidays in an hourly job.** If holidays are unpaid, reduce the weeks worked; at 48 weeks, 25 an hour is 48,000 a year.
- **Ignoring benefits.** Pension contributions, health cover and bonuses can be worth a large share of base pay and are not in this conversion.
