## How to use the steel plate weight calculator

Choose the **material** and enter the **length**, **width**, **thickness** and **quantity**. The weight appears in kg or lb.

## Steel plate weight formula

<p class="formula">Weight = length × width × thickness × density</p>

## Example

A 2,000 × 1,000 × 6 mm mild steel plate:

Weight = 2 × 1 × 0.006 × 7,850 = **94.2 kg**

## Chequer (tread) plate

Chequer plate weighs more than its nominal thickness suggests because of the raised pattern — typically about 0.5–1 mm extra equivalent thickness. Check the supplier's table.

## Holes and cut-outs

Subtract cut-outs: a 100 mm hole in 10 mm steel removes π × 0.05² × 0.01 × 7,850 = 0.62 kg.

## Quick rule

Mild steel plate weighs **7.85 kg per m² per mm** of thickness. Aluminium weighs about 2.7 kg per m² per mm.

## Worked example: a column base cut list

The default plate, 2,000 × 1,000 × 6 mm in mild steel, weighs 2 × 1 × 0.006 × 7,850 = **94.2 kg**. A real job usually mixes sizes and shapes, for example one column base:

- Base plate 400 × 400 × 20 mm: 0.4 × 0.4 × 0.02 × 7,850 = 25.12 kg
- Four 22 mm bolt holes: each removes π × 0.011² × 0.02 × 7,850 = 0.060 kg, so the net plate is **24.88 kg**
- Four triangular gussets 150 × 150 × 10 mm: half of a 150 × 150 rectangle, 0.883 kg each
- Assembly total = 24.88 + 4 × 0.883 = **28.41 kg**

For a triangle, enter the rectangle that encloses it and halve the answer. Made in stainless steel (8,000 kg/m³) the same base plate would weigh 25.6 kg before holes.

## Theoretical and actual weight

Mills and stockists usually invoice plate on theoretical weight from the nominal thickness, but rolled plate has a thickness tolerance (set by standards such as IS 2062 and EN 10029) and is often slightly over nominal, so a weighbridge figure can be a few percent higher. For lifting, add the weight of slings, shackles and any lifting beam to the plate weight. For costing a cut part, the bought weight is the whole rectangle the part is nested in, not the finished shape, so add the offcut or scrap allowance.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing millimetres and metres.** Multiplying 2,000 × 1,000 × 6 by 7,850 gives a number a billion times too large; convert to metres first.
- **Using the steel density for other metals.** Aluminium is about a third as heavy and copper about 14% heavier than mild steel.
- **Forgetting the quantity.** A cut list of 24 identical plates needs n = 24, not a single plate weight.
- **Ignoring holes and cut-outs on thick plate.** On 20 mm plate a few large holes can remove several kilograms.
- **Taking chequer plate at its nominal thickness.** The raised pattern adds weight; use the supplier's kg/m² figure.
