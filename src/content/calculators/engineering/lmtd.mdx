## How to use the LMTD calculator

Choose **counterflow** or **parallel flow** and enter the inlet and outlet temperatures of both fluids. The calculator returns ΔT₁, ΔT₂ and the LMTD. Enter the **overall heat transfer coefficient U** and the **heat duty Q** to get the required area — or clear Q and enter an area to find how much heat an existing exchanger can transfer.

## LMTD formula

<p class="formula">LMTD = (ΔT₁ − ΔT₂) ÷ ln(ΔT₁ ÷ ΔT₂) &nbsp;·&nbsp; Q = U × A × LMTD</p>

## Worked example

Hot oil cools from 150 °C to 90 °C, water heats from 30 °C to 70 °C, counterflow, U = 500 W/m²·K, duty 250 kW:

- ΔT₁ = 150 − 70 = **80 K**, ΔT₂ = 90 − 30 = **60 K**
- LMTD = (80 − 60) ÷ ln(80 ÷ 60) = **69.52 K**
- A = 250,000 ÷ (500 × 69.52) = **7.19 m²**

In parallel flow the same temperatures give ΔT₁ = 120 K, ΔT₂ = 20 K and LMTD = 55.81 K, so 8.96 m² would be needed.

## LMTD vs arithmetic mean

| ΔT₁ | ΔT₂ | Arithmetic mean | LMTD |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80 K | 60 K | 70 K | 69.52 K |
| 60 K | 40 K | 50 K | 49.33 K |
| 50 K | 10 K | 30 K | 24.85 K |
| 40 K | 20 K | 30 K | 28.85 K |
| 30 K | 15 K | 22.5 K | 21.64 K |
| 20 K | 5 K | 12.5 K | 10.82 K |
| 100 K | 50 K | 75 K | 72.13 K |
| 10 K | 9 K | 9.5 K | 9.49 K |

The arithmetic mean always overstates the driving force; the error is small only when ΔT₁ and ΔT₂ are close.

## Notes

- If ΔT₁ = ΔT₂ the formula is 0 ÷ 0 — the LMTD then equals that common ΔT.
- A temperature cross (ΔT ≤ 0 at one end) is impossible for the chosen arrangement — check the temperatures.
- Typical U values: water–water plate exchanger 2,000–6,000 W/m²·K, shell-and-tube water–water 800–1,500, oil–water 100–400, gas–gas 10–50.

## Checking the duty from the fluid flows

The 250 kW duty in the worked example must match both streams, Q = ṁ c<sub>p</sub> ΔT:

- Water, c<sub>p</sub> = 4.18 kJ/kg·K, heated 40 K: ṁ = 250 ÷ (4.18 × 40) = **1.495 kg/s** (5.38 t/h)
- Hot oil, c<sub>p</sub> = 2.1 kJ/kg·K, cooled 60 K: ṁ = 250 ÷ (2.1 × 60) = **1.984 kg/s** (7.14 t/h)

## Shell and tube correction factor (1 shell pass, 2 or more tube passes)

For a 1-2 exchanger the counterflow LMTD is multiplied by F, which depends on R = (T<sub>hi</sub> − T<sub>ho</sub>) ÷ (T<sub>co</sub> − T<sub>ci</sub>) and P = (T<sub>co</sub> − T<sub>ci</sub>) ÷ (T<sub>hi</sub> − T<sub>ci</sub>). For the example R = 1.50 and P = 0.333, giving **F = 0.910** and a required area of 250,000 ÷ (500 × 0.910 × 69.52) = **7.90 m²** instead of 7.19 m² in pure counterflow.

| P (at R = 1.50) | F |
|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.997 |
| 0.2 | 0.982 |
| 0.3 | 0.939 |
| 0.333 | 0.911 |
| 0.4 | 0.803 |
| 0.45 | 0.579 |
| 0.5 | not possible |

F falls steeply as P rises. Values below about 0.8 mean a single shell is a poor choice and two shells in series are needed.

## Using LMTD to monitor fouling

With four temperature transmitters and one flow meter, the overall U of an operating exchanger can be tracked: U = Q ÷ (A × LMTD). Suppose that months later, at the same flows and inlet temperatures, the water only reaches 64 °C. Then Q = 212.5 kW, the oil leaves at 99.0 °C, the LMTD is 77.19 K and U = 212,500 ÷ (7.19 × 77.19) = **383 W/m²·K**. The added fouling resistance is 1/383 − 1/500 = **6.12 × 10⁻⁴ m²·K/W**, a useful trigger for cleaning.

## Common mistakes

- **Pairing the wrong ends.** In counterflow, ΔT₁ is hot inlet minus cold outlet; using hot inlet minus cold inlet gives a parallel flow LMTD.
- **Converting ΔT in °F with an offset.** A temperature difference converts by 1.8 only (1 K = 1.8 °F), with no 32 offset.
- **Leaving out F for multipass exchangers.** A 1-2 shell and tube unit sized on the counterflow LMTD alone is undersized.
- **Ignoring a temperature cross.** If the cold outlet rises above the hot outlet in a 1-2 exchanger, F drops sharply or no solution exists.
- **Using a clean U for design.** Add fouling resistances from TEMA or experience, or the exchanger will fall short after a few months.
