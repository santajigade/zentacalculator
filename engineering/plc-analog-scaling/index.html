    A PLC analog input card converts a 4 to 20 mA or 0 to 10 V signal into a raw integer, and the program must scale it to bar, °C or m³/h. This PLC analog scaling calculator converts raw counts to engineering units and back, with percent of range and the resolution of one count.

    Controls engineers use it when writing scaling blocks, and technicians use it during PLC analog scaling from raw counts checks and loop testing. See also analog signal resolution in PLCs.

    How to use the PLC analog scaling calculator

    Enter the raw count range of the analog input card for your signal type, the engineering range of the transmitter and the raw count the PLC is reading. You get the value in engineering units, the percent of range and the resolution of one count. To go the other way, clear the raw count and enter a value in engineering units.

    PLC analog scaling formula

    EU = EUmin + (Raw − Rawmin) ÷ (Rawmax − Rawmin) × (EUmax − EUmin)

    This is what the Siemens NORM_X / SCALE_X pair, the Rockwell SCP instruction and most PLC “scale” blocks calculate.

    Example

    A 0–10 bar transmitter on a Siemens S7-1200 input (0–27648) reads 20736 counts:

    • Percent of range = 20736 ÷ 27648 = 75.0%
    • Pressure = 0 + 0.75 × 10 = 7.5 bar
    • Resolution = 10 ÷ 27648 = 0.000362 bar per count

    Raw ranges depend on card model and configuration — confirm with the manufacturer’s manual.

    Tips

    • Scale in floating point (REAL) to avoid integer truncation.
    • Clamp or alarm values outside the range: below about 3.8 mA or above 20.5 mA usually means a sensor fault (NAMUR NE 43).
    • If the transmitter already does square-root extraction, scale linearly; otherwise apply the root after scaling.

    Going the other way: engineering units to counts

    A temperature transmitter ranged −50 to 150 °C feeds the same 0 to 27,648 input. To find the count the PLC should show at 85 °C, clear the raw count and enter 85 as the value:

    • Raw = 0 + (85 − (−50)) ÷ (150 − (−50)) × 27,648 = 18,662 counts

    This is the number to compare with the PLC watch table while a calibrator simulates 85 °C at the transmitter, and the value to type into a test sequence that writes simulated inputs.

    Cards with a live zero

    Some cards report a non-zero count at 4 mA. Suppose a card is configured so 4 mA reads 6,400 and 20 mA reads 32,000, and it carries a 0 to 6 m level signal. Set raw low to 6,400 and raw high to 32,000; a reading of 19,200 counts is then (19,200 − 6,400) ÷ 25,600 × 6 = 3.00 m. Entering 0 as raw low by mistake would show 3.60 m instead, an error of 0.60 m that is largest at the bottom of the range.

    Counts below zero and above 27,648 are real readings on Siemens cards, which report down to about −4,864 and up to 32,511 before flagging under or overflow (32,767 or −32,768). Use them for fault alarms instead of clamping them away.

    Checking the scaling during commissioning

    Disconnect the transmitter and drive the input with a loop calibrator in source mode, or put the calibrator in simulate mode in series with a loop powered input. Step through 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20 mA and record the raw count and the scaled value from the PLC watch table. Every point should land within a few counts of the values in the table above. A constant offset points to a wrong raw low value; an error that grows with signal points to a wrong raw high value or a wrong engineering span.

    Common mistakes

    • Using 32,767 as raw high on a Siemens card. The default reading of 20,736 counts would then show 6.328 bar instead of 7.5 bar, about 16% low across the whole range.
    • Doing the division in integers. (20,736 ÷ 27,648) in integer maths is 0, so the result sticks at the bottom of the range until the count reaches full scale. Convert to REAL before dividing.
    • Scaling the transmitter range twice. If the transmitter or the card already outputs engineering units (HART variable or a card set to scale internally), scaling again in the program distorts the value.
    • Mismatching the range in the PLC and the transmitter. A transmitter re-ranged to 0 to 16 bar in the field while the PLC still scales 0 to 10 bar reads 37.5% low everywhere.
    • Clamping before alarming. Limiting the value to 0 to 100% first hides the 3.6 mA and 21 mA fault currents that the diagnostics need to see.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the PLC analog scaling formula?

    EU = EU_min + (raw − raw_min) ÷ (raw_max − raw_min) × (EU_max − EU_min). It is the equation of a straight line through the two range end points.

    Why does Siemens use 27648 instead of 32767?

    Siemens maps the nominal range to 0–27648 and keeps the counts above it (up to 32511) for overrange, so the program can detect a signal above 20 mA before it saturates.

    How do I convert engineering units back to raw counts?

    Clear the raw count, enter the value in engineering units and calculate — raw = raw_min + (EU − EU_min) ÷ (EU_max − EU_min) × (raw_max − raw_min).

    Does the raw range change for 4–20 mA versus 0–20 mA?

    On many cards, yes — some map 4–20 mA to the full raw range while others give a non-zero count at 4 mA (for example 3277–16384 on older 16384-count cards). Always check the input card manual.

    What does the PLC show if I scale with 32767 instead of 27648?

    Every reading is low by the ratio 27,648 ÷ 32,767, about 15.6%. A 0 to 10 bar signal at 20,736 counts would display 6.328 bar instead of 7.5 bar.

    How do I scale a range that starts below zero?

    Enter the negative value as engineering units at range low. For −50 to 150 °C on a 0 to 27,648 input, 85 °C corresponds to 18,662 counts and zero counts is −50 °C.
