## How to use the temperature converter

Type a temperature in **any** of the three fields. The other two update as you type. Negative values work for Celsius and Fahrenheit.

## Temperature formulas

<p class="formula">°F = °C × 9/5 + 32<br/>°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9<br/>K = °C + 273.15</p>

## Temperature differences

A **change** of 1 °C equals a change of 1.8 °F, with no +32. If the temperature rises by 10 °C, it rises by 18 °F.

## Mental shortcut

For a rough °C → °F, double the Celsius value and add 30. For 20 °C: 40 + 30 = 70 °F (exact: 68 °F). It is close for everyday weather but drifts at high temperatures.

## Where each scale is used

- **Celsius** — everyday use in almost every country, and in science.
- **Fahrenheit** — everyday use in the United States and a few other places.
- **Kelvin** — science and engineering; it starts at absolute zero, so it never goes negative.
