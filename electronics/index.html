    Design faster, with fewer mistakes

    Each calculator works in both directions: get the output of a circuit, or enter the output you want and solve for the component value. Results are shown in convenient units — kΩ, µF, mH, kHz — and pages list the nearest standard values.

    Learn as you calculate

    Every page explains the formula, gives a worked example and includes reference tables, so the calculators double as a quick electronics reference.

    Analog and power circuits

    For op-amp stages there are op-amp gain, summing, differential, instrumentation amplifier, integrator and transimpedance calculators, plus Sallen-Key and RC filters. Power designers can size buck, boost and buck-boost converters, an LM317 regulator, a heat sink and MOSFET losses.

    Sensors, digital and RF

    Sensor front ends are covered by the Wheatstone bridge, strain gauge, NTC thermistor, shunt resistor and Hall-effect current sensor calculators. For microcontrollers, check ADC resolution, I2C pull-up values and UART baud rate error. For RF and PCB work there are dBm to watts, free space path loss, antenna length, microstrip impedance and PCB trace width.

    Frequently asked questions

    What resistor do I need for an LED?

    R = (supply voltage − LED forward voltage) ÷ LED current. The LED resistor calculator does this for single LEDs and series strings.

    Can I solve for a component value?

    Yes. Enter the result you want, then press × on the component field to have it calculated.

    Are standard E12/E24 values included?

    Pages such as the LED resistor and resistor colour code calculators list standard values to help you pick real parts.
