## How to use the ligation calculator

Enter the **vector amount** and **length**, the **insert length** and the desired **molar ratio**. The mass of insert to use is calculated.

## Ligation formula

<p class="formula">ng insert = ng vector × (insert bp ÷ vector bp) × ratio</p>

## Example

50 ng of a 3,000 bp vector, 1,000 bp insert, 3:1 ratio:

ng insert = 50 × (1,000 ÷ 3,000) × 3 = **50 ng**

## Insert amounts for common ratios

50 ng of 3,000 bp vector:

| Insert size | 1:1 | 3:1 | 5:1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 bp | 1.7 ng | 5.0 ng | 8.3 ng |
| 250 bp | 4.2 ng | 12.5 ng | 20.8 ng |
| 500 bp | 8.3 ng | 25.0 ng | 41.7 ng |
| 1,000 bp | 16.7 ng | 50.0 ng | 83.3 ng |
| 2,000 bp | 33.3 ng | 100.0 ng | 166.7 ng |
| 5,000 bp | 83.3 ng | 250.0 ng | 416.7 ng |

## Choosing a ratio

| Situation | Typical insert : vector ratio |
|---|---|
| Standard sticky-end cloning | 3:1 |
| Blunt-end cloning | 3:1 to 5:1 |
| Very small inserts (under 200 bp) | 5:1 to 10:1 |
| Large inserts (similar to vector size) | 1:1 |

## Controls

- **Vector only** (no insert): shows background from uncut or self-ligated vector.
- **No ligase**: shows uncut vector.
- **Positive control**: a known insert that works.

## Improving ligation

- Dephosphorylate the vector to stop self-ligation.
- Gel-purify the vector and insert to remove small fragments.
- Use fresh ligase buffer — ATP degrades with freeze–thaw cycles.
- Keep total DNA around 50–200 ng in a 10–20 µL reaction.

## The same example in femtomoles

The defaults (50 ng of a 3,000 bp vector, a 1,000 bp insert, 3:1) give **50 ng of insert**. Equal masses can be a 3:1 molar ratio because the insert is a third the length. Using the double stranded DNA molar mass of 617.96 g/mol per base pair plus 36.04:

- Vector: 50 ng ÷ 1,853,916 g/mol = **27.0 fmol**
- Insert: 50 ng ÷ 617,996 g/mol = **80.9 fmol**
- Ratio: 80.9 ÷ 27.0 = 3.00

## Same insert, different vectors

For a 1,000 bp insert at 3:1 with 50 ng of vector, the insert mass falls as the vector gets longer, because 50 ng of a big plasmid is fewer molecules:

| Vector | Length | 50 ng is | Insert for 3:1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| pUC19 | 2,686 bp | 30.1 fmol | 55.8 ng |
| pET-28a(+) | 5,369 bp | 15.1 fmol | 27.9 ng |
| pcDNA3.1(+) | 5,428 bp | 14.9 fmol | 27.6 ng |
| 10 kb backbone | 10,000 bp | 8.1 fmol | 15.0 ng |

Use the length of the cut backbone that actually goes into the reaction. If a 600 bp stuffer fragment is removed during digestion, the backbone is shorter than the full plasmid map.

## Setting up the reaction

Convert the mass to a volume from the measured concentration. At 25 ng/µL the 50 ng of insert is 2.0 µL, an easy volume to pipette. At 120 ng/µL it would be only 0.42 µL, so dilute the insert 1:10 first and add 4.2 µL. A typical 20 µL T4 ligation then takes 2 µL of 10× buffer, the vector and insert, 1 µL of ligase and water to volume. Thaw the buffer on ice and mix it well, since its ATP and DTT settle out and break down with repeated thawing.

## Common mistakes

- **Using a 3:1 mass ratio.** Three times the vector mass is a 3:1 molar ratio only when the insert and vector are the same length.
- **Entering the uncut plasmid size.** Remove any fragment cut out during digestion from the vector length.
- **Trusting a UV reading of gel extracted DNA.** Guanidine salts and agarose absorb in the UV and can make concentrations look two or three times higher; check on a gel against a ladder.
- **Adding far too much DNA.** Above about 200 ng in 20 µL, inserts join into concatemers and multiple insert clones appear.
- **Skipping the vector only control.** Without it you cannot tell real recombinants from vector that recircularised.
