faq:
  - q: What is Peukert's law?
    a: t = H × (C ÷ (I × H))^k, where C is the rated capacity at the rated discharge time H, I the actual current and k the Peukert exponent. With k = 1 it reduces to the simple t = C ÷ I.
  - q: What is a typical Peukert exponent?
    a: About 1.2–1.3 for flooded lead-acid, 1.05–1.15 for AGM, 1.1–1.2 for gel and close to 1.0 (1.02–1.05) for lithium-ion. The battery maker can quote it, or you can calculate it from two rated capacities at different discharge times.
  - q: Why does a battery last longer than Ah ÷ A at low currents?
    a: Below the rated current the formula predicts more than the rated capacity, because the losses fall. In practice self-discharge and temperature limit that gain, so do not count on more than the rated Ah.
  - q: How is internal resistance measured?
    a: Measure the open-circuit voltage, apply a known load current and measure the voltage again. R = (V_open − V_load) ÷ I. Rising internal resistance is a good sign of an ageing battery.

## How to use the Peukert battery runtime calculator

Enter the battery's **rated capacity** and the **discharge time** it is rated at (C20 batteries: 20 h; C10: 10 h), the **load current** and the **Peukert exponent**. The calculator returns the corrected runtime, the simple runtime for comparison and the effective capacity. To estimate **internal resistance**, also enter the open-circuit voltage and the voltage measured while the load current flows.

## Peukert's law formula

<p class="formula">t = H × (C ÷ (I × H))<sup>k</sup> &nbsp;·&nbsp; C<sub>eff</sub> = I × t</p>

<p class="formula">R<sub>int</sub> = (V<sub>oc</sub> − V<sub>load</sub>) ÷ I</p>

## Worked example

A 100 Ah C20 lead-acid battery (k = 1.2) supplying 10 A:

- Simple estimate: 100 ÷ 10 = **10.0 h**
- Peukert: 20 × (100 ÷ (10 × 20))<sup>1.2</sup> = 20 × 0.5<sup>1.2</sup> = **8.71 h**
- Effective capacity at 10 A = **87.1 Ah** — about 13% less than rated
- Voltage falls from 12.7 V to 12.5 V under load: R<sub>int</sub> = 0.2 ÷ 10 = **20 mΩ**, losing **2 W** as heat

## Practical notes

- Runtime to **100% discharge** shortens lead-acid life; for daily cycling, plan on using 50% of capacity (lithium: about 80–90%).
- Peukert's law assumes a constant current. For a constant-power load (inverter), the current rises as the voltage falls — use the average current.
- Capacity also falls in the cold: a lead-acid battery at 0 °C gives roughly 80% of its 25 °C capacity.
- Finding k from two ratings: k = ln(t₂/t₁) ÷ ln(I₁/I₂), using the currents and times of two published capacity ratings.
