## How to use this calculator

Enter your **current annual salary** and the **raise %** to get the new salary, the yearly increase and the new monthly pay. Already know the new salary? Press × on Raise and type it in to see the percentage.

## Formula

<p class="formula">New salary = Current × (1 + raise %)</p>

## Worked example

Current salary 60,000 with an 8% raise:

- New salary = 60,000 × 1.08 = **64,800**
- Increase = **4,800** per year
- New monthly salary = **5,400**

## Real vs nominal raise

A raise only improves your living standard if it beats inflation:

| Raise | Inflation | Real change (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| 3% | 2% | +1% |
| 8% | 6% | +2% |
| 5% | 6% | −1% |

## Typical raises

- **Annual increment**: often 3–10%, depending on country, industry and performance.
- **Promotion**: commonly 10–20%.
- **Job switch**: often 15–30% or more, which is why many people see the biggest jumps when they change employers.

## Raises compound

A raise is applied to your already-raised salary. Five years of 8% raises lift 60,000 to 88,160, a total increase of 46.9%, not 40%. Over a career, small differences in yearly raises add up to a large difference in lifetime earnings.

## Fixed vs variable pay

When an offer quotes a total package, check the split:

- **Fixed pay** is guaranteed every month.
- **Variable pay or bonus** depends on performance and may not be paid in full.
- **Benefits** such as insurance, retirement contributions or stock options add value but are not cash in hand.

Calculate the raise on fixed pay to compare offers fairly.

## Negotiation tips

- Research the market rate for your role and location.
- Show measurable results from the past year.
- Consider the total package — bonus, benefits, flexibility and training.
