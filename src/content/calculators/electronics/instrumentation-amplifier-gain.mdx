## How to use the instrumentation amplifier gain calculator

Choose the **amplifier**, then enter the **gain** you want to get the gain resistor R<sub>G</sub> — or enter R<sub>G</sub> and press × on Gain to find the gain. Add a **differential input** to see the output voltage.

## Instrumentation amplifier gain formula

<p class="formula">G = 1 + k ÷ R<sub>G</sub> &nbsp;·&nbsp; R<sub>G</sub> = k ÷ (G − 1)</p>

where k is the internal resistor constant from the datasheet.

## Typical application: load cell

A 2 mV/V load cell on 5 V excitation gives 10 mV at full load. A gain of 250 (R<sub>G</sub> ≈ 200 Ω for INA128) scales it to 2.5 V for a 0–5 V ADC.

## Output range

The output cannot swing to the rails and is also limited by the common-mode voltage at the inputs — check the datasheet's input/output range charts, especially on single 3.3 or 5 V supplies.

## Worked example: bridge sensor into an ADC

The default inputs describe a common job: a 350 Ω load cell rated 2 mV/V, excited at 10 V, gives **20 mV** at full load. To turn that into 2 V for a 0 to 2.5 V ADC with headroom, pick the INA128 and a gain of 100:

- R<sub>G</sub> = 50,000 ÷ (100 − 1) = **505.05 Ω**
- Output at full load = 100 × 20 mV = **2.00 V**
- The nearest 1% E96 value is 511 Ω, which gives G = 98.85, a gain error of **-1.15%**
- Both inputs sit near 5 V, half the excitation, so that common mode voltage must lie inside the amplifier's input range at the chosen supply

If -1.15% is too much, use a 0.1% E192 value of 505 Ω, or a 499 Ω resistor in series with a small trim resistor, and calibrate with known weights.

## How it is set up on a real board

The R<sub>G</sub> pins connect to internal nodes of the input stage, so place the resistor right next to the package with short, equal traces. Drive the REF pin from a low impedance source such as a buffered mid-supply voltage; any resistance in series with REF unbalances the internal difference amplifier and degrades common mode rejection. After assembly, check the gain with a precision source or a calibrator by applying two known differential voltages and fitting a straight line, which removes the offset at the same time.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving no DC path for input bias current.** A floating thermocouple or a transformer coupled signal lets the inputs drift to a rail. Tie each input to ground or REF through a resistor of 10 kΩ to 1 MΩ.
- **Ignoring trace resistance at high gain.** At G = 1000, R<sub>G</sub> is only 50.1 Ω, so an extra 0.1 Ω of copper and solder lowers the gain by about **0.20%**.
- **Buying R<sub>G</sub> on tolerance alone.** A 0.1% part adds about 0.10% gain error at G = 100, but a 50 ppm/K mismatch to the internal resistors adds another 0.20% over a 40 K swing.
- **Checking only the output swing.** With a high gain the internal nodes can clip before the output does, so read the datasheet's common mode versus output voltage chart for your supply.
- **Expecting the formula to hold for any part.** Some in-amps, such as the INA333 and AD8237, use different constants or two gain resistors, so take k from the exact datasheet.
