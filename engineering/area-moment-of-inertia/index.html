    How to use the area moment of inertia calculator

    For a rectangle, enter the width b and the depth h measured in the direction of bending. For a round section, enter the outer diameter and the inner diameter (0 for a solid bar). The calculator returns the second moment of area I in cm⁴ and the elastic section modulus Z in cm³ for both shapes — ready for the beam deflection and bending stress calculators.

    Second moment of area formulas

    Rectangle: I = b h³ ÷ 12  ·  Z = b h² ÷ 6

    Circle / tube: I = π (D⁴ − d⁴) ÷ 64  ·  Z = π (D⁴ − d⁴) ÷ (32 D)

    1 cm⁴ = 10⁻⁸ m⁴ and 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³.

    Example

    A 100 × 200 mm timber beam bending about its strong axis, and a 60 mm solid steel bar:

    • Rectangle: I = 100 × 200³ ÷ 12 = 66,666,667 mm⁴ = 6,666.7 cm⁴; Z = 100 × 200² ÷ 6 = 666.7 cm³
    • Solid bar: I = π × 60⁴ ÷ 64 = 63.62 cm⁴; Z = π × 60³ ÷ 32 = 21.21 cm³

    Notes

    • Turning a rectangle on its side swaps b and h: a 100 × 200 mm plank is four times stiffer on edge than flat.
    • For built-up shapes, add the I of each part about the common axis using the parallel-axis theorem, I = Ic + A d².

    From I and Z to a real beam check

    Take the default 100 × 200 mm section as a C24 timber beam (E = 11,000 MPa) spanning 3 m, simply supported, with a uniform load of 2 kN/m:

    • Maximum moment M = wL² ÷ 8 = 2 × 3² ÷ 8 = 2.25 kN·m
    • Bending stress σ = M ÷ Z = 2,250,000 N·mm ÷ 666,667 mm³ = 3.38 MPa
    • Midspan deflection = 5wL⁴ ÷ (384 E I) = 2.88 mm, which is span ÷ 1,043

    Laid flat, the same plank has I = 1,666.7 cm⁴ and Z = 333.3 cm³. The stress doubles to 6.75 MPa and the deflection quadruples to 11.51 mm, because Z follows h² and I follows h³.

    Why hollow sections are efficient

    Boring out the centre of the default 60 mm bar removes material that sits close to the neutral axis and contributes little to I:

    Section (D × d) Area I Z I per unit area
    60 × 0 mm 2,827 mm² 63.62 cm⁴ 21.21 cm³ 225 mm²
    60 × 20 mm 2,513 mm² 62.83 cm⁴ 20.94 cm³ 250 mm²
    60 × 30 mm 2,121 mm² 59.64 cm⁴ 19.88 cm³ 281 mm²
    60 × 40 mm 1,571 mm² 51.05 cm⁴ 17.02 cm³ 325 mm²
    60 × 50 mm 864 mm² 32.94 cm⁴ 10.98 cm³ 381 mm²

    A 60 × 40 mm tube keeps about 80% of the solid bar’s stiffness with only 56% of its weight. The limit is wall buckling: very thin walls fail locally before the section reaches its bending capacity.

    Elastic and plastic section modulus

    Z here is the elastic modulus, the value that gives the stress when the outer fibre first reaches yield. Once the whole section yields, the plastic modulus applies: b h² ÷ 4 = 1,000.0 cm³ for the default rectangle (1.5 times Z) and D³ ÷ 6 = 36.00 cm³ for the 60 mm bar (1.70 times Z). Eurocode tables list these as Wel and Wpl, while AISC tables use S for elastic and Z for plastic, so always check which one a table means.

    Common mistakes

    • Swapping b and h. h is the depth in the direction of the load. Getting it backwards on the default section understates I by a factor of four.
    • Mixing mm⁴ and cm⁴. 1 cm⁴ = 10,000 mm⁴, so a 66,666,667 mm⁴ result is 6,666.7 cm⁴, not 6.7 million.
    • Using radius in a diameter formula. π D⁴ ÷ 64 and π r⁴ ÷ 4 are the same thing; mixing them gives an error of 16 times.
    • Using I for torsion of a rectangle. The polar moment J = 2I holds only for round sections. Rectangles and open shapes need a separate torsion constant.
    • Adding parts without the parallel-axis term. Each flange of a built-up section must include A d² about the common neutral axis, which usually dominates the total.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the moment of inertia of a rectangle?

    I = b h³ ÷ 12 about the centroidal axis parallel to b, where h is the depth in the direction of bending. The section modulus is Z = b h² ÷ 6.

    What is the moment of inertia of a circle and a tube?

    Solid circle I = π d⁴ ÷ 64; hollow tube I = π (D⁴ − d⁴) ÷ 64. The section modulus is I divided by the outer radius, Z = π (D⁴ − d⁴) ÷ (32 D).

    Is area moment of inertia the same as mass moment of inertia?

    No. The area moment (m⁴) describes a cross-section's resistance to bending; the mass moment (kg·m²) describes a body's resistance to rotation. They share a name and the r² weighting but are used for different problems.

    Why are I-beams so efficient?

    I grows with the square of the distance from the neutral axis, so putting most material in flanges far from the centre gives a high I and Z for little weight.

    What is the polar moment of inertia of a round shaft?

    For a circular section J = π D⁴ ÷ 32, exactly twice the bending I. The default 60 mm solid bar has J = 127.23 cm⁴, the value used for shaft torsion stress and twist.

    How do I convert cm⁴ to in⁴?

    1 in⁴ = 2.54⁴ = 41.623 cm⁴. The default 100 × 200 mm rectangle with I = 6,666.7 cm⁴ is about 160.2 in⁴.
