## How to use the low flow cutoff calculator

Enter the **cutoff** as a percentage of maximum flow, the **maximum flow** and the **DP at maximum flow** (the transmitter's calibrated span). You get the cutoff in DP terms, the actual cutoff flow and DP, and the noise gain at that point. To find the flow corresponding to a DP cutoff set in a transmitter, clear the flow cutoff and enter the DP percentage.

## Low flow cutoff formulas

<p class="formula">Flow fraction q = √(DP fraction) &nbsp;⇔&nbsp; DP fraction = q²</p>

<p class="formula">Noise gain = d(q) ÷ d(DP) = 1 ÷ (2q)</p>

## Example

Cutoff at 5% flow on a 0–100 m³/h meter with 250 mbar DP span:

- DP at cutoff = 0.05² = **0.25%** of span → **0.625 mbar**
- Cutoff flow = **5 m³/h**
- Noise gain = 1 ÷ (2 × 0.05) = **10** — each 0.1% of DP noise shows as 1% flow

| Flow (% of max) | DP (% of max) | Noise gain |
|---|---|---|
| 1% | 0.01% | 50 |
| 2% | 0.04% | 25 |
| 3% | 0.09% | 16.6667 |
| 5% | 0.25% | 10 |
| 7% | 0.49% | 7.14286 |
| 10% | 1% | 5 |
| 20% | 4% | 2.5 |
| 50% | 25% | 1 |
| 100% | 100% | 0.5 |

## Where to apply the cutoff

- In the **transmitter** (many smart DP transmitters have a configurable low-flow cutoff and linear-to-root transition).
- Or in the **DCS/PLC** flow block — but only in one place, not both.
- Fix zero drift first: re-zero the transmitter at line pressure with equalising valve open before blaming noise.

## How a small zero error becomes false flow

With the pipe blocked in and no flow, any zero offset in the transmitter is square rooted too. A zero shift of only 0.1% of the 250 mbar span (0.25 mbar) reads as √0.001 = **3.16%** flow, or 3.16 m³/h on a 100 m³/h meter, and the totalizer keeps counting it. With the 5% cutoff in this example that error is hidden, which is the purpose of the cutoff. Noise behaves the same way: at the cutoff, a DP disturbance of 0.05% of span moves the flow reading by √(0.05² + 0.0005) − 0.05 = **0.48%** of flow.

## Setting the cutoff in practice

Re-zero the transmitter at line pressure with the equalising valve open, then watch the flow reading with the meter blocked in. The cutoff should sit comfortably above the noise band seen there, but below the lowest flow the process needs to see. Many smart transmitters offer a **linear section** below a break point instead of a hard cutoff, so the output follows DP in a straight line near zero rather than jumping. In a DCS, add a little hysteresis (for example cut out at 5% and back in at 6%) so the reading does not chatter around the set point.

## Is the cutoff above the transmitter's own error?

A cutoff only works if the DP it corresponds to is clearly larger than what the transmitter can resolve. Taking a reference accuracy of ±0.065% of calibrated span, the table compares the cutoff DP with that error band (the multiple in brackets) for four spans:

| DP span | Error band | 3% flow | 5% flow | 10% flow |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 62.5 mbar | 0.041 mbar | 0.056 mbar (1.4×) | 0.156 mbar (3.8×) | 0.625 mbar (15.4×) |
| 250 mbar | 0.163 mbar | 0.225 mbar (1.4×) | 0.625 mbar (3.8×) | 2.500 mbar (15.4×) |
| 1000 mbar | 0.650 mbar | 0.900 mbar (1.4×) | 2.500 mbar (3.8×) | 10.000 mbar (15.4×) |
| 2500 mbar | 1.625 mbar | 2.250 mbar (1.4×) | 6.250 mbar (3.8×) | 25.000 mbar (15.4×) |

The ratio does not depend on the span, because both the cutoff DP and the error scale with it: a 3% cutoff sits only 1.4 times above the error band, while 5% gives 3.8 times and 10% gives 15.4 times. Zero drift with static pressure and ambient temperature adds to the reference figure in service, which is why settings below about 5% of flow often chatter.

## Linear section instead of a hard cutoff

With a linear section the output follows a straight line from zero up to a break point q₀, where it joins the root curve. The line has slope 1 ÷ q₀ on the DP fraction, so with q₀ = 5% a DP of 0.1% of span reads 0.001 ÷ 0.05 = **2.0%** flow instead of √0.001 = 3.16% on the pure root curve. The noise gain below the break is a constant 20, twice the root curve's gain at the break but far below the 50 it reaches at 1% flow, and there is no step from zero.

## What the cutoff costs the totalizer

Suppose a line runs at 4 m³/h for 6 hours each night, just under the 5 m³/h cutoff in the example. The flow reads zero, so the totalizer misses 24 m³ a day, about 8,760 m³ a year. If the plant needs that flow measured, a smaller orifice, a second low range transmitter or a meter with wider turndown is the fix, not a lower cutoff. When hysteresis is used, the 5% cut out and 6% cut in points correspond to 0.625 mbar and 0.900 mbar on the 250 mbar span.

## Common mistakes

- **Entering the cutoff as a DP percentage.** A setting of 5% in a field marked DP is about 22% of flow (√0.05), which hides a large part of the range.
- **Square rooting twice.** If the transmitter is set to square root output, the DCS input must be linear, or the reading will follow the fourth root of DP.
- **Applying the cutoff in two places.** Two cutoffs with different settings make it hard to tell why the reading drops to zero.
- **Using the cutoff to hide a bad zero.** A cutoff masks drift but does not fix it; recalibrate when the blocked-in reading wanders.
- **Forgetting the totalizer.** Flow below the cutoff is not counted, so batch and custody totals read low on long low-flow periods.
