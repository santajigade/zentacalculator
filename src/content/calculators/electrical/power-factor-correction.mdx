## How to use the power factor correction calculator

Enter the **active load** in kW, the **present** and **target power factor**, and the supply **voltage** and **frequency**. You get the capacitor kVAr required, the capacitance per phase for a delta-connected bank, the kVA before and after, and how much the line current falls.

For more background, read [Power Factor Improvement Calculation](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/power-factor-improvement-calculation/) and [Power Factor, kW, kVA and kVAr Explained](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/power-factor-kw-kva-kvar/).

## Power factor correction formula

<p class="formula">Q<sub>c</sub> = P × (tan φ₁ − tan φ₂), &nbsp; φ = cos⁻¹(PF)</p>

For a delta-connected three-phase bank, each capacitor sees the line voltage:

<p class="formula">C per phase = Q<sub>c</sub> ÷ (3 × 2πf × V<sub>L</sub>²)</p>

## Example

100 kW at 0.75 PF, corrected to 0.95 at 415 V, 50 Hz:

- tan φ₁ = 0.882, tan φ₂ = 0.329
- Q<sub>c</sub> = 100 × (0.882 − 0.329) = **55.3 kVAr**
- kVA falls from 133.3 to 105.3 and line current falls by **21%**

## Why correct power factor?

- **Lower bills**: avoid utility penalties for low PF and reduce kVA maximum demand charges.
- **Free up capacity**: the same transformer and cables can carry more kW.
- **Lower losses**: I²R losses in cables and transformers fall with current.
- **Better voltage**: less reactive current means less voltage drop.

## Fixed vs automatic correction

- **Fixed capacitors** at individual motors suit constant loads. Size them to about 90% of the motor's no-load magnetising kVAr to avoid self-excitation.
- **APFC panels** (automatic power factor correction) switch capacitor steps as the plant load changes. They are the usual choice for a main LV board.

## Harmonics

Capacitors lower the system's resonant frequency. With VFDs, UPSs and other non-linear loads, check harmonic levels with the [THD calculator](/electrical/thd/) and use detuned reactors where needed.

## Current and loss savings in the example

At 415 V the default 100 kW load draws 185.5 A at PF 0.75 and **146.4 A** at PF 0.95. Cable and transformer copper losses scale with current squared, so they fall by 1 − (0.75 ÷ 0.95)² = **37.7%**, even though the current itself falls by only 21%. The capacitance per phase for a delta bank is 340.8 µF.

## Finding the present power factor

The best source is the electricity bill or the main incomer meter. Average power factor over a month is kWh ÷ √(kWh² + kVArh²). With 42,000 kWh and 37,000 kVArh that is **0.750**, the default value used here. For kW, take the average demand during working hours rather than the peak, because the bank is sized for the usual operating point.

## Capacitor voltage rating

Capacitor kVAr is quoted at the rated voltage and varies with voltage squared. A 50 kVAr unit rated at 440 V delivers only 50 × (415 ÷ 440)² = **44.5 kVAr** on a 415 V system. To get the 55.3 kVAr calculated here from 440 V units, the total nameplate must be about **62.2 kVAr**. In a detuned bank with 7% reactors the capacitor voltage rises to 415 ÷ (1 − 0.07) = 446 V, so 480 V or higher rated capacitors are used.

## Splitting the bank into APFC steps

An automatic panel switches steps to follow the load. For the 62 kVAr nameplate found above, a five-step panel of 5 + 10 + 15 + 15 + 20 = 65 kVAr gives 5 kVAr resolution, fine enough for the relay to hold the target without hunting as machines start and stop. The controller needs a CT on one phase of the incomer, upstream of the capacitor connection, and a voltage reference from the other two phases.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving a fixed bank on at light load.** If the load drops to 30 kW at PF 0.75 with the full 55.3 kVAr connected, net reactive power is -28.9 kVAr and the power factor becomes 0.72 leading, which raises voltage and can be penalised.
- **Ignoring the voltage derating.** Using 440 V capacitors on 415 V without allowing for the 11% kVAr loss leaves the bank short.
- **Fitting plain capacitors where drives and UPSs create harmonics.** They can resonate near the 5th or 7th harmonic and fail; use detuned banks.
- **Connecting capacitors to a VFD output.** The switching waveform destroys them and can trip the drive; correct on the supply side only.
- **Placing the APFC CT downstream of the bank.** The relay then never sees the correction and keeps switching steps in.
