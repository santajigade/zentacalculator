## How to use the percent composition calculator

Enter how many **atoms** of the element are in the formula, the element's **atomic mass** and the compound's **molar mass**. Repeat for each element to get the full composition.

## Percent composition formula

<p class="formula">% element = (atoms × atomic mass) ÷ molar mass × 100</p>

## Worked example — water (H₂O)

- Hydrogen: 2 × 1.008 ÷ 18.015 = **11.2%**
- Oxygen: 1 × 15.999 ÷ 18.015 = **88.8%**

## Percent composition examples

| Compound | Element | Percent by mass |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O | O | 88.81% |
| CO₂ | C | 27.29% |
| NaCl | Na | 39.34% |
| CaCO₃ | Ca | 40.04% |
| Fe₂O₃ | Fe | 69.94% |
| C₆H₁₂O₆ | C | 40.00% |
| NH₄NO₃ | N | 35.00% |

## Empirical formula from percent composition

1. Assume 100 g of compound so percentages become grams.
2. Divide each by its atomic mass to get moles.
3. Divide all by the smallest number of moles.
4. Multiply to get whole numbers if needed.

**Example**: 40.0% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O → 3.33 mol C, 6.65 mol H, 3.33 mol O → ratio 1 : 2 : 1 → empirical formula **CH₂O**.

## Fertiliser labels

Fertiliser grades such as 46-0-0 (urea) give nitrogen, phosphate (as P₂O₅) and potash (as K₂O) percentages. Urea (CO(NH₂)₂, 60.06 g/mol) contains 2 × 14.007 ÷ 60.06 = **46.6% nitrogen**.

## Hydrates

Water of crystallisation counts in the mass. Copper sulphate pentahydrate is 36.1% water by mass.

## Water of crystallisation in common hydrates

Use k = number of water molecules, "atomic mass" = 18.015 g/mol for H₂O and the molar mass of the whole hydrate:

| Hydrate | Molar mass (g/mol) | H₂O per formula | Water by mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| CuSO₄·5H₂O | 249.68 | 5 | 36.08% |
| MgSO₄·7H₂O (Epsom salt) | 246.47 | 7 | 51.17% |
| Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (washing soda) | 286.14 | 10 | 62.96% |
| CaSO₄·2H₂O (gypsum) | 172.16 | 2 | 20.93% |

Heating a weighed hydrate to constant mass and comparing the loss with this percentage is a classic way to find the number of water molecules.

## What the default values give

The calculator opens with hydrogen in water: k = 2, atomic mass 1.008 g/mol and molar mass 18.015 g/mol.

- Mass of hydrogen per mole = 2 × 1.008 = 2.016 g
- Percent hydrogen = 2.016 ÷ 18.015 × 100 = **11.19%**
- Oxygen is the remainder, 88.81%, and 1 kg of water holds 111.9 g of hydrogen

## Full composition of glucose

Run the calculator once per element with M = 180.156 g/mol:

| Element | Atoms | Mass per mole (g) | Mass percent |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | 6 | 72.066 | 40.00% |
| H | 12 | 12.096 | 6.71% |
| O | 6 | 95.994 | 53.28% |

The three percentages add to 100.00%. If your own total is off by more than a few hundredths, an atom count or atomic mass is wrong.

## How percent composition is used in practice

A CHN combustion analyser burns a few milligrams of sample and measures the CO₂, H₂O and N₂ formed, reporting carbon, hydrogen and nitrogen as mass percent. Chemists compare those numbers with the calculated composition. Many journals accept a new compound when each element is within 0.4 percentage points of theory, so a glucose sample must read between 39.60% and 40.40% carbon. On fertiliser labels phosphorus and potassium are quoted as oxides: %P = %P₂O₅ × 0.4364 and %K = %K₂O × 0.8302, factors that come straight from this formula.

## Common mistakes

- **Missing atoms inside parentheses.** Ca(OH)₂ has two oxygens: 43.19% O, not the 21.59% that k = 1 gives.
- **Leaving out water of crystallisation.** The molar mass of a hydrate includes the water, so the percentages of the other elements fall.
- **Using the molar mass of the element gas.** Oxygen in a formula counts 15.999 g/mol per atom, not 31.998 for O₂.
- **Reading fertiliser grades as elements.** The middle number is P₂O₅, so a 0-46-0 grade holds 20.1% phosphorus, not 46%.
- **Rounding atomic masses to whole numbers.** Using 1 for hydrogen gives 2 ÷ 18 = 11.11% for water instead of 11.19%, an error of 0.08 points before any measurement is made.
