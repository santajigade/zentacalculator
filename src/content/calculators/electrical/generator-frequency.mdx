## How to use the generator frequency calculator

Enter the **number of poles** and the **rotor speed** in rpm to get the output frequency. To find the speed for a target frequency, enter the frequency and clear the speed field; to find the pole count for a turbine speed, clear the poles field.

## Generator frequency formula

<p class="formula">f = P × N ÷ 120 &nbsp;·&nbsp; N = 120 f ÷ P</p>

## Example

A 4-pole diesel generator set running at 1,500 rpm:

- f = 4 × 1500 ÷ 120 = **50 Hz**
- At 1,800 rpm the same machine gives 4 × 1800 ÷ 120 = **60 Hz**

## Notes

- The governor holds speed — and therefore frequency — constant as load changes; a 2% droop governor lets a 50 Hz set fall to 49 Hz at full load.
- Induction motors run slightly slower than synchronous speed; see the [induction motor slip calculator](/electrical/induction-motor-slip/).
- Before paralleling with the grid, a generator must match its frequency, voltage and phase angle.

## Frequency under governor droop

The default 4 pole set at 1,500 rpm gives 4 × 1,500 ÷ 120 = **50 Hz**. Most gensets that share load use a droop governor, so speed and frequency fall slightly as load rises. With 4% droop set to give 50 Hz at full load, the no load frequency is 50 × (1 + 0.04) = 52 Hz:

| Load | Frequency | Rotor speed |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | 52.00 Hz | 1,560 rpm |
| 25% | 51.50 Hz | 1,545 rpm |
| 50% | 51.00 Hz | 1,530 rpm |
| 75% | 50.50 Hz | 1,515 rpm |
| 100% | 50.00 Hz | 1,500 rpm |

An isochronous governor holds 50 Hz at every load, which suits a single set running alone but makes load sharing between parallel sets unstable without a load sharing controller.

## Choosing the pole count for a hydro turbine

A water turbine has a best efficiency speed set by head and flow, and the generator must use a whole number of pole pairs. If the turbine runs best at about 214.3 rpm, the ideal pole count is 120 × 50 ÷ 214.3 = 28.00. Choosing **28 poles** gives a synchronous speed of 120 × 50 ÷ 28 = **214.3 rpm**, the nearest standard speed, and the runner is then designed for that speed.

## Measuring frequency and speed on site

Frequency is read from the generator control panel, a clamp meter with a Hz range or a portable power analyser. Speed is checked with an optical tachometer on a reflective mark on the shaft or from the magnetic pickup on the flywheel teeth. If a tachometer reads **1,488 rpm** on the 4 pole set, the expected frequency is 4 × 1,488 ÷ 120 = **49.6 Hz**; a panel reading of exactly 50 Hz would then point to a tachometer or meter error.

## Common mistakes

- **Using pole pairs instead of poles.** f = P N ÷ 120 uses the total number of poles. With pole pairs the formula is f = p N ÷ 60.
- **Counting the engine's cylinders or the alternator's terminal leads.** Neither tells you the pole count; use the nameplate rated speed and frequency.
- **Expecting a 60 Hz alternator to work at 50 Hz with the same voltage.** At 5/6 of rated speed the voltage, cooling fan airflow and output kVA all drop, so the set must be derated.
- **Confusing generator speed with induction motor speed.** A 4 pole motor runs at about 1,440 to 1,480 rpm because of slip, but a 4 pole synchronous generator must turn at exactly 1,500 rpm for 50 Hz.
