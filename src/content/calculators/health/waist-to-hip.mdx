## How to use this calculator

Enter your **waist** and **hip** measurements. The ratio appears instantly — compare it with the table below for your sex.

## The formula

<p class="formula">WHR = waist ÷ hip</p>

## Worked example

Waist 85 cm, hips 95 cm: WHR = 85 ÷ 95 = **0.895**. For a man this is just under the WHO threshold of 0.90; for a woman it would be above 0.85.

## WHO thresholds

| | Low risk | Increased risk |
|---|---|---|
| Men | ≤ 0.90 | > 0.90 |
| Women | ≤ 0.85 | > 0.85 |

Source: World Health Organization, *Waist circumference and waist–hip ratio* (2008).

## Waist size alone

The WHO also gives waist circumference thresholds:

| | Increased risk | Substantially increased risk |
|---|---|---|
| Men (European) | above 94 cm | above 102 cm |
| Women (European) | above 80 cm | above 88 cm |
| Men (South Asian) | above 90 cm | — |
| Women (South Asian) | above 80 cm | — |

Ethnic-specific thresholds come from the International Diabetes Federation.

## Which measure is best?

| Measure | Strength |
|---|---|
| BMI | Simple, widely used, but ignores fat distribution |
| Waist circumference | Easy, captures abdominal fat |
| Waist-to-hip ratio | Shows body shape (apple vs pear) |
| [Waist-to-height ratio](/health/waist-to-height/) | Works across heights, sexes and ethnic groups |

Using two of these together gives a better picture than any one alone.

## How to measure

- **Waist:** midway between the lowest rib and the top of the hip bone, after a normal breath out.
- **Hips:** around the widest part of the buttocks.
- Stand relaxed with feet together; keep the tape snug but not compressing the skin.

This is a screening tool, not a diagnosis.
