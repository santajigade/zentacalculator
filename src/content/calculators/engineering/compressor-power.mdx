## How to use the compressor power calculator

Enter the **actual inlet flow**, the **suction and discharge pressures in absolute units**, the **suction temperature**, the gas's **ratio of specific heats k** and the compressor's **isentropic efficiency**. The calculator returns the ideal power, the shaft power and the ideal (isentropic) discharge temperature.

## Isentropic compression formulas

<p class="formula">P = k/(k−1) × p₁ V₁ × [(p₂/p₁)<sup>(k−1)/k</sup> − 1] ÷ η &nbsp;·&nbsp; T₂ = T₁ (p₂/p₁)<sup>(k−1)/k</sup></p>

## Worked example

600 m³/h (0.1667 m³/s) of air at 1.01325 bar(a) and 20 °C compressed to 8 bar(a), k = 1.4, η = 75%:

- Pressure ratio = 8 ÷ 1.01325 = 7.895; exponent (k−1)/k = 0.2857
- Ideal power = 3.5 × 101,325 × 0.1667 × (1.8046 − 1) = **47.6 kW**
- Shaft power = 47.6 ÷ 0.75 = **63.4 kW**
- Ideal discharge temperature = 293.15 × 1.8046 − 273.15 = **256 °C**

## Notes

- Real discharge temperature is higher than the ideal value, roughly T₁ + (T₂ − T₁) ÷ η.
- Add motor efficiency and transmission losses to size the driver.

## Real discharge temperature

The ideal T₂ assumes no losses. The extra work of an inefficient stage also ends up as heat in the gas, so a better estimate is T₂ ≈ T₁ + (T₂,ideal − T₁) ÷ η. For the example, T₂ ≈ 20 + (256 − 20) ÷ 0.75 = **335 °C**. That is far above the limit for most lubricants and seals, which is why single-stage dry compression to 8 bar is rare and oil-injected screws cool the gas with the oil.

## Two stages with intercooling

Splitting the same duty into two stages with equal pressure ratios and cooling back to 20 °C between them:

- Ratio per stage = √7.895 = **2.810**, so the intermediate pressure is 2.847 bar(a)
- Each stage handles the same p × V, so each needs the same power: 20.30 ÷ 0.75 = 27.06 kW
- Total shaft power = **54.1 kW** against 63.4 kW for one stage, a saving of 15%
- Ideal discharge temperature of each stage = **121 °C**

The isothermal limit, p₁V₁ ln(p₂/p₁) = **34.9 kW** of ideal work, is the floor that more stages approach.

## Using the result in practice

The inlet flow must be the **actual** volume at suction conditions. Vendors often quote free air delivery (FAD) or normal m³/h; convert those to actual inlet flow with the [gas flow correction calculator](/engineering/gas-flow-correction/) first. Add the motor efficiency (typically 94 to 96% for this size) and any belt or gearbox loss to get the electrical input, and leave a margin for filter fouling and higher summer inlet temperature.

## Common mistakes

- **Entering gauge pressure.** 7 bar(g) is 8.013 bar(a); using 7 and 0 bar gives a ratio that is meaningless.
- **Using normal or standard flow as inlet flow.** At a hot or low pressure inlet the actual volume is larger than the normal volume, and the power scales with it.
- **Confusing isentropic and isothermal efficiency.** Screw and reciprocating data usually quote isentropic efficiency; turbo compressor maps may quote polytropic efficiency, which is higher for the same machine.
- **Sizing the motor on shaft power alone.** Motor, drive and transmission losses add several percent, and the motor should not run continuously above its rating.
- **Ignoring inlet temperature.** Power is proportional to absolute inlet temperature for a given mass flow, so warm intake air costs energy.
