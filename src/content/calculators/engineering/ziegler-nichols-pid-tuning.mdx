## How to use the Ziegler–Nichols PID tuning calculator

Enter the **ultimate gain** Ku and the **ultimate period** Pu from your closed-loop test. The calculator gives the controller gain for P, PI and PID control and the integral and derivative times in seconds. Settings assume the ideal (non-interacting) PID form with integral time in seconds per repeat; check whether your controller uses gain or proportional band, and repeats per minute or minutes per repeat.

## Ziegler–Nichols closed-loop rules

| Controller | K<sub>c</sub> | T<sub>i</sub> | T<sub>d</sub> |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | 0.5 K<sub>u</sub> | — | — |
| PI | 0.45 K<sub>u</sub> | P<sub>u</sub> ÷ 1.2 | — |
| PID | 0.6 K<sub>u</sub> | P<sub>u</sub> ÷ 2 | P<sub>u</sub> ÷ 8 |

## Example

A loop oscillates steadily at **Ku = 2** with a period **Pu = 60 s**:

| Rule | K<sub>c</sub> | PB | T<sub>i</sub> | T<sub>d</sub> |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P only | 1 | 100% | — | — |
| PI | 0.9 | 111% | 50 s | — |
| PID (classic) | 1.2 | 83% | 30 s | 7.5 s |
| PI (Tyreus–Luyben) | 0.625 | 160% | 132 s | — |
| PID (Tyreus–Luyben) | 0.909 | 110% | 132 s | 9.52 s |

## Cautions

- The test drives the loop to the edge of instability — don't do it on loops where oscillation is unsafe; use a step test and the [lambda tuning calculator](/engineering/lambda-tuning/) instead.
- Valve stiction and sensor lag change Ku and Pu; fix mechanical problems first.
- After tuning, test with a small set-point step and reduce gain if overshoot is excessive.

## Worked example: from a relay test to PID settings

Driving a loop to steady oscillation with a P-only controller is risky, so many DCS auto-tuners use a **relay test** instead: the output is switched ±d around its working value and the PV settles into a limit cycle of amplitude a. The describing function gives the ultimate gain:

<p class="formula">K<sub>u</sub> = 4d ÷ (π a)</p>

With a relay step of d = ±5% and a measured PV amplitude of a = 3.2% (peak), K<sub>u</sub> = 4 × 5 ÷ (π × 3.2) = **1.989**, and the period of the cycle is P<sub>u</sub>. Taking the calculator defaults K<sub>u</sub> = 2 and P<sub>u</sub> = 60 s, the classic PID settings are:

- K<sub>c</sub> = 0.6 × 2 = **1.2** (PB 83%)
- T<sub>i</sub> = 60 ÷ 2 = **30 s** (0.5 min, or 2 repeats per minute)
- T<sub>d</sub> = 60 ÷ 8 = **7.5 s** (0.125 min)

## Settings for a series (interacting) PID

Many controllers use the series form. The classic rule has T<sub>i</sub> = 4 T<sub>d</sub> exactly, which is the limit for converting ideal settings to series ones:

<p class="formula">K<sub>c</sub>′ = (K<sub>c</sub>/2)(1 + √(1 − 4T<sub>d</sub>/T<sub>i</sub>)) &nbsp;·&nbsp; T<sub>i</sub>′ = (T<sub>i</sub>/2)(1 + √…) &nbsp;·&nbsp; T<sub>d</sub>′ = (T<sub>i</sub>/2)(1 − √…)</p>

For the example the root is zero, so the series settings are K<sub>c</sub>′ = **0.6**, T<sub>i</sub>′ = **15 s** and T<sub>d</sub>′ = **15 s**. Typing the ideal values into a series controller gives a different, more aggressive loop.

## Estimating Ku and Pu from a step test model

If a step test has already given a first order plus dead time model (gain K, time constant τ, dead time θ), the ultimate values can be calculated instead of measured. The loop is at the stability limit at the frequency ω where the process phase lag reaches 180°, atan(ωτ) + ωθ = π, and the ultimate gain is K<sub>u</sub> = √(1 + (ωτ)²) ÷ K. For K = 1.5, τ = 120 s and θ = 20 s, solving numerically gives **K<sub>u</sub> = 6.71** and **P<sub>u</sub> = 75.2 s**, so the classic PID settings would be K<sub>c</sub> = 4.03, T<sub>i</sub> = 37.6 s and T<sub>d</sub> = 9.4 s.

How the ultimate values depend on the dead time ratio (for a process gain of 1; divide K<sub>u</sub> by the real gain):

| θ ÷ τ | K<sub>u</sub> × K | P<sub>u</sub> ÷ θ |
|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 32.06 | 3.92 |
| 0.1 | 16.35 | 3.85 |
| 0.2 | 8.50 | 3.72 |
| 0.5 | 3.81 | 3.42 |
| 1 | 2.26 | 3.10 |
| 2 | 1.52 | 2.75 |

Lag dominant loops (small θ ÷ τ) tolerate high gain and cycle at about four dead times or less; in dead time dominant loops K<sub>u</sub> × K falls towards 1 and P<sub>u</sub> towards 2θ, which is where Ziegler-Nichols settings are least robust.

## A slow temperature loop in minutes

A jacketed reactor temperature loop cycles steadily at K<sub>u</sub> = 8 with P<sub>u</sub> = 12 min. The classic PID settings are K<sub>c</sub> = **4.8** (PB 20.8%), T<sub>i</sub> = **6 min** (0.167 repeats per minute) and T<sub>d</sub> = **1.5 min**. With a period this long, wait several cycles before reading P<sub>u</sub>, because a single peak to peak time can be distorted by load changes.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving integral action on during the test.** Ku and Pu must come from a proportional-only loop or a relay test; integral action shortens the period.
- **Testing with a saturating valve.** If the output hits 0 or 100% during the oscillation, the measured Ku is wrong.
- **Ignoring the controller form.** Ideal, series and parallel PID forms need different numbers for the same response.
- **Using derivative on a noisy PV.** Flow and liquid pressure loops are noisy; use PI there and keep derivative for slow temperature loops.
- **Keeping the settings without a check.** Ziegler-Nichols aims for quarter amplitude decay; most process loops need the gain reduced after a set point test.
