## How to use the commission calculator

Enter the **sales amount**, the **commission rate** and any **base pay** for the period. The calculator shows the commission and total earnings. To find the sales needed for a target income, enter the target in Total earnings and press × on Sales amount.

## Commission formulas

<p class="formula">Commission = Sales × Rate &nbsp;·&nbsp; Total pay = Base + Commission</p>

## Common commission structures

| Structure | How it works |
|---|---|
| Straight commission | Pay is only commission — no base |
| Base + commission | Fixed salary plus a percentage of sales |
| Tiered | Rate rises above targets (e.g. 5% up to 50,000, 8% above) |
| Gross margin commission | Paid on profit, not revenue |
| Draw against commission | Advance paid, then deducted from later commission |

## Tiered example

5% on the first 50,000 and 8% above it, on sales of 80,000:

- First tier: 50,000 × 5% = 2,500
- Second tier: 30,000 × 8% = 2,400
- **Total commission = 4,900** (an effective 6.1%)

For tiers, run the calculator once per tier and add the results.

## Commission on margin

Paying on gross margin rather than revenue stops salespeople discounting heavily to close deals. Check whether your plan pays on invoice value, collected cash or margin.

## Worked example with the default values

Sales of 50,000 at a 5% rate with a base of 2,000 for the period:

- Commission = 50,000 × 0.05 = **2,500**
- Total earnings = 2,000 + 2,500 = **4,500**

To earn 6,000 in total with the same base and rate, the commission must cover 4,000, so sales must reach 4,000 ÷ 0.05 = **80,000**. Enter 6,000 as total earnings and press × on sales amount to get the same answer.

At low rates the required sales climb steeply, which is why low-rate plans such as property or wholesale usually come with a base salary.

## Marginal versus retroactive tiers

Plans with tiers work in one of two ways, and the difference is large. On 80,000 of sales with 5% up to 50,000 and 8% above:

- **Marginal (step) tier:** 5% on the first 50,000 and 8% on the next 30,000 = **4,900**.
- **Retroactive tier:** once sales pass 50,000 the 8% applies to everything, 80,000 × 8% = **6,400**.

A retroactive plan creates a sharp jump at the threshold, so a sale that crosses it can be worth far more than its own value. Read the plan document to see which rule applies.

## When commission is earned and paid

Most plans pay on invoiced sales, but many pay only once the customer has paid, and some claw back commission on returns or cancellations. On a GST invoice, commission is normally calculated on the value before tax: an invoice of 59,000 including 18% GST has a taxable value of 50,000, so 5% commission is 2,500, not 2,950. Commission on gross margin is another variant: a 50,000 sale costing 35,000 leaves 15,000 of margin, and 20% of that pays 3,000.

## Common mistakes

- **Entering 5 instead of 5%.** The rate field is a percentage; a rate typed as a decimal elsewhere must be converted.
- **Applying the top tier to all sales.** Unless the plan says retroactive, only the sales above the threshold earn the higher rate.
- **Including tax in the sales figure.** Use the net sale value excluding GST or sales tax unless the plan says otherwise.
- **Ignoring returns.** Commission paid on cancelled orders is usually recovered from later pay.
- **Comparing plans on rate alone.** A 3% plan on large invoices with a base salary can pay more than 10% on small ones.
