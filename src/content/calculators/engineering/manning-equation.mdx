  - q: What is the Manning equation?
    a: In SI units V = (1/n) R^(2/3) S^(1/2), where V is mean velocity in m/s, R the hydraulic radius in m, S the slope and n the roughness coefficient. Flow Q = V × A.
  - q: What is the hydraulic radius?
    a: Flow area divided by wetted perimeter. For a rectangular channel of width b and depth y, R = b·y ÷ (b + 2y).

## How to use the Manning equation calculator

Enter the **roughness coefficient n**, the channel **bottom width**, the **flow depth** and the **slope**. You get the flow area, wetted perimeter, hydraulic radius, mean velocity and flow rate. To find the normal depth for a known flow, clear the depth and enter the flow.

## Manning equation (SI)

<p class="formula">V = (1/n) × R<sup>2/3</sup> × S<sup>1/2</sup> &nbsp;·&nbsp; Q = V × A</p>

<p class="formula">Rectangular channel: A = b·y &nbsp;·&nbsp; P = b + 2y &nbsp;·&nbsp; R = A ÷ P</p>

## Example

Concrete channel (n = 0.013), 2 m wide, 0.5 m deep, slope 0.1%:

- A = **1 m²**, P = **3 m**, R = **0.3333 m**
- V = (1/0.013) × 0.3333<sup>2/3</sup> × √0.001 = **1.169 m/s**
- Q = 1.169 × 1 = **1.169 m³/s** (4,210 m³/h)

## Worked example: normal depth for a design flow

The same 2 m concrete channel (n = 0.013, slope 0.1%) must carry **2 m³/s**. Clear the depth, enter the flow and the calculator solves for the depth at which Manning gives exactly that flow:

- Normal depth y = **0.731 m**
- Area = 2 × 0.731 = 1.461 m², velocity = 2 ÷ 1.461 = **1.369 m/s**
- Check the default case too: Froude number Fr = V ÷ √(g y) = 1.169 ÷ √(9.80665 × 0.5) = **0.53**, so the flow is subcritical (Fr below 1) and calm.

## Effect of slope (2 m wide, 0.5 m deep, n = 0.013)

| Slope | Velocity | Flow | Froude number |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05% (1 in 2,000) | 0.827 m/s | 0.827 m³/s | 0.37 |
| 0.1% (1 in 1,000) | 1.169 m/s | 1.169 m³/s | 0.53 |
| 0.2% (1 in 500) | 1.654 m/s | 1.654 m³/s | 0.75 |
| 0.5% (1 in 200) | 2.615 m/s | 2.615 m³/s | 1.18 |
| 1% (1 in 100) | 3.698 m/s | 3.698 m³/s | 1.67 |

Velocity rises with the square root of slope, so doubling the slope gives only 41% more flow. Steep channels quickly become supercritical (Fr above 1), with standing waves and hydraulic jumps where the slope eases.

## How it is used in practice

Manning is a design and estimating tool. Engineers use it to size storm drains, irrigation canals and plant effluent channels, and to estimate flow from a measured depth where no flume exists (the slope-area method). For flow that must be measured accurately, a primary device such as a weir or Parshall flume with an ultrasonic or radar level sensor is installed, because a flume's depth to flow relation is fixed by its geometry and does not depend on an estimated n.

## Notes

- Manning assumes steady, uniform, fully turbulent flow; it is not valid near weirs, gates or sudden changes.
- For flow measurement, use a calibrated structure such as a weir or flume — see the [weir flow calculator](/engineering/weir-flow/).

## Common mistakes

- **Entering slope as a whole number.** A fall of 1 m in 1,000 m is 0.1%, or 0.001 as a fraction. Entering 0.1 as a fraction makes the flow ten times too high.
- **Using the US form with metric data.** The 1.486 factor belongs only with feet; mixing systems gives a 49% error.
- **Choosing n for a new surface.** Weed growth, sediment and joint deterioration raise n over time; design with a value that reflects the channel as it will be maintained.
- **Applying Manning near structures.** Flow approaching a weir, gate or bend is not uniform, so the depth there is not the normal depth.
- **Ignoring the minimum velocity.** Channels running below about 0.6 m/s at normal flow tend to collect silt, which changes both the area and the roughness.
