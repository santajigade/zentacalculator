## How to use the per-unit System calculator

Choose a **base power** and **base voltage**, and enter an **actual impedance** in ohms. You get the base impedance, base current and per-unit impedance. Enter a **new base** to convert the per-unit value — for example from a transformer's own rating to a 100 MVA system base.

For more background, read [How to Calculate Short Circuit Fault Current](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/calculate-short-circuit-fault-current/).

## Per-unit System formulas

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Base impedance | Z<sub>b</sub> = kV<sub>b</sub>² ÷ MVA<sub>b</sub> |
| Base current | I<sub>b</sub> = MVA<sub>b</sub> ÷ (√3 × kV<sub>b</sub>) |
| Per-unit | Z<sub>pu</sub> = Z<sub>Ω</sub> ÷ Z<sub>b</sub> |
| Change of base | Z<sub>new</sub> = Z<sub>old</sub> × (V<sub>old</sub>/V<sub>new</sub>)² × (S<sub>new</sub>/S<sub>old</sub>) |

## Example: transformer on a system base

A 2.5 MVA transformer with 6.25% impedance on its own base, converted to 100 MVA:

Z = 0.0625 × (100 ÷ 2.5) = **2.5 pu** on 100 MVA.

## Short-circuit with per-unit

Fault current in pu = 1 ÷ Z<sub>total,pu</sub>; multiply by base current for amps. Adding source, transformer and cable impedances in per unit is how quick fault-level studies are done by hand.

## Worked example with the default inputs

An 11 kV cable has an impedance of 0.6 Ω. On a 10 MVA, 11 kV base:

- Z<sub>b</sub> = 11² ÷ 10 = **12.1 Ω** and I<sub>b</sub> = 10,000,000 ÷ (√3 × 11,000) = **524.86 A**
- Z<sub>pu</sub> = 0.6 ÷ 12.1 = **0.04959 pu**
- Moved to a 100 MVA base at the same voltage: 0.04959 × (11 ÷ 11)² × (100 ÷ 10) = **0.49587 pu**

## A hand fault study on one base

Take a 250 MVA, 11 kV grid feeding that cable, then a 2.5 MVA, 11/0.415 kV, 6.25% transformer, with a fault on the LV busbar. Everything goes on 100 MVA:

| Element | Per-unit impedance (100 MVA) | How it was found |
|---|---|---|
| Grid | 0.4000 | 100 ÷ 250 |
| 11 kV cable | 0.4959 | from the calculator above |
| Transformer | 2.5000 | 0.0625 × 100 ÷ 2.5 |
| **Total** | **3.3959** | series sum |

Base current at 415 V on 100 MVA is 139,121 A, so the fault current is 139,121 ÷ 3.3959 = **41.0 kA**. No turns ratio was needed because the base voltage changed from 11 kV to 0.415 kV across the transformer, exactly in step with its ratio. (Resistance and reactance are added as plain magnitudes here, which is a common conservative shortcut.)

## Equipment rated at a different voltage

Nameplate values are on the equipment's own rating. A 6% transformer rated 10.5 kV used on an 11 kV base becomes 0.06 × (10.5 ÷ 11)² = **0.0547 pu** before any MVA change. Ignoring the voltage term here is a small but real error.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing phase and line quantities.** Use three-phase MVA with line kV, or single-phase MVA with phase kV, never one of each.
- **Keeping one base voltage across a transformer.** The voltage base must follow the turns ratio from zone to zone, or the per-unit values are wrong on one side.
- **Inverting the change of base.** Per-unit impedance rises with the new MVA base and falls with a higher new kV base; check that a 100 MVA base gives a bigger pu figure than a 10 MVA base.
- **Forgetting to convert back.** A fault result of 0.3 pu current means nothing until it is multiplied by the base current of that voltage zone.
- **Treating % and pu as different impedances.** 6% and 0.06 pu are the same thing on the same base.
