## How to use the escape velocity calculator

Enter the **mass** of the planet, moon or star in kilograms and its **radius** — or, for escape from an orbit, the distance from the centre (radius + altitude). The calculator gives the escape velocity in km/h (or mph) and km/s, the circular orbital velocity at the same distance and the gravitational acceleration there. The defaults are for the Earth.

## Escape velocity formula

<p class="formula">v<sub>esc</sub> = √(2GM ÷ R) &nbsp;·&nbsp; v<sub>orbit</sub> = √(GM ÷ R) = v<sub>esc</sub> ÷ √2</p>

G = 6.6743 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg². Because g = GM/R², the formula can also be written v<sub>esc</sub> = √(2gR).

## Worked example

Earth: M = 5.972 × 10²⁴ kg, R = 6,371 km:

- v<sub>esc</sub> = √(2 × 6.6743 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.972 × 10²⁴ ÷ 6.371 × 10⁶) = **11.186 km/s** = **40,270 km/h**
- Circular orbital velocity at the surface = 11.186 ÷ √2 = **7.910 km/s**
- Surface gravity = **9.820 m/s²**

## What the default result means in energy

The default Earth values give v<sub>esc</sub> = **11.186 km/s** (40,270 km/h), v<sub>orbit</sub> = **7.910 km/s** and g = **9.820 m/s²**. As a check, √(2 g R) = √(2 × 9.820 × 6.371 × 10⁶) gives the same 11,186 m/s. Escaping means supplying kinetic energy ½ v² = GM ÷ R = **62.6 MJ per kilogram**, so a 1,000 kg probe needs 62.6 GJ, ignoring drag and the rocket's own fuel.

## Second scenario: leaving from orbit

Enter R = 6,771 km (the surface plus 400 km). The escape velocity drops to 10.85 km/s, and a spacecraft already circling there at 7.67 km/s only needs a burn of **3.18 km/s** to escape. That is why interplanetary probes start from a parking orbit: the hard part, reaching orbit, has already been done.

## Escape velocity and atmospheres

A planet keeps a gas over billions of years roughly when its escape velocity is at least six times the gas's typical molecular speed, √(3 k T ÷ m). The table compares the Earth's 11.19 km/s with molecular speeds at about 1,000 K, a typical temperature high in the Earth's thin outer atmosphere, and the Moon's 2.38 km/s with speeds at its daytime surface temperature of about 390 K:

| Gas | Speed at 1,000 K | Earth ratio | Speed at 390 K | Moon ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen, H₂ | 3.52 km/s | 3.2 | 2.20 km/s | 1.1 |
| Helium, He | 2.50 km/s | 4.5 | 1.56 km/s | 1.5 |
| Water vapour, H₂O | 1.18 km/s | 9.5 | 0.73 km/s | 3.2 |
| Nitrogen, N₂ | 0.94 km/s | 11.9 | 0.59 km/s | 4.0 |
| Carbon dioxide, CO₂ | 0.75 km/s | 14.9 | 0.47 km/s | 5.1 |

Hydrogen and helium fall well below the factor of six for the Earth and leak away slowly, while nitrogen and carbon dioxide are held. On the Moon every gas falls short, which matches its lack of an atmosphere.

## Beyond a single planet

Escaping the Earth does not mean leaving the solar system. At the Earth's distance from the Sun, solar escape velocity is √(2 GM<sub>Sun</sub> ÷ 1 AU) = **42.1 km/s**, and the Earth already orbits at 29.8 km/s, so a probe launched in the direction of the Earth's motion needs 12.3 km/s on top once it has left the Earth's gravity. At the other extreme, squeezing the Earth's mass until its escape velocity equals the speed of light would need a radius of 2GM ÷ c² = **8.87 mm**.

## Common mistakes

- **Entering the altitude instead of the distance from the centre.** For 400 km above the Earth, R is 6,771 km, not 400 km.
- **Mixing kilometres and metres in a hand calculation.** G is in SI units, so R must be in metres; using kilometres makes the answer √1000, about 31.6 times, too large.
- **Thinking the launch must point straight up.** Without air drag, any direction that does not hit the ground works, because escape depends on energy, not direction.
- **Confusing it with orbital velocity.** Circular orbit needs only 7.91 km/s at the surface; escape needs √2 times that.
- **Forgetting the Earth's rotation and the Sun.** The ground at the equator already moves at 0.47 km/s eastward, and escaping the Earth still leaves an object in orbit round the Sun.

## Practical notes

- Launching **eastward** from near the equator gains up to 0.47 km/s from the Earth's rotation.
- Real launches need more than escape velocity in total because of **gravity losses and air drag** — typically 1.5–2 km/s extra for reaching orbit.
- The **Moon has no atmosphere** largely because its escape velocity is only about 2.4 km/s: over millions of years, fast-moving gas molecules escape.
- A **black hole** is an object whose escape velocity at the event horizon equals the speed of light: R = 2GM ÷ c².
