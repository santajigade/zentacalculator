## How to use the hydraulic cylinder force calculator

Enter the **working pressure**, **bore** and **rod diameter**. You get the extend (push) and retract (pull) forces. To find the pressure needed for a load, press × on Working pressure and type the force.

## Hydraulic cylinder force formulas

<p class="formula">F<sub>push</sub> = P × π d² ÷ 4<br/>F<sub>pull</sub> = P × π (d² − r²) ÷ 4</p>

## Worked example

A 50 mm bore, 28 mm rod cylinder at 160 bar:

- Push = 16,000,000 Pa × 0.001963 m² = **31.4 kN** (about 3.2 tonnes)
- Pull = 16,000,000 Pa × 0.001348 m² = **21.6 kN**

## Cylinder speed

Speed depends on oil flow and the effective area:

<p class="formula">v = Q ÷ A</p>

With 20 L/min into a 50 mm bore (19.6 cm²), extend speed is 20,000 cm³/min ÷ 19.6 cm² ≈ **1,019 cm/min**, about 0.17 m/s. Retraction is faster because the rod side has a smaller area.

## Regenerative circuits

In a regenerative circuit, oil from the rod end is fed back to the cap end during extension. Extension becomes faster but the push force is reduced to pressure × rod area.

## Safety margins

- Size cylinders for **at least 25% more** force than the load requires, to cover friction and pressure losses.
- Check **rod buckling** for long strokes under compression.
- Use pressure relief valves set below the cylinder's rated pressure.

## Quick sizing tips

- Add a safety margin of **20–30%** over the calculated load.
- Check the rod for **buckling** on long strokes — slender rods can fail before the force limit is reached.
- Cylinder speed depends on pump flow: speed = flow ÷ effective area.
