## How to use the slope calculator

Enter the coordinates of two points, **(x₁, y₁)** and **(x₂, y₂)**. You get the slope, the straight-line distance between the points and the angle of the line.

## Slope formulas

<p class="formula">m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)<br/>d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)<br/>θ = tan⁻¹(m)</p>

## Worked example

Points (1, 2) and (4, 6):

- Rise = 6 − 2 = 4, run = 4 − 1 = 3
- Slope m = 4 ÷ 3 = **1.333**
- Distance = √(3² + 4²) = **5**
- Angle = tan⁻¹(1.333) = **53.13°**
- Y-intercept b = 2 − 1.333 × 1 = **0.667**, so the line is **y = 1.333x + 0.667**
- Percent grade = 1.333 × 100 = **133.3%**

## Equation of the line

With the slope, the line is y = m x + c. Find c from either point: c = y₁ − m x₁ = 2 − 1.333 × 1 = 0.667. So y = 1.333x + 0.667.

## Slope, grade and angle

| Slope (m) | Grade | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | 2% | 1.15° |
| 0.05 | 5% | 2.86° |
| 0.0833 | 8.33% | 4.76° |
| 0.1 | 10% | 5.71° |
| 0.25 | 25% | 14.04° |
| 0.5 | 50% | 26.57° |
| 1 | 100% | 45.00° |
| 2 | 200% | 63.43° |

A slope of 1 (100% grade) is 45°, not vertical. A vertical line has an undefined slope.

## Parallel and perpendicular lines

- **Parallel** lines have equal slopes.
- **Perpendicular** lines have slopes that multiply to −1. A line with slope 2 is perpendicular to one with slope −1/2.

## Midpoint

The midpoint between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) is ((x₁ + x₂) ÷ 2, (y₁ + y₂) ÷ 2). For (1, 2) and (4, 6) it is (2.5, 4).

## Slope in real life

- **Roads**: steep hills are often 10–15% grade.
- **Drainage pipes**: typically 1–2% fall so water flows.
- **Wheelchair ramps**: maximum 1 : 12 (8.33%) in many building codes.
- **Graphs**: slope shows a rate of change, such as speed on a distance-time graph.

## Does the order of the points matter?

No. Taking the points the other way round gives (2 − 6) ÷ (1 − 4) = 1.3333, the same 1.3333 as before, because both the rise and the run change sign. What matters is that you subtract in the **same order** on the top and the bottom.

## Second example: the fall of a drain pipe

A site level reads the pipe invert at **100.00 m** at the manhole and **99.50 m** at a point 25 m downstream. Take x as distance and y as level, so the points are (0, 100.00) and (25, 99.50):

- Slope m = (99.50 − 100.00) ÷ 25 = **-0.020**, a fall of 2% or 1 in 50
- Angle = tan⁻¹(-0.020) = **-1.146°**
- Length along the pipe = √(25² + 0.50²) = 25.005 m, almost the same as the plan distance

The negative sign simply says the pipe falls in the direction of flow, which is what you want.

Ratios are common on drawings: embankments are written as 1 : 2 or 1 : 3, and access ramps as 1 : 12. A ramp rising 600 mm at 1 : 12 needs 7.2 m of run and a sloping length of 7.22 m.

## How slope is measured on site

Surveyors find slope with a level and staff: they read the height at two points, measure the horizontal distance between them, and divide. Laser levels and digital inclinometers show the angle or percent grade directly, and road designers take grades from the vertical profile of a survey. On a graph, pick two points far apart on the line so reading errors in the coordinates have less effect on the slope.

## Common mistakes

- **Dividing run by rise.** For the default points that gives 0.75 instead of 1.333; slope is always change in y over change in x.
- **Mixing the order of subtraction.** Using y₂ − y₁ on top but x₁ − x₂ underneath flips the sign of the answer.
- **Reading percent grade as degrees.** A 10% grade is only 5.71°, and a 100% grade is 45°.
- **Using the sloping length as the run.** The run is the horizontal distance; on steep ground the length along the surface is noticeably longer.
- **Expecting a number for a vertical line.** When x₁ equals x₂ the run is zero and the slope is undefined, so the calculator cannot return a value.
