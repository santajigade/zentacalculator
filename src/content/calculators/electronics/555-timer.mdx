## How to use the 555 timer calculator

Enter **R1**, **R2** and the timing capacitor **C**. You get the output frequency, the high and low times and the duty cycle. To design for a frequency, enter it with two components and press × on the third.

## The formulas (astable)

<p class="formula">t<sub>H</sub> = 0.693 (R₁ + R₂) C &nbsp;·&nbsp; t<sub>L</sub> = 0.693 R₂ C</p>

<p class="formula">f = 1.44 ÷ ((R₁ + 2R₂) C) &nbsp;·&nbsp; D = (R₁ + R₂) ÷ (R₁ + 2R₂)</p>

## Example frequencies (R1 = 1 kΩ)

| R2 | C | Frequency | Duty cycle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 kΩ | 100 µF | 0.69 Hz | 52% |
| 10 kΩ | 10 µF | 6.86 Hz | 52% |
| 47 kΩ | 1 µF | 15.16 Hz | 51% |
| 10 kΩ | 100 nF | 685.71 Hz | 52% |
| 10 kΩ | 10 nF | 6.86 kHz | 52% |
| 1 kΩ | 10 nF | 48.00 kHz | 67% |

## Monostable mode

In one-shot (monostable) mode, the output pulse lasts t = 1.1 × R × C after a trigger. 100 kΩ and 10 µF give about 1.1 s.

## 555 pin functions

| Pin | Name | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | GND | 0 V |
| 2 | Trigger | Starts timing below ⅓ V<sub>cc</sub> |
| 3 | Output | Up to about 200 mA |
| 4 | Reset | Tie to V<sub>cc</sub> if unused |
| 5 | Control | 10 nF to ground for stability |
| 6 | Threshold | Ends timing above ⅔ V<sub>cc</sub> |
| 7 | Discharge | Discharges the timing capacitor |
| 8 | V<sub>cc</sub> | 4.5–16 V (bipolar NE555) |

The CMOS version (7555, TLC555) runs from 2 V and uses far less current.

## Worked example with the default values

With R1 = 1 kΩ, R2 = 10 kΩ and C = 10 µF:

1. High time t<sub>H</sub> = 0.693 × (1,000 + 10,000) × 0.00001 = **76.23 ms**
2. Low time t<sub>L</sub> = 0.693 × 10,000 × 0.00001 = **69.30 ms**
3. Period = 76.23 + 69.30 = 145.53 ms, so f = 1 ÷ 0.14553 s = **6.87 Hz**
4. Duty cycle = 76.23 ÷ 145.53 = **52.4%**

The 0.693 is ln 2: the capacitor charges and discharges between ⅓ and ⅔ of V<sub>cc</sub>, and each half step takes one RC time multiplied by ln 2. That is also why the supply voltage cancels out of the formula.

## Why the first pulse is longer

At power up the capacitor starts at 0 V, not ⅓ V<sub>cc</sub>, so the first high period runs from 0 to ⅔ V<sub>cc</sub> and takes ln 3 × (R1 + R2) × C = **120.8 ms** instead of 76.2 ms. If a circuit counts pulses from switch on, ignore the first one.

## Effect of capacitor tolerance

Timing scales directly with C, and general purpose electrolytics are often ±20%:

| Capacitor tolerance | Actual C | Frequency | Period |
|---|---|---|---|
| −20% | 8 µF | 8.59 Hz | 116.4 ms |
| −10% | 9 µF | 7.63 Hz | 131.0 ms |
| Nominal | 10 µF | 6.87 Hz | 145.5 ms |
| +10% | 11 µF | 6.25 Hz | 160.1 ms |
| +20% | 12 µF | 5.73 Hz | 174.6 ms |

So the default circuit could run anywhere from 5.73 Hz to 8.59 Hz before resistor tolerance is even counted. For a repeatable clock, use a 5% film capacitor and 1% resistors.

## Designing for a target frequency

For 1 kHz with C = 10 nF and R1 = 1 kΩ, rearrange f = 1.44 ÷ ((R1 + 2R2) C): R2 = (1.44 ÷ (1,000 × 10 nF) − 1,000) ÷ 2 = 71.5 kΩ. The nearest E12 value, 68 kΩ, gives 1053 Hz at 50.4% duty. Measure the result on pin 3 with an oscilloscope or a multimeter frequency range, and trim with a potentiometer in series with R2 if the exact value matters.

## Common mistakes

- **Making R1 very small.** When the discharge transistor turns on, pin 7 shorts R1 to ground; with 100 Ω on a 9 V supply that is 90 mA through the transistor, well above what it is meant to sink.
- **Using huge resistors with an electrolytic.** Above a few hundred kΩ, capacitor leakage current becomes comparable to the charging current and the period stretches or the timer stops.
- **Leaving reset (pin 4) floating.** It can pick up noise and stop the oscillator at random; tie it to V<sub>cc</sub>.
- **No supply decoupling on the bipolar NE555.** Its output stage draws a current spike at every transition, so put 100 nF close to pins 8 and 1.
- **Expecting below 50% duty from the standard circuit.** t<sub>H</sub> always includes R1, so it is always longer than t<sub>L</sub>.
