## How to use this converter

Type a torque value in **any** unit and the others update instantly — useful for torque wrench settings, bolt specs and motor data.

## Formula

<p class="formula">1 N·m = 0.73756 lbf·ft = 8.8507 lbf·in   1 kgf·m = 9.80665 N·m</p>

**Example:** a wheel nut spec of 110 N·m = **81.1 lbf·ft**. 25 lbf·in for a small fastener = **2.82 N·m**.

## lbf·ft vs "ft-lbs"

Torque is often written "ft-lbs" on wrenches and manuals; strictly, foot-pounds is a unit of energy, but in torque specs the two mean the same thing.

## Typical tightening torques

| Application | Typical torque |
|---|---|
| Car wheel nuts | 80–140 N·m (59–103 lbf·ft) |
| Spark plugs | 20–30 N·m |
| Bicycle stem bolts | 4–6 N·m |
| Engine head bolts | Varies — follow the manual |

Always use the value from the manufacturer's manual and a calibrated torque wrench.

## Torque and power

Power = torque × angular speed. In practical units:

<p class="formula">P (kW) = T (N·m) × rpm ÷ 9,549</p>

An engine producing 300 N·m at 4,000 rpm delivers about 126 kW.

## Torque and motors

For motors, torque links power and speed: T (N·m) = 9549 × P (kW) ÷ rpm. Use the [torque, power & speed calculator](/engineering/torque-power-speed/).
