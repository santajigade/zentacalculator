  - q: What is the Rule of 72?
    a: A shortcut to estimate doubling time — divide 72 by the annual percentage return. At 8%, money doubles in about 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years.
  - q: How accurate is it?
    a: Very close for rates between about 6% and 10%. It slightly overestimates at low rates and underestimates at high ones.
  - q: Does it work for inflation too?
    a: Yes. At 6% inflation, prices double in about 12 years — so money halves in value in the same time.

## How to use this calculator

Enter an **annual return** to see the exact years needed to double your money and the Rule of 72 estimate. Or press × on the return and type how many years you want — the required rate is calculated.

## Formulas

<p class="formula">Exact: t = ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + r)<br/>Rule of 72: t ≈ 72 ÷ (r × 100)</p>

## Doubling again and again

At 8%, 10,000 becomes 20,000 in 9 years, 40,000 in 18 years and 80,000 in 27 years. Each extra 9 years doubles it again — the essence of compounding.
