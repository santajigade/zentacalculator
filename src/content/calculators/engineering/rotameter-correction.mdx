## How to use the rotameter correction calculator

Enter the **scale reading**, the **density of the calibration fluid** (998 kg/m³ for water at 20 °C), the **density of the liquid you are measuring** and the **float density**. You get the correction factor, the actual volume flow and the mass flow. Enter a desired actual flow and clear the scale reading to find what the scale should show.

## Rotameter density correction formula

<p class="formula">Q<sub>actual</sub> = Q<sub>scale</sub> × √[ (ρ<sub>f</sub> − ρ<sub>2</sub>) ρ<sub>1</sub> ÷ ((ρ<sub>f</sub> − ρ<sub>1</sub>) ρ<sub>2</sub>) ]</p>

## Example

A water-calibrated rotameter with a stainless-steel float (8000 kg/m³) reads 10 L/min on a liquid of 800 kg/m³:

- CF = √[(8000 − 800) × 998 ÷ ((8000 − 998) × 800)] = **1.1326**
- Actual flow = 10 × 1.1326 = **11.33 L/min**
- Mass flow = **544 kg/h**

| Seawater | 1025 kg/m³ | 0.985 |
| Glycol 50% | 1070 kg/m³ | 0.961 |
| Sulphuric acid 98% | 1840 kg/m³ | 0.691 |

Densities are typical values at about 20 °C; use your actual fluid density.

## Notes

- Correction assumes the same float and tube and a viscosity close to the calibration fluid.
- For large density differences it is better to order the meter scaled for the actual fluid.

## Setting a target flow on the scale

To deliver a required actual flow, divide by the correction factor. For 12 L/min of the 800 kg/m³ liquid through the same meter, set the float at 12 ÷ 1.1326 = **10.60 L/min** on the water scale. The float material matters: with an aluminium float (2,700 kg/m³) the same liquid gives a factor of **1.1801**, a larger correction, because a light float is more sensitive to the liquid's buoyancy.

Temperature counts too. If the liquid warms and its density falls to 790 kg/m³, the factor rises to 1.1405, so the same scale reading means 0.7% more flow.

## Reading a rotameter in practice

The meter must be truly vertical, with flow upward, and the reading taken at the edge the manufacturer marks, usually the widest part of a shaped float or the centre of a ball float. Read at eye level to avoid parallax on glass tubes. On metal tube meters the float position is shown by a magnetic follower and indicator, which can also give a 4 to 20 mA output; the same density correction applies to that signal.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing SG and kg/m³.** All three densities must be in the same unit; entering the float density in kg/m³ and the liquids as SG gives a nonsense factor.
- **Correcting a meter already scaled for the fluid.** Many meters are ordered with a scale for the actual liquid; correcting them again doubles the error.
- **Ignoring viscosity.** The density correction assumes viscosity close to the calibration fluid. Thick oils need the manufacturer's viscosity correction or a new scale.
- **Reading the wrong part of the float.** Using the top of a float that should be read at its widest edge shifts every reading by the float height.
- **Using it on pulsating flow.** A bouncing float from a reciprocating pump gives an unreliable average; add a damper or use another meter type.
