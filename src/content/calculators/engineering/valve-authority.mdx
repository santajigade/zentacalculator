## How to use the valve authority calculator

Enter the **pressure drop across the valve** when fully open at design flow and the **pressure drop of the rest of the circuit** (coil, branch pipe and fittings) at the same flow. The calculator returns the authority N and rates it.

## Valve authority formula

<p class="formula">N = Δp<sub>valve</sub> ÷ (Δp<sub>valve</sub> + Δp<sub>circuit</sub>)</p>

## Worked example

A valve dropping 0.5 bar in a branch whose coil and pipework drop 1.0 bar:

- N = 0.5 ÷ (0.5 + 1.0) = **0.333** — acceptable, but a smaller valve (higher Δp) would control better.

To lift the authority of this branch to 0.5, the valve itself must drop 1 bar at design flow, so select a valve with a smaller Kv. With N = 0.333, an equal-percentage valve at half stroke already passes about 24% of design flow.

## Authority for different pressure drops

| Valve Δp | Circuit 0.5 bar | Circuit 1 bar | Circuit 2 bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 bar | 0.17 | 0.09 | 0.05 |
| 0.25 bar | 0.33 | 0.20 | 0.11 |
| 0.5 bar | 0.50 | 0.33 | 0.20 |
| 1 bar | 0.67 | 0.50 | 0.33 |
| 1.5 bar | 0.75 | 0.60 | 0.43 |
| 2 bar | 0.80 | 0.67 | 0.50 |
| 3 bar | 0.86 | 0.75 | 0.60 |

## Installed flow of an equal-percentage valve

Flow as a share of design flow at each stroke position, for an equal-percentage valve with an inherent rangeability of 50:1, computed from Q/Q<sub>max</sub> = f ÷ √(N + (1 − N) f²) with f = 50<sup>(x − 1)</sup>:

| Stroke | N = 1 | N = 0.5 | N = 0.33 | N = 0.25 | N = 0.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | 3% | 4% | 5% | 6% | 9% |
| 25% | 5% | 8% | 9% | 11% | 17% |
| 50% | 14% | 20% | 24% | 27% | 41% |
| 75% | 38% | 50% | 58% | 63% | 79% |
| 90% | 68% | 79% | 85% | 88% | 95% |

At N = 1 the curve is the inherent characteristic. As authority falls, the curve bows upward, so most of the flow change is squeezed into the first part of the stroke and the loop gain at low load becomes very high.

## How to get the pressure drops

- **Valve Δp:** from the valve sizing at design flow, Δp = (Q ÷ Kv)² in bar with Q in m³/h for water. Pick the next smaller standard Kv and recalculate.
- **Circuit Δp:** add the coil or heat exchanger drop from its datasheet, the branch pipe friction and the fittings at the same design flow. Only include the part of the circuit whose flow changes when this valve moves.
- **On a running plant:** read pressure gauges or test points across the valve and across the branch at full flow with the valve wide open.

## Common mistakes

- **Including the whole pump head.** Pressure drops held constant by a differential pressure controller or in the common header do not belong in the denominator.
- **Using the valve's Δp at part load.** Authority is defined with the valve fully open at design flow.
- **Oversizing to save pump head.** A valve sized for a very low Δp gives N well below 0.25, and the loop will cycle at light load.
- **Treating authority as fixed.** When other branches close, the available pressure across the branch rises and the effective authority changes; pressure-independent valves avoid this.
- **Ignoring the valve characteristic.** Low authority hurts an equal-percentage valve less than a linear one in coil circuits, so check both together.

## Notes

- Low authority distorts an equal-percentage characteristic toward quick-opening, so most flow change happens in the first part of the stroke.
- To raise authority choose a valve with a smaller Kv / Cv — check it with the [control valve Kv/Cv calculator](/engineering/control-valve-kv-cv/).
