## How to use the current divider calculator

Enter the **total current** entering the parallel pair and the two **resistances**. You get each branch current, the equivalent resistance and the voltage across the pair.

## Current divider formulas

<p class="formula">I₁ = I<sub>T</sub> × R₂ ÷ (R₁ + R₂) &nbsp;·&nbsp; I₂ = I<sub>T</sub> × R₁ ÷ (R₁ + R₂)</p>

## Worked example

A total current of 1 A enters 100 Ω and 300 Ω in parallel:

- I₁ = 1 × 300 ÷ (100 + 300) = **0.75 A**
- I₂ = 1 × 100 ÷ (100 + 300) = **0.25 A** (check: 0.75 + 0.25 = 1 A)
- R<sub>eq</sub> = 100 × 300 ÷ 400 = **75 Ω**, so the voltage across the pair is 1 A × 75 Ω = **75 V**

## Current split by resistance ratio

| R₂ ÷ R₁ | Share through R₁ | Share through R₂ | R<sub>eq</sub> |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50.00% | 50.00% | 0.5 × R₁ |
| 2 | 66.67% | 33.33% | 0.6667 × R₁ |
| 3 | 75.00% | 25.00% | 0.75 × R₁ |
| 4 | 80.00% | 20.00% | 0.8 × R₁ |
| 9 | 90.00% | 10.00% | 0.9 × R₁ |
| 10 | 90.91% | 9.09% | 0.9091 × R₁ |
| 99 | 99.00% | 1.00% | 0.99 × R₁ |
| 999 | 99.90% | 0.10% | 0.999 × R₁ |

The branch with the **smaller** resistance always takes the larger share; with a 1 : 999 ratio it carries 99.9% of the current, which is how an ammeter shunt works.

## Three or more branches

Use conductances G = 1/R: I<sub>n</sub> = I<sub>T</sub> × G<sub>n</sub> ÷ ΣG. For 1 A into 100 Ω, 200 Ω and 400 Ω: I through 100 Ω = 0.5714 A · I through 200 Ω = 0.2857 A · I through 400 Ω = 0.1429 A.

## Ammeter shunts

To extend a meter that reads 1 mA full scale with 100 Ω internal resistance to read 1 A, the shunt must carry 999 mA: R<sub>shunt</sub> = 100 × 0.001 ÷ 0.999 ≈ **0.1001 Ω**.

## 4–20 mA and 250 Ω

A 250 Ω resistor converts a 4–20 mA loop current into 1–5 V for a voltage input — a common trick that relies on Ohm's law rather than division. Keep loop resistance within the transmitter's limits — see the [loop resistance calculator](/engineering/loop-resistance/).

## Practical notes

- Include wire and connector resistance when one branch is a low-value shunt — 10 mΩ of wiring is 10% of a 0.1 Ω shunt.
- For accurate current sharing between parallel power devices, add small equal ballast resistors; tiny differences in on-resistance otherwise make one device carry most of the current.
- The rule assumes an ideal current source. If the source has internal resistance, the total current changes with the load — solve with the source voltage and [Ohm's law](/physics/ohms-law/) instead.
