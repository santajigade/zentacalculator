## How to use the strain gauge calculator

Enter the **gauge resistance**, **gauge factor**, **strain** in microstrain, **bridge excitation** and choose the **bridge configuration**. You get the resistance change per gauge, the bridge output and its sensitivity in mV/V.

## Strain gauge formulas

<p class="formula">ΔR = R × GF × ε &nbsp;·&nbsp; V<sub>out</sub> ≈ V<sub>ex</sub> × N × GF × ε ÷ 4</p>

where N is the number of active arms (1, 2 or 4). The quarter-bridge result is slightly non-linear at large strains — see the [Wheatstone bridge calculator](/electronics/wheatstone-bridge/) for the exact value.

## From strain to stress

For steel (E ≈ 200 GPa), stress = E × ε: 1,000 µε corresponds to about 200 MPa.

## Load cells

Load cells are rated in **mV/V** at full load — commonly 2 mV/V. With 10 V excitation, a 2 mV/V cell gives 20 mV at full capacity. Amplify it with an [instrumentation amplifier](/electronics/instrumentation-amplifier-gain/).

## Worked example

The defaults are a 350 Ω foil gauge, GF = 2, 1,000 µε, 10 V excitation, quarter bridge:

- ΔR = 350 × 2 × 0.001 = **0.70 Ω** (700 mΩ)
- V<sub>out</sub> ≈ 10 × 1 × 2 × 0.001 ÷ 4 = **5.0 mV**
- Sensitivity = 5.0 ÷ 10 = **0.50 mV/V**

The exact quarter bridge output is 10 × (GFε/4) ÷ (1 + GFε/2) = 4.995 mV, so the linear formula reads 0.10% high at this strain. For a steel part (E = 200 GPa) that strain means about 200 MPa; in 6061 aluminium (E = 68.9 GPa) it is about 68.9 MPa.

## Lead wires and temperature

In a two wire quarter bridge both leads sit in the gauge arm. With 0.5 Ω per lead the arm becomes 351 Ω, which desensitises the reading by 0.9972, so a true 1,000 µε reads about 997 µε. Worse, if those copper leads warm by 10 K their resistance rises 39.3 mΩ, which the bridge reports as **56 µε** of strain that is not there. A three wire connection puts one lead in each adjacent arm so the changes cancel, and the desensitisation drops to 0.9986.

## Shunt calibration

To check a channel without loading the part, a precision resistor is switched across one bridge arm to simulate a known strain. The resistor for ε is R<sub>s</sub> = R ÷ (GF × ε) − R. For 1,000 µε on a 350 Ω gauge that is **174,650 Ω**; the indicator should then show 1,000 µε (as compression when shunting the active arm).

## Common mistakes

- **Entering strain in m/m instead of µε.** 0.001 typed into a µε field means 0.001 µε, a millionth of the intended value.
- **Using the half bridge factor for a Poisson arrangement.** A gauge across the grain only adds about 0.3 of the main gauge's signal, so the output is about 1.3 times a quarter bridge, not 2 times.
- **Mismatched gauge and material.** A gauge self compensated for steel (11 ppm/K) bonded to aluminium (23 ppm/K) shows apparent strain with every temperature change.
- **Too much excitation on a small gauge.** Self heating in a 120 Ω gauge on plastic at 10 V causes drift; 2 to 5 V is safer on poor heat sinks.
- **Ignoring zero offset.** Bonding leaves a small initial unbalance; take a zero reading before loading and subtract it.
