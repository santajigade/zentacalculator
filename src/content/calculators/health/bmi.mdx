## How to use the BMI calculator

Enter your **weight and height** in any units — kg or lb, cm or ft — and your BMI and category appear instantly. To find a target weight, type your height and a **BMI value** instead; the weight is worked out for you.

## BMI formula

<p class="formula">BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²</p>

Example: 70 kg and 1.72 m → 70 ÷ 1.72² = 70 ÷ 2.958 = **23.7**.

## BMI categories

| BMI | WHO category | Asian / Indian guideline |
|---|---|---|
| Below 18.5 | Underweight | Underweight |
| 18.5 – 22.9 | Normal | Normal |
| 23 – 24.9 | Normal | Overweight |
| 25 – 29.9 | Overweight | Obese (grade 1) |
| 30 and above | Obese | Obese (grade 2) |

The coloured bar under the BMI field uses the Asian cut-off between 23 and 25 so that both systems are visible.

## BMI cut-offs for Asian populations

Studies show that people of South Asian, Chinese and other Asian backgrounds tend to have more body fat and higher diabetes risk at the same BMI. A WHO expert consultation suggested lower action points, and several countries use them:

| Category | Standard (WHO) | Common Asian cut-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Healthy | 18.5–24.9 | 18.5–22.9 |
| Overweight | 25–29.9 | 23–24.9 (or 23–27.4) |
| Obese | 30 or more | 25 or more (or 27.5+) |

If you are of Asian heritage, a BMI of 23 or above is worth discussing with a doctor.

## BMI for children and teenagers

For people under 18, BMI is compared with growth charts for the same age and sex, giving a **percentile**. The adult categories above do not apply. Use your country's paediatric BMI charts or ask a health professional.

## BMI Prime

BMI Prime is your BMI divided by 25, the upper limit of the healthy range. A BMI Prime of 0.74–1.00 is in the healthy range; 1.10 means 10% above the upper limit.

## Better together: BMI plus waist size

Waist size shows where fat is stored, which BMI cannot. Check your [waist-to-height ratio](/health/waist-to-height/) as well — keeping your waist below half your height is a simple target.

## Limitations

BMI is a quick screening number, not a diagnosis. It does not consider muscle mass, bone density, age, sex or where fat is stored. Pregnant women and children need different charts. Talk to a doctor for a full assessment.

## How much weight is one BMI point?

Because BMI = weight ÷ height², one BMI point always equals **height² in kilograms**. This makes it easy to turn a BMI goal into a weight goal.

## Imperial worked example

A person who is 5 ft 9 in (69 in) tall and weighs 170 lb:

- BMI = 703 × 170 ÷ 69² = 119,510 ÷ 4,761 = **25.1**
- That is just above the 25.0 line, so a loss of about 1 lb would bring it into the normal range.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing units**, such as pounds with metres. Use kg with metres, or lb with inches and the factor 703.
- **Rounding height.** At 5 ft 9 in, entering 5 ft 8 in raises BMI by about 0.75, enough to change the category near a cut-off.
- **Using adult cut-offs for children.** Under 18, BMI must be compared with age and sex percentiles.
- **Relying on one weigh-in.** Body weight swings by 1 to 2 kg over a day. Weigh in the morning on a few days and use the average.

## Worked example at the default values

The calculator opens with 70 kg and 172 cm:

- Height in metres squared: 1.72 × 1.72 = **2.9584 m²**
- BMI = 70 ÷ 2.9584 = **23.7 kg/m²**
- BMI Prime = 23.66 ÷ 25 = **0.95**

Under the WHO table this is normal weight (18.5 to 24.9). Under the Asian cut-offs it falls in the 23 to 24.9 overweight band. At 172 cm the WHO healthy range is **54.7 kg to 73.7 kg**, and reaching the top of the Asian normal range (22.9) would mean a weight of 67.7 kg, about 2.3 kg less.

## Measuring weight and height properly

A BMI is only as good as the two numbers behind it. Measure height barefoot, standing straight against a wall with heels together, and mark the top of the head with a flat object held level. Weigh in light clothing on a flat, hard floor, ideally in the morning after using the toilet and before eating.

The sensitivity is larger than most people expect. At the default values, a height entered 1 cm too short gives a BMI of 23.9 instead of 23.7, and weighing 1.5 kg heavier after a meal and clothes gives 24.2. Near a category boundary either error can flip the result, so use measured rather than remembered figures.

## What BMI is used for in practice

Clinics record BMI at routine visits because it is quick and needs only a scale and a stadiometer. It is used to flag people who may need a check for blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol, to set eligibility for some treatments such as bariatric surgery, and to adjust doses or anaesthesia plans. Public health surveys use it to track population trends. For an individual, a change in BMI over months is often more informative than a single reading.
