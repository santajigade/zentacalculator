## How to use the pH calculator

Enter **any one** of pH, [H⁺], pOH or [OH⁻] — the rest are calculated (at 25 °C). For a strong acid like HCl, [H⁺] equals the acid concentration.

## PH formulas

<p class="formula">pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]   pOH = 14 − pH   [OH⁻] = 10<sup>−pOH</sup></p>

## Worked example

pH 4.5:

- [H⁺] = 10⁻⁴·⁵ = **3.16e-05 mol/L**
- pOH = **9.5**, [OH⁻] = **3.16e-10 mol/L**

## pH and [H⁺] table

| pH | [H⁺] (mol/L) | Compared with pH 7 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 × 10<sup>−1</sup> | 1e+06× more H⁺ |
| 3 | 1 × 10<sup>−3</sup> | 10000× more H⁺ |
| 5 | 1 × 10<sup>−5</sup> | 100× more H⁺ |
| 7 | 1 × 10<sup>−7</sup> | — |
| 9 | 1 × 10<sup>−9</sup> | 100× less H⁺ |
| 11 | 1 × 10<sup>−11</sup> | 10000× less H⁺ |
| 13 | 1 × 10<sup>−13</sup> | 1e+06× less H⁺ |

Each pH unit is a **tenfold** change in hydrogen ion concentration.

## pH of strong acids and bases

- Strong acid: pH = −log₁₀(concentration). 0.01 M HCl has pH 2.
- Strong base: pOH = −log₁₀([OH⁻]), then pH = 14 − pOH. 0.01 M NaOH has pH 12.

## Weak acids

For a weak acid of concentration C with dissociation constant Ka:

<p class="formula">[H⁺] ≈ √(Ka × C)</p>

0.1 M acetic acid (Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) has [H⁺] ≈ 1.34 × 10⁻³ M, so pH ≈ **2.87**.

## pH and temperature

The value 14 for pH + pOH applies at 25 °C. At higher temperatures, water ionises more, so neutral pH falls — about 6.14 at 100 °C. A neutral solution is one where [H⁺] = [OH⁻], not necessarily pH 7.

## Measuring pH

| Method | Accuracy |
|---|---|
| Universal indicator paper | ±0.5–1 pH |
| Indicator solutions | ±0.2–0.5 pH |
| Calibrated pH meter | ±0.01–0.05 pH |

Calibrate pH meters with at least two buffers (often pH 4 and 7, or 7 and 10) bracketing your sample.

## pH of everyday substances

| Substance | pH |
|---|---|
| Battery acid | ~0–1 |
| Lemon juice | ~2 |
| Coffee | ~5 |
| Pure water | 7 |
| Sea water | ~8 |
| Baking soda solution | ~9 |
| Household bleach | ~12–13 |

Each pH unit is a **tenfold** change: pH 3 is ten times more acidic than pH 4.
