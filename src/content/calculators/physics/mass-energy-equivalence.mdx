## How to use the E = mc² calculator

Enter a **mass** in grams, kilograms or another unit. The calculator returns the equivalent rest energy in kWh (switch to J, MJ or BTU), in terajoules and in kilotonnes of TNT. To find the mass equivalent of an energy, enter the energy and clear the mass field.

## Mass–energy equivalence formula

<p class="formula">E = m c² &nbsp;·&nbsp; c = 299,792,458 m/s</p>

## Example

Converting 1 gram of mass entirely into energy:

- E = 0.001 × 299,792,458² = **8.9876 × 10¹³ J**
- = **24,965,422 kWh** (24.97 GWh)
- = **21.48 kilotonnes of TNT**

## Where mass really turns into energy

No practical process converts a whole gram. Real reactions convert only the mass defect, the difference between the masses before and after:

- **Deuteron binding.** A proton (1.007276467 u) and a neutron (1.008664916 u) together outweigh a deuteron (2.013553212 u) by 0.002388171 u. Multiplying by 931.494 MeV/u gives **2.225 MeV**, the energy of the gamma ray released when the two fuse.
- **Fusion of deuterium and tritium.** D + T → ⁴He + n loses 0.018883 u, releasing **17.6 MeV**, about 0.38% of the fuel mass.
- **Fission of U-235.** About 200 MeV per split nucleus is 0.091% of its mass.
- **The Sun.** Radiating 3.828 × 10²⁶ W, it loses L ÷ c² = **4.26 × 10⁹ kg** of mass every second.

A 1 GW electric reactor at 33% efficiency releases 9.556 × 10¹⁶ J of heat in a year, which corresponds to only **1.06 kg** of mass, spread across tonnes of fuel. Per kilogram, fission releases about 1.8 million times more energy than burning petrol.

## Converting between units

Nuclear physics works in MeV and atomic mass units rather than joules. 1 MeV = 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹³ J, and 1 u × c² = 931.494 MeV. To use the calculator with particle data, convert the mass in u to kilograms (1 u = 1.66054 × 10⁻²⁷ kg), or convert the result in joules back to MeV by dividing by 1.6022 × 10⁻¹³.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving mass in grams when working by hand.** E = mc² needs kilograms for joules; 1 g written as 1 gives a result 1,000 times too large. The calculator converts units for you.
- **Confusing rest energy with kinetic energy.** mc² is the energy of a body at rest. Motion adds (γ − 1) mc², which at everyday speeds is just ½mv².
- **Assuming fission converts all the fuel.** Only about 0.09% of a U-235 nucleus becomes energy; the rest remains as fission products.
- **Rounding c to 3 × 10⁸ m/s for precise work.** It overstates c² by 0.14%, enough to spoil MeV level comparisons.
- **Mixing tonnes and kilotonnes of TNT.** 1 g of mass is 21.48 kilotonnes, which is 21,481 tonnes.
