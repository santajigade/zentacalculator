## How to use the DP level range calculator

Enter the **specific gravity** of the process liquid, the **measuring span** (distance between minimum and maximum level), and the height of the **minimum level above the HP tap**. Choose **open/dry leg** or **wet leg**; for a wet leg, enter the fill fluid's SG and the leg height. The calculator gives LRV, URV and span in mm of water column (1 mmH₂O ≈ 0.0981 mbar).

## DP level range formulas

<p class="formula">LRV = SG<sub>p</sub> × h₀ − SG<sub>s</sub> × H<sub>s</sub></p>

<p class="formula">URV = SG<sub>p</sub> × (h₀ + H) − SG<sub>s</sub> × H<sub>s</sub></p>

The wet-leg term is zero for open tanks and dry legs.

## Example: wet-leg tank

Process SG 1.0, span 3,000 mm, minimum level 500 mm above the HP tap, wet leg filled with water (SG 1.0) 4,000 mm high:

- LRV = 500 − 4,000 = **−3,500 mmH₂O**
- URV = 3,500 − 4,000 = **−500 mmH₂O**
- Span = **3,000 mmH₂O**

## Choosing the reference leg

| Tank | LP side | Range type |
|---|---|---|
| Open to atmosphere | Vented | Suppressed or zero-based |
| Closed, non-condensing gas | Dry leg | Suppressed or zero-based |
| Closed, condensing vapour (steam drums) | Wet leg | Elevated (negative LRV) |
| Closed, either | Remote diaphragm seals (capillary) | Elevated, fill fluid SG |

## Tips

- Wet legs must stay full — a partly drained leg causes large level errors.
- With diaphragm seals, use the capillary fill fluid SG and the vertical distance between seals.
- Density changes shift the reading; for varying density see [DP level measurement with varying density](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/dp-level-measurement-varying-density/).

## Worked example with the default values

The defaults are an **open tank** with water (SG 1), a measuring span of 3,000 mm and the minimum level 500 mm above the HP tap:

- LRV = 1 × 500 = **500 mmH₂O** (4.90 kPa), a suppressed zero
- URV = 1 × (500 + 3,000) = **3,500 mmH₂O** (34.32 kPa)
- Span = **3,000 mmH₂O**

With the level 2,000 mm above the minimum, the transmitter sees 2,500 mmH₂O and outputs 4 + 16 × (2,500 − 500) ÷ 3,000 = **14.67 mA**, which is 66.7% of range.

## Ranging the transmitter in practice

On the bench, connect a calibrator to the HP port with the LP port vented, apply the LRV pressure and set 4 mA, then apply the URV and set 20 mA. With a HART communicator the same values are simply typed in, but a check at both ends against a reference is still good practice. After installation, a wet leg must be filled completely and vented before the zero is checked, and on steam drums the condensate pot must be allowed to fill before commissioning.

## Effect of a density change

The range is only correct at the design SG. If this transmitter is ranged for water but the liquid is actually SG 0.95, a true level 2,000 mm above the minimum gives 2,375 mmH₂O, which the transmitter reads as **1,875 mm**, an error of 125 mm. The suppressed zero makes it worse, because the 500 mm below the minimum level is also lighter than assumed.

## Common mistakes

- **Measuring h₀ from the tank bottom.** It must be from the HP tap centreline (or diaphragm centre) to the minimum level.
- **Getting the wet-leg sign wrong.** The filled reference leg pushes on the LP side, so it is subtracted and the LRV goes negative.
- **Using the process SG for capillary seals.** With remote seals the elevation uses the fill fluid SG times the vertical distance between the seals.
- **Mixing pressure units.** Calibrators may read mmH₂O at 4 °C or 20 °C, inH₂O or mbar; 1 mmH₂O is 0.0981 mbar at the conventional reference.
- **Forgetting that the wet leg changes with temperature.** A hot or partly evaporated leg shifts the zero, so check it after start-up.
