## How to use the water heater heating time calculator

Enter the **tank volume**, **cold** and **target temperatures**, the heater's **power** and **efficiency**. Energy used and heating time are calculated.

## Water heater heating time formula

<p class="formula">time = m c ΔT ÷ (P × efficiency)</p>

## Example

25 L from 20 °C to 55 °C with a 2 kW heater at 90%:

- Energy = 25 × 4.186 × 35 ÷ 3,600 ÷ 0.9 = **1.13 kWh**
- Time = **34 minutes**

## Heating times for common tanks

From 15 °C to 55 °C at 90% efficiency:

| Tank | 1.5 kW | 2 kW | 3 kW |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 L | 21 min | 16 min | 10 min |
| 15 L | 31 min | 23 min | 16 min |
| 25 L | 52 min | 39 min | 26 min |
| 50 L | 103 min | 78 min | 52 min |
| 100 L | 207 min | 155 min | 103 min |
| 150 L | 310 min | 233 min | 155 min |

## Running cost

Energy used × electricity price. Heating 50 L by 40 °C uses about 2.6 kWh; at 0.15 per kWh that is about 0.39 per full heat-up.

## Standing losses

Stored hot water loses heat through the tank walls. Well-insulated tanks lose 1–2 kWh a day; older tanks can lose more. Lagging pipes and setting a sensible temperature reduce this.

## Temperature settings

- **Storage tanks**: keep at **60 °C** or above to control Legionella bacteria.
- **At the tap**: 50 °C or below reduces scald risk; thermostatic mixing valves help.
- Each 10 °C reduction in set temperature saves a noticeable amount of energy in standing losses.

## Heat pumps and solar

Heat-pump water heaters use about a third of the electricity of a resistance heater for the same hot water. Solar water heaters can cover most needs in sunny climates.

## Reading the default result

The defaults (a 25 L tank from 20 °C to 55 °C, a 2 kW element, 90% efficiency) give **1.13 kWh** and **33.9 minutes**. The water itself needs 25 × 4.186 × 35 ÷ 3,600 = 1.017 kWh, and the efficiency adds the heat lost from the tank and pipes during heating.

## Seasonal inlet temperature

Mains water is much colder in winter, which is why the same geyser seems slow in January. For the 25 L, 2 kW heater set to 55 °C, at a tariff of 8 per kWh:

| Cold water | Rise | Energy | Time | Cost per heat up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 °C | 45 °C | 1.45 kWh | 44 min | 11.63 |
| 15 °C | 40 °C | 1.29 kWh | 39 min | 10.34 |
| 20 °C | 35 °C | 1.13 kWh | 34 min | 9.04 |
| 25 °C | 30 °C | 0.97 kWh | 29 min | 7.75 |
| 30 °C | 25 °C | 0.81 kWh | 24 min | 6.46 |

## Is the tank big enough for a shower?

Hot water from the tank is mixed with cold at the tap. A shower at 8 L/min for 8 minutes at 40 °C, with 55 °C stored water and 15 °C cold water, needs a hot share of (40 − 15) ÷ (55 − 15) = 62.5%, so it draws **40 L of hot water**. A 25 L tank runs short, and in practice only about 70 to 80% of a tank comes out hot before mixing with incoming cold water cools it. Either set the thermostat higher (with a mixing valve) or choose a larger tank.

## Low supply voltage

An element is a fixed resistance, so its power falls with the square of voltage. A 2 kW element rated at 230 V has R = 230² ÷ 2000 = 26.45 Ω. At 210 V it delivers only 1.67 kW, and the default heat up takes **40.7 minutes** instead of 33.9. The energy used barely changes; only the waiting time grows. To check the real power, switch off everything else and time the energy meter pulses with the [energy meter constant calculator](/electrical/energy-meter-constant/).

## Common mistakes

- **Using the summer inlet temperature all year.** A 10 °C winter supply needs 29% more energy than the default 20 °C.
- **Typing the element rating in watts.** 2000 entered as kW gives a time a thousand times too short.
- **Treating the whole tank as usable hot water.** Mixing with incoming cold water means only part of the volume comes out at full temperature.
- **Ignoring scale on the element.** In hard water areas a crusted element heats slowly and can burn out; descale or fit a softener.
- **Leaving the geyser on all day.** Standing losses continue whether or not water is used; a timer cuts them.
