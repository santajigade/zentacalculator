## How to use the generator size calculator

Enter the **total running load** in kW, its **power factor**, a **margin** for growth, the **largest motor** that starts while the rest of the load is running, and its **starting kVA per kW** for the starter type. The calculator gives the kVA needed for running and for the motor start; choose a standard rating at or above the larger figure.

## Generator size formula

<p class="formula">kVA<sub>run</sub> = kW ÷ PF × (1 + margin)</p>

<p class="formula">kVA<sub>start</sub> = [(kW − kW<sub>motor</sub>) ÷ PF + kW<sub>motor</sub> × k<sub>s</sub>] ÷ 1.5</p>

The 1.5 factor assumes the generator can carry 150% of its rating momentarily during motor starting while keeping voltage dip acceptable. Check the manufacturer's motor-starting curves for an exact selection.

## Example

80 kW running load at 0.8 PF with 20% margin; the last motor is 22 kW on a star-delta starter:

- Running: 80 ÷ 0.8 × 1.2 = **120 kVA**
- Starting: (58 ÷ 0.8 + 22 × 2.5) ÷ 1.5 = **85 kVA**
- Choose **125 kVA**

## Standard DG set ratings (India)

15, 20, 25, 30, 40, 62.5, 82.5, 100, 125, 160, 200, 250, 320, 380, 500, 625, 750, 1,010, 1,250, 1,500 and 2,000 kVA.

## Other checks

- **Non-linear loads** (VFDs, UPSs) may need generator derating or a larger alternator.
- **Altitude and temperature** reduce output — derate above 1,000 m or 40 °C.
- **Step loading**: start the largest motors first, when the generator is lightly loaded.

## Worked example with the default values

- Running check: 80 ÷ 0.8 = 100 kVA, plus 20% margin = **120 kVA**
- Starting check: the other 58 kW need 58 ÷ 0.8 = 72.5 kVA, the star-delta motor needs 22 × 2.5 = 55 kVA, total 127.5 kVA ÷ 1.5 = **85 kVA**
- The larger figure is 120 kVA, so the next standard set is **125 kVA**

At 125 kVA (a 100 kW set) the 80 kW load is 80% of the kW rating today, a healthy level for a diesel engine, with the margin left for growth.

## How the starter changes the answer

Same 80 kW load and 22 kW motor:

| Starter | kVA per kW | Starting check | Required | Standard set |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DOL | 6.5 | 143.7 kVA | 143.7 kVA | 160 kVA |
| Autotransformer | 3.5 | 99.7 kVA | 120.0 kVA | 125 kVA |
| Star-delta | 2.5 | 85.0 kVA | 120.0 kVA | 125 kVA |
| Soft starter | 2 | 77.7 kVA | 120.0 kVA | 125 kVA |
| VFD | 1.35 | 68.1 kVA | 120.0 kVA | 125 kVA |

With a DOL starter the motor start, not the running load, decides the size and pushes the set up to 160 kVA. Fitting a star-delta starter, soft starter or VFD on the largest motor is often cheaper than buying the bigger generator.

## Prime, standby and continuous ratings

ISO 8528-1 defines several ratings for the same engine. A **standby** rating covers emergency use for limited hours a year with no overload; a **prime** rating allows unlimited hours at a varying load with a 10% overload for one hour in twelve, and is typically about 10% lower than standby; a **continuous** rating is for constant base load. For a factory that runs on the DG during regular grid outages, size on the prime rating.

## Load testing after installation

A new set is commissioned on a resistive load bank at 25, 50, 75 and 100% for set periods while voltage, frequency, oil pressure and coolant temperature are logged, then the actual largest motor is started with the rest of the load connected while a recorder captures the voltage dip and frequency recovery.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding motor kW to kVA directly.** Convert the running load to kVA with its power factor before adding the starting kVA.
- **Sizing only on the running load.** With a DOL motor the starting check here needs 144 kVA, more than the 120 kVA running figure.
- **Starting the biggest motor last.** Started first on an unloaded set it needs only 36.7 kVA of equivalent capacity, so sequence the large motors first in the AMF logic.
- **Using standby rating for daily running.** A set run for hours every day on its standby rating wears out early and voids warranty.
- **Ignoring UPS and VFD harmonics.** Non-linear loads heat the alternator; many suppliers ask for an oversized alternator on the same engine.
