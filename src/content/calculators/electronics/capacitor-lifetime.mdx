## How to use the electrolytic capacitor life calculator

Enter the capacitor's **rated life** and **rated temperature** from the datasheet (for example 2,000 h at 105 °C), and the **actual core temperature** in your circuit. You get the expected life in hours and years of continuous operation.

For more background, read [Capacitor ESR Explained](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/capacitor-esr-equivalent-series-resistance/).

## Electrolytic capacitor life formula

<p class="formula">L = L₀ × 2<sup>(T₀ − T) ÷ 10</sup></p>

## Life at different temperatures

| Core temperature | 1,000 h @ 85 °C | 2,000 h @ 105 °C | 5,000 h @ 105 °C | 10,000 h @ 105 °C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 85 °C | 1,000 h | 8,000 h | 20,000 h | 40,000 h |
| 75 °C | 2,000 h | 16,000 h | 40,000 h | 80,000 h |
| 65 °C | 4,000 h | 32,000 h | 80,000 h | 160,000 h |
| 55 °C | 8,000 h | 64,000 h | 160,000 h | 320,000 h |
| 45 °C | 16,000 h | 128,000 h | 320,000 h | 640,000 h |
| 40 °C | 22,627 h | 181,019 h | 452,548 h | 905,097 h |

One year of continuous use is 8,760 hours. Manufacturers often cap estimates at 15 years because of seal ageing.

## Extending capacitor life

- Keep capacitors away from heat sinks, resistors and transformers.
- Choose 105 °C, long-life series for power supplies and drives.
- Stay within the ripple current rating — ripple heats the core.
- Improve enclosure ventilation; every 10 °C counts double.

## Why it matters

Electrolytic capacitors are the most common wear-out part in power supplies, VFDs and UPSs. Rising ESR and falling capacitance are early warning signs, which is why drive makers recommend replacing DC-bus capacitors on a schedule.

## Worked example with the default values

A 2,000 h, 105 °C capacitor running with its core at 55 °C:

1. Temperature margin = 105 − 55 = 50 °C, which is 5 steps of 10 °C
2. Life factor = 2<sup>5</sup> = **32**
3. L = 2,000 × 32 = **64,000 h**, or 64,000 ÷ 8,760 = **7.3 years** of continuous running

## Where the core temperature comes from

The core is the ambient around the can plus the heat the ripple current makes in the ESR. Suppose the capacitor carries 1.5 A RMS ripple with an ESR of 100 mΩ, and the datasheet gives a thermal resistance of 30 °C/W for that can size. It dissipates 1.5² × 0.1 = 0.225 W, a rise of 0.225 × 30 = **6.75 °C**. A core of 55 °C therefore means an ambient of about 48 °C. If the enclosure warms to give a 65 °C core, life halves to 32,000 h (3.7 years).

## Calendar life by running hours

| Operation | Core temperature | Life in hours | Calendar life |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 h/day | 55 °C | 64,000 h | 7.3 years |
| 24 h/day | 65 °C | 32,000 h | 3.7 years |
| 16 h/day | 55 °C | 64,000 h | 11.0 years |
| 16 h/day | 65 °C | 32,000 h | 5.5 years |
| 8 h/day | 55 °C | 64,000 h | 21.9 years |
| 8 h/day | 65 °C | 32,000 h | 11.0 years |

Figures above about 15 years are not reliable, because the rubber seal and the oxide layer age even at low temperature.

## Checking capacitors in service

Measure the can top with a thermocouple or thermal camera at full load; the core is normally a few degrees hotter than the case top. During maintenance, an ESR meter or LCR meter shows wear: datasheets usually define end of life as capacitance falling more than 20%, tan δ rising above twice the initial limit, or leakage exceeding the specification. A bulging vent or electrolyte residue means the part is already past end of life.

## Common mistakes

- **Using room temperature instead of core temperature.** A cabinet at 25 °C can still have capacitors at 60 °C next to a heat sink.
- **Applying the 10 degree rule to polymer capacitors.** Conductive polymer types usually follow a 20 degree rule; for the same rating at 55 °C they would give 632,456 h, so use the maker's formula.
- **Counting calendar time when the equipment is mostly off.** Life is consumed only at temperature, so a machine running 8 h a day lasts three times as many years.
- **Extrapolating far below 40 °C.** The doubling rule overstates life at low temperature, where seal ageing dominates.
- **Mixing up load life and shelf life.** Rated hours apply with voltage and ripple applied; capacitors stored unpowered for years may need reforming before use.
