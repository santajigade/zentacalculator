## How to use the torque, power & speed calculator

Enter any **two** of power, speed and torque to get the third. Switch units freely — kW or hp, N·m or lbf·ft.

## Torque, power & speed formula

<p class="formula">P = 2π n T ÷ 60   →   T = 9549 × P(kW) ÷ n(rpm)</p>

## Worked example

An 11 kW, 4-pole motor running at 1,460 rpm:

T = 9549 × 11 ÷ 1460 = **71.9 N·m**

Through a 10:1 gearbox (95% efficient) the output is about 146 rpm and 683 N·m.

## Gearboxes

A gearbox trades speed for torque. With a ratio i and efficiency η:

- Output speed = input speed ÷ i
- Output torque = input torque × i × η

An 11 kW motor (72 N·m at 1,460 rpm) through a 20:1 gearbox at 95% efficiency gives about **1,368 N·m** at 73 rpm.

## Imperial form

<p class="formula">HP = Torque (lbf·ft) × rpm ÷ 5,252</p>

At 5,252 rpm, torque in lbf·ft and horsepower are numerically equal — which is why car torque and power curves cross there.

## Starting and breakdown torque

Induction motors typically produce 150–250% of rated torque at start and up to 200–300% at breakdown. Check the motor datasheet when driving high-inertia or heavily loaded machines.

## Why it matters

Low-speed applications such as conveyors, mixers and winches need high torque. Selecting the motor on kW alone can leave it unable to start the load — always check torque at the driven speed.

## The default case step by step

The defaults are an **11 kW motor at 1,460 rpm**. Converting speed to angular velocity, ω = 2π × 1,460 ÷ 60 = 152.89 rad/s, and T = P ÷ ω = 11,000 ÷ 152.89 = **71.95 N·m**, or 7.34 kgf·m. Using the synchronous speed of 1,500 rpm instead would give 70.03 N·m, about 3% low, so always use the full-load speed from the nameplate.

## Choosing a motor for a conveyor

A belt conveyor drum needs **1,800 N·m at 40 rpm**. The power at the drum is 2π × 40 × 1,800 ÷ 60 = 7.54 kW. Through a helical gearbox at 94% efficiency the motor must deliver 8.02 kW, so the next standard rating, 11 kW, is chosen. With a 1,460 rpm motor the gear ratio is 1,460 ÷ 40 = 36.5:1, and the motor supplies 1,800 ÷ (36.5 × 0.94) = **52.5 N·m**, well inside its 71.9 N·m rating. Check starting torque as well: a loaded conveyor may need 150% of running torque to break away.

## Torque on a variable frequency drive

Below base speed a VFD holds rated torque and power falls with speed; above base speed it holds power and torque falls. For the default 11 kW, 4-pole motor (slip taken as 40 rpm):

| Frequency | Shaft speed | Available torque | Shaft power |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 Hz | 260 rpm | 71.9 N·m | 1.96 kW |
| 25 Hz | 710 rpm | 71.9 N·m | 5.35 kW |
| 50 Hz | 1,460 rpm | 71.9 N·m | 11.00 kW |
| 60 Hz | 1,760 rpm | 60.0 N·m | 11.05 kW |
| 75 Hz | 2,210 rpm | 48.0 N·m | 11.10 kW |

A self-cooled motor run at full torque near 10 Hz may also need derating, because its shaft fan moves little air at low speed.

## How torque is measured

On a test bed, an in-line rotary torque transducer with strain gauges, or a dynamometer, gives torque directly, and power follows from the measured speed. In the plant, most VFDs estimate shaft torque from the motor current and flux model to within a few percent, which is good enough for trending load. A clamp meter reading on a direct-on-line motor only hints at load, because current includes a large magnetising part at light load.

## Common mistakes

- **Using synchronous speed.** 1,500 rpm instead of 1,460 rpm understates torque by about 3%.
- **Mixing rpm and rad/s.** P = T × ω needs ω in rad/s; with rpm the factor 2π ÷ 60 must be included.
- **Treating nameplate kW as input power.** IEC motor ratings are shaft output; electrical input is higher by the motor efficiency.
- **Leaving out gearbox losses.** Worm gearboxes can be only 50% to 85% efficient, far below a helical unit.
- **Confusing horsepower types.** Metric horsepower is 735.5 W and mechanical (imperial) horsepower is 745.7 W.
