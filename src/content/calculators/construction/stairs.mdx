## How to use this calculator

Enter the **total rise**, your **maximum riser height** and the **tread depth**. The number of risers, actual riser height, total run and angle are calculated.

## Formulas

<p class="formula">risers = ⌈rise ÷ max riser⌉   riser = rise ÷ risers   run = (risers − 1) × tread</p>

## Example

Floor-to-floor 2.7 m, max riser 180 mm, tread 270 mm:

- Risers = **15**, each **180 mm**
- Total run = 14 × 270 = **3.78 m**
- Angle = **33.7°**
- Check: 2R + G = 630 mm ✓

Always check your local building code for limits on riser, going, headroom and handrails.

## Comfortable stair dimensions

A common comfort rule is:

<p class="formula">2 × riser + going ≈ 600–630 mm</p>

| Riser | Going (tread) | 2R + G |
|---|---|---|
| 150 mm | 300 mm | 600 |
| 170 mm | 280 mm | 620 |
| 180 mm | 260 mm | 620 |
| 190 mm | 250 mm | 630 |

## Typical building code limits

| | UK (private stairs) | US (IRC residential) |
|---|---|---|
| Max riser | 220 mm | 7¾ in (197 mm) |
| Min going / tread | 220 mm | 10 in (254 mm) |
| Max pitch | 42° | — |
| Min headroom | 2.0 m | 6 ft 8 in (2.03 m) |

Codes vary — check your local building regulations.

## Treads vs risers

A straight flight always has **one fewer tread than risers**, because the top step is the upper floor itself. The calculator's total run already accounts for this.
