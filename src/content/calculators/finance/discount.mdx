## How to use this calculator

Enter the **original price** and the **discount %** to see the sale price and how much you save. It also works backwards:

- Know the original and sale prices? Press × on Discount and type the sale price to find the real percentage off.
- Know the sale price and discount? Press × on Original price to find what it used to cost.

## The formula

<p class="formula">sale price = price × (1 − discount)   saving = price − sale price</p>

## Worked example

A jacket costs 80 with 25% off:

- Saving = 80 × 0.25 = **20**
- Sale price = 80 − 20 = **60**

## Stacked discounts

Shops sometimes offer "an extra 20% off sale prices". Discounts multiply rather than add:

| Offer | Real total discount |
|---|---|
| 20% + 20% | 36% |
| 30% + 10% | 37% |
| 50% + 20% | 60% |

To check a stacked offer, apply the first discount, then put the result into the Original price field and apply the second.

## Discount plus sales tax

Tax is usually charged on the **discounted** price. An 80 item at 25% off with 8% tax:

- Discounted price = 80 × 0.75 = 60
- Tax = 60 × 0.08 = 4.80
- Total = **64.80**

Use the [sales tax calculator](/finance/sales-tax-vat/) for the second step.

## Quick mental maths

- **10% off**: move the decimal point one place left and subtract. 10% of 80 is 8.
- **20% off**: double the 10% figure. **25% off**: divide by 4.
- **15% off**: 10% plus half of 10%.
- **“Buy one, get one 50% off”** is 25% off the pair.
- **“Buy 2, get 1 free”** is 33.3% off three items.

## Is it really a deal?

- Compare the **final price** with other shops, not the claimed original price.
- Check the **unit price** for multi-packs with the [unit price calculator](/everyday-life/unit-price/).
- A coupon worth a fixed amount beats a percentage off on cheaper items, and the reverse on expensive ones.

## Discount vs markup

A 25% discount on 80 gives 60, but marking 60 back up by 25% only gives 75. Use the [profit margin calculator](/finance/profit-margin/) for markups.
