## How to use the inductor AL value calculator

Enter the core's **A<sub>L</sub> value** in nH per turn² and the **target inductance**. The calculator returns the exact turns, the whole number of turns to wind (rounded up) and the inductance those turns give. To find the inductance of an existing winding, clear the target inductance and enter the turns in the exact-turns field.

## AL value formula

<p class="formula">L = A<sub>L</sub> × N² &nbsp;⇒&nbsp; N = √(L ÷ A<sub>L</sub>)</p>

## Example

A ferrite core with A<sub>L</sub> = 250 nH/N² and a target of 150 µH:

- N = √(150 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ 250 × 10⁻⁹) = √600 = **24.495 turns**
- Wind **25 turns**
- L = 250 nH × 25² = **156.25 µH**

## Notes

- Gapped cores have a lower A<sub>L</sub> but store more energy before saturating — check peak current against the core's saturation flux.
- Doubling the turns quadruples the inductance; to change L by 10% change the turns by about 5%.
- For coils without a magnetic core, use the [air core inductor calculator](/electronics/air-core-inductor/).

## Why the rounded winding is never exact

Turns come in whole numbers, so the achievable inductances are steps. On the default 250 nH/N² core, 24 turns give **144 µH** and 25 turns give **156.25 µH**, a step of about 9%. Rounding up keeps you at or above the target, which suits filter chokes where too little inductance means more ripple. If you need a value between steps, change the core, add a gap or accept the nearer value.

## Measuring A<sub>L</sub> yourself

When a core has no marking, wind a test coil and measure it with an LCR meter at a low test level. For example, 10 turns reading **24 µH** give A<sub>L</sub> = 24 µH ÷ 10² = **240 nH/N²**. With that core, 150 µH needs √(150,000 ÷ 240) = **25.00** turns, so 25 turns again. Use at least 10 turns spread evenly around a toroid so that leakage does not inflate the reading, and measure at a frequency close to the one the inductor will see.

## Checking for saturation

The peak flux density is B = L × I ÷ (N × A<sub>e</sub>), where A<sub>e</sub> is the core's effective area. For the 25 turn, 156.25 µH winding on a core with A<sub>e</sub> = 25 mm² carrying 1 A peak, B = **0.250 T**. Power ferrite saturates near 0.3 T when hot, so this winding can only carry about **1.20 A** before the inductance collapses. Use a larger core, a gapped core or fewer turns on a higher A<sub>L</sub> core if the peak current is higher.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up the A<sub>L</sub> units.** A value of 250 in µH per 100 turns is only 25 nH/N², which needs about 3.2 times as many turns.
- **Counting turns wrongly on a toroid.** Each pass of the wire through the centre hole is one turn; the wire lying along the outside does not count again.
- **Using A<sub>L</sub> under DC bias.** The datasheet value is measured at near zero current; powdered iron and ungapped ferrite lose a large part of it as DC current rises, so check the permeability versus bias curve.
- **Taking A<sub>L</sub> from the wrong material grade.** The same core shape is sold in several ferrite mixes whose A<sub>L</sub> values differ by a factor of five or more.
- **Rounding the turns down.** That leaves the inductance below target, and the tolerance can then push it far lower.
