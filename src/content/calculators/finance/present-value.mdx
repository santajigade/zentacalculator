## How to use this calculator

Enter a **future amount**, the **discount rate** and the **number of years** to get its present value. To go the other way, press × on Future amount and type today's value.

## The formula

<p class="formula">PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)<sup>t</sup></p>

## Worked example

You will receive 10,000 in 10 years. With an 8% discount rate:

PV = 10,000 ÷ 1.08¹⁰ = **4,631.93**

So 10,000 in 10 years is worth about the same as 4,632 today — because 4,632 invested at 8% would grow to 10,000.

## Why money now is worth more

- It can be invested to earn a return.
- Inflation reduces what future money buys.
- Future payments carry a risk of not arriving.

## Present value of 10,000 received later

| Discount rate | In 5 years | In 10 years | In 20 years |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3% | 8,626.09 | 7,440.94 | 5,536.76 |
| 5% | 7,835.26 | 6,139.13 | 3,768.89 |
| 8% | 6,805.83 | 4,631.93 | 2,145.48 |
| 10% | 6,209.21 | 3,855.43 | 1,486.44 |
| 12% | 5,674.27 | 3,219.73 | 1,036.67 |

The higher the rate and the longer the wait, the less a future amount is worth today.

## Present value of a series of payments

For equal payments every year (an annuity), the present value is:

<p class="formula">PV = PMT × [1 − (1 + r)<sup>−n</sup>] ÷ r</p>

1,000 a year for 10 years at 8% is worth 6,710.08 today, not 10,000. This is how pensions, lottery payouts and lease payments are valued.

## Net present value (NPV)

Businesses compare projects with NPV: the present value of all future cash inflows minus the cost today. A positive NPV means the project earns more than the discount rate.

Example: spend 20,000 today to receive 6,000 a year for 5 years, discount rate 10%. PV of inflows = 22,744.72, so NPV = **2,744.72**. The project is worth doing.

## Choosing a discount rate

- **Personal decisions**: the return you could earn elsewhere, such as a deposit or index fund.
- **Inflation-adjusted value**: use the inflation rate to see a future amount in today's money.
- **Business projects**: the company's cost of capital, plus a premium for risk.

## Uses

- Comparing a lump sum today with payments later (lottery, settlements, pensions).
- Valuing bonds and business projects (net present value).
- Deciding whether to pay a bill early for a discount.
