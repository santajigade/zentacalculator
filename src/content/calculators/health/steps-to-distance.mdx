## How to use the steps to distance calculator

Enter your **steps**, **height** and **weight**. The calculator estimates your step length, the distance covered and calories burned. If you know your exact step length, type it in to override the estimate.

## Steps to distance formulas

<p class="formula">Step length ≈ 0.414 × height<br/>Distance = steps × step length<br/>Calories ≈ steps × 0.00057 × weight (kg)</p>

## Worked example

10,000 steps at 175 cm and 70 kg:

- Step length = 0.414 × 175 = 72.5 cm
- Distance = 10,000 × 0.725 m = **7.25 km** (4.5 miles)
- Calories ≈ **400 kcal**

Running strides are longer — often 1.2–1.5 times walking step length — so you cover more distance per step when you run.

## Steps and walking pace

| Pace | Steps per minute (approx.) | Intensity |
|---|---|---|
| Slow stroll | 60–80 | Light |
| Normal walk | 90–100 | Light to moderate |
| Brisk walk | 100–120 | Moderate |
| Very brisk / power walk | 120–140 | Vigorous for many people |

About **100 steps per minute** is a widely used marker for moderate-intensity walking in adults.

## How many steps do you need?

Large studies, including a 2022 meta-analysis in *The Lancet Public Health*, found that the risk of early death fell as daily steps increased, levelling off at around **6,000–8,000 steps** for adults over 60 and **8,000–10,000** for younger adults. Any increase from a low base helps.

## Ways to add steps

- Walk during phone calls.
- Take the stairs for short trips.
- Get off one stop early or park further away.
- A 10-minute walk after meals adds about 1,000 steps each time.

## Measure your own step length

1. Mark out 10 m (or 30 ft) on flat ground.
2. Walk it at your normal pace and count the steps.
3. Step length = 10 m ÷ steps.

## Is 10,000 steps a day necessary?

Research published in *The Lancet Public Health* (2022) found that health benefits rise steadily up to about 6,000–8,000 steps a day for adults over 60 and 8,000–10,000 for younger adults. Any increase helps.
