## How to use the win percentage calculator

Enter **wins**, **losses** and **ties**. The calculator returns the win percentage, with each tie counted as half a win, and the win to loss ratio (wins divided by losses).

## Win percentage formula

<p class="formula">Win % = (W + T/2) ÷ (W + L + T)</p>

## Worked example

A team has a record of 42-28-2 (wins, losses, ties):

- Games played = 42 + 28 + 2 = **72**
- Win % = (42 + 2 ÷ 2) ÷ 72 = 43 ÷ 72 = **59.7%**, written as **.597** in US standings
- Win to loss ratio = 42 ÷ 28 = **1.50**

If the league ignored ties, the same record would give 42 ÷ 70 = .600 (60.0%), so always check which rule a table uses.

### Wins needed for a target

With 10 games left in an 82-game season, finishing at .600 needs 0.600 × 82 = 49.2 win points. The team already has 43, so it needs 6.2 more, which means **7 wins** from the last 10 games (assuming no more ties).

## Win percentage benchmarks

| Win % | Typical result (82-game NBA season) |
|---|---|
| 70%+ | Title contender (57+ wins) |
| 60% | Strong playoff team (49 wins) |
| 50% | Average (41 wins) |
| 40% | Missing the playoffs (33 wins) |

## How ties are counted

Different leagues treat ties differently:

- **Half a win** (this calculator): common in the NFL and many school sports.
- **Ignored**: some sports calculate win % from wins and losses only.
- **Points systems**: football (soccer) uses league points instead — see the [football points calculator](/sports/football-points/).

## Games behind

In standings, “games behind” compares two teams:

<p class="formula">GB = ((leader wins − team wins) + (team losses − leader losses)) ÷ 2</p>

## Pythagorean expectation

In baseball and other sports, expected win % can be estimated from points scored (S) and allowed (A): S² ÷ (S² + A²). Teams winning more than expected may have been lucky in close games.

## Ties counted as half or ignored

The rule for ties matters most when a record has many of them. Both versions for some example records:

| Record (W-L-T) | Games | Ties as half a win | Ties ignored |

| 9-6-1 | 16 | .594 (59.4%) | .600 (60.0%) |
| 8-5-3 | 16 | .594 (59.4%) | .615 (61.5%) |
| 42-28-2 | 72 | .597 (59.7%) | .600 (60.0%) |
| 12-3-2 | 17 | .765 (76.5%) | .800 (80.0%) |
| 25-25-4 | 54 | .500 (50.0%) | .500 (50.0%) |

The NFL has counted ties as half a win since 1972, and most school and college standings do the same. Records without ties give the same answer either way.

## Games behind example

If the division leader is 50-22 and this team is 42-28, the team is ((50 - 42) + (28 - 22)) ÷ 2 = **7 games behind**. Games behind and win percentage can disagree when teams have played different numbers of games, which is why final standings are ranked by win percentage.

## Pythagorean expectation example

A baseball team that scores 750 runs and allows 650 has an expected win percentage of 750² ÷ (750² + 650²) = **.571**, or about 93 wins over 162 games. Using the refined exponent of 1.83 gives .565 (about 92 wins). A team far above its expected record has usually won an unusual share of close games, and tends to drift back towards the expectation.

## Common mistakes

- **Dividing wins by losses.** 42 ÷ 28 = 1.50 is the win to loss ratio, not the win percentage of 59.7%.
- **Mixing up the decimal and the percentage.** A record of .597 means 59.7%, not 0.597%.
- **Ranking early season tables by wins.** A 6-2 team (75%) is ahead of an 8-4 team (66.7%) even though it has fewer wins.
- **Treating overtime losses as ties.** Ice hockey leagues award a point for an overtime loss and rank by points percentage (points ÷ twice the games played) instead.
- **Applying the wrong tie rule.** Some competitions ignore ties or use league points, so copy the rule from the league before comparing.
