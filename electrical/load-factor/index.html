    Load factor is the average load over a billing period divided by the maximum demand, so it shows how steadily a site uses its electrical capacity. This load factor calculator finds average load, load factor and demand factor from bill kWh and kW figures. See maximum demand calculation with diversity.

    Energy managers, plant engineers and consultants use it to understand demand charges, plan peak shaving and size transformers.

    How to use the load factor calculator

    Enter the energy consumed (kWh) and the length of the period from your electricity bill or meter readings, the maximum demand recorded in that period and, optionally, the total connected load. The calculator returns the average load, the load factor and the demand factor.

    Load factor formulas

    Average load = E ÷ t  ·  Load factor = Average load ÷ Maximum demand

    Demand factor = Maximum demand ÷ Connected load

    Example

    A factory used 216,000 kWh in a 30-day month with a recorded maximum demand of 500 kW and 800 kW of connected load:

    • Average load = 216,000 ÷ 720 = 300 kW
    • Load factor = 300 ÷ 500 = 60%
    • Demand factor = 500 ÷ 800 = 62.5%

    Notes

    • Utilities measure maximum demand as the highest average over a 15- or 30-minute window, not the instantaneous peak.
    • With kVA-based demand tariffs, improving power factor also cuts the billed maximum demand.
    • The transformer only needs to be sized for the diversified maximum demand, not the connected load — see the transformer size calculator.

    How load factor sets the average price per kWh

    On a two-part tariff the demand charge is fixed by the peak, so the more kWh that peak is spread over, the cheaper each unit becomes. With an illustrative tariff of 10 per kW of maximum demand per month plus 0.08 per kWh, the default month costs 500 × 10 + 216,000 × 0.08 = 22,280, an average of 0.1031 per kWh.

    Reading the figures from a bill or meter

    The kWh figure is the difference between the opening and closing energy register readings, multiplied by the meter constant or CT ratio where the bill does not do it. The maximum demand is the highest average power over one integration window, commonly 15 or 30 minutes, so a 2 second motor inrush barely moves it. If interval data is available, the peak window and the time it occurred show which loads to reschedule.

    Many tariffs bill demand in kVA. At 0.9 power factor the default 500 kW peak is 556 kVA, and a kVA based load factor would be 300 ÷ 556 = 54% unless the kWh are also converted. Keep both quantities on the same basis.

    Load factor and losses

    Copper losses in cables and transformers follow the square of current, so they depend on the shape of the load curve, not just the average. Distribution planners often estimate the loss factor with the empirical relation 0.3 LF + 0.7 LF². At 60% load factor that gives 0.432: average losses are 43.2% of the losses at maximum demand.

    Common mistakes

    • Using the wrong number of hours. A 31 day month has 744 h; the same bill over 744 h gives 58.06%, not 60%.
    • Taking an instantaneous peak as maximum demand. Utilities bill the highest integrated window, which is lower than a spot reading from a clamp meter.
    • Confusing demand factor with diversity factor. Demand factor is MD ÷ connected load (below 1); diversity factor is the sum of individual maximum demands ÷ the combined maximum (above 1).
    • Mixing kVA demand with kWh energy. The result understates load factor by the power factor.
    • Averaging monthly load factors for a year. The annual figure uses the single highest peak of the year, so it is normally lower than the average of the twelve monthly values.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do you calculate load factor?

    Load factor = energy consumed ÷ (maximum demand × hours in the period). 216,000 kWh in a 30-day month (720 h) with a 500 kW maximum demand gives 216,000 ÷ 360,000 = 60%.

    What is a good load factor?

    Higher is better — continuous process plants reach 70–90%, while offices and single-shift factories are often 20–40%. A high load factor means fixed demand charges are spread over more kWh.

    What is the difference between load factor and demand factor?

    Load factor is average ÷ peak load over time. Demand factor is maximum demand ÷ total connected load, and shows how much of the installed equipment runs at the same time.

    How can I improve load factor?

    Reduce the peak rather than the energy — stagger motor starts, shift batch loads to off-peak hours and use demand controllers or energy storage.

    What is the difference between load factor and capacity factor?

    Load factor compares average load with the actual peak demand. Capacity factor compares average output with the rated capacity of a generator or plant. A solar farm may run at a 30% capacity factor simply because the sun sets, while load factor describes how flat a consumer demand profile is.

    What is loss factor?

    Loss factor is the average power loss divided by the loss at peak load. It is commonly estimated as 0.3 LF + 0.7 LF², so a 60% load factor gives a loss factor of about 0.43.
