## How to use this calculator

Enter any **two** of power, speed and torque to get the third. Switch units freely — kW or hp, N·m or lbf·ft.

## The formula

<p class="formula">P = 2π n T ÷ 60   →   T = 9549 × P(kW) ÷ n(rpm)</p>

## Worked example

An 11 kW, 4-pole motor running at 1,460 rpm:

T = 9549 × 11 ÷ 1460 = **71.9 N·m**

Through a 10:1 gearbox (95% efficient) the output is about 146 rpm and 683 N·m.

## Typical motor speeds at 50 Hz

| Poles | Synchronous | Typical full-load |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3,000 rpm | ~2,900 rpm |
| 4 | 1,500 rpm | ~1,460 rpm |
| 6 | 1,000 rpm | ~970 rpm |
| 8 | 750 rpm | ~730 rpm |

At 60 Hz, multiply speeds by 1.2.

## Why it matters

Low-speed applications such as conveyors, mixers and winches need high torque. Selecting the motor on kW alone can leave it unable to start the load — always check torque at the driven speed.
