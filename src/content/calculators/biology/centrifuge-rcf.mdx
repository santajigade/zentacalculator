## How to use the centrifuge RPM ↔ RCF calculator

Enter the **speed** in rpm and the **rotor radius**. The RCF is calculated. To convert a protocol's × g to rpm, press × on Speed and type the RCF.

## Centrifuge RPM ↔ RCF formula

<p class="formula">RCF = 1.118 × 10⁻⁵ × r (cm) × rpm²</p>

## Example

12,000 rpm with an 8.5 cm rotor:

RCF = 1.118 × 10⁻⁵ × 8.5 × 12,000² = **13,684 × g**

## rpm from RCF

<p class="formula">rpm = √(RCF ÷ (1.118 × 10⁻⁵ × r))</p>

To get 1,500 × g in a rotor with a 10 cm radius: rpm = √(1,500 ÷ 0.0001118) ≈ **3,663 rpm**. Press × on Speed in the calculator and type the RCF.

## RCF vs RPM: what is the difference?

**RPM** is how fast the rotor turns. **RCF** (relative centrifugal force, written × g) is the force the sample actually feels, as a multiple of Earth's gravity. Two centrifuges at the same rpm give different g-forces if their rotors have different radii — which is why protocols specify × g.

## Why protocols use × g

Different centrifuges have different rotor radii, so the same rpm gives different forces. Always report and follow **× g** rather than rpm so results are reproducible.

## Typical spins

| Task | Typical force |
|---|---|
| Pelleting mammalian cells | 200–400 × g, 5 min |
| Separating plasma from blood | 1,000–2,000 × g, 10–15 min |
| Pelleting bacteria | 3,000–5,000 × g |
| Microcentrifuge DNA spins | 12,000–16,000 × g |
| Ultracentrifugation | 100,000 × g and above |

## Which radius?

Use the radius to the bottom of the tube (r<sub>max</sub>) for the maximum force, or the average radius (r<sub>av</sub>) to compare rotors. The rotor manual lists both.
