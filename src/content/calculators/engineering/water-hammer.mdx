## How to use the water hammer calculator

Enter the liquid **density**, the **pressure wave speed** for your pipe material, the **velocity change** (usually the full flow velocity for a sudden stop) and the **pipe length** to the nearest reservoir or open end. You get the surge pressure, the surge head and the critical closure time.

## Joukowsky equation

<p class="formula">ΔP = ρ × a × Δv &nbsp;·&nbsp; ΔH = a × Δv ÷ g &nbsp;·&nbsp; t<sub>c</sub> = 2L ÷ a</p>

## Worked example

Water (1,000 kg/m³) flows at 2 m/s in a 500 m steel line with a wave speed of 1,200 m/s, and a valve at the end shuts suddenly:

- Surge pressure ΔP = 1,000 × 1,200 × 2 = 2,400,000 Pa = **24.0 bar**
- Surge head ΔH = 1,200 × 2 ÷ 9.80665 = **244.7 m**
- Critical closure time t<sub>c</sub> = 2 × 500 ÷ 1,200 = **0.833 s**

The surge adds to the pressure already in the line, so a pipe running at 6 bar briefly sees about 30 bar at the valve. If the valve instead closes over 5 s, much longer than 0.83 s, Michaud's estimate ΔH ≈ 2Lv ÷ (g t) gives only 40.8 m (4.0 bar).

## Surge for water in steel pipe (a = 1,200 m/s)

| Velocity stopped | Surge pressure | Surge head |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 m/s | 6.0 bar | 61 m |
| 1 m/s | 12.0 bar | 122 m |
| 1.5 m/s | 18.0 bar | 184 m |
| 2 m/s | 24.0 bar | 245 m |
| 3 m/s | 36.0 bar | 367 m |

## Typical wave speeds (water)

| Pipe | Wave speed |
|---|---|
| Steel | 1,000–1,400 m/s |
| Ductile iron | 1,000–1,300 m/s |
| Concrete | 900–1,200 m/s |
| PVC | 300–500 m/s |
| HDPE | 200–400 m/s |

Flexible plastic pipes have lower wave speeds, so surges are smaller.

## Protecting instruments

Pressure gauges and transmitters on lines prone to surges need snubbers, liquid-filled gauges or pulsation dampers, and a pressure rating above the surge pressure.

## Wave speed from the pipe wall

The wave speed depends on how stiff the liquid and the pipe wall are. The Korteweg formula is a = √[(K/ρ) ÷ (1 + (K/E)(D/e))], with K the bulk modulus of the liquid (water about 2.19 GPa), E the elastic modulus of the pipe wall, D the internal diameter and e the wall thickness. Values for water, computed with that formula:

| Pipe material | E | D/e = 20 | D/e = 50 | D/e = 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Steel | 207 GPa | 1,344 m/s | 1,197 m/s | 1,032 m/s |
| Ductile iron | 170 GPa | 1,320 m/s | 1,154 m/s | 978 m/s |
| PVC | 3 GPa | 375 m/s | 242 m/s | 172 m/s |
| HDPE | 0.9 GPa | 210 m/s | 134 m/s | 95 m/s |

Thin flexible walls stretch and absorb part of the surge, which is why plastic pipe sees much smaller pressure spikes than steel at the same velocity. Air or gas bubbles in the liquid lower the wave speed even more, but they make the surge less predictable.

## Effect of valve closing time

For the example pipeline (500 m, 2 m/s, t<sub>c</sub> = 0.83 s). Closures slower than t<sub>c</sub> use Michaud's linear estimate, which is a first check only; a transient analysis is needed for a final design.

| Closing time | Surge head | Surge pressure | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.50 s | 244.7 m | 24.0 bar | full Joukowsky surge |
| 0.83 s | 244.7 m | 24.0 bar | full Joukowsky surge |
| 2.00 s | 102.0 m | 10.0 bar | Michaud estimate |
| 5.00 s | 40.8 m | 4.0 bar | Michaud estimate |
| 10.00 s | 20.4 m | 2.0 bar | Michaud estimate |
| 20.00 s | 10.2 m | 1.0 bar | Michaud estimate |

Most of the velocity change in a gate or butterfly valve happens in the last 10 to 20% of travel, so the effective closing time is far shorter than the full stroke time. Two-stage or characterised closing helps.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the stroke time of the valve as the closing time.** Flow barely changes until the valve is nearly shut, so the real deceleration happens in a fraction of the stroke.
- **Forgetting the pump trip case.** When a pump stops, the pressure first drops on the discharge side. If it falls to vapour pressure, the column separates and the rejoining slug can cause a surge larger than the Joukowsky value.
- **Taking the steel wave speed for plastic pipe.** PVC and HDPE give wave speeds three to five times lower, so using 1,200 m/s overstates their surge.
- **Comparing the surge alone with the pipe rating.** Add the steady operating pressure to the surge before comparing with the pressure class of the pipe, valves and flanges.
- **Using the length of the whole network.** L is the distance from the valve to the nearest point where the wave reflects, such as a reservoir, a large tank or a big header.
