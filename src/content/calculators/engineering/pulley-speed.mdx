## How to use the pulley speed and belt length calculator

Enter the **driver speed**, **driver pulley diameter**, **driven pulley diameter** and **centre distance** between shafts. You get the driven speed, speed ratio, belt speed and approximate belt length.

## Pulley speed and belt length formulas

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Driven speed | N₂ = N₁ × D₁ ÷ D₂ |
| Belt speed | v = π × D₁ × N₁ ÷ 60 |
| Belt length | L ≈ 2C + π(D₁ + D₂)/2 + (D₂ − D₁)²/(4C) |

Use pitch diameters for V-belts.

## Example

1,440 rpm motor, 100 mm → 250 mm, 500 mm centres:

- Driven speed = **576 rpm**
- Belt speed = π × 0.1 × 1,440 ÷ 60 = **7.54 m/s**
- Belt length ≈ **1561 mm**

## Belt drive tips

- Keep the speed ratio below about 6:1 for single V-belt stages to maintain wrap angle on the small pulley.
- Do not go below the minimum recommended pulley diameter for the belt section — small pulleys shorten belt life.
- Check alignment and tension; misalignment is a leading cause of belt failure.
- Real speed is 1–3% lower than calculated because of belt slip and creep.

## Choosing a standard belt

Round the calculated length to the nearest standard belt size, then adjust the centre distance with the motor slide rails to set tension.

## Centre distance for a standard belt

Belts come in fixed lengths, so after choosing a size you usually work back to the centre distance. Rearranging the belt length formula gives:

<p class="formula">C = [b + √(b² − 8 (D₂ − D₁)²)] ÷ 8, where b = 2L − π (D₁ + D₂)</p>

For the example drive with a standard 1,600 mm pitch length belt:

- b = 2 × 1,600 − π × (100 + 250) = **2,100.4 mm**
- C = [2,100.4 + √(2,100.4² − 8 × 150²)] ÷ 8 = **519.7 mm**

So the motor must sit about 20 mm further away than the 500 mm assumed above, which is within the travel of normal slide rails.

## Wrap angle and torque

The belt wraps less than half way round the small pulley, and grip depends on that arc:

<p class="formula">Wrap angle on the small pulley = 180° − 2 × asin[(D₂ − D₁) ÷ (2C)]</p>

For the example, wrap = 180 − 2 × asin(150 ÷ 1000) = **162.7°**. A 5.5 kW motor at 1,440 rpm gives 9,550 × 5.5 ÷ 1,440 = 36.5 N·m on its shaft, and the 2.5:1 ratio raises that to about **91.2 N·m** on the driven shaft before belt losses. To run the driven shaft at 720 rpm instead, the driven pulley must be 100 × 1,440 ÷ 720 = **200 mm**.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the outside diameter of a V-belt pulley.** Speed ratio and belt length use the pitch diameter, which is smaller than the rim diameter; using the rim figure gives a belt that is too long.
- **Using synchronous speed.** A 4 pole motor on 50 Hz turns at about 1,440 rpm under load, not 1,500 rpm. Use the nameplate speed.
- **Ignoring a short centre distance.** Below about 120° of wrap the small pulley slips; increase the centre distance or use an idler on the slack side.
- **Exceeding the pulley rim speed.** A large pulley on a 2 pole motor can pass the maker's maximum rim speed for cast iron. Check before speeding up a drive.
- **Fitting one new belt on a multi-belt drive.** Old belts have stretched, so the new one carries most of the load. Replace matched sets together.
