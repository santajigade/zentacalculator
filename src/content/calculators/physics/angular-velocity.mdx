## How to use the angular velocity calculator

Enter the **speed in rpm** and a **radius**. You get the angular velocity in rad/s and degrees per second, the rotational frequency in Hz, the tangential speed at that radius and the time for one revolution. Every field can be the unknown — enter rad/s or a rim speed instead and clear rpm to convert the other way, or enter a rim speed and rpm to find the radius.

## Angular velocity formulas

<p class="formula">ω = 2π × N ÷ 60 &nbsp;·&nbsp; f = N ÷ 60 &nbsp;·&nbsp; v = ω × r = π D N ÷ 60 &nbsp;·&nbsp; T = 1 ÷ f</p>

N is the speed in rpm, ω the angular velocity in rad/s, r the radius and D the diameter. Angular velocity is the same everywhere on a rigid rotating body; the tangential speed grows in proportion to the distance from the axis.

## Worked example

A 4-pole motor at 1,450 rpm driving a 200 mm diameter disc (r = 100 mm):

- ω = 2π × 1,450 ÷ 60 = **151.84 rad/s** (8,700 °/s)
- f = 1,450 ÷ 60 = **24.17 Hz**, so one turn takes **41.38 ms**
- Rim speed v = 151.84 × 0.1 = **15.18 m/s** (54.7 km/h)

## Practical notes

- **Gears and belts** keep the rim speed equal on both sides of a belt or at the pitch circle of a gear mesh, so the angular velocities are in the inverse ratio of the diameters — see the [pulley speed calculator](/engineering/pulley-speed/) and the [gear ratio calculator](/engineering/gear-ratio/).
- **Power and torque:** P = T × ω, so a 15 kW motor at 151.84 rad/s delivers 98.8 N·m — use the [torque, power and speed calculator](/engineering/torque-power-speed/).
- **Centripetal acceleration** at the rim is a = ω² r (2,306 m/s², about 235 g, in the example), which sets the stress in rotating discs and the force on any unbalance; see the [centripetal force calculator](/physics/centripetal-force/).

## Measuring rotational speed

A tachometer counts events per revolution and divides by time. At the default 1,450 rpm, different sensors give these pulse frequencies:

| Sensor | Pulses per revolution | Pulse frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Reflective tape, 1 mark | 1 | 24.17 Hz |
| Shaft key with proximity sensor | 1 | 24.17 Hz |
| 60 tooth gear wheel | 60 | 1,450.00 Hz |
| Incremental encoder, 360 ppr | 360 | 8,700.00 Hz |
| Incremental encoder, 1024 ppr | 1024 | 24,746.67 Hz |

A 1024 pulse encoder resolves 0.352° per pulse, so it can measure speed accurately in a few milliseconds, while a single reflective mark gives only 24.17 readings per second. Handheld optical tachometers read the tape mark; drive controllers and PLC high speed counters read encoders or gear tooth pickups.

## Second example: a car wheel

At 100 km/h (27.78 m/s) a tyre with a rolling radius of 315 mm turns at ω = v ÷ r = 27.78 ÷ 0.315 = **88.18 rad/s**, which is **842 rpm**. Here the rim speed is known and the angular velocity is the unknown: enter the speed and radius and clear rpm. Speedometers work the same way in reverse, so fitting larger tyres makes the car go faster than the speedometer shows.

## Very slow and very fast rotation

The Earth turns once per sidereal day (86,164 s), only 7.2921e-05 rad/s, yet the equator moves at 465 m/s because the radius is 6,378 km. At the other extreme, a 230 mm angle grinder disc at 6,600 rpm has a rim speed of 79.5 m/s, which is why such discs carry a maximum speed rating of 80 m/s.

## Common mistakes

- **Using diameter as the radius.** v = ω r uses the radius; entering the diameter doubles the rim speed.
- **Leaving ω in rpm inside a formula.** P = T ω, a = ω² r and E = ½ I ω² all need rad/s; using 1,450 instead of 151.84 gives answers about 9.5 times too large for P and 91 times too large for a.
- **Confusing ω with f.** Frequency is revolutions per second (24.17 Hz here), ω is 2π times larger.
- **Trusting a stroboscope at the first frozen image.** A strobe flashing at 12.08 Hz also freezes a shaft turning at 24.17 Hz; start from a high flash rate and come down to the first single steady image.
- **Assuming the motor runs at its synchronous speed.** A 4 pole induction motor on 50 Hz runs near 1,450 rpm under load, not 1,500 rpm; read the nameplate or measure it.
