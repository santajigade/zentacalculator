## Ecological density

In ecology, population density helps compare species abundance between habitats:

- **Crude density**: individuals per total area.
- **Ecological density**: individuals per area of **suitable** habitat.

Plant ecologists often count individuals in quadrats (for example 1 m²) and scale up.

## Physiological density

For countries, physiological density divides population by **arable** land only. Egypt has a moderate crude density but a very high physiological density because most people live along the Nile.

## Worked example with the default values

A city of 1,250,000 people on 450 km²:

- Density per km² = 1,250,000 ÷ 450 = **2,777.8 per km²**
- Density per square mile = 2,777.8 × 2.589988 = **7,194.4 per square mile**
- Density per hectare = 2,777.8 ÷ 100 = **27.78 per hectare** (or 11.24 per acre)

Turned around, each person has on average 1,000,000 ÷ 2,777.8 = **360 m²** of land, which includes roads, parks and workplaces as well as homes.

## Worked example: crude and ecological density

A survey counts 84 deer in a 12 km² reserve, of which 7.5 km² is woodland they actually use:

- Crude density = 84 ÷ 12 = **7.0 per km²**
- Ecological density = 84 ÷ 7.5 = **11.2 per km²**

The ecological figure is the one to compare with other woodlands, because open fields and water in the reserve hold almost no deer.

## Worked example: scaling up from quadrats

Five 1 m² quadrats contain 12, 8, 15, 10, 9 plants. The mean is **10.8 per m²** (standard deviation 2.8), which scales to 10.8 × 10,000 = **108,000 plants per hectare**. Place quadrats at random or on a grid, and use more of them when counts vary a lot between plots.

## Practical notes

- Use land area, not total area, for countries with large lakes or coastal waters; the two can differ by several percent.
- City figures depend on where the boundary is drawn. A city proper and its wider metropolitan area can differ in density by a factor of five or more.
- For mobile animals, density from a single count is a snapshot. Repeat surveys, or estimate the population with the mark and recapture method before dividing by area.
