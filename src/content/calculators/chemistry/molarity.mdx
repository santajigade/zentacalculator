## How to use the molarity calculator

Enter the **mass of solute**, its **molar mass** and the **solution volume** to get molarity. To prepare a solution, press × on Mass and type the molarity you want — the mass to weigh out is calculated.

## Molarity formula

<p class="formula">c = n ÷ V = m ÷ (M × V)</p>

## Worked example

5.844 g of NaCl (58.44 g/mol) in 100 mL of solution:

- n = 5.844 ÷ 58.44 = 0.1 mol
- c = 0.1 ÷ 0.100 L = **1.0 mol/L**

## Mass needed for common solutions

For 1 L of solution:

| Compound | Molar mass (g/mol) | 0.1 M | 0.5 M | 1 M |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NaCl | 58.44 | 5.84 g | 29.22 g | 58.44 g |
| NaOH | 40.0 | 4.00 g | 20.00 g | 40.00 g |
| KCl | 74.55 | 7.46 g | 37.27 g | 74.55 g |
| Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) | 180.16 | 18.02 g | 90.08 g | 180.16 g |
| CuSO₄·5H₂O | 249.69 | 24.97 g | 124.84 g | 249.69 g |
| Na₂CO₃ | 105.99 | 10.60 g | 52.99 g | 105.99 g |

## Molarity units

| Unit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| M (mol/L) | moles per litre |
| mM | millimoles per litre (0.001 M) |
| µM | micromoles per litre (10⁻⁶ M) |
| nM | nanomoles per litre (10⁻⁹ M) |

## Normality

Normality (N) counts reactive equivalents rather than moles: N = M × number of equivalents. 1 M H₂SO₄ is 2 N for acid–base reactions because each molecule gives two H⁺ ions.

## Hydrates

When using a hydrated salt, include the water in the molar mass. Copper sulphate pentahydrate (CuSO₄·5H₂O, 249.69 g/mol) needs much more mass than anhydrous CuSO₄ (159.61 g/mol) for the same molarity.

## Preparing a solution

1. Calculate the mass needed.
2. Weigh it accurately and dissolve in less water than the final volume.
3. Transfer to a volumetric flask and fill to the mark.
4. Mix thoroughly.

To make a weaker solution from a stock, use the [dilution calculator](/chemistry/dilution/).
