## How to use the download time calculator

Enter the **file size**, your **connection speed** in Mbps, and a **real-world efficiency**. The transfer time and effective speed are calculated.

## Download time formula

<p class="formula">time = size (bits) ÷ (speed × efficiency)</p>

## Example

A 5 GB file on a 100 Mbps connection at 90%: 40,000 Mb ÷ 90 Mbps = **444 s ≈ 7.4 minutes** (11.25 MB/s).

## Download times at different speeds

| File size | 25 Mbps | 100 Mbps | 300 Mbps | 1 Gbps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 MB app | 36 s | 9 s | 3 s | 1 s |
| 1 GB | 5 min 55 s | 1 min 28 s | 30 s | 9 s |
| 5 GB game update | 29 min 37 s | 7 min 24 s | 2 min 28 s | 44 s |
| 25 GB 4K movie | 148 min 8 s | 37 min 2 s | 12 min 20 s | 3 min 42 s |
| 100 GB game | 592 min 35 s | 148 min 8 s | 49 min 22 s | 14 min 48 s |

Times assume 90% real-world efficiency.

## Why real downloads are slower

- **Server speed**: the other end may limit your download.
- **Wi-Fi**: walls and distance reduce speed; a wired connection is faster.
- **Network congestion**: evening peak times are slower.
- **Protocol overhead**: TCP/IP headers use a few percent of bandwidth.

## Upload time

Many connections have much slower upload speeds. A 100/20 Mbps plan uploads a 5 GB video in about 37 minutes at 90% efficiency, versus about 7.4 minutes to download it.
