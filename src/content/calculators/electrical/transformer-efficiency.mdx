## How to use the transformer efficiency calculator

Enter the **rating**, the **load** as a percentage, the load **power factor**, and the **no-load** and **full-load losses** from the test certificate or datasheet. You get the output, total losses, efficiency and the load at which efficiency peaks.

For more background, read [Transformer Cooling Methods: ONAN, ONAF](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/transformer-cooling-methods-onan-onaf/) and [Transformer Maintenance Checklist](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/transformer-maintenance-checklist/).

## Transformer efficiency formulas

<p class="formula">η = x·S·PF ÷ (x·S·PF + P<sub>i</sub> + x²·P<sub>c</sub>)</p>

<p class="formula">Maximum efficiency at x = √(P<sub>i</sub> ÷ P<sub>c</sub>)</p>

Maximum efficiency occurs at √(1.4 ÷ 9.5) = **38% load**.

## Core loss vs copper loss

| Loss | Depends on | Present |
|---|---|---|
| Core (iron, no-load) | Voltage and frequency | Whenever energised, even with no load |
| Copper (load) | Current squared | Rises with load |

Because core loss is constant, a lightly loaded transformer runs 24 hours a day with relatively poor efficiency. Low-loss amorphous-core transformers cut core loss by 60–70%.

## All-day (energy) efficiency

For distribution transformers, compare energy rather than power: total kWh delivered ÷ (kWh delivered + core loss × 24 h + Σ copper loss × hours at each load). This is how loss capitalisation and energy-efficiency standards rank transformers.

## Worked example with the default values

A 1,000 kVA transformer with 1.4 kW no-load loss and 9.5 kW full-load copper loss runs at 75% load and PF 0.9:

- Output = 0.75 × 1,000 × 0.9 = **675 kW**
- Losses = 1.4 + 0.75² × 9.5 = 1.4 + 5.3438 = **6.7438 kW**
- Efficiency = 675 ÷ (675 + 6.7438) = **99.01%**
- Maximum efficiency at x = √(1.4 ÷ 9.5) = **38.4%** load, where it reaches 99.20% at PF 0.9

## Worked example: all-day efficiency for a daily load profile

The same unit feeds a plant whose load follows this pattern each day at PF 0.9:

| Hours | Load | Energy delivered | Copper loss energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 h | 20% | 1,080 kWh | 2.28 kWh |
| 10 h | 75% | 6,750 kWh | 53.44 kWh |
| 6 h | 50% | 2,700 kWh | 14.25 kWh |
| 2 h | 100% | 1,800 kWh | 19.00 kWh |

Delivered energy is 12,330 kWh, copper loss is 88.97 kWh and core loss is 24 × 1.4 = 33.6 kWh. The all-day efficiency is 12,330 ÷ (12,330 + 88.97 + 33.6) = **99.02%**. Over a year at ₹8 per kWh the core loss costs about ₹98,112 and the copper loss about ₹2,59,785.

## How the losses are measured

The two loss figures come from routine factory tests to IEC 60076-1 or IS 2026. The **open circuit test** energises one winding at rated voltage with the other open; the input power is the core loss. The **short circuit test** shorts one winding and raises the voltage on the other until rated current flows; the input power, corrected to 75 °C reference temperature, is the full-load copper loss. Both values are printed on the test certificate, and tender specifications cap them with penalties for any excess.

## Choosing a lower loss design

For a lightly loaded or always energised unit, core loss matters most. An amorphous core version with 0.5 kW no-load loss would save 7,884 kWh a year, and its efficiency at the default load rises to 99.14%. Buyers compare offers by capitalising each kW of no-load and load loss at a rupee value per kW and adding it to the price.

## Common mistakes

- **Using kVA as output power.** Output in kW is kVA × load × PF; a low power factor lowers efficiency even when the losses stay the same.
- **Scaling copper loss linearly.** Load loss follows the square of current, so at half load it is a quarter of the full-load figure, not half.
- **Forgetting that core loss never stops.** An energised transformer on no load is 0% efficient, still drawing 1.4 kW.
- **Taking copper loss at ambient temperature.** Winding resistance rises about 0.4% per °C; use the 75 °C corrected figure.
- **Entering losses in watts with the kW unit selected.** A 1,400 W core loss entered as 1,400 kW gives a nonsense efficiency.
