## How to use the sales tax / VAT / GST calculator

Enter a **net price** and **tax rate** to add tax. To **remove** tax from a price that already includes it, press × on the net price and type the gross price — the net price and tax are calculated.

## Sales tax / VAT / GST formulas

<p class="formula">Gross = Net × (1 + rate)   Tax = Gross − Net<br/>Net = Gross ÷ (1 + rate)</p>

## Worked examples

**Adding 18%:** net 100 → tax 18 → gross **118**.

**Removing 20% VAT:** gross 120 → net = 120 ÷ 1.2 = **100**, VAT = **20**.

## A common mistake

To remove 20% VAT from 120, many people take 20% off: 120 × 0.8 = 96. That is wrong — the tax was 20% of the **net** price, not of the gross. The correct net is 100.

## Sales tax vs VAT vs GST

| | Sales tax (e.g. USA) | VAT (e.g. UK, EU) | GST (e.g. India, Australia) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charged | Only at the final sale | At every stage of the supply chain | At every stage |
| Shown in prices | Usually added at the till | Usually included in shelf prices | Included in retail prices |
| Credit for business purchases | No | Yes (input VAT) | Yes (input tax credit) |

For India-specific GST rates and the CGST/SGST split, use the [GST calculator](/finance/gst/).

## Combined state and local rates

In the United States, the rate you pay is the state rate plus any county and city rates. Add them together and enter the total — for example, a 6% state rate plus 2.25% local is 8.25%.

## Invoices and rounding

Businesses often round tax per line item, while shops may round on the total. The difference is usually a few cents but can matter for accounting. Follow the rule your tax authority or accounting software uses.

## Worked example with the default values

The defaults are a net price of **100** and a tax rate of **18%**:

- Gross = 100 × (1 + 0.18) = **118.00**
- Tax = 118.00 − 100 = **18.00**

Removing tax works the other way. A price of 59.99 including 20% VAT has a net value of 59.99 ÷ 1.2 = **49.99** and VAT of **10.00**. Leave the net price blank and type 59.99 into the gross field to get the same split.

## How much of a tax-inclusive price is tax

When a price already includes tax, the tax share of that price is rate ÷ (1 + rate), which is always smaller than the rate itself. Accountants call it the tax fraction; for 20% VAT it is exactly one sixth.

| Tax rate | Multiplier to add tax | Tax share of the gross price | Net for a gross of 100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% | 1.05 | 4.76% | 95.2381 |
| 10% | 1.10 | 9.09% | 90.9091 |
| 18% | 1.18 | 15.25% | 84.7458 |
| 20% | 1.20 | 16.67% | 83.3333 |
| 25% | 1.25 | 20.00% | 80.0000 |

## Discounts and invoices in practice

Tax is normally charged on the price actually paid, so apply the discount first. An item listed at 2,500 with a 10% discount costs 2,250 before tax, then 405.00 of tax at 18%, for a total of **2,655.00**.

On invoices, the rounding rule matters. Take three lines at 18%: 3 × 12.35, 7 × 4.99 and 2 × 19.95, a net total of 111.88. Rounding the tax on each line gives 6.67 + 6.29 + 7.18 = **20.14**, while rounding once on the total gives **20.14**. Both are accepted in most systems, but an invoice must use one rule consistently and the tax figure must match the sum shown.

## Common mistakes

- **Taking the rate off the gross price.** 118 less 18% is 96.76, not 100; divide by 1.18 instead.
- **Applying tax before a discount.** Unless local rules say otherwise, tax follows the discounted price.
- **Entering 18 instead of 18% in a spreadsheet.** A rate of 18 multiplies the price by 19; the calculator takes the percent sign for you.
- **Charging tax on a price that already includes it.** Adding 18% to a gross of 118 gives 139.24, taxing the tax; check which price you start from.
- **Treating a tax-inclusive quote as net.** Check whether a supplier quote says "plus VAT" or "incl. GST" before comparing two offers.
