## How to use the vertical tank volume calculator

Enter the tank's **inside diameter**, its **straight-side height** and the **liquid level** measured from the bottom. The calculator gives the liquid volume, total capacity, percentage full and the volume per millimetre of level. To find the level for a given volume, enter the volume and press × on Liquid level.

## Vertical tank volume formula

<p class="formula">V = (π × D² ÷ 4) × h</p>

## Worked example

A tank with a **3 m** inside diameter and **5 m** straight side holds liquid at **3.2 m**:

- Cross-section = π × 3² ÷ 4 = **7.0686 m²**, which is **7.069 L per mm** of level
- Liquid volume = 7.0686 × 3.2 = 22.619 m³ = **22,619 L**
- Total capacity = 7.0686 × 5 = **35,343 L**
- Percent full = 3.2 ÷ 5 = **64%**, leaving room for 12,723 L

To receive a total of 30,000 L the level must reach 30 ÷ 7.0686 = **4.244 m**.

## Using it with level instruments

Because volume is linear with level, a DCS can show volume by scaling the level transmitter directly: 0 to 5 m becomes 0 to 35,343 L. Remember that the transmitter's zero is often a nozzle some distance above the floor, so add that offset to the measured level first. Manual gauging works two ways: an **innage dip** measures the liquid depth directly, and an **ullage (outage) dip** measures from the reference point at the top down to the surface, so level = reference height − ullage. Multiply volume by density for mass; for that, a hydrostatic transmitter measures mass more directly than level does.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the outside diameter.** For a 3 m outside diameter with an 8 mm wall, the inside diameter is 2.984 m, and using the outside figure overstates the volume by 1.1%.
- **Measuring level from the wrong datum.** Level must be measured from the bottom of the straight side. A transmitter nozzle 300 mm above the floor needs that 300 mm added.
- **Leaving out the bottom.** A cone or dished bottom holds liquid below the straight side; add it separately or the tank seems to empty early.
- **Calling percent full by level the usable capacity.** Overflow nozzles, high level alarms and the roof take up the top, so the safe fill is often 90% or less.
- **Ignoring internals and temperature.** Heating coils, agitators and the thermal expansion of a large tank all shift volume, which is why custody transfer tanks use calibrated strapping tables.

## Bottoms and tops

Cone and dished ends add volume below the straight side. Strapping tables for custody transfer also allow for wall thickness, deadwood (internals) and tank expansion with temperature.
