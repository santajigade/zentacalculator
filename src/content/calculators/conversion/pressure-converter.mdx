## How to use the pressure converter

Type a pressure in **any** unit and every other field updates instantly — ideal for checking transmitter ranges, gauge readings and datasheets.

## Pressure formula

<p class="formula">1 bar = 100 kPa = 14.5038 psi   1 atm = 1.01325 bar   1 mH₂O = 98.0665 mbar</p>

**Example:** a 0–10 bar pressure transmitter covers **0–145 psi** or **0–1 MPa**. A tyre at 32 psi is **2.21 bar**.

## Gauge vs absolute pressure

Most gauges read **gauge pressure** (barg, psig) — relative to the surrounding atmosphere. **Absolute pressure** (bara, psia) includes the atmosphere: bara ≈ barg + 1.013. Always check which a datasheet means.

## Pressure in everyday life

| Example | Typical pressure |
|---|---|
| Atmosphere at sea level | 1.013 bar, 14.7 psi |
| Car tyre | 2.0–2.5 bar, 29–36 psi |
| Bicycle tyre (road) | 6–8 bar, 80–120 psi |
| Domestic water supply | 1.5–4 bar |
| Pressure cooker | about 1 bar above atmospheric |
| Scuba tank | 200–300 bar |

## Water column

10 metres of water exerts about 1 bar. That is why pressure in a tank rises by about 0.1 bar for every metre of depth. See the [hydrostatic pressure calculator](/engineering/hydrostatic-pressure/).

## Units by industry

| Industry | Common units |
|---|---|
| Process instrumentation | bar, mbar, kPa, psi, inH₂O |
| Hydraulics | bar, MPa, psi |
| Medicine (blood pressure) | mmHg |
| Weather | hPa (= mbar) |
| HVAC and draft | Pa, inH₂O |

The inH₂O value here uses water at 4 °C (the conventional reference); some industries use 60 °F or 68 °F, which differ by about 0.2%.
