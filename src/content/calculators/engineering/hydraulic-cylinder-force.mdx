## How to use the hydraulic cylinder force calculator

Enter the **working pressure**, **bore** and **rod diameter**. You get the extend (push) and retract (pull) forces. To find the pressure needed for a load, press × on Working pressure and type the force.

## Hydraulic cylinder force formulas

<p class="formula">F<sub>push</sub> = P × π d² ÷ 4<br/>F<sub>pull</sub> = P × π (d² − r²) ÷ 4</p>

## Worked example

A 50 mm bore, 28 mm rod cylinder at 160 bar:

- Push = 16,000,000 Pa × 0.001963 m² = **31.4 kN** (about 3.2 tonnes)
- Pull = 16,000,000 Pa × 0.001348 m² = **21.6 kN**

## Cylinder speed

Speed depends on oil flow and the effective area:

<p class="formula">v = Q ÷ A</p>

With 20 L/min into a 50 mm bore (19.6 cm²), extend speed is 20,000 cm³/min ÷ 19.6 cm² ≈ **1,019 cm/min**, about 0.17 m/s. Retraction is faster because the rod side has a smaller area.

## Regenerative circuits

In a regenerative circuit, oil from the rod end is fed back to the cap end during extension. Extension becomes faster but the push force is reduced to pressure × rod area.

## Safety margins

- Size cylinders for **at least 25% more** force than the load requires, to cover friction and pressure losses.
- Check **rod buckling** for long strokes under compression.
- Use pressure relief valves set below the cylinder's rated pressure.

## Pressure needed for a load

To lift a 25 kN load with the default 50 mm cylinder and a 25% margin, the cylinder must produce 31.25 kN. The pressure is F ÷ A = 31,250 N ÷ 19.635 cm² = **159 bar**. In the calculator, clear Working pressure and enter 31.25 kN as the push force.

For a 10 tonne press at 210 bar the minimum bore is √(4F ÷ πP) = **77.1 mm**, so the next standard size, 80 mm, is chosen.

## Extend and retract speed

With 20 L/min from the pump the default cylinder extends at 0.170 m/s and retracts at **0.247 m/s**, because the annulus is only 13.48 cm² against 19.63 cm² for the full bore. The area ratio φ = 1.46 also means the cap end returns 1.46 times the oil the pump supplies during retraction, so the return line and filter must be sized for that larger flow.

## Pressure intensification on the rod side

If the rod port is blocked while full pressure acts on the cap end, the oil trapped on the rod side is squeezed by the area ratio. At 160 bar on the default cylinder the rod side can reach **233 bar**, enough to burst a hose or blow a seal rated for system pressure. Meter-out flow controls and pilot operated check valves on the rod port need this checked.

## Measuring the real force

Fit a gauge or pressure transducer at the cylinder port, not at the pump, because hoses and valves drop pressure during motion. The force on the load is then the port pressure times area minus seal friction, typically 2 to 5% of rated force. A load cell under the rod end gives the true force for press calibration.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing bar and MPa.** 160 bar is 16 MPa; entering 160 MPa gives a force ten times too high.
- **Using the rod diameter as the annulus.** The pull area is π(d² − r²) ÷ 4, not π r² ÷ 4.
- **Reading pressure at the pump.** Pressure at the cylinder is lower while moving, so the force is less than the pump gauge suggests.
- **Assuming a single rod cylinder pulls as hard as it pushes.** Only a through rod (double rod) cylinder has equal areas on both sides; the default one pulls 69% of its push force.
- **Ignoring back pressure.** Pressure in the return line acts against the piston and reduces the net force.
