## How to use the elastic collision calculator

Enter the two **masses** and their **initial velocities** along the same line, using a negative sign for motion in the opposite direction. You get the final velocities if the collision is perfectly elastic, the common velocity if the objects stick together, and the kinetic energy lost in that inelastic case. Real collisions lie between the two.

## Collision formulas

<p class="formula">Momentum: m<sub>1</sub>u<sub>1</sub> + m<sub>2</sub>u<sub>2</sub> = m<sub>1</sub>v<sub>1</sub> + m<sub>2</sub>v<sub>2</sub></p>

<p class="formula">Elastic: v<sub>1</sub> = [(m<sub>1</sub> − m<sub>2</sub>)u<sub>1</sub> + 2m<sub>2</sub>u<sub>2</sub>] ÷ (m<sub>1</sub> + m<sub>2</sub>) &nbsp;·&nbsp; v<sub>2</sub> = [(m<sub>2</sub> − m<sub>1</sub>)u<sub>2</sub> + 2m<sub>1</sub>u<sub>1</sub>] ÷ (m<sub>1</sub> + m<sub>2</sub>)</p>

<p class="formula">Perfectly inelastic: v = (m<sub>1</sub>u<sub>1</sub> + m<sub>2</sub>u<sub>2</sub>) ÷ (m<sub>1</sub> + m<sub>2</sub>)</p>

## Example

A 2 kg cart at 3 m/s meets a 1 kg cart moving at −1 m/s (towards it):

- Total momentum = 2 × 3 + 1 × (−1) = **5 kg·m/s**
- Elastic: v<sub>1</sub> = (1 × 3 − 2) ÷ 3 = **0.333 m/s**, v<sub>2</sub> = (1 + 12) ÷ 3 = **4.333 m/s**
- Stuck together: v = 5 ÷ 3 = **1.667 m/s**
- Kinetic energy before = 9.5 J; after sticking = 4.167 J → **5.333 J lost**

## Coefficient of restitution

Real collisions are described by e = (v<sub>2</sub> − v<sub>1</sub>) ÷ (u<sub>1</sub> − u<sub>2</sub>): e = 1 for elastic, e = 0 for perfectly inelastic. Typical values: steel ball on steel about 0.6–0.9, basketball about 0.75–0.85, clay about 0.

## Checking the default result

The default carts (2 kg at 3 m/s and 1 kg at -1 m/s) give elastic final velocities of **0.333 m/s** and **4.333 m/s**, a common velocity of **1.667 m/s** if they stick, and **5.333 J** lost. Three quick checks confirm the elastic answer:

- Momentum after = 2 × 0.333 + 1 × 4.333 = 5.000 kg·m/s, equal to the 5 kg·m/s before
- Kinetic energy after = 9.500 J, equal to the 9.5 J before
- Relative velocity reverses: u<sub>1</sub> − u<sub>2</sub> = 4 m/s before, v<sub>2</sub> − v<sub>1</sub> = 4.000 m/s after

The energy lost when sticking also follows from the reduced mass μ = m<sub>1</sub>m<sub>2</sub> ÷ (m<sub>1</sub> + m<sub>2</sub>) = 0.6667 kg: ΔKE = ½ μ (u<sub>1</sub> − u<sub>2</sub>)² = 0.5 × 0.6667 × 16 = **5.333 J**.

## Partially elastic collisions

Real impacts fall between the two limits and are described by the coefficient of restitution e. The final velocities are v<sub>1</sub> = [p − m<sub>2</sub>e(u<sub>1</sub> − u<sub>2</sub>)] ÷ (m<sub>1</sub> + m<sub>2</sub>) and v<sub>2</sub> = [p + m<sub>1</sub>e(u<sub>1</sub> − u<sub>2</sub>)] ÷ (m<sub>1</sub> + m<sub>2</sub>). For the default carts:

| e | v<sub>1</sub> | v<sub>2</sub> | Energy lost | Share of initial KE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1.667 m/s | 1.667 m/s | 5.333 J | 56.1% |
| 0.25 | 1.333 m/s | 2.333 m/s | 5.000 J | 52.6% |
| 0.5 | 1.000 m/s | 3.000 m/s | 4.000 J | 42.1% |
| 0.75 | 0.667 m/s | 3.667 m/s | 2.333 J | 24.6% |
| 1 | 0.333 m/s | 4.333 m/s | 0.000 J | 0.0% |

The loss scales with 1 − e², so a ball with e = 0.5 still loses three quarters of the energy available in the centre of mass frame.

## Mass ratio and energy transfer

When mass 1 at 3 m/s hits a stationary mass 2 elastically, the share of kinetic energy handed over is 4m<sub>1</sub>m<sub>2</sub> ÷ (m<sub>1</sub> + m<sub>2</sub>)²:

| m<sub>2</sub> ÷ m<sub>1</sub> | v<sub>1</sub> | v<sub>2</sub> | Energy transferred |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 2.455 m/s | 5.455 m/s | 33.1% |
| 0.5 | 1.000 m/s | 4.000 m/s | 88.9% |
| 1 | 0.000 m/s | 3.000 m/s | 100.0% |
| 2 | -1.000 m/s | 2.000 m/s | 88.9% |
| 12 | -2.538 m/s | 0.462 m/s | 28.4% |
| 100 | -2.941 m/s | 0.059 m/s | 3.9% |

Transfer is complete only for equal masses. This is why nuclear reactors slow neutrons with light nuclei: a neutron loses up to all of its energy to a hydrogen nucleus but at most 28.4% to carbon 12, which is the ratio of 12 in the table.

## Measuring collisions in the lab

Air tracks with photogates or ultrasonic motion sensors remove most friction, so carts with magnetic bumpers come close to e = 1 and carts with Velcro pads give e = 0. Measure each velocity just before and just after contact, because the carts slow over longer distances.

## Common mistakes

- **Dropping the sign.** Velocities are vectors along the line; a body moving the other way must be entered as negative.
- **Expecting kinetic energy to be conserved when objects stick.** Only momentum is; the loss goes into heat, sound and deformation.
- **Using the 1D formulas for glancing impacts.** Off-centre collisions need momentum conserved in two directions.
- **Mixing units.** Enter both masses in the same unit and both velocities in the same unit before comparing results.
