## How to use the EPF calculator

Enter your **monthly basic salary plus DA**, your **age**, your **current EPF balance** (from the EPFO passbook), the **yearly salary increase** you expect and the **interest rate**. The calculator estimates your EPF balance at age 58, the total contributions and the interest earned.

## How EPF contributions work

| Contribution | Share of basic + DA | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Employee | 12% | EPF |
| Employer | 3.67% (or 12% minus ₹1,250 above ₹15,000 basic) | EPF |
| Employer | 8.33%, capped at ₹1,250 a month | EPS (pension) |

So on a basic of ₹30,000 the monthly EPF credit is 3,600 + 2,350 = **₹5,950**.

## EPF formula

Within a year, interest is simple — each month's deposit earns interest for the months left in the year. That makes a year's deposits worth about C × (12 + 5.5 × r) at year end. The balance then compounds yearly, and contributions grow with your salary at rate g:

<p class="formula">F = Bal × (1 + r)<sup>Y</sup> + C × (12 + 5.5r) × [(1 + r)<sup>Y</sup> − (1 + g)<sup>Y</sup>] ÷ (r − g)</p>

where Y = 58 − your age.

## Worked example

Age 30, basic + DA ₹30,000, balance ₹2,00,000, 5% yearly raise, 8.25% interest:

- Monthly EPF contribution = **₹5,950**
- Total contributions to 58 = **₹41,69,944**
- EPF at 58 = **₹1,38,88,244**
- Interest earned = **₹95,18,299**

## Boost your EPF with VPF

You can contribute more than 12% through the **Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF)**. It earns the same 8.25% rate and is just as safe. Remember that interest on employee contributions above ₹2.5 lakh a year is taxable.

## Keep in mind

- This is an estimate. The interest rate is set every year and your salary may grow faster or slower.
- EPS gives a separate monthly pension and is not included here.
- Do not withdraw EPF when you change jobs — transfer it with your UAN so it keeps compounding.

## Where the ₹5,950 a month comes from

On the default basic + DA of ₹30,000, you put in 12% = ₹3,600. Your employer also pays 12% = ₹3,600, but ₹1,250 of that (8.33%, capped at ₹1,250 because the pension wage ceiling is ₹15,000) goes to the Employees' Pension Scheme. Only ₹2,350 of the employer share reaches EPF, so the monthly credit is 3,600 + 2,350 = **₹5,950**. In the first year, the running balance method gives about ₹19,691 of interest on the opening ₹2,00,000 plus twelve deposits.

## Worked example: adding VPF, and leaving the balance alone

Take the same 30 year old and add a Voluntary Provident Fund deposit of ₹3,000 a month (10% of basic), rising with salary at 5% a year. Over 28 years the extra deposits total ₹21,02,493, and the projected corpus at 58 rises from ₹1,38,88,244 to **₹1,99,62,575**.

The opening balance matters just as much. Left untouched at 8.25%, today's ₹2,00,000 alone grows to **₹18,40,820** by 58. Withdrawing it on a job change gives up that growth, which is why a transfer through your UAN is almost always the better choice.

## Checking the estimate against your passbook

Download the passbook from the EPFO member portal with your UAN. It shows the employee share, the employer EPF share and the pension share separately, plus the interest credited for each financial year. If the yearly employee deposits do not equal 12% of the basic on your salary slip, ask HR why. Interest for a year usually appears some months after the rate is announced, so a balance that looks short in April is often just waiting for that credit.

## Common mistakes

- **Using gross or CTC salary.** EPF is 12% of basic plus dearness allowance only; HRA, special allowance and bonus are excluded.
- **Counting the pension share as EPF.** The ₹1,250 a month that goes to EPS builds a pension entitlement, not a withdrawable balance.
- **Withdrawing before 5 years of service.** The amount becomes taxable, and TDS of 10% applies when the withdrawal is ₹50,000 or more and PAN is linked.
- **Reading the result in today's money.** A crore at 58 buys far less than a crore today; compare it with an inflation-adjusted retirement target.
- **Assuming the rate never changes.** It has moved between 8.10% and 8.50% in recent years, and a 0.25 point shift changes the corpus by several lakh.
