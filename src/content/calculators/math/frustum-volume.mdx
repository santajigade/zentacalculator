## How to use the frustum volume calculator

Enter the **large radius**, the **small radius** and the **height** between the two parallel faces. For a bucket or pot measured by diameter, halve each diameter first. The calculator gives the volume, the slant height and the surface areas.

## Frustum formulas

<p class="formula">V = (π h / 3)(R² + R r + r²) &nbsp;·&nbsp; s = √(h² + (R − r)²)</p>

<p class="formula">Lateral area = π (R + r) s &nbsp;·&nbsp; Total area = π (R + r) s + π R² + π r²</p>

## Worked example

R = 5 cm, r = 3 cm, h = 4 cm:

- V = (π × 4 ÷ 3)(25 + 15 + 9) = **205.25 cm³**
- Slant height = √(16 + 4) = **4.472 cm**
- Lateral area = π × 8 × 4.472 = **112.40 cm²**; total area = **219.21 cm²**

## Notes

- If r = 0 the formula becomes the cone volume πR²h/3; if r = R it becomes the cylinder πR²h.
- For a partly filled bucket, use the radius at the liquid surface as the top radius and the liquid depth as h.

## Volume of a partly filled bucket

A tapered bucket holds less in its lower half than in its upper half, so the fill level is not proportional to volume. Take a bucket with a 30 cm top, a 25 cm bottom and a depth of 30 cm. Full, it holds (π × 30 ÷ 3)(12.5² + 12.5 × 15 + 15²) = **17.87 L**.

Filled to 20 cm, the liquid surface radius is 12.5 + (15 − 12.5) × 20 ÷ 30 = **14.167 cm**, so the liquid is a smaller frustum:

- V = (π × 20 ÷ 3)(12.5² + 12.5 × 14.167 + 14.167²) = **11.18 L**
- That is 62.6% of the full volume at 66.7% of the depth.

## Hoppers and silo cones

The outlet cone of a round hopper is an upside down frustum. A hopper with a 2 m top, a 0.3 m outlet and a 1.5 m tall cone holds (π × 1.5 ÷ 3)(1² + 1 × 0.15 + 0.15²) = **1.842 m³**. Filled with grain at about 750 kg/m³ that is roughly **1,381 kg**. The wall makes an angle of atan((1 − 0.15) ÷ 1.5) = **29.5°** with the vertical, which matters because steep walls are needed for mass flow of sticky powders.

## Why averaging the radii is not good enough

A quick estimate uses a cylinder with the mean radius. For the default frustum (R = 5 cm, r = 3 cm, h = 4 cm) that gives π × 4² × 4 = 201.06 cm³, which is 2.0% below the true 205.25 cm³. The error grows as the two ends differ more, so use the exact formula for anything you will pay for by volume.

## Common mistakes

- **Entering diameters as radii.** Buckets and pots are usually sold by diameter; entering 30 cm instead of 15 cm makes the volume four times too large.
- **Measuring the slant side as the height.** h is the vertical distance between the two flat faces. Measuring down the sloping wall overstates it.
- **Using outside dimensions.** For capacity, use the inside radii and depth. A thick plastic or concrete wall can cut the usable volume by 10% or more.
- **Assuming half depth means half full.** In a bucket wider at the top, filling to half depth gives well under half the volume, as the table shows.
- **Forgetting the unit conversion.** 1,000 cm³ is 1 litre and 1 m³ is 1,000 litres; mixing cm and m in one calculation gives wrong powers of ten.
