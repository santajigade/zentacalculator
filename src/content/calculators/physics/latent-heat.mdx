## How to use the latent heat calculator

Enter the **mass** and choose the **substance and phase change**. The calculator gives the heat that must be added (melting, boiling) or removed (freezing, condensing) in kJ and kWh. To find how much mass a given amount of heat can melt or evaporate, clear the mass and enter the energy.

## Latent heat formula

<p class="formula">Q = m × L</p>

L<sub>f</sub> is the specific latent heat of fusion (solid ↔ liquid) and L<sub>v</sub> the specific latent heat of vaporisation (liquid ↔ gas). This calculation covers only the phase change; add the sensible heat m c ΔT from the [specific heat calculator](/chemistry/specific-heat/) to bring the substance to its melting or boiling point first.

## Example

Boiling off 2 kg of water that is already at 100 °C:

- Q = 2 kg × 2,257 kJ/kg = **4,514 kJ**
- = 4,514 ÷ 3,600 = **1.254 kWh**

| Substance and change | Energy per kg | In kWh |

| Water — melting / freezing | 334 kJ | 0.09278 kWh |
| Water — boiling at 100 °C | 2,257 kJ | 0.6269 kWh |
| Ethanol — melting | 108 kJ | 0.03 kWh |
| Ammonia — boiling at −33 °C | 1,369 kJ | 0.3803 kWh |
| Aluminium — melting | 397 kJ | 0.1103 kWh |
| Copper — melting | 209 kJ | 0.05806 kWh |
| Iron — melting | 247 kJ | 0.06861 kWh |
| Lead — melting | 23 kJ | 0.006389 kWh |

Values are at the normal melting or boiling point at 1 atm; the latent heat of vaporisation of water falls as pressure (and boiling temperature) rises.

## Notes

- Melting ice absorbs as much energy as heating the same water by about 80 °C — the basis of ice storage cooling.
- Steam tables give h<sub>fg</sub> for water at other pressures, for example about 2,015 kJ/kg at 10 bar(a).
- Use the [heat duty calculator](/engineering/heat-duty/) when a stream changes temperature at a steady flow rate.

## From ice to steam: where the energy goes

The default 2 kg of boiling water is only the last step. Starting from ice at −20 °C (c<sub>ice</sub> = 2.09 kJ/kg·K, c<sub>water</sub> = 4.186 kJ/kg·K):

| Step | Heat | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Warm ice from −20 °C to 0 °C | 83.6 kJ | 1.4% |
| Melt the ice at 0 °C | 668.0 kJ | 10.9% |
| Heat water from 0 °C to 100 °C | 837.2 kJ | 13.7% |
| Boil off at 100 °C | 4,514.0 kJ | 74.0% |

The total is **6,103 kJ** (1.695 kWh), and the two phase changes together take 85% of it while the temperature barely moves.

## How long a kettle takes to boil it dry

A 2 kW kettle delivering about 90% of its power to the water needs 4,514 kJ ÷ (1.8 kW) = **2,508 s**, roughly 42 minutes, to evaporate the 2 kg once it is boiling. That is why a kettle switches off at boiling point: the latent heat is far larger than the energy needed to reach 100 °C.

## Latent heat of steam at higher pressure

Boilers rarely run at atmospheric pressure, and h<sub>fg</sub> falls as pressure rises. Approximate saturated steam table values:

| Pressure | Saturation temperature | h<sub>fg</sub> | Heat released by 2 kg condensing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 bar(a) | 99.6 °C | 2,257.5 kJ/kg | 4,515 kJ |
| 2 bar(a) | 120.2 °C | 2,201.6 kJ/kg | 4,403 kJ |
| 5 bar(a) | 151.8 °C | 2,107.4 kJ/kg | 4,215 kJ |
| 10 bar(a) | 179.9 °C | 2,014.6 kJ/kg | 4,029 kJ |
| 20 bar(a) | 212.4 °C | 1,889.8 kJ/kg | 3,780 kJ |
| 40 bar(a) | 250.4 °C | 1,714.1 kJ/kg | 3,428 kJ |

Use the value at the steam pressure in the heater shell, not the 2,257 kJ/kg of the dropdown, when sizing a steam heat exchanger.

## How latent heat is measured

The classic method is mixing calorimetry: drop a weighed piece of ice into warm water in an insulated cup and work out L<sub>f</sub> from the temperature fall. Laboratories now use differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), which measures the heat flow into a few milligrams of sample as it melts. In a plant, the latent heat duty is usually found from the condensate flow: every kilogram of condensate collected has given up h<sub>fg</sub>. For ice storage cooling, 1 kWh of heat melts 10.8 kg of ice.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing J and kJ.** Tables give L in kJ/kg; the calculator multiplies by 1,000 so Q comes out in joules.
- **Forgetting the sensible heat.** Water at 20 °C needs warming to 100 °C first; add m c ΔT.
- **Picking fusion for a boiling problem.** 334 kJ/kg is for melting; boiling water needs 2,257 kJ/kg.
- **Using 100 °C values for pressurised steam.** At 10 bar(a) only about 2,015 kJ/kg is released per kg.
- **Assuming all heater power reaches the substance.** Kettles, pans and vessels lose heat to their surroundings, so real times are longer.
