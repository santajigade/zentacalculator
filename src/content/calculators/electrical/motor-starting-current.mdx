## How to use the motor starting current calculator

Enter the motor's **full-load current** from the nameplate, the **DOL starting current ratio** (I<sub>A</sub>/I<sub>N</sub> from the datasheet) and choose the **starting method**. You get the DOL inrush, the line current with your starter and the starting torque as a percentage of DOL torque.

## Motor starting current formula

<p class="formula">I<sub>DOL</sub> = k × I<sub>FL</sub> &nbsp;·&nbsp; I<sub>start</sub> = I<sub>DOL</sub> × (V<sub>start</sub>/V<sub>rated</sub>)²</p>

For star-delta the factor is (1/√3)² = 1/3; for autotransformers it is the tap ratio squared.

## Comparison of starting methods

| Method | Line current (× DOL) | Torque (× DOL) | Typical line current |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct-on-line | 1.00 | 1.00 | 6–8 × FLC |
| Star-delta | 0.33 | 0.33 | 2–2.7 × FLC |
| Autotransformer 80% | 0.64 | 0.64 | 4–5 × FLC |
| Autotransformer 65% | 0.42 | 0.42 | 2.5–3.4 × FLC |
| Soft starter (current limit) | adjustable | low | 2.5–4 × FLC |
| VFD | — | up to 1.5 × rated | 1–1.5 × FLC |

## Example

A 11 kW, 415 V motor with 20 A FLC and a 6.5 × starting ratio:

- DOL starting current = **130 A**
- Star-delta starting current = **43 A**, with one third of the DOL starting torque

## Choosing a method

- **DOL** suits small motors (typically up to 7.5–11 kW) on a stiff supply.
- **Star-delta** suits pumps and fans that start lightly loaded, but causes a current spike at changeover.
- **Soft starters** give smooth acceleration and reduce mechanical shock.
- **VFDs** give the lowest current and full torque, plus speed control.

Check the voltage dip a large motor causes on a generator or weak supply — see the [generator size calculator](/electrical/generator-size/).

## Will the motor still start? A torque check

Cutting current also cuts torque, so the starter must leave enough torque to accelerate the load. Take the default 20 A motor with a 6.5 × current ratio and, from a typical datasheet, a DOL starting torque of 2.2 × rated torque:

| Method | Line current | Current ratio | Starting torque |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct-on-line | 130.0 A | 6.50 × FLC | 2.20 × rated |
| Star-delta | 43.3 A | 2.17 × FLC | 0.73 × rated |
| Autotransformer 80% | 83.2 A | 4.16 × FLC | 1.41 × rated |
| Autotransformer 65% | 54.9 A | 2.75 × FLC | 0.93 × rated |
| Autotransformer 50% | 32.5 A | 1.62 × FLC | 0.55 × rated |

With star-delta the motor starts with only 0.73 × rated torque. That is plenty for a centrifugal pump or fan, whose load torque at standstill is small, but a loaded conveyor needing close to full torque would stall. The 65% autotransformer tap gives 0.93 × rated torque for 54.9 A, a useful middle ground.

## Starting current for different motor ratios

20 A full-load current:

| DOL ratio I<sub>A</sub>/I<sub>N</sub> | DOL current | Star-delta current |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 100 A | 33.3 A |
| 6 | 120 A | 40.0 A |
| 6.5 | 130 A | 43.3 A |
| 7 | 140 A | 46.7 A |
| 8 | 160 A | 53.3 A |

Premium efficiency IE3 and IE4 motors tend to sit at the top of this range, so replacing an old motor can raise the inrush on the same supply.

## Measuring starting current

A clamp meter with an inrush function captures the RMS current over the first 100 ms or so and gives a figure that can be compared with k × FLC. A power quality recorder also shows how long the current stays high, which is the run-up time. Heavy, high inertia loads keep the current near locked-rotor value for many seconds, so the protection and the motor's thermal limit must both be checked against that time, not only the peak.

## Common mistakes

- **Fitting star-delta to the wrong winding.** The motor must be rated for delta at the supply voltage, for example 400 V delta / 690 V star on a 400 V system. A 230/400 V motor cannot be started star-delta on 400 V.
- **Ignoring the changeover spike.** In open transition star-delta the motor is briefly disconnected, and reconnecting in delta can draw a current close to DOL if it changes over too early.
- **Using rated torque instead of starting torque.** The one third reduction applies to the DOL starting torque from the datasheet, not to the rated torque.
- **Taking FLC from a different voltage.** A dual voltage nameplate lists two currents; use the one for your supply.
- **Forgetting the first half cycle.** The asymmetrical peak is higher than the RMS locked-rotor current, so instantaneous trips are set with margin above it.
