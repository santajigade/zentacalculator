## How to use the hemocytometer cell count calculator

Enter the **total cells counted**, the **number of large squares**, the **dilution factor** and your **sample volume**. Cells per mL and total cells are calculated.

## Hemocytometer cell count formula

<p class="formula">cells/mL = (cells counted ÷ squares) × dilution × 10⁴</p>

## Example

180 cells in 4 squares, 1:1 trypan blue, 10 mL sample:

- Cells/mL = 45 × 2 × 10⁴ = **9.0 × 10⁵**
- Total = **9.0 × 10⁶ cells**

## Hemocytometer grid

A standard Neubauer chamber has nine large squares, each 1 mm × 1 mm, with a depth of 0.1 mm. Each large square holds **0.1 µL** (10⁻⁴ mL), which is where the 10,000 factor comes from.

## Viability

With trypan blue, count live (clear) and dead (blue) cells separately:

<p class="formula">Viability = live cells ÷ (live + dead) × 100%</p>

Healthy cultures are usually above 90% viable. Many protocols require above 80–85% before experiments.

## How many cells to count

For good accuracy, count at least **100–200 cells** in total across the squares. If there are more than about 100 cells per large square, dilute further; if fewer than 10, concentrate or count more squares.

## Seeding density

To seed a plate, find the volume of cell suspension needed:

<p class="formula">Volume = target cells ÷ cells per mL</p>

To seed 300,000 cells from a suspension at 900,000 cells/mL, use **0.33 mL**.

## Counting rules

Count cells touching the top and left borders of each square, but not those touching the bottom and right. This avoids double-counting cells on shared lines.

## Worked example with the default values

180 cells counted in 4 large squares, mixed 1:1 with trypan blue (dilution 2), from a 10 mL suspension:

- Average per square = 180 ÷ 4 = **45 cells**
- Cells per mL = 45 × 2 × 10⁴ = **900,000 cells/mL** (9.0e+05)
- Total cells = 900,000 × 10 = **9,000,000 cells** (9.0e+06)

If 20 blue (dead) cells were also seen in the same squares, viability = 180 ÷ (180 + 20) × 100 = **90%**, and the 180 clear cells are the live count used above.

## How precise is a count?

Cells settle at random, so the count follows Poisson statistics and its relative standard deviation is 1 ÷ √N. Counting 180 cells gives about 7.5%, so the true default concentration lies roughly between 768,519 and 1,031,481 cells/mL (95%).

| Cells counted | Coefficient of variation | 95% range |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 20.0% | ±39.2% |
| 50 | 14.1% | ±27.7% |
| 100 | 10.0% | ±19.6% |
| 200 | 7.1% | ±13.9% |
| 400 | 5.0% | ±9.8% |
| 800 | 3.5% | ±6.9% |

Doubling the number of squares counted is the easiest way to tighten the result.

## Seeding from the count

To seed 200,000 cells per well in a 6 well plate from the default suspension, each well needs 200,000 ÷ 900,000 = **0.222 mL** (222 µL). For all 6 wells, take 1333 µL and top up with medium to the final volume.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the trypan blue dilution.** Equal volumes of cells and dye halve the concentration; leaving out the factor of 2 underestimates the culture by half.
- **Counting clumps as one cell.** Pipette gently but thoroughly before loading; clumps make counts low and uneven between squares.
- **Overfilling or underfilling the chamber.** Liquid that flows into the moats or a coverslip that is not seated changes the 0.1 mm depth the 10⁴ factor relies on.
- **Waiting too long before counting.** Trypan blue slowly stains live cells too, so count within about 5 minutes of mixing.
- **Mixing up large and small squares.** The 10⁴ factor is for the 1 mm² corner squares; the small central squares need a different factor.
