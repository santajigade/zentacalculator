## How to use the HRA exemption calculator

Enter your yearly **basic salary plus dearness allowance**, the **HRA** your employer pays, the **rent** you pay and choose your **city**. The calculator shows the tax-exempt part of your HRA and the part added to your taxable income.

If your salary or rent changed during the year, calculate each period separately and add the results.

## The HRA rule

The exempt amount is the **lowest** of:

1. Actual HRA received
2. Rent paid − 10% of (basic + DA)
3. 50% of (basic + DA) in a metro, or 40% in other cities

<p class="formula">Exempt HRA = min(HRA, Rent − 0.1 × Basic, 0.5 or 0.4 × Basic)</p>

## Worked example

Basic + DA ₹6,00,000, HRA ₹2,40,000, rent ₹20,000 a month (₹2,40,000), living in Mumbai:

1. Actual HRA = ₹2,40,000
2. Rent − 10% of basic = 2,40,000 − 60,000 = **₹1,80,000**
3. 50% of basic = ₹3,00,000

The lowest is **₹1,80,000**, so that much is exempt and ₹60,000 is taxable.

## How rent changes the exemption

Same salary (basic ₹6,00,000, HRA ₹2,40,000), metro:

| Monthly rent | Exempt HRA | Taxable HRA |
|---|---|---|
| ₹5,000 | ₹0 | ₹2,40,000 |
| ₹10,000 | ₹60,000 | ₹1,80,000 |
| ₹15,000 | ₹1,20,000 | ₹1,20,000 |
| ₹20,000 | ₹1,80,000 | ₹60,000 |
| ₹25,000 | ₹2,40,000 | ₹0 |
| ₹30,000 | ₹2,40,000 | ₹0 |

Below ₹5,000 a month (10% of basic) there is no exemption at all. Above ₹25,000 the exemption is capped by the HRA itself.

## Documents you need

- Rent receipts or a rent agreement.
- Landlord’s PAN if rent is above ₹1,00,000 a year.
- Submit proofs to your employer during the year so TDS is reduced.

## Keep in mind

- HRA exemption applies only in the **old regime**. Use the [income tax calculator](/finance/income-tax-india/) to see if the old regime still saves you tax.
- If you do not receive HRA but pay rent, you may be able to claim a smaller deduction under section 80GG in the old regime.
- You cannot claim HRA for a house you own and live in.

## Which cities use the 50% limit from 2026-27

Until 31 March 2026 only Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai counted as metros for HRA. The Income-tax Act 2025 and the Income-tax Rules 2026, which apply from 1 April 2026, extend the 50% limit to **Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad** as well. The Metro option in the calculator applies the 50% limit, so select it if you live in any of these eight cities, even though the option label names only the original four. The exemption is still available only under the old regime; the new regime, which is the default, gives no HRA exemption at all.

## Worked example: when the city limit decides the answer

With the default inputs the city does not matter: rent minus 10% of basic (₹2,40,000 minus ₹60,000 = ₹1,80,000) is the lowest limit, in a metro or not. The city limit bites only when rent is high compared with salary. Take a Pune employee with basic + DA of ₹12,00,000, HRA of ₹6,00,000 and rent of ₹55,000 a month (₹6,60,000 a year):

1. Actual HRA = ₹6,00,000
2. Rent minus 10% of basic = 6,60,000 minus 1,20,000 = ₹5,40,000
3. City limit = 40% of basic = ₹4,80,000 (up to 2025-26), or 50% = ₹6,00,000 (from 2026-27)

The exempt HRA rises from **₹4,80,000** to **₹5,40,000**, so ₹60,000 less salary is taxed. In the 30% slab with 4% cess that saves about **₹18,720** a year.

## Exemption by salary level

Rent fixed at ₹40,000 a month (₹4,80,000 a year), with HRA at 50% of basic, a common structure in large cities:

| Basic + DA (year) | HRA received | Exempt, 50% city | Exempt, 40% city |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₹3,00,000 | ₹1,50,000 | ₹1,50,000 | ₹1,20,000 |
| ₹6,00,000 | ₹3,00,000 | ₹3,00,000 | ₹2,40,000 |
| ₹9,00,000 | ₹4,50,000 | ₹3,90,000 | ₹3,60,000 |
| ₹12,00,000 | ₹6,00,000 | ₹3,60,000 | ₹3,60,000 |
| ₹18,00,000 | ₹9,00,000 | ₹3,00,000 | ₹3,00,000 |

At lower salaries the HRA or the city limit is the cap, so a 40% city exempts less. As salary rises, the 10% of basic taken off the rent grows until rent minus 10% becomes the lowest limit, and from there the city no longer matters.

## Changing city or rent during the year

The rule is applied month by month, so split the year at the change and add the parts. An employee on ₹50,000 basic and ₹25,000 HRA a month pays ₹15,000 rent for 5 months in a 40% city, then ₹30,000 for 7 months in a 50% city. The first period gives ₹50,000 exempt and the second ₹1,75,000, a total of **₹2,25,000**. Running the whole year with the later rent and city would overstate it.

## Common mistakes

- **Claiming HRA in the new regime.** It is not allowed there; compare both regimes first with the [income tax calculator](/finance/income-tax-india/).
- **Using gross salary instead of basic + DA.** Only basic pay and dearness allowance that counts for retirement benefits go into the 10% and 40% or 50% limits.
- **Including months with no rent.** Months spent in your own house or with no rent paid earn no exemption, even if HRA was received.
- **Paying rent to a spouse.** Tax officers routinely reject it; rent to parents is accepted only when they own the house and declare the income, and the 2026 rules ask you to disclose your relationship with the landlord.
- **Submitting proofs late.** If your employer has no rent proof by the cut-off, TDS is deducted on the full HRA and you must claim the exemption in your return.
