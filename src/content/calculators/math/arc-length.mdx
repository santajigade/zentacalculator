## How to use the arc length & sector area calculator

Enter the **radius** and **central angle** to get the arc length, sector area and chord. Or enter the arc length and radius to find the angle. Angles can be in degrees or radians.

## Formulas (θ in radians)

<p class="formula">L = r θ   A = ½ r² θ   chord = 2r sin(θ ÷ 2)</p>

To convert: radians = degrees × π ÷ 180.

## Worked example

Radius 10 cm, angle 60°:

- θ = 60 × π ÷ 180 = 1.0472 rad
- Arc length = 10 × 1.0472 = **10.47 cm**
- Sector area = ½ × 100 × 1.0472 = **52.36 cm²**
- Chord = 2 × 10 × sin 30° = **10 cm**

## Degrees and radians

<p class="formula">radians = degrees × π ÷ 180</p>

The arc-length formula L = rθ only works with the angle in **radians**. If you prefer degrees, use L = 2πr × (θ ÷ 360). The calculator converts automatically when you pick degrees.

## Segment area

The area between the chord and the arc (a circular segment) is the sector minus the triangle:

<p class="formula">Segment area = r² ÷ 2 × (θ − sin θ)</p>

For r = 10 and θ = 60°, that is 50 × (1.0472 − 0.8660) = **9.06**.

## Finding the angle or radius

- Angle from arc length: θ = L ÷ r
- Radius from arc length: r = L ÷ θ
- Radius from a chord and height (sagitta s): r = (c² ÷ 4 + s²) ÷ (2s)

The last one is handy for measuring large curves, such as an arch or a curved wall, where you cannot reach the centre.

## Practical uses

- **Bending pipe or bar:** the arc length is the material needed for a curved section.
- **Pie charts:** each slice is a sector — its angle is the share × 360°.
- **Curved paths and tracks:** arc length gives the distance travelled around a bend.
