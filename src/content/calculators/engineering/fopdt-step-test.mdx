## How to use the FOPDT step test calculator

With the controller in **manual**, make a step in the output and record the PV until it settles. Enter the **output step**, the total **PV change** (both in % of span), and the times when the PV reached **28.3%** and **63.2%** of its final change. You get the process gain, time constant, dead time and the θ/τ ratio. Use the result in the [lambda tuning calculator](/engineering/lambda-tuning/).

## Two-point (Smith) method

<p class="formula">K = ΔPV ÷ ΔOP &nbsp;·&nbsp; τ = 1.5 (t<sub>63.2</sub> − t<sub>28.3</sub>) &nbsp;·&nbsp; θ = t<sub>63.2</sub> − τ</p>

The points come from the first-order response 1 − e<sup>−t/τ</sup>, which reaches 28.3% at τ/3 and 63.2% at τ after the dead time.

## Example

A 10% output step raises a temperature PV by 20% of span; the PV reaches 28.3% of its change at 25 s and 63.2% at 40 s:

- K = 20 ÷ 10 = **2**
- τ = 1.5 × (40 − 25) = **22.5 s**
- θ = 40 − 22.5 = **17.5 s**
- θ/τ = **0.778**

## First-order response reference

| Time after dead time | Response (% of final) |
|---|---|
| τ/3 | 28.3% |
| τ/2 | 39.3% |
| τ | 63.2% |
| 2τ | 86.5% |
| 3τ | 95.0% |
| 4τ | 98.2% |
| 5τ | 99.3% |

## Step test tips

- Make the step large enough to see clearly above noise (typically 5–10% output), but small enough to stay safe.
- Wait for the process to be steady before the step, and repeat in the opposite direction to check for non-linearity.
- Valve deadband and stiction add apparent dead time — check valve response separately.

## From the model to controller settings

With K = 2, τ = 22.5 s and θ = 17.5 s, lambda tuning gives a robust PI controller. Choose λ as the larger of τ and 3θ, here λ = max(22.5, 52.5) = **52.5 s**:

- Kc = τ ÷ (K × (λ + θ)) = 22.5 ÷ (2 × (52.5 + 17.5)) = **0.161**
- Ti = τ = **22.5 s**

Because θ/τ is high, the loop is dead-time heavy and should not be tuned aggressively. The [lambda tuning calculator](/engineering/lambda-tuning/) compares this with Cohen-Coon.

## Checking the fit

The fitted model predicts PV change = K × ΔOP × (1 − e<sup>−(t − θ)/τ</sup>) after the dead time. Compare it with the recorded trend; the temperature column assumes a 0 to 200 °C transmitter span:

| Time after step | Predicted PV change | Share of final | In °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 s | 0.00% | 0.0% | 0.0 °C |
| 17.5 s | 0.00% | 0.0% | 0.0 °C |
| 25.0 s | 5.67% | 28.3% | 11.3 °C |
| 40.0 s | 12.64% | 63.2% | 25.3 °C |
| 62.5 s | 17.29% | 86.5% | 34.6 °C |
| 85.0 s | 19.00% | 95.0% | 38.0 °C |
| 130.0 s | 19.87% | 99.3% | 39.7 °C |

If the recorded curve rises later and then faster than this, the process is higher order and the two-point model is only an approximation. Hold the output steady for at least θ + 4τ = **107.5 s** after the step, when the response is 98% complete.

## Running the test in practice

Put the loop in manual at a steady operating point, record the PV with fast sampling (at least 10 samples per time constant) and step the output once. Note the exact time of the step from the trend or the event log. Make the step 5 to 10% of output if the process allows, large enough to stand clear of noise and valve deadband. Repeat the step back down to check that gain and timing are similar in both directions; a big difference points to a nonlinear valve or process.

## Common mistakes

- **Timing from the start of the PV movement.** Both t28 and t63 must be measured from the output step; timing from the first PV movement loses the dead time.
- **Mixing engineering units and percent.** The gain is in % of PV span per % of output. A 40 °C change on a 0 to 200 °C span is 20%, not 40.
- **Stopping before the PV settles.** If the final value is guessed too early, the 28.3% and 63.2% levels are wrong and so are τ and θ.
- **Testing during a disturbance.** A feed or load change during the test shows up as part of the response; repeat the test.
- **Using the method on an integrating loop.** Most level loops never settle after a step, so the FOPDT model does not apply.
