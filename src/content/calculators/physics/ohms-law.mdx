  - q: What is Ohm's law?
    a: Ohm's law states that the current through a conductor is proportional to the voltage across it. V = I × R, where V is volts, I is amperes and R is ohms.
  - q: How do I find resistance from power and voltage?
    a: Use R = V² ÷ P. A 230 V, 60 W lamp has a hot resistance of 230² ÷ 60 ≈ 882 Ω. Enter V and P above and the calculator does this for you.
  - q: Does Ohm's law apply to every component?
    a: No. It applies to ohmic (linear) components like resistors. Diodes, transistors and lamp filaments change resistance with current or temperature.

## How to use this calculator

Fill in **any two** fields — for example voltage and power — and the remaining two are solved automatically. Change units with the dropdowns (mA, kΩ, kW…) and the values convert in place. Use **Show steps** to see the formula with your numbers substituted.

## The formulas

<p class="formula">V = I × R   P = V × I</p>

Combining these gives every other form you may need:

| Find | From V and I | From I and R | From V and R | From P and … |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Voltage | — | I × R | — | √(P × R) |
| Current | — | — | V ÷ R | P ÷ V |
| Resistance | V ÷ I | — | — | V² ÷ P |
| Power | V × I | I² × R | V² ÷ R | — |

## Worked example: 4–20 mA loop

A 4–20 mA transmitter drives a 250 Ω sense resistor. At full scale (20 mA):

- V = 0.020 A × 250 Ω = **5 V**
- P = 5 V × 0.020 A = **0.1 W**

That is why 250 Ω resistors convert a 4–20 mA signal into a 1–5 V input. The default values above show the 24 V supply case; set Current to 20 mA to try it.

## Reading the graph

The chart plots one quantity against another while holding your other inputs fixed. With voltage fixed, current falls as resistance rises — the inverse curve I = V ÷ R. Switch the axes to plot power, or change the range.

## Practical tips

- **Resistor wattage:** choose a resistor rated at least 2× the calculated power.
- **Wire and fuse sizing** depend on current, so calculate I first.
- Remember to convert mA to A and kΩ to Ω when working by hand — the calculator handles this for you.
