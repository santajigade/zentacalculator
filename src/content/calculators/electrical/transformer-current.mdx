## How to use the transformer current calculator

Enter the **transformer rating** in kVA, choose **three-phase** or **single-phase** and enter the **primary** and **secondary line voltages**. You get the full-load current on each side and the voltage ratio.

## Transformer current formulas

| Supply | Full-load current |
|---|---|
| Three-phase | I = kVA × 1,000 ÷ (√3 × V<sub>L</sub>) |
| Single-phase | I = kVA × 1,000 ÷ V |

## Selecting protection

- **LV breaker**: rated at or just above the secondary full-load current.
- **HV fuse or breaker**: sized for full-load current while riding through magnetising inrush (often 8–12 × rated current for a few cycles).
- **Cables and busbars**: rated for full-load current with installation derating factors.

## Loading

Distribution transformers are most efficient at about 40–60% load. Continuous overloading shortens insulation life; follow the manufacturer's loading guide (IEC 60076-7).

## Fault current

Short-circuit current depends on the transformer's impedance. See the [transformer short-circuit calculator](/electrical/transformer-short-circuit/).

## Worked example: 500 kVA, 11 kV to 433 V

With the default three-phase inputs:

- Primary: I = 500 × 1,000 ÷ (√3 × 11,000) = 500,000 ÷ 19,053 = **26.24 A**
- Secondary: I = 500 × 1,000 ÷ (√3 × 433) = 500,000 ÷ 750.0 = **666.7 A**
- Voltage ratio = 11,000 ÷ 433 = **25.40**

The current ratio is the inverse of the voltage ratio: 666.7 ÷ 26.24 = 25.40.

## Using the result on site

- **Checking load.** A clamp meter reading of 480 A per phase on the LV side means 480 ÷ 666.7 = **72%** loading, or about 360 kVA.
- **Choosing LV current transformers.** An 800/5 A CT gives 4.17 A on its secondary at full load, leaving headroom for overload without saturating.
- **HV fuse selection.** Magnetising inrush of around 10 times rated current, about 262 A for a few cycles, must not blow the HV fuse.
- **Fault level.** With 4.75% impedance the LV short-circuit current is roughly 666.7 ÷ 0.0475 = **14.0 kA**, which sets the breaker breaking capacity.

## Common mistakes

- **Using phase voltage with the √3 formula.** Three-phase full-load current uses line voltage; using 250 V instead of 433 V inflates the answer by √3.
- **Applying power factor to kVA.** Rating current depends only on kVA; power factor matters only when converting a kW load to kVA.
- **Sizing on 433 V and running at 400 V.** A lower secondary voltage means more current for the same kVA.
- **Forgetting delta winding current.** Inside a delta winding the current is line current ÷ √3, which matters for winding and internal CT checks.
- **Ignoring harmonics.** Non-linear loads heat a transformer more than the RMS current suggests; derate using the K factor.
