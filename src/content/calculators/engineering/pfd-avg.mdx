## How to use the PFDavg calculator

Enter the **dangerous undetected failure rate** λDU for the whole safety function (sensor + logic solver + final element) in FIT, and the **proof test interval** in years. You get PFDavg, the risk reduction factor and the SIL band that PFDavg alone supports.

## PFDavg formula (1oo1, low demand)

<p class="formula">PFD<sub>avg</sub> ≈ λ<sub>DU</sub> × TI ÷ 2 &nbsp;·&nbsp; RRF = 1 ÷ PFD<sub>avg</sub></p>

This simplified formula assumes perfect proof testing and ignores repair time and common-cause failures.

## SIL bands (low-demand mode)

| SIL | PFDavg | Risk reduction factor |
|---|---|---|
| SIL 1 | ≥ 10⁻² to < 10⁻¹ | 10 – 100 |
| SIL 2 | ≥ 10⁻³ to < 10⁻² | 100 – 1,000 |
| SIL 3 | ≥ 10⁻⁴ to < 10⁻³ | 1,000 – 10,000 |
| SIL 4 | ≥ 10⁻⁵ to < 10⁻⁴ | 10,000 – 100,000 |

## PFDavg vs proof test interval (λDU = 500 FIT)

| Test interval | PFDavg | RRF |
|---|---|---|
| 3 months | 5.48e-04 | 1,826 |
| 6 months | 1.10e-03 | 913 |
| 1 year | 2.19e-03 | 457 |
| 2 years | 4.38e-03 | 228 |
| 5 years | 1.09e-02 | 91 |

## Redundant architectures

| Architecture | Simplified PFDavg |
|---|---|
| 1oo1 | λ<sub>DU</sub> TI / 2 |
| 1oo2 | (λ<sub>DU</sub> TI)² / 3 + β λ<sub>DU</sub> TI / 2 |
| 2oo3 | (λ<sub>DU</sub> TI)² + β λ<sub>DU</sub> TI / 2 |

β is the common-cause factor (typically 2–10%). For a full assessment, follow IEC 61508/61511 — see [SIL determination methods](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/sil-determination-risk-graph-lopa-consequence-severity-matrix/).

## Worked example: high-pressure trip loop

A typical safety function has a pressure transmitter (λ<sub>DU</sub> = 50 FIT), a certified safety PLC (10 FIT) and a shutdown valve with its solenoid (400 FIT). The loop total is **460 FIT**, proof tested once a year:

- PFD<sub>avg</sub> = 460 × 10⁻⁹ × 8,760 ÷ 2 = **2.01 × 10⁻³**
- RRF = 1 ÷ 0.00201 ≈ **496**, which is in the **SIL 2** band

The valve alone contributes 87% of the result. Testing only the valve every 6 months, while the rest stays at 1 year, lowers PFD<sub>avg</sub> to about 1.14 × 10⁻³ (RRF ≈ 880). This is why partial stroke testing of valves is so common.

## Imperfect proof testing

Real proof tests do not find every dangerous failure. With proof test coverage PTC and mission time MT (the time until the device is fully overhauled or replaced):

<p class="formula">PFD<sub>avg</sub> ≈ PTC × λ<sub>DU</sub> × TI ÷ 2 + (1 − PTC) × λ<sub>DU</sub> × MT ÷ 2</p>

For the loop above with PTC = 90% and MT = 15 years, PFD<sub>avg</sub> rises from 2.01 × 10⁻³ to about **4.84 × 10⁻³** (RRF ≈ 207). The failures that are never tested quietly add up over the mission time, so a better test procedure can matter more than a shorter test interval.

## Common mistakes

- **Using λ<sub>D</sub> instead of λ<sub>DU</sub>.** Dangerous detected failures are caught by diagnostics and handled separately. Only the undetected part goes into this formula.
- **Mixing units.** FIT is failures per 10⁹ hours. A rate given per year must be divided by 8,760 first.
- **Leaving out the final element** or the solenoid valve. They are usually the weakest link.
- **Claiming a SIL from PFD<sub>avg</sub> alone.** Hardware fault tolerance and systematic capability must also meet the target SIL.
- **Assuming a 100% proof test.** Use the coverage formula above unless the test really proves every failure mode.

## Checking the default inputs

With the calculator's starting values, a single channel with λ<sub>DU</sub> = 500 FIT tested once a year:

- PFD<sub>avg</sub> = 500 × 10⁻⁹ × 8,760 ÷ 2 = **2.19 × 10⁻³**
- RRF = 1 ÷ 0.00219 = **457**
- log₁₀(457) = 2.66, so PFD<sub>avg</sub> alone supports **SIL 2**

## Largest λDU allowed for each SIL

Rearranging the 1oo1 formula gives the highest total λ<sub>DU</sub> a loop can have and still meet the upper PFD<sub>avg</sub> limit of a band: λ<sub>DU,max</sub> = 2 × PFD<sub>limit</sub> ÷ TI.

| Proof test interval | SIL 1 (PFD < 10⁻¹) | SIL 2 (PFD < 10⁻²) | SIL 3 (PFD < 10⁻³) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 months | 45,662 FIT | 4,566 FIT | 456.6 FIT |
| 1 year | 22,831 FIT | 2,283 FIT | 228.3 FIT |
| 2 years | 11,416 FIT | 1,142 FIT | 114.2 FIT |
| 3 years | 7,610 FIT | 761 FIT | 76.1 FIT |
| 5 years | 4,566 FIT | 457 FIT | 45.7 FIT |

This table is a quick screening tool: if the sum of the device λ<sub>DU</sub> values from the safety manuals is above the figure for your test interval, a single channel cannot meet the target and redundancy or a better final element is needed.

## From PFDavg to hazard frequency

PFD<sub>avg</sub> becomes meaningful when it is combined with how often the process actually challenges the function. In a LOPA study the hazardous event frequency is the demand rate times PFD<sub>avg</sub>. If the high pressure condition is expected 0.1 times a year, the default loop leaves a residual frequency of 0.1 × 0.00219 = **2.19 × 10⁻⁴ per year**, roughly once in 4,566 years. The formula on this page assumes low demand mode, which IEC 61511 defines as no more than one demand per year; above that, the function is in high demand mode and must be judged on its dangerous failure frequency (PFH) instead.

## Where λDU values come from

Use the λ<sub>DU</sub> printed in each device's IEC 61508 certificate or safety manual, which is normally derived from an FMEDA. Where a device is justified by prior use, the rate should come from your own maintenance records over enough operating hours to be credible. Generic databases are acceptable for early screening but tend to give higher rates than certified data, so the final calculation should state which source was used for each element.
