## How to use the wavelength & frequency calculator

Choose the **type of wave**, then enter the **frequency** (in Hz) or the **wavelength**. The other is calculated.

## Wavelength & frequency formula

<p class="formula">v = f × λ</p>

## Worked examples

- **FM radio at 100 MHz:** λ = 299,792,458 ÷ 100,000,000 = **3.00 m**
- **Wi-Fi at 2.4 GHz:** λ = **12.5 cm**
- **Sound at 440 Hz in air:** λ = 343 ÷ 440 = **78 cm**

## Radio and wireless frequencies

| Use | Frequency | Wavelength |
|---|---|---|
| AM radio | 1 MHz | 300 m |
| FM radio | 100 MHz | 3 m |
| Mobile (4G band) | 1.8 GHz | 0.167 m |
| Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz | 2.4 GHz | 0.125 m |
| Microwave oven | 2.45 GHz | 0.122 m |
| Wi-Fi 5 GHz | 5 GHz | 0.06 m |
| 5G mmWave | 28 GHz | 0.0107 m |

A quarter-wave antenna for 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi is about 3.1 cm long — a quarter of the 12.5 cm wavelength.

## Sound waves

For sound, use the speed of sound instead of light:

| Medium | Speed of sound |
|---|---|
| Air, 20 °C | 343 m/s |
| Water | about 1,480 m/s |
| Steel | about 5,900 m/s |

Middle A (440 Hz) in air has a wavelength of 343 ÷ 440 = **0.78 m**.

## Photon energy

The energy of a photon is E = h × f, where h = 6.626 × 10<sup>−34</sup> J·s. Higher frequency means more energy — which is why ultraviolet can damage skin and X-rays can pass through tissue.

## Worked example with the default values

The default is a 100 MHz radio signal in vacuum:

1. λ = c ÷ f = 299,792,458 ÷ 100,000,000 = **2.9979 m**, or 9.84 ft
2. The period is T = 1 ÷ f = **10 ns** per cycle
3. A half wave dipole for this frequency is λ ÷ 2 = 1.499 m from tip to tip; real dipoles are cut about 5% shorter, close to 1.42 m, because the wire ends add capacitance

## The same 1 kHz tone in different media

Frequency is set by the source and does not change when a wave crosses into another medium; the wavelength does:

| Medium | Wave speed | Wavelength at 1 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| Air, 20 °C | 343 m/s | 34.3 cm |
| Water | 1,481 m/s | 148.1 cm |
| Steel | 5,960 m/s | 596.0 cm |

## Wavelength inside a cable

Radio waves travel slower in coaxial cable than in air. The ratio is the velocity factor, and a quarter wave matching section must be cut to the shorter wavelength:

| Medium | Velocity factor | Wavelength at 100 MHz |
|---|---|---|
| Free space | 1.00 | 2.998 m |
| Foam PE coax | 0.85 | 2.548 m |
| Solid PE coax (RG58, RG213) | 0.66 | 1.979 m |
| PTFE coax | 0.70 | 2.099 m |

## Measuring wavelength in practice

For sound, a resonance tube or a pair of microphones on an oscilloscope shows the distance between points in phase. For microwaves, a classic kitchen check is to remove the turntable, heat a plate of chocolate briefly and measure between melted spots: they are half a wavelength apart, about 6.1 cm for a 2.45 GHz oven. RF engineers measure frequency with a counter or spectrum analyser and calculate the wavelength, while optical wavelengths are measured with a spectrometer and a diffraction grating.

## Common mistakes

- **Typing MHz or GHz as Hz.** The frequency box is in hertz, so 2.4 GHz must be entered as 2,400,000,000.
- **Using 343 m/s at every temperature.** The speed of sound in air is about 331.3 + 0.606 T m/s, so at 30 °C it is 349.5 m/s and every wavelength is about 1.9% longer.
- **Cutting coax to the free space wavelength.** Multiply by the cable's velocity factor first, or a stub for 100 MHz will be far too long.
- **Forgetting refractive index for light.** Inside glass with n = 1.5, 500 nm light has a wavelength of 333 nm, while its colour and frequency stay the same.
