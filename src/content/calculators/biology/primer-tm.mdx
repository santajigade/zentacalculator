## How to use the primer melting temperature calculator

Count the **A, T, G and C** bases in your primer and enter them. The melting temperature, a suggested annealing temperature and GC content are calculated.

## Formula (Wallace rule)

<p class="formula">Tm = 2(A + T) + 4(G + C) &nbsp;·&nbsp; Tm = 64.9 + 41 × (G + C − 16.4) ÷ N</p>

## Example

A 20-mer with 5 of each base:

- Tm = 2 × 10 + 4 × 10 = **60 °C**
- Annealing ≈ **55 °C**
- GC = **50%**
- Basic GC formula: 64.9 + 41 × (10 − 16.4) ÷ 20 = **51.8 °C**

The two estimates differ by about 8 °C — the Wallace rule runs high for 20-mers, which is why a gradient PCR is the safest way to set the annealing temperature.

For longer primers or exact work, nearest-neighbour methods that account for salt concentration are more accurate.

## Primer design guidelines

| Property | Recommended |
|---|---|
| Length | 18–25 nucleotides |
| GC content | 40–60% |
| Tm | 52–65 °C |
| Tm difference between primers | Within 2–5 °C |
| 3′ end | End in G or C (GC clamp), avoid more than 3 G/C in the last 5 bases |
| Repeats | Avoid runs of 4+ identical bases |

## Other Tm methods

| Method | Best for |
|---|---|
| Wallace rule (this calculator) | Primers under about 14–20 nt, quick estimates |
| Basic GC formula: 64.9 + 41(GC − 16.4)/N (this calculator) | Longer oligos |
| Nearest-neighbour thermodynamics | Most accurate; accounts for salt and primer concentration |

The Wallace rule tends to overestimate Tm for longer primers. Many polymerase suppliers provide nearest-neighbour Tm calculators tuned to their buffers.

## Annealing temperature

Start about 3–5 °C below the lower primer Tm. If you see non-specific bands, raise it; if there is no product, lower it or run a gradient PCR.

## How accurate is this Tm calculator?

This calculator shows the Wallace rule and the basic GC formula. The Wallace rule gives a quick, reliable estimate for primers of about 14–25 bases at standard PCR salt conditions. Commercial and polymerase-specific Tm calculators usually use the **nearest-neighbor** method with corrections for salt, Mg²⁺ and primer concentration, so their results can differ by 2–5 °C.

For the best results:

- Use the **same calculator for both primers** so they are matched to each other.
- Aim for both primers within **2–5 °C** of each other.
- Confirm the annealing temperature with a **gradient PCR** — the optimum is often 3–5 °C below the lower Tm.
- If you use a high-fidelity polymerase, follow its supplier's Tm method, as some recommend annealing above the calculated Tm.

## Primer problems to avoid

- **Self-dimers and hairpins**: primers binding to themselves.
- **Primer-dimers**: forward and reverse primers binding each other at the 3′ ends.
- **Off-target binding**: check specificity against the genome with a sequence search tool.
