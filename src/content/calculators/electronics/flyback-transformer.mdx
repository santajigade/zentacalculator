## How to use the flyback transformer calculator

Enter the **minimum and maximum DC input voltage**, the **output voltage**, the **rectifier forward voltage**, the **maximum duty cycle** you want at minimum input, the **output power**, an expected **efficiency** and the **switching frequency**. The calculator returns the turns ratio, the reflected voltage and the switch voltage, the peak primary and secondary currents, and the largest primary inductance that keeps the converter in discontinuous mode at full load (boundary mode at minimum input).

## Flyback transformer formulas

<p class="formula">n = N<sub>p</sub> ÷ N<sub>s</sub> = V<sub>in,min</sub> D<sub>max</sub> ÷ [(1 − D<sub>max</sub>)(V<sub>out</sub> + V<sub>f</sub>)] &nbsp;·&nbsp; V<sub>OR</sub> = n (V<sub>out</sub> + V<sub>f</sub>)</p>

<p class="formula">V<sub>DS</sub> = V<sub>in,max</sub> + V<sub>OR</sub> &nbsp;·&nbsp; I<sub>pk</sub> = 2 P<sub>in</sub> ÷ (V<sub>in,min</sub> D<sub>max</sub>) &nbsp;·&nbsp; L<sub>p</sub> = V<sub>in,min</sub> D<sub>max</sub> ÷ (I<sub>pk</sub> f<sub>s</sub>)</p>

The inductance and peak current are for the boundary between continuous and discontinuous conduction at minimum input and full load; any lower inductance keeps the converter in DCM.

## Worked example

A 10 W, 5 V flyback from an 18–32 V DC bus, Schottky diode (V<sub>f</sub> = 0.5 V), D<sub>max</sub> = 45%, efficiency 80%, 100 kHz:

- Turns ratio n = 18 × 0.45 ÷ (0.55 × 5.5) = **2.678**
- Reflected voltage V<sub>OR</sub> = 2.678 × 5.5 = **14.73 V**; switch voltage at 32 V input = **46.73 V** (choose a ≥ 80 V MOSFET to allow for the leakage spike)
- Input power 10 ÷ 0.8 = **12.5 W**, peak primary current = 2 × 12.5 ÷ (18 × 0.45) = **3.086 A**
- Primary inductance L<sub>p</sub> = 8.1 ÷ (3.086 × 100,000) = **26.24 µH**; peak secondary current = **8.26 A**
- At 32 V input the duty cycle drops to about **31.5%**

The leakage-inductance spike typically adds 30–50% of V<sub>OR</sub>, which is why 650 V or 800 V MOSFETs are used on universal-input flybacks.

## Practical notes

- **Clamp the leakage spike** with an RCD clamp or TVS; set the clamp voltage about 1.5 × V<sub>OR</sub> and keep it below the MOSFET rating with 10–20% margin.
- The core must store ½ L<sub>p</sub> I<sub>pk</sub>² without saturating — flyback cores need an air gap, and the gap stores most of the energy.
- Round the turns to whole numbers and recalculate the duty cycle; an auxiliary winding for the controller supply uses the same volts-per-turn.
- Secondary RMS current is much higher than the DC output current at low duty cycle, so size the output capacitor for ripple current, not just capacitance.

## Second example: universal AC input, 12 V at 24 W

An offline adapter with a 100 V to 375 V bulk capacitor, 12 V output, fast silicon rectifier (V<sub>f</sub> = 0.7 V), D<sub>max</sub> = 45%, efficiency 85% and 65 kHz:

1. Turns ratio n = 100 × 0.45 ÷ (0.55 × 12.7) = 45 ÷ 6.985 = **6.442**
2. Reflected voltage V<sub>OR</sub> = 6.442 × 12.7 = **81.8 V**; switch voltage at 375 V = **456.8 V** before the leakage spike
3. Input power 24 ÷ 0.85 = **28.24 W**; peak primary current = 2 × 28.24 ÷ 45 = **1.255 A**
4. Primary inductance L<sub>p</sub> = 45 ÷ (1.255 × 65,000) = **552 µH**; peak secondary current = **8.08 A**
5. Duty cycle at 375 V input = **17.9%**

The energy stored each cycle is ½ L<sub>p</sub> I<sub>pk</sub>² = 434 µJ, exactly P<sub>in</sub> ÷ f<sub>s</sub>, which is the figure the gapped core must hold without saturating. The primary RMS current is I<sub>pk</sub> √(D ÷ 3) = **0.486 A** and the secondary RMS current is I<sub>sp</sub> √((1 − D) ÷ 3) = **3.46 A**, against a DC output of only 2 A; use these RMS values for winding wire and capacitor ripple ratings.

## From the result to a real winding

Pick a whole number of secondary turns from the core's volts per turn, then round the primary. With N<sub>s</sub> = 8, the primary is 6.442 × 8 = 51.5, rounded to **52 turns**, a ratio of 6.500. Recalculating, the duty cycle at 100 V becomes 45.2%, V<sub>OR</sub> becomes 82.5 V and the switch sees 457.6 V. Allowing a leakage spike of half V<sub>OR</sub> brings the peak to about 499 V, comfortable for a 650 V MOSFET with clamp tolerance included. A 15 V auxiliary winding for the controller uses the same volts per turn as the secondary: 10 turns give about 15.2 V after its diode. Finally, choose a primary inductance slightly below the calculated maximum, typically 10% lower, so that inductance tolerance and low line never push the design into continuous mode.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving the diode drop out of the turns ratio.** For the 12 V example, using 12 V instead of 12.7 V gives n = 6.818, and the output then sits low at minimum input.
- **Choosing L<sub>p</sub> above the boundary value.** The converter slips into continuous mode at low line, adding a right half plane zero that a DCM compensation network was not designed for.
- **Sizing the MOSFET on V<sub>in,max</sub> + V<sub>OR</sub> alone.** The leakage spike on top can add 30% to 50% of V<sub>OR</sub>; without a clamp the device avalanches every cycle.
- **Taking the average bulk voltage as V<sub>in,min</sub>.** For AC input, V<sub>in,min</sub> is the valley of the bulk capacitor ripple at low line, not the average; designing on the average undersizes the turns ratio and the duty cycle saturates.
- **Ignoring core saturation at start up.** Current limit and peak flux must be checked at the controller's current limit, not just at the nominal I<sub>pk</sub>, or the core saturates during load steps and short circuits.
