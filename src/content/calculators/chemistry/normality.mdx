## How to use the normality calculator

Enter the **mass of solute**, its **molar mass**, the **equivalents per mole** and the final **solution volume**. The calculator returns the molarity, normality and equivalent weight. To find how much to weigh out, clear the mass and enter the normality you want.

## Normality formula

<p class="formula">N = M × n = (m ÷ EW) ÷ V &nbsp;·&nbsp; EW = molar mass ÷ n</p>

## Example

4.904 g of sulfuric acid (98.079 g/mol, n = 2) made up to 500 mL:

- Molarity = 4.904 ÷ 98.079 ÷ 0.500 = **0.1000 mol/L**
- Normality = 0.1000 × 2 = **0.2000 N**
- Equivalent weight = 98.079 ÷ 2 = **49.039 g/eq**

## Using the default solution in a titration

The default solution is 0.2000 N sulfuric acid, so a 25.00 mL aliquot holds 0.2000 × 25.00 = **5.000 meq** of acid. If it takes 24.60 mL of sodium hydroxide to reach the end point, N₁V₁ = N₂V₂ gives N(NaOH) = 0.2000 × 25.00 ÷ 24.60 = **0.2033 N**. Since NaOH has n = 1, that is also 0.2033 mol/L, or 8.13 g of NaOH per litre.

## Making it from concentrated acid

Nobody weighs out pure sulfuric acid. Concentrated acid is about 98% w/w with a density of 1.84 g/mL, which works out to 1,840 × 0.98 ÷ 98.079 = 18.39 mol/L or 36.77 N. For 500 mL of 0.2000 N you need 0.2000 × 500 ÷ 36.77 = **2.72 mL** of concentrated acid, added slowly to about 400 mL of water before making up to the mark. Because the strength of the concentrated acid is never exact, the diluted solution is then standardised against a primary standard such as sodium carbonate.

## One molarity, several normalities

The value of n belongs to the reaction, not to the bottle:

| Solution | Reaction used | n | Normality |
|---|---|---|---|
| H₃PO₄, 0.1 M | to H₂PO₄⁻ (methyl orange end point) | 1 | 0.1 N |
| H₃PO₄, 0.1 M | to HPO₄²⁻ (phenolphthalein end point) | 2 | 0.2 N |
| KMnO₄, 0.02 M | to Mn²⁺ (acid solution) | 5 | 0.10 N |
| KMnO₄, 0.02 M | to MnO₂ (neutral or weakly alkaline) | 3 | 0.06 N |
| KMnO₄, 0.02 M | to MnO₄²⁻ (strongly alkaline) | 1 | 0.02 N |

Always write the reaction next to a normality on a label or in a method, or the number cannot be checked.

## Normality in water testing

Standard methods for alkalinity and hardness still use normal solutions because the arithmetic becomes simple. Calcium carbonate has an equivalent weight of 50.04 g/eq, so each millilitre of 0.02 N acid neutralises 0.02 meq, which is 1.001 mg of CaCO₃. Titrating a 100 mL sample therefore gives alkalinity in mg/L as CaCO₃ as ten times the titre in millilitres.

## Common mistakes

- **Taking n from the wrong reaction.** Permanganate is 5 equivalents per mole only in acid solution, as the table shows.
- **Using molarities in N₁V₁ = N₂V₂.** 0.1 M H₂SO₄ needs twice its own volume of 0.1 M NaOH; with normalities (0.2 N against 0.1 N) the equation works directly.
- **Weighing the anhydrous mass for a hydrate.** Oxalic acid dihydrate is 126.065 g/mol, not 90.03 g/mol for the anhydrous acid.
- **Trusting the weighed mass of sodium hydroxide.** NaOH pellets pick up water and CO₂ from the air, so standardise the solution by titration rather than relying on the balance.
- **Adding solute to the full volume of water.** V is the final volume of solution: dissolve, then make up to the mark in a volumetric flask.
