## How to use the video file size calculator

Enter the **bitrate** in Mbps and the **duration**. The file size is calculated. Or enter a storage size to find the recording time.

## Video file size formula

<p class="formula">size (bytes) = bitrate (bits/s) × seconds ÷ 8</p>

## Example

60 minutes at 20 Mbps: 20 × 3,600 ÷ 8 = 9,000 MB = **9 GB**.

## Typical bitrates

| Video | Typical bitrate |
|---|---|
| 720p streaming | 2–5 Mbps |
| 1080p streaming | 5–8 Mbps |
| 1080p camera recording (H.264) | 15–30 Mbps |
| 4K streaming | 15–25 Mbps |
| 4K camera recording (H.264/H.265) | 50–150 Mbps |
| ProRes 422 HQ, 4K | about 700 Mbps |

## File size per hour

## Codecs

Newer codecs give similar quality at lower bitrates:

| Codec | Size vs H.264 at similar quality |
|---|---|
| H.264 (AVC) | Baseline |
| H.265 (HEVC) | about 40–50% smaller |
| AV1 | about 50% smaller |

## Recording time on a card

Card capacity (GB) × 8,000 ÷ bitrate (Mbps) ÷ 60 = minutes. A 128 GB card at 100 Mbps holds about **170 minutes**.

## Audio

Audio is usually small next to video: 128–320 kbps for stereo, adding about 1–2.4 MB per minute.

## Default result, in GB and GiB

The defaults (20 Mbps for 60 minutes) give 20 × 10⁶ × 3,600 ÷ 8 = **9.0 GB**. Your computer may show the same file as 8.38 GB, because Windows and many apps divide by 1,024³ (GiB) while card makers and this calculator use 10⁹ bytes.

## Worked example: a two-camera 4K event

A 2 hour wedding is filmed on two cameras, each recording 4K at 100 Mbps. Each camera writes 100 × 10⁶ × 7,200 ÷ 8 = **90 GB**, so the shoot needs 180 GB plus spare cards. A separate stereo audio recorder at 320 kbps adds only 288 MB. Uploading both camera files to a client over a 40 Mbps connection takes about **10 hours**, which is why many editors deliver a compressed copy and keep the camera originals on a drive.

## Recording time on common cards

Minutes of continuous recording at a constant bitrate (decimal GB as printed on the card):

| Card | 20 Mbps | 50 Mbps | 100 Mbps | 150 Mbps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 GB (59.6 GiB) | 427 min | 171 min | 85 min | 57 min |
| 128 GB (119.2 GiB) | 853 min | 341 min | 171 min | 114 min |
| 256 GB (238.4 GiB) | 1,707 min | 683 min | 341 min | 228 min |
| 512 GB (476.8 GiB) | 3,413 min | 1,365 min | 683 min | 455 min |

Leave around 5% free; cameras stop when the card reports full and the file system needs some space.

## Finding the real bitrate of a file

Camera manuals list the bitrate for each resolution and frame rate mode. For an existing file, a tool such as MediaInfo or `ffprobe` reports the overall bitrate, which already includes audio and container overhead. Streaming platforms publish recommended upload bitrates; for YouTube these are around 8 Mbps for 1080p at 30 fps and 35 to 45 Mbps for 4K at 30 fps. Variable bitrate files spend more bits on fast motion, so a short busy clip can run well above the stated average.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing Mbps and MB/s.** 100 Mbps is only 12.5 MB/s; dividing by 8 is the step most often forgotten.
- **Expecting the full card capacity.** A 128 GB card shows about 119 GiB on a computer, and part of it is used by the file system.
- **Using the average for a variable bitrate.** Size cards for the maximum bitrate of the mode if a shoot cannot be interrupted.
- **Forgetting the 4 GiB FAT32 limit.** On FAT32 cards cameras split long recordings; at 20 Mbps a new file starts about every 29 minutes, which is normal and not lost footage.
- **Planning only for camera files.** Editing proxies, render caches and exports often need as much space again as the original footage.
