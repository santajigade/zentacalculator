## How to use the SCSS calculator

Enter the **deposit amount** (up to ₹30 lakh) and the **interest rate** (currently 8.2%). You get the interest paid every quarter, its monthly equivalent, the yearly total and the total interest over the 5-year term. The deposit is returned at maturity.

## SCSS formula

<p class="formula">Quarterly interest = Deposit × Rate ÷ 4</p>

Interest is paid on the first working day of April, July, October and January. It is simple interest — it does not compound unless you reinvest it.

## Key rules

- **Tenure**: 5 years, which can be extended by a further 3 years.
- **Premature closure**: after 1 year with 1.5% penalty; after 2 years with 1% penalty.
- **Joint account**: allowed with spouse only; the deposit limit applies to the first holder.
- **Where**: post offices and authorised banks.

## Worked example: ₹15 lakh at 8.2%

With the default deposit of ₹15,00,000:

- Quarterly interest = ₹15,00,000 × 8.2% ÷ 4 = **₹30,750**
- Monthly equivalent = ₹30,750 ÷ 3 = **₹10,250**
- Yearly interest = **₹1,23,000**, and over 5 years **₹6,15,000**, with the full ₹15,00,000 returned at maturity

The first credit is usually smaller. A deposit made on 10 November earns interest only from that date to 31 December, about ₹15,00,000 × 8.2% × 52 ÷ 365 = ₹17,523, paid on 1 January. Full quarterly credits follow on the first working day of each April, July, October and January.

## Tax and TDS on SCSS interest

SCSS interest is fully taxable at your slab rate. Tax is deducted at source at 10% once the year's interest from the account exceeds the senior citizen threshold of ₹1,00,000. The default deposit earns ₹1,23,000 a year, so about ₹12,300 would be deducted unless you submit Form 15H, which you can do only if your total tax for the year is nil. To keep a single account below the threshold, the deposit must not exceed about ₹12,19,512 at 8.2%.

Take home income on the default deposit (tax including 4% cess, ignoring other income and deductions):

| Your slab rate | Yearly interest | Tax | Net income | Net yield |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | ₹1,23,000 | ₹0 | ₹1,23,000 | 8.20% |
| 5% | ₹1,23,000 | ₹6,396 | ₹1,16,604 | 7.77% |
| 20% | ₹1,23,000 | ₹25,584 | ₹97,416 | 6.49% |
| 30% | ₹1,23,000 | ₹38,376 | ₹84,624 | 5.64% |

Under the old regime, the deposit itself can be claimed in the ₹1.5 lakh investment deduction and senior citizens can deduct up to ₹50,000 of deposit interest. The new regime allows neither, but its rebate means many retirees with modest total income pay no tax anyway.

## Getting the most from the scheme

- **Split between spouses.** Each spouse aged 60 or more can hold their own account up to ₹30 lakh, doubling the family limit to ₹60 lakh.
- **Reinvest the interest.** SCSS pays simple interest. If each quarterly credit is reinvested at the same rate, the effective yield rises to about 8.46% a year.
- **Lock in a high rate.** The rate on the date of deposit stays fixed for the whole 5 years, even if later quarters announce a lower rate.
- **Extend on maturity.** The account can be extended in blocks of 3 years at the rate ruling on the maturity date; apply within a year of maturity.

## Premature closure on a ₹15,00,000 deposit

| Closure | Deduction | Amount deducted | Paid out |
|---|---|---|---|
| After 1 year, before 2 years | 1.5% | ₹22,500 | ₹14,77,500 |
| After 2 years, before 5 years | 1% | ₹15,000 | ₹14,85,000 |

Closing before 1 year means any interest already paid is recovered from the deposit. Closure on the holder's death carries no penalty.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the monthly figure as a monthly payout.** SCSS pays every quarter; the monthly equivalent is only for budgeting.
- **Assuming interest compounds.** Unclaimed quarterly interest earns nothing extra, so link a savings account for automatic credit.
- **Forgetting Form 15H.** Without it, eligible retirees with nil tax still lose 10% to TDS and must claim a refund.
- **Depositing more than ₹30 lakh across banks.** The limit covers all SCSS accounts a person holds together, and the excess is refunded without SCSS interest.
- **Missing the retirement window.** Retirees aged 55 to 60 must invest within three months of receiving their retirement benefits.
