## How to use the microstrip impedance calculator

Enter the **trace width**, **copper thickness**, the **dielectric height** between the trace and its reference plane, and the board's **dielectric constant**. The calculator returns Z₀, the propagation delay and the w/h ratio — keep w/h between 0.1 and 2.0, where the formula is valid. Clear the width and enter a target Z₀ to find the width.

## Microstrip impedance formula (IPC-2141)

<p class="formula">Z₀ = 87 / √(ε<sub>r</sub> + 1.41) × ln[5.98 h / (0.8 w + t)]</p>

<p class="formula">t<sub>pd</sub> = 85 √(0.475 ε<sub>r</sub> + 0.67) ps/in</p>

## Worked example

0.3 mm trace, 35 µm copper, 0.2 mm prepreg, ε<sub>r</sub> = 4.3:

- Z₀ = 87 ÷ √5.71 × ln(1.196 ÷ 0.275) = **53.5 Ω**
- Propagation delay = **5.51 ps/mm** (140 ps/in)

## Notes

- Wider traces, thinner dielectric and higher ε<sub>r</sub> all lower the impedance.
- Keep the reference plane continuous under the trace — a split plane breaks the return path and the impedance.
- Differential pairs, stripline and coplanar waveguide need different formulas.

## Finding the width for 50 Ω

Running the formula backwards gives w = [5.98 h ÷ e<sup>Z₀√(εr + 1.41)/87</sup> − t] ÷ 0.8. For the default stackup (h = 0.2 mm, 35 µm copper, ε<sub>r</sub> = 4.3), a 50 Ω trace is **0.335 mm** wide, slightly wider than the 0.3 mm default that gives 53.5 Ω.

Outer layers are plated after drilling, which typically adds about 25 µm. With 60 µm total copper the 0.3 mm trace drops to **50.4 Ω**, and the 50 Ω width becomes 0.304 mm. Fabricators adjust etch compensation for this, which is one reason to send them the target impedance rather than a fixed width.

## Delay and when a trace needs controlling

At 5.51 ps/mm a 100 mm trace delays the signal by about **551 ps**. A common rule of thumb treats a line as a transmission line once its one way delay exceeds about one sixth of the signal rise time. For a 1 ns edge that is about **30 mm** on this stackup, so even a short trace from a fast logic driver can ring if it is not terminated or matched.

## How impedance is checked in production

Fabricators place test coupons on the panel edge with the same stackup and trace geometry, then measure them with a time domain reflectometer (TDR). The usual tolerance is ±10%, or ±5% at extra cost. The TDR trace shows the impedance along the coupon, so a change at a via or connector appears as a step, which is also how impedance discontinuities are found on a finished board.

## Common mistakes

- **Measuring h to the wrong plane.** If the real reference is a 0.1 mm prepreg rather than 0.2 mm, the same 0.3 mm trace is about **28.3 Ω**, not 53.5 Ω.
- **Using the laminate's 1 MHz ε<sub>r</sub>.** FR-4 datasheet values vary with frequency and resin content; moving from 4.3 to 4.6 already lowers Z₀ to 52.2 Ω.
- **Forgetting plating and solder mask.** Both lower the impedance on outer layers, together often by 3 to 5 Ω.
- **Routing across a plane split.** The return current has to detour around the gap, which adds inductance and turns a 50 Ω trace into a discontinuity.
- **Reusing microstrip widths for inner layers.** An inner trace between two planes is stripline and needs a narrower width for the same impedance.
