## How to use the sleep calculator

Enter your **bedtime**, choose the number of **sleep cycles** and how long you usually take to **fall asleep**. The best wake-up time is calculated. To plan a bedtime, press × on Bedtime and type the time your alarm must ring.

## Why cycles?

Sleep moves through light, deep and REM stages in cycles of roughly 90 minutes. Waking at the end of a cycle — rather than in deep sleep — usually feels easier.

## Example

Bed at 22:45, 15 minutes to fall asleep, 5 cycles: wake at **06:30** (7.5 hours of sleep).

## How much sleep do you need?

| Age group | Recommended hours per night |
|---|---|
| Teenagers (13–18) | 8–10 |
| Adults (18–64) | 7–9 |
| Older adults (65+) | 7–8 |

These ranges come from the US National Sleep Foundation and the American Academy of Sleep Medicine. Five cycles (7.5 hours) suits many adults.

## Cycles are an average

Sleep cycles last about 80–110 minutes and change through the night. Treat the 90-minute cycle as a guide. If you often wake before the alarm feeling refreshed, your body has found its own rhythm.

## When to seek help

Talk to a doctor if you snore loudly, stop breathing during sleep, feel very sleepy in the day despite enough hours, or have trouble sleeping most nights for weeks.

## Better sleep habits

- Keep a regular schedule, even at weekends.
- Avoid caffeine late in the day.
- Keep the bedroom dark, quiet and cool.
- Put screens away 30–60 minutes before bed.
