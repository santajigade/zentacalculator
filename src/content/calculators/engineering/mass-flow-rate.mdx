## How to use this calculator

Enter the **volumetric flow** and **density** to get mass flow, or enter the mass flow to get volume. Every field has its own units.

## The formula

<p class="formula">ṁ = ρ × Q</p>

## Worked example

50 m³/h of water at 20 °C (998 kg/m³):

ṁ = 998 × 50 = **49,900 kg/h** = 49.9 t/h = 13.86 kg/s

## Density changes with temperature

Water is 998 kg/m³ at 20 °C but about 965 kg/m³ at 90 °C. A volumetric meter reading the same m³/h at 90 °C represents about 3% less mass. For oils and fuels the effect is larger — around 0.07–0.1% per °C.

## Typical densities

| Fluid | kg/m³ |
|---|---|
| Water (20 °C) | 998 |
| Diesel | ~835 |
| Milk | ~1,030 |
| Air (20 °C, 1 atm) | 1.204 |
| Natural gas (standard) | ~0.75 |
