## How to use the coffee ratio calculator

Choose a **brew method** and enter the **water** amount. The grams of ground coffee are calculated. Or enter the coffee you have to find how much water to use.

## Example

500 ml of water at 1:16 → **31 g** of coffee.

1 ml of water weighs about 1 g, so ratios work with either.

## Ratios by brew method

| Method | Typical ratio (coffee : water) | Grind |
|---|---|---|
| Espresso | 1 : 2 (by weight of drink) | Very fine |
| AeroPress | 1 : 12–16 | Fine to medium |
| Pour-over (V60, Chemex) | 1 : 15–17 | Medium-fine to medium |
| Drip machine | 1 : 16–18 | Medium |
| French press | 1 : 12–15 | Coarse |
| Cold brew (concentrate) | 1 : 4–8 | Coarse |

## Water temperature

Most brewing guides recommend 90–96 °C, just off the boil. Too hot extracts bitterness; too cool gives a sour, weak cup.

## Adjusting taste

| Taste | Try |
|---|---|
| Weak, watery | More coffee (lower ratio) |
| Too strong | More water |
| Sour | Finer grind or hotter water |
| Bitter | Coarser grind or shorter brew time |

## Scoops

A standard coffee scoop holds about 2 tablespoons, roughly 10 g of ground coffee. Weighing is more consistent.

## Worked example with the defaults

At the default pour-over ratio of 1:16, 500 mL of water needs 500 ÷ 16 = **31.25 g** of coffee, which most people round to 31 g. The grounds hold back about 2 g of water per gram, so the cup ends up at roughly 500 − 2 × 31.25 = **438 mL** of coffee. If a refractometer reads 1.35% total dissolved solids, the extraction yield is 0.0135 × 438 ÷ 31.25 = **18.9%**, inside the 18% to 22% range the Specialty Coffee Association treats as balanced.

For espresso the 1:2 ratio describes coffee in to drink out, for example 18 g of coffee giving 36 g of espresso, not 1:2 with water poured in.

## Cold brew and dilution

Cold brew is made strong and diluted later. 1,000 mL of water at 1:8 takes 125 g of coarse coffee; after steeping 12 to 18 hours the grounds keep about 2.5 g of water per gram, leaving roughly 688 mL of concentrate. Mixing it 1:1 with water or milk brings the cup back to about a 1:16 strength.

## Getting the ratio right in practice

Weigh both coffee and water on a kitchen scale reading to 0.1 g and tare between steps. Grind just before brewing, because ground coffee loses aroma within minutes. Change one thing at a time: keep the ratio fixed while you adjust grind, and only then move the ratio a step, say from 1:16 to 1:15, if the cup is still thin.

## Common mistakes

- **Scooping instead of weighing.** A level tablespoon of a light, coarse grind can weigh 30% less than one of a dark, fine grind.
- **Counting the water that boils off or stays in the kettle.** Weigh the water that actually goes onto the coffee.
- **Fixing sourness with more coffee.** Sour usually means under-extraction; grind finer or brew longer before changing the ratio.
- **Reading the ratio by cup volume.** Machines rate a cup at 125 to 150 mL, so a 10 cup carafe is not 10 mugs.
- **Applying a pour-over ratio to cold brew concentrate.** Brewing at 1:16 cold gives a weak, flat result.
