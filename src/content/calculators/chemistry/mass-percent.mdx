## How to use the mass percent concentration calculator

Enter the **solute** and **solvent** masses to get the mass percent. To make a solution of a given strength, enter the percent and total mass — the masses of solute and solvent are calculated.

## Mass percent concentration formula

<p class="formula">mass % = m<sub>solute</sub> ÷ (m<sub>solute</sub> + m<sub>solvent</sub>) × 100</p>

## Worked example

25 g of salt dissolved in 475 g of water:

- Total solution mass = 25 + 475 = **500 g**
- Mass fraction = 25 ÷ 500 = 0.05
- Mass percent = 0.05 × 100 = **5%**

## Mass percent, molarity and ppm

| Concentration | What it means |
|---|---|
| 1% (w/w) | 1 g solute in 100 g solution |
| 1% (w/v) | 1 g solute in 100 mL solution |
| 1% (v/v) | 1 mL solute in 100 mL solution |
| 1 ppm | 1 mg per kg (or about 1 mg/L in water) |
| 10,000 ppm | 1% (w/w) |

Always state which kind of percent you mean.

## Converting mass percent to molarity

<p class="formula">Molarity = (w × ρ × 1,000) ÷ M</p>

with w the mass fraction, ρ the solution density in g/mL and M the molar mass. Concentrated hydrochloric acid at 37% (ρ = 1.19 g/mL, M = 36.46): 0.37 × 1.19 × 1000 ÷ 36.46 = **12.1 M**.

## Common concentrated reagents

| Reagent | Mass percent | Density (g/mL) | Molarity (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrochloric acid | 37% | 1.19 | 12.1 M |
| Sulphuric acid | 98% | 1.84 | 18.4 M |
| Nitric acid | 70% | 1.42 | 15.8 M |
| Ammonia solution | 28% | 0.90 | 14.8 M |

## Finding solute needed

To make 500 g of a 5% salt solution: solute = 0.05 × 500 = 25 g, solvent = 475 g.

## The 5% example in other units

The 5% salt solution can be restated without new measurements, except for molarity, which needs a density:

- Moles of NaCl = 25 ÷ 58.443 = 0.4278 mol
- Molality = 0.4278 mol ÷ 0.475 kg water = **0.9006 mol/kg**
- Mole fraction of NaCl = 0.4278 ÷ (0.4278 + 475 ÷ 18.015) = **0.01596**
- In ppm by mass: 0.05 × 10⁶ = **50,000 ppm**
- With a measured density of 1.0340 g/mL at 20 °C, the 500 g occupy 483.6 mL, so the molarity is **0.8846 mol/L** and the strength is 5.17% w/v

## Mixing and diluting by mass

Solute mass is conserved, so mass percent mixes by a mass weighted average. Combining 200 g of 10% solution with 300 g of 2% solution gives (200 × 0.1 + 300 × 0.02) ÷ 500 = **5.2%**, not the simple average of 6%.

To dilute 100 g of 37% hydrochloric acid to 10%, the final mass must be 100 × 0.37 ÷ 0.1 = 370 g, so add **270 g of water**. Weigh the water first and add the acid to it slowly, never the other way round.

## Salt in water by mass percent

Solute per kilogram of water and molality for sodium chloride (M = 58.443 g/mol). These figures follow from the mass fraction alone and need no density.

| Mass percent | ppm | NaCl per kg water | Molality (mol/kg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5% | 5,000 | 5.0 g | 0.086 |
| 1% | 10,000 | 10.1 g | 0.173 |
| 2% | 20,000 | 20.4 g | 0.349 |
| 5% | 50,000 | 52.6 g | 0.901 |
| 10% | 100,000 | 111.1 g | 1.901 |
| 20% | 200,000 | 250.0 g | 4.278 |
| 26% | 260,000 | 351.4 g | 6.012 |

Sodium chloride saturates at about 26.4% by mass at 20 °C, so stronger brines cannot be made at room temperature.

## Weighing hydrated salts

Many solids are sold as hydrates, and the water of crystallisation counts as solvent. To make 500 g of a 2% copper(II) sulfate solution from CuSO₄·5H₂O, note that the anhydrous salt is 159.60 ÷ 249.68 = 63.92% of the hydrate mass. You need 10 g of CuSO₄, which means weighing **15.64 g of the pentahydrate** and making up to 500 g with 484.36 g of water. In practice, tare the beaker on a balance, add the solid, then add solvent until the total reaches the target mass.

## Common mistakes

- **Dividing by the solvent mass.** 25 ÷ 475 = 5.26%, not 5%; the denominator is the whole solution.
- **Averaging percentages when mixing.** Weight each percentage by its solution mass, as in the 5.2% example above.
- **Treating w/w and w/v as equal.** For dense solutions they differ a lot: 37% w/w hydrochloric acid at 1.19 g/mL is 44.0% w/v.
- **Ignoring water of hydration.** Weighing 10 g of CuSO₄·5H₂O supplies only 6.39 g of CuSO₄.
- **Using molarity tables at the wrong temperature.** Mass percent does not change with temperature, but the density used to convert it to molarity does.

## Common uses

- Food labels and recipes (sugar or salt content)
- Cleaning products and disinfectants
- Fertiliser and pesticide mixing
- Lab reagents where weighing is more accurate than measuring volume

For very small concentrations, use [ppm](/chemistry/ppm/) instead.
