## How to use the mass flow rate calculator

Enter the **volumetric flow** and **density** to get mass flow, or enter the mass flow to get volume. Every field has its own units.

## Mass flow rate formula

<p class="formula">ṁ = ρ × Q</p>

## Worked example

50 m³/h of water at 20 °C (998 kg/m³):

ṁ = 998 × 50 = **49,900 kg/h** = 49.9 t/h = 13.86 kg/s

## Gases: actual vs standard flow

For gases, density changes strongly with pressure and temperature, so volumetric flow is often quoted at standard or normal conditions:

- **Nm³/h**: normal cubic metres at 0 °C and 1.01325 bar(a) (common in Europe).
- **Sm³/h**: standard cubic metres, often at 15 °C (check which standard).
- **SCFM**: standard cubic feet per minute, often at 60 °F and 14.696 psia.

Mass flow = standard volume flow × gas density at those standard conditions.

## Coriolis meters

Coriolis meters measure mass flow directly, along with density and temperature, independent of fluid properties. They are widely used for custody transfer, batching and chemical dosing.

## Energy flow

For fuels, multiply mass flow by calorific value to get energy flow. Natural gas at 50 kg/h with a calorific value of about 50 MJ/kg delivers 2,500 MJ/h, roughly **694 kW**.

## Density changes with temperature

Water is 998 kg/m³ at 20 °C but about 965 kg/m³ at 90 °C. A volumetric meter reading the same m³/h at 90 °C represents about 3% less mass. For oils and fuels the effect is larger — around 0.07–0.1% per °C.

## Gas example: normal flow to actual flow

A compressor delivers **500 Nm³/h** of air. Dry air at 0 °C and 1.01325 bar(a) has a density of p ÷ (R T) = 101,325 ÷ (287.05 × 273.15) = 1.292 kg/m³, so the mass flow is **646 kg/h**. In the 7 barg header at 30 °C the density is 801,325 ÷ (287.05 × 303.15) = 9.21 kg/m³, so the same mass occupies only **70.2 m³/h** of actual volume. Pipe velocity and meter sizing use the actual figure; compressor ratings and billing use the normal one.

## Liquid fuel example

A burner needs **2,000 kg/h** of diesel at 835 kg/m³. Enter the mass flow and density and clear the volume: Q = 2,000 ÷ 835 = **2.395 m³/h**, or 39.9 L/min, which is the figure for choosing a gear pump or a positive displacement meter.

## How mass flow is measured

A [Coriolis flow meter](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/coriolis-flow-meter-working-principle/) measures mass directly from the twist of vibrating tubes and also reports density. Most other meters (magnetic, vortex, turbine, differential pressure) measure volume or velocity, and the flow computer multiplies by a density calculated from temperature and pressure or from a densitometer. That density value is where most mass flow errors come from.

## Common mistakes

- **Using 998 kg/m³ for hot water.** At 80 °C water is about 971.8 kg/m³; using the cold value overstates the mass by 2.7% on a volumetric meter.
- **Taking gauge pressure in the gas density.** p ÷ (R T) needs absolute pressure; 7 barg is 8.01 bar(a), not 7.
- **Mixing Nm³ and Sm³.** Normal (0 °C) and standard (15 °C or 60 °F) conditions differ by about 5% in density.
- **Converting specific gravity with 1,000 kg/m³ when the reference is 15 °C water.** SG values for oils are referenced to water at 15 °C or 60 °F (999.0 to 999.1 kg/m³), a small but real offset in custody transfer.
- **Confusing kg/h and kg/s.** A factor of 3,600 error is common when copying results into energy balances that need kg/s.
