## How to use the RC phase shift oscillator calculator

Enter the **resistance** and **capacitance** used in each of the three identical RC sections. The calculator returns the oscillation frequency and the minimum feedback resistor for an inverting op-amp stage. To design for a frequency, clear R or C and enter the frequency.

## RC phase shift oscillator formula

<p class="formula">f = 1 ÷ (2π R C √6) &nbsp;·&nbsp; |A| ≥ 29 → R<sub>f</sub> ≥ 29 R</p>

## Worked example

R = 10 kΩ, C = 10 nF in each section:

- f = 1 ÷ (2π × 10,000 × 10 × 10⁻⁹ × 2.449) = **649.7 Hz**
- R<sub>f</sub> ≥ 29 × 10 kΩ = **290 kΩ** (use 300–330 kΩ)

## Notes

- The formula assumes the ladder is not loaded — drive it from a low impedance and let the last R go to the op-amp virtual ground.
- For a transistor version the √6 factor changes slightly with the loading of the transistor's input.

## Why the gain has to be 29

The table shows the unloaded three section CR ladder (series C, shunt R) with the default 10 kΩ and 10 nF. Only at 649.7 Hz is the phase shift exactly 180°, and there the ladder passes 1/29 of the signal.

| Frequency | Gain | As a fraction | Phase lead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 324.9 Hz | 0.0068 | 1/148.1 | 216.5° |
| 519.8 Hz | 0.0213 | 1/47.0 | 192.7° |
| 649.7 Hz | 0.0345 | 1/29.0 | 180.0° |
| 812.2 Hz | 0.0535 | 1/18.7 | 166.9° |
| 1,299.5 Hz | 0.1173 | 1/8.5 | 139.7° |

## Designing for a target frequency

For a 1 kHz tone with 10 nF capacitors, R = 1 ÷ (2π × 1000 × 10 nF × √6) = 6,497 Ω. The nearest E24 value is 6.8 kΩ, which gives **955.5 Hz**; the feedback resistor must be at least 29 × 6.8 kΩ = 197.2 kΩ, so fit 220 kΩ. If the exact frequency matters, use 1% resistors from the E96 series or trim one resistor.

## Practical build notes

- With ±5% capacitors the default circuit can land anywhere from 619 Hz to 684 Hz, so check the frequency with a counter or oscilloscope.
- The op-amp must have plenty of gain left at the operating frequency. A gain of 29 at 650 Hz needs at least 18,843 Hz of gain bandwidth, and ten times that keeps the phase error small, so a general purpose 1 MHz op-amp is fine at audio frequencies but not at 50 kHz.
- Expect start-up to take several cycles. Set the gain about 5% to 10% above 29 and add back to back diodes in series with a resistor across R<sub>f</sub> to limit the amplitude.
- Take the output from the op-amp output, not from the ladder, and buffer it before driving a load.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the high pass formula for an RC low pass ladder.** With series R and shunt C the frequency is √6 ÷ (2πRC), which is 3,898 Hz for the defaults, not 649.7 Hz.
- **Forgetting that the last R is the amplifier input resistor.** If a separate input resistor is added, the third section is loaded and both frequency and gain change.
- **Setting the gain to exactly 29.** Tolerances and op-amp limits mean it may never start; aim for 30 to 32.
- **Driving the ladder from a high impedance.** The source impedance adds to the first section and lowers the frequency.
- **Choosing very small R values.** Below about 1 kΩ the ladder loads the op-amp output heavily and distortion rises.
