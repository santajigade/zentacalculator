## How to use the cylinder volume calculator

Enter the **radius** and **height** to get the volume and total surface area. Pick any unit for each field — for example radius in cm and volume in litres.

You can also solve backwards: enter the volume you need and either the radius or the height to find the other dimension.

## Cylinder volume formulas

<p class="formula">V = π r² h   S = 2πr(r + h)</p>

- **Volume** is the circle's area (πr²) multiplied by the height.
- **Surface area** is the two circular ends (2πr²) plus the curved side (2πrh).

## Worked example: water tank

A vertical tank has an inside diameter of 1.2 m and is 1.5 m tall.

- Radius = 0.6 m
- V = π × 0.6² × 1.5 = 1.696 m³ = **1,696 litres**

## Horizontal tank: partly filled

For a cylinder lying on its side and filled to depth h, the liquid volume is the segment area times the length L:

<p class="formula">V = L × [r² × acos((r − h) ÷ r) − (r − h) × √(2rh − h²)]</p>

A tank of radius 50 cm and length 200 cm filled to 25 cm holds about **307 L** — less than a quarter of its 1,571 L capacity, because the bottom is narrow.

## Pipe volume

Pipes are long cylinders. For the water in a pipe, use the **inner** diameter:

- 25 mm pipe, 10 m long: π × 0.0125² × 10 = **4.9 L**
- 100 mm pipe, 50 m long: π × 0.05² × 50 = **393 L**

## Surface area for painting or insulation

- **Curved surface only** (open both ends): 2πrh
- **One closed end** (open-top tank): 2πrh + πr²
- **Closed cylinder**: 2πr(r + h)

## Unit tips

- 1 m³ = 1,000 litres
- 1 litre = 1,000 cm³
- 1 US gallon = 3.785 litres
- 1 ft³ = 28.32 litres

## Using diameter instead of radius

Most pipes and tanks are specified by diameter. Divide by two before entering, or use the [circle calculator](/math/circle/) to convert first.
