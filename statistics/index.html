    Describing your data

    Paste a list of numbers into the mean, median and mode calculator for the basic summary, then use standard deviation and quartiles to see the spread. The coefficient of variation compares spread between data sets with different units, and the geometric mean is the right average for growth rates. To see how unusual a single value is, convert it to a z-score or check it against the empirical rule.

    Probability and distributions

    For counting problems, start with combinations and permutations. Then choose the distribution that matches your situation: binomial for a fixed number of yes or no trials, Poisson for events per hour or per metre, normal for measurements, exponential for waiting times, and hypergeometric for sampling without replacement. Bayes' theorem updates a probability with new evidence, which is how medical test results are interpreted.

    Testing and quality

    Before a survey, use the sample size calculator; after it, report the margin of error and a confidence interval. To compare groups, run a one-sample or two-sample t-test and read the p-value. In manufacturing, process capability, six sigma and control charts show whether a process can hold its tolerance, and measurement uncertainty combines error sources for calibration work.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I paste a list of numbers?

    Yes. Data-set calculators accept numbers separated by commas, spaces or new lines.

    Sample or population standard deviation?

    The standard deviation calculator shows both.

    What does a p-value of 0.05 mean?

    If there were really no effect, a result at least as extreme as yours would happen about 5% of the time. It does not mean there is a 95% chance your hypothesis is true.

    Which distribution should I use?

    Binomial for a count of successes in n trials, Poisson for a count of events in a time or space interval, normal for continuous measurements, and exponential for the time between random events.
