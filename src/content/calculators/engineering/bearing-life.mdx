## How to use the bearing life calculator

Choose **ball** or **roller** bearing, then enter the **basic dynamic load rating C** from the catalogue, the **equivalent dynamic load P** acting on the bearing and the **speed**. The calculator returns the L10 life in millions of revolutions and in operating hours. To pick a bearing, enter the required hours and clear C to get the load rating you need.

## Bearing life formula (ISO 281)

<p class="formula">L<sub>10</sub> = (C ÷ P)<sup>p</sup> &nbsp;·&nbsp; L<sub>10h</sub> = 10⁶ ÷ (60 n) × L<sub>10</sub></p>

p = 3 for ball bearings, 10/3 for roller bearings. When both radial and axial loads act, P = X F<sub>r</sub> + Y F<sub>a</sub> with the X and Y factors from the catalogue.

## Example

A deep-groove ball bearing with C = 32.5 kN carries 4 kN at 1,500 rpm:

- L<sub>10</sub> = (32.5 ÷ 4)³ = **536.4 million revolutions**
- L<sub>10h</sub> = 10⁶ ÷ (60 × 1500) × 536.4 = **5,960 hours**

## Notes

- Modern catalogues add a modified life L<sub>nm</sub> = a₁ a<sub>ISO</sub> L₁₀, which accounts for lubrication, contamination and the required reliability.
- Real bearings often fail early from poor lubrication, misalignment or contamination rather than fatigue. On inverter-fed motors, [shaft currents from VFDs](ib:vfd-motor-bearing-currents) can also damage the raceways.

## Life at higher reliability (factor a<sub>1</sub>)

L10 accepts that 1 bearing in 10 fails first. For aerospace, medical or hard-to-reach equipment a higher survival rate is specified, and ISO 281 scales the life by a reliability factor a<sub>1</sub>. For the default 6208-size bearing (C = 32.5 kN, P = 4 kN, 1,500 rpm):

| Reliability | a<sub>1</sub> | Life (million rev) | Life at 1,500 rpm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90% | 1.00 | 536.4 | 5,960 h |
| 95% | 0.64 | 343.3 | 3,814 h |
| 96% | 0.55 | 295.0 | 3,278 h |
| 97% | 0.47 | 252.1 | 2,801 h |
| 98% | 0.37 | 198.5 | 2,205 h |
| 99% | 0.25 | 134.1 | 1,490 h |

Asking for 99% survival instead of 90% cuts the usable life to a quarter, from 5,960 h to 1,490 h.

## Choosing a bearing for a target life

Work backwards from the hours the machine needs. For 20,000 h at 1,500 rpm the bearing must survive 20,000 × 60 × 1,500 ÷ 10⁶ = **1,800 million revolutions**. The required rating is then C = P × L<sub>10</sub><sup>1/p</sup> = 4 × 1,800<sup>1/3</sup> = **48.7 kN**. The default bearing at 32.5 kN falls short, so the designer moves to a wider series or a larger bore, or reduces the load.

## Getting the load right in practice

The load P in the equation is the equivalent dynamic load, not the static weight. Gear meshing, belt pull, unbalance and shock all add to it, so catalogues multiply the calculated load by a service factor, commonly 1.0 to 1.2 for smooth electric motor drives and 1.5 or more for crushers and vibrating screens. Applying a factor of 1.3 to the default case drops the life from 5,960 h to **2,713 h**, because life falls with the cube of load. Speed matters linearly: the same bearing at 3,000 rpm lasts only 2,980 h.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the static rating C<sub>0</sub>.** C<sub>0</sub> limits permanent deformation under a stationary load; the life equation needs the dynamic rating C.
- **Using p = 3 for a roller bearing.** Cylindrical, taper and spherical roller bearings use 10/3, which gives noticeably longer life at the same C/P.
- **Ignoring the axial load.** A thrust component can raise P well above the radial load; use P = X F<sub>r</sub> + Y F<sub>a</sub> with the catalogue factors.
- **Reading L10 as a guaranteed minimum.** One bearing in ten is expected to fail before L10, so stock spares accordingly.
- **Mixing N and kN.** C and P must be in the same unit; a 32.5 kN rating against a 4,000 N load gives a life a billion times too small.
