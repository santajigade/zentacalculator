## How to use the op-amp gain calculator

Choose **non-inverting** or **inverting**, and enter the **feedback resistor**, the **input resistor** and an **input voltage**. You get the gain (as a ratio and in dB) and the ideal output. To pick a resistor for a target gain, enter the gain and press × on Rf or Rin.

## Op-amp gain formulas

| Configuration | Gain |
|---|---|
| Non-inverting | G = 1 + R<sub>f</sub> ÷ R<sub>in</sub> |
| Inverting | G = −R<sub>f</sub> ÷ R<sub>in</sub> |
| Voltage follower | G = 1 |

Input impedance is very high for the non-inverting amplifier and equal to R<sub>in</sub> for the inverting amplifier.

## Worked example

Non-inverting amplifier with R<sub>f</sub> = 100 kΩ, R<sub>in</sub> = 10 kΩ and a 100 mV input:

- Gain = 1 + 100 ÷ 10 = **11**, or 20 log₁₀(11) = **20.83 dB**
- Output = 11 × 100 mV = **1.1 V**

Switch to **inverting** with the same resistors: gain = −100 ÷ 10 = **-10** (20.00 dB) and the output is **-1 V**.

## Resistor pairs for common gains

| Target gain | Non-inverting R<sub>f</sub> / R<sub>in</sub> → actual gain | Inverting R<sub>f</sub> / R<sub>in</sub> → actual gain |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 10 k / 10 k → 2 | 20 k / 10 k → −2 |
| 5 | 40.2 k / 10 k → 5.02 | 49.9 k / 10 k → −4.99 |
| 10 | 90.9 k / 10 k → 10.09 | 100 k / 10 k → −10 |
| 11 | 100 k / 10 k → 11 | 110 k / 10 k → −11 |
| 101 | 100 k / 1 k → 101 | 100 k / 990 Ω → −101 |

Standard E96 (1%) values get within about 1% of most targets; use 0.1% resistors when the gain must be precise.

## Bandwidth

Every op-amp has a gain-bandwidth product (GBW): the closed-loop bandwidth is roughly GBW ÷ noise gain, where the noise gain is 1 + R<sub>f</sub>/R<sub>in</sub> for **both** configurations. An inverting amplifier with a gain of −1 therefore has the bandwidth of a gain-of-2 stage.

| Noise gain | Bandwidth, GBW = 1 MHz | Bandwidth, GBW = 10 MHz |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1,000.0 kHz | 10,000.0 kHz |
| 2 | 500.0 kHz | 5,000.0 kHz |
| 10 | 100.0 kHz | 1,000.0 kHz |
| 11 | 90.9 kHz | 909.1 kHz |
| 100 | 10.0 kHz | 100.0 kHz |
| 1000 | 1.0 kHz | 10.0 kHz |

## Instrumentation amplifiers

For bridge sensors and thermocouples, use an instrumentation amplifier, which rejects common-mode voltage and sets gain with a single resistor.

## Practical notes

- Keep resistors roughly 1 kΩ–1 MΩ: low values load the op-amp output and the source, high values add noise and offset from input bias current.
- The inverting amplifier's input impedance is only R<sub>in</sub> — buffer high-impedance sensors or use the non-inverting form.
- Check the output swing against the rails and the slew rate (a 10 V peak at 20 kHz needs about 1.3 V/µs).
