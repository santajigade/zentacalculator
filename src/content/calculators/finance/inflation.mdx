## How to use this calculator

Enter an **amount**, the **inflation rate** and the **number of years**. You get two views:

- **Future cost** — what the same basket of goods will cost.
- **Purchasing power** — what today's amount will be worth in today's terms.

## Formulas

<p class="formula">Future cost = C × (1 + inflation)<sup>t</sup><br/>Purchasing power = C ÷ (1 + inflation)<sup>t</sup></p>

## Worked example

Something costing 100 today, with 6% inflation for 10 years:

- Future cost = 100 × 1.06¹⁰ = **179.08**
- 100 in cash will buy what **55.84** buys today

## Inflation over 10 years

| Inflation | Future cost of 100 | Value of 100 cash |
|---|---|---|
| 2% | 121.90 | 82.03 |
| 4% | 148.02 | 67.56 |
| 6% | 179.08 | 55.84 |
| 8% | 215.89 | 46.32 |

## Why it matters

- **Savings** must earn more than inflation to grow in real terms.
- **Retirement plans** must allow for much higher expenses in the future — see the [retirement corpus calculator](/finance/retirement-corpus/).
- **Salary increases** below inflation are a pay cut in real terms.
