## How to use the battery charging time calculator

Enter the **battery capacity**, the **charging current**, the **starting** and **target state of charge**, and choose the **battery type** for charging efficiency. You get the charge needed in Ah, the estimated time and the C-rate.

## Battery charging time formula

<p class="formula">t (h) = C × (SoC<sub>target</sub> − SoC<sub>start</sub>) ÷ (I × η)</p>

## Charging time for a 150 Ah lead-acid battery (20% → 100%)

| Charging current | C-rate | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 5 A | 0.03C | 30.0 h |
| 10 A | 0.07C | 15.0 h |
| 15 A | 0.10C | 10.0 h |
| 20 A | 0.13C | 7.5 h |
| 30 A | 0.20C | 5.0 h |

Add 1–3 hours for the absorption stage of lead-acid batteries.

## Recommended charging currents

| Battery | Typical charge rate |
|---|---|
| Flooded / tubular lead-acid (inverter batteries) | C/10 – C/8 |
| AGM / gel lead-acid | C/5 – C/10 |
| LiFePO4 | 0.2C – 1C |
| Li-ion (phone, laptop) | 0.5C – 1C, fast charge up to 2C+ |

## Charging stages (lead-acid)

1. **Bulk** — constant current until about 80% charge.
2. **Absorption** — constant voltage (about 14.4–14.8 V for 12 V) while current tapers.
3. **Float** — lower voltage (about 13.5–13.8 V) to maintain full charge.

## Worked example

The defaults describe a 150 Ah tubular inverter battery at 20% charge on a 15 A charger:

- Charge needed = 150 × (100% − 20%) = **120 Ah**
- Time = 120 ÷ (15 × 0.8) = **10 h**
- C-rate = 15 ÷ 150 = **0.1C**

To get the same battery back to full in an 8 hour night, the charger must supply 120 ÷ (8 × 0.8) = **18.8 A**, so a 20 A charger is the practical choice.

## The same charge on different chemistries

120 Ah into a battery at 15 A:

| Battery type | Efficiency | Time | Ah drawn from charger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flooded / tubular lead-acid | 80% | 10.00 h | 150.0 Ah |
| AGM / gel lead-acid | 85% | 9.41 h | 141.2 Ah |
| LiFePO4 / Li-ion | 95% | 8.42 h | 126.3 Ah |

## A lithium example

A 100 Ah LiFePO4 battery taken from 10% to 90% at 20 A needs 80 Ah and takes 80 ÷ (20 × 0.95) = **4.21 h** at 0.2C. Lithium cells stay in constant current mode until about 95%, so the estimate is close to what you will see, while lead-acid needs the extra absorption time noted above.

## Estimating the starting charge

Measure the open circuit voltage after the battery has rested for a few hours with no load or charger. For a 12 V flooded lead-acid battery, about 12.7 V is full, 12.4 V is about 75%, 12.2 V about 50% and 12.0 V about 25%. LiFePO4 voltage is very flat between 20% and 90%, so its state of charge is better read from a coulomb counting battery monitor than from voltage.

## Energy from the wall

Charging 120 Ah at an efficiency of 0.8 at an average 13.8 V through an 85% efficient charger takes about **2.44 kWh** from the mains, worth knowing when the charger runs from a generator.

## Common mistakes

- **Using rated capacity when the battery is aged.** A battery at 80% of its original capacity holds 120 Ah, and the charge needed falls with it.
- **Taking the charger's label current as the actual current.** Many chargers fold back when hot or when the battery voltage rises; measure with a clamp meter.
- **Charging lead-acid at too high a C-rate.** Flooded cells above about 0.2C gas heavily and heat up, which shortens life.
- **Ignoring loads connected during charging.** An inverter feeding 3 A of load leaves only 12 A for the battery, stretching 10 h to 12.5 h.
- **Stopping at the bulk stage.** Lead-acid left at 80% to 90% sulphates over time; let the absorption stage finish regularly.
