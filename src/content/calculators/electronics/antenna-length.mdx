## How to use the antenna length calculator

Enter the **operating frequency** and a **velocity (end-effect) factor** — 0.95 suits a thin bare-wire dipole. The calculator gives the free-space wavelength, the total half-wave dipole length, the length of each dipole leg and the quarter-wave whip or monopole length.

## Antenna length formulas

<p class="formula">λ = c ÷ f &nbsp;·&nbsp; L<sub>dipole</sub> = k × λ ÷ 2 &nbsp;·&nbsp; L<sub>monopole</sub> = k × λ ÷ 4</p>

c = 299,792,458 m/s. With k = 0.95 the dipole formula becomes L (m) = 142.5 ÷ f (MHz), or L (ft) ≈ 468 ÷ f (MHz).

## Example

A dipole for 433.92 MHz with k = 0.95:

- λ = 299,792,458 ÷ 433.92 × 10⁶ = **69.09 cm**
- Half-wave dipole = 0.5 × 69.09 × 0.95 = **32.82 cm** (16.41 cm per leg)
- Quarter-wave whip = **16.41 cm**

## Notes

- Cut slightly long and trim — it is easier to shorten an antenna than to lengthen it.
- PCB trace antennas and helical or ceramic chip antennas follow their own design notes, not these formulas.
- Estimate the range of the link with the [free-space path loss calculator](/electronics/free-space-path-loss/).

## Second example: trimming to resonance

Cut the default 433.92 MHz dipole to 32.82 cm and sweep it with an antenna analyser. Suppose the lowest SWR appears at 420 MHz, so the antenna is electrically long. Length scales inversely with frequency:

- New length = 32.82 × 420 ÷ 433.92 = **31.76 cm**
- Remove **1.05 cm** in total, about 0.53 cm from each leg

Trim in two or three steps rather than all at once, because the first cut also changes how the feed line and nearby objects interact with the antenna. Insulated wire slows the wave slightly; with k = 0.92 the starting length is 31.78 cm.

## Reading SWR from the analyser

| VSWR | Reflection coefficient | Return loss | Power reflected | Mismatch loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | 0.048 | 26.4 dB | 0.2% | 0.01 dB |
| 1.5 | 0.200 | 14.0 dB | 4.0% | 0.18 dB |
| 2.0 | 0.333 | 9.5 dB | 11.1% | 0.51 dB |
| 3.0 | 0.500 | 6.0 dB | 25.0% | 1.25 dB |
| 5.0 | 0.667 | 3.5 dB | 44.4% | 2.55 dB |

Below 1.5:1 only 4% of the power comes back, so chasing a perfect 1.0 is rarely worth the effort. A centre fed half-wave dipole in free space presents about 73 Ω, and a quarter-wave monopole over a good ground plane about 36 Ω; drooping ground-plane radials downward to about 45° raises the monopole toward 50 Ω.

## Practical placement

Mount the antenna clear of metal and away from the circuit board's noisy sections. A plastic enclosure, a hand or a wall within a few centimetres lowers the resonant frequency, so do the final trim with the antenna installed in its housing.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the coax velocity factor.** The 0.66 or 0.82 figure on a cable data sheet applies to the feed line, not the radiating wire.
- **Confusing total length with leg length.** At 433.92 MHz each leg is 16.41 cm; making each leg 32.82 cm gives a full wave dipole with a high feed impedance.
- **Running a quarter-wave whip without a ground plane.** The feed line becomes the missing half and the pattern and match become unpredictable.
- **Trimming in free air, then fitting the case.** The enclosure shifts resonance by several megahertz at UHF.
- **Coiling surplus wire.** A coiled length acts as an inductor and detunes the antenna; cut it instead.
