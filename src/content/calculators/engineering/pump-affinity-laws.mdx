## How to use the pump affinity laws calculator

Enter the pump's known duty point — **flow, head and shaft power** — at the original speed and impeller diameter, then enter the **new speed** and/or **new diameter**. Keep D₂ = D₁ for a pure speed change, or N₂ = N₁ for an impeller trim.

## Pump affinity laws

<p class="formula">Q₂ = Q₁ (N₂/N₁)(D₂/D₁) &nbsp;·&nbsp; H₂ = H₁ (N₂/N₁)²(D₂/D₁)² &nbsp;·&nbsp; P₂ = P₁ (N₂/N₁)³(D₂/D₁)³</p>

## Worked example

A pump delivers 100 m³/h at 50 m head and absorbs 18.5 kW at 1,450 rpm. A VFD slows it to 1,160 rpm (ratio 0.80):

- Q₂ = 100 × 0.80 = **80.0 m³/h**
- H₂ = 50 × 0.80² = **32.0 m**
- P₂ = 18.5 × 0.80³ = **9.47 kW** — a 49% power reduction

## Notes

- Efficiency is assumed constant — real savings are slightly lower because motor and VFD losses rise at low load.
- Check that the reduced head still overcomes the static head of the system.
- For fans and blowers the same laws apply; see the [fan affinity laws calculator](/engineering/fan-affinity-laws/).

## Impeller trim example

At constant speed the diameter ratio works the same way. Trimming the 250 mm impeller to 235 mm (ratio 0.94):

- Flow = 100 × 0.94 = **94.0 m³/h**
- Head = 50 × 0.94² = **44.2 m**
- Power = 18.5 × 0.94³ = **15.37 kW**

To reach a target head of 45 m at the same flow ratio, the diameter must be 250 × √(45 ÷ 50) = **237.2 mm**. Trim a little less than calculated and test, because metal cannot be put back.

## Energy saving with a VFD

In the speed example, power falls from 18.5 kW to 9.47 kW. Over 6,000 running hours a year that is (18.5 − 9.47) × 6,000 = **54,168 kWh** of shaft energy, before motor and VFD losses. The saving is this large only when the system is mostly friction head. If the system has, say, 20 m of static head, the pump cannot deliver any flow once its shutoff head falls below 20 m, and the real duty point must be found where the scaled pump curve crosses the system curve H = H<sub>static</sub> + kQ².

## Worked example with 20 m of static head

To see how static head changes the answer, model the pump curve at full speed as H = 65 − 0.0015 Q² (shutoff head 65 m, passing through 100 m³/h at 50 m). At speed ratio r the curve scales to H = 65 r² − 0.0015 Q². The system needs 20 m of static lift plus friction, H = 20 + 0.003 Q², which also passes through the original duty. The duty point is where the two curves cross: Q = √((65 r² − 20) ÷ 0.0045).

At 80% speed this gives Q = **69.3 m³/h** at **34.4 m**, not the 80 m³/h and 32 m the affinity laws predict. The original duty has an efficiency of ρ g Q H ÷ P = 13.62 ÷ 18.5 = 73.6%; holding that efficiency, the shaft power is about **8.82 kW**.

| Speed | Affinity flow | Real flow | Real head | Estimated power | Affinity power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100% (1450 rpm) | 100.0 m³/h | 100.0 m³/h | 50.0 m | 18.50 kW | 18.50 kW |
| 90% (1305 rpm) | 90.0 m³/h | 85.2 m³/h | 41.8 m | 13.16 kW | 13.49 kW |
| 80% (1160 rpm) | 80.0 m³/h | 69.3 m³/h | 34.4 m | 8.82 kW | 9.47 kW |

| 60% (870 rpm) | 60.0 m³/h | 27.5 m³/h | 22.3 m | 2.26 kW | 4.00 kW |

Below r = √(20 ÷ 65) = 0.555, or 804 rpm, the shutoff head is lower than the static lift and the pump delivers nothing while still absorbing power. Set the VFD minimum speed well above this point.

## Common mistakes

- **Applying the laws to a system with high static head.** The new duty point is not on the affinity parabola; flow falls faster than speed and savings are smaller.
- **Slowing below the minimum continuous flow.** Low flow causes recirculation, heating and vibration; check the pump's minimum flow and the motor's cooling at low speed.
- **Trimming too far.** Beyond about 10 to 15% of diameter, efficiency drops and the laws overpredict flow and head.
- **Forgetting NPSH.** Speeding a pump up raises the NPSH it requires roughly with the square of speed, which can start cavitation.
- **Using power at a different duty.** P₁ must be the shaft power at the same operating point as Q₁ and H₁, not the motor rating.
