## How to use this calculator

Enter any **two** sides of a right-angled triangle. The third side is calculated straight away. The two shorter sides (a and b) meet at the right angle; the hypotenuse (c) is the longest side, opposite the right angle.

Each side has its own unit, so you can enter a in metres and b in feet if you need to.

## The formula

<p class="formula">a² + b² = c²</p>

- **Hypotenuse:** c = √(a² + b²)
- **Missing side:** a = √(c² − b²)

## Worked example

A ladder reaches 6 m up a wall with its foot 2.5 m from the wall. How long is the ladder?

c = √(6² + 2.5²) = √(36 + 6.25) = √42.25 = **6.5 m**

## Common Pythagorean triples

| a | b | c |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 4 | 5 |
| 5 | 12 | 13 |
| 8 | 15 | 17 |
| 7 | 24 | 25 |
| 20 | 21 | 29 |

Any multiple of a triple also works — 6-8-10 and 30-40-50 are both right triangles.

## Practical uses

- **Checking a right angle on site.** Builders mark 3 units on one side and 4 on the other; if the diagonal is exactly 5, the corner is square.
- **Screen sizes.** A TV's size is its diagonal. A screen 120 cm wide and 67.5 cm tall has a diagonal of about 137.7 cm (54 inches).
- **Distance on a map.** Moving 3 km east and 4 km north puts you 5 km from where you started in a straight line.
