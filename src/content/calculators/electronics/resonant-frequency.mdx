## How to use the LC resonant frequency calculator

Enter the **inductance** in microhenries and the **capacitance**. The calculator gives the resonant frequency and the angular frequency. Add the **series resistance** of the coil and capacitor to get the reactance at resonance, the Q factor and the bandwidth. To design for a target frequency, enter the frequency and one component and press × on the other.

## LC resonant frequency formulas

<p class="formula">f₀ = 1 ÷ (2π√(LC)) &nbsp;·&nbsp; ω₀ = 2πf₀</p>

<p class="formula">X<sub>L</sub> = X<sub>C</sub> = ω₀L = √(L/C) &nbsp;·&nbsp; Q = X ÷ R &nbsp;·&nbsp; BW = f₀ ÷ Q</p>

## Worked example

L = 10 µH, C = 100 nF and a total series resistance of 1 Ω:

- f₀ = 1 ÷ (2π√(10 × 10⁻⁶ × 1e-07)) = **159,155 Hz** (159.15 kHz), ω₀ = **1,000,000 rad/s**
- Reactance at resonance X = ω₀L = **10.000 Ω** (equal to √(L/C))
- Q = 10.000 ÷ 1 = **10.00**, bandwidth = 159,155 ÷ 10.00 = **15,915 Hz**

## Series vs parallel resonance

| | Series LC | Parallel LC (tank) |
|---|---|---|
| Impedance at f₀ | Minimum (≈ R) | Maximum (≈ Q × X) |
| Current at f₀ | Maximum | Minimum from source |
| Use | Band-pass in series path, notch filters | Oscillators, tuned amplifiers |

## Practical notes

- **Stray capacitance** of the coil, PCB and transistor adds to C, so real circuits resonate lower than calculated — at high frequencies leave room to trim.
- Coil resistance rises with frequency (skin effect), so Q measured at the working frequency is lower than a DC-resistance estimate suggests.
- Capacitors for tuned circuits should be stable types: C0G/NP0 ceramic, silver mica or polypropylene. X7R and Y5V ceramics drift with temperature and voltage.
- For a second-order low-pass filter (inductor in series, capacitor across a load), use the [LC filter calculator](/electronics/lc-filter/) to see the attenuation and peaking.

## Where the default circuit's bandwidth edges fall

For the default 10 µH, 100 nF and 1 Ω, the bandwidth of 15,915 Hz is not split exactly evenly around 159,155 Hz. The exact −3 dB frequencies of a series RLC circuit are f₀ × (√(1 + 1/4Q²) ∓ 1/2Q), which gives **151,396 Hz** and **167,312 Hz**. Their geometric mean is f₀, which is why resonance curves look symmetrical on a logarithmic frequency axis.

Driven by 1 V at resonance, the current is 1 V ÷ 1 Ω = 1 A and the voltage across the capacitor and the inductor is Q times the drive, **10 V** each, in antiphase so they cancel. Used as a parallel tank with the same coil, the impedance at resonance is about Q × X = **100 Ω**.

## Second example: tuning the AM broadcast band

A 240 µH ferrite rod coil covering 530 to 1,700 kHz needs **375.7 pF** at the bottom of the band and **36.5 pF** at the top. Because f varies as 1/√C, a frequency ratio of 3.21 needs a capacitance ratio of 10.3. A classic variable capacitor of about 10 to 365 pF just covers it once wiring and coil stray capacitance are added.

## Measuring resonance on the bench

Drive the circuit from a signal generator through a small coupling capacitor or a high resistance, watch the voltage across the capacitor on an oscilloscope, and sweep for the peak. Note the two frequencies where the amplitude falls to 0.707 of the peak; their difference is the bandwidth and Q = f₀ ÷ BW. A vector network analyser or an LCR meter that sweeps frequency does the same job automatically.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving out the 2π.** 1 ÷ √(LC) is the angular frequency, 1,000,000 rad/s here; quoting it as hertz is 6.28 times too high.
- **Driving it straight from a 50 Ω generator.** The source resistance adds to R, so Q falls from 10 to 0.20 and the peak almost disappears; couple in loosely.
- **Rating the capacitor for the supply voltage.** At resonance it sees Q times the drive, 10 V from 1 V in this example.
- **Ignoring the scope probe.** A ×10 probe adds roughly 10 to 15 pF, enough to pull a small-capacitance tank noticeably lower in frequency.
- **Using series-circuit formulas for a parallel tank fed by a resistive source.** The source and load resistances also damp a parallel circuit and lower its Q.
