## How to use the percentage change calculator

Enter the **original** and **new** values to get the percentage change and the difference. You can also work backwards: enter the original value and a percentage change (for example −20%) to find the new value.

## Percentage change formula

<p class="formula">percentage change = (new − original) ÷ original × 100</p>

Example: a price rises from 80 to 92.

- Difference = 92 − 80 = 12
- Percentage change = 12 ÷ 80 = 0.15 = **15% increase**

## Increase or decrease?

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Positive (for example +15%) | The value increased |
| Zero | No change |
| Negative (for example −13%) | The value decreased |

## Percentage change is not symmetric

Going up 15% and then down 15% does not bring you back to where you started:

- 80 + 15% = 92
- 92 − 15% = 78.2

That is because each change is measured against a different base. To undo a 15% rise you need a fall of 13.04%.

## Percentage change vs percentage points

If an interest rate moves from 4% to 5%, it rose by **1 percentage point**, which is a **25% increase** in the rate itself. News reports often mix these up — this calculator gives the percentage change.

## Percentage change examples

| From | To | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | 75 | +50.0% |
| 75 | 50 | -33.3% |
| 100 | 250 | +150.0% |
| 200 | 150 | -25.0% |
| 1200 | 1380 | +15.0% |
| 40 | 10 | -75.0% |

## Percentage difference (no “original” value)

When neither value is the starting point — comparing two shops' prices, say — use percentage difference, which divides by the average:

<p class="formula">% difference = |a − b| ÷ ((a + b) ÷ 2) × 100</p>

For 80 and 92 that is 12 ÷ 86 × 100 = **13.95%**.

## Reversing a percentage change

To undo a change, you need a different percentage:

| Change | To get back you need |
|---|---|
| +10% | −9.09% |
| +25% | −20% |
| +50% | −33.3% |
| −20% | +25% |
| −50% | +100% |

This is why a stock that falls 50% must double to recover.

## Over several years

For growth over several years, the yearly average is not the total change divided by the number of years. Use the [CAGR calculator](/finance/cagr/) for the compound annual rate.
