## How to use the Schmitt trigger calculator

Enter the **reference voltage**, the two resistors of the positive-feedback divider and the comparator's **output high and low levels**. The signal drives the inverting input. The calculator returns the two switching points, the hysteresis and the centre of the band. To design, choose the hysteresis you need and adjust R₂/R₁.

## Schmitt trigger formulas

<p class="formula">V<sub>TH</sub> = (V<sub>ref</sub> R₂ + V<sub>OH</sub> R₁) / (R₁ + R₂) &nbsp;·&nbsp; V<sub>TL</sub> = (V<sub>ref</sub> R₂ + V<sub>OL</sub> R₁) / (R₁ + R₂)</p>

<p class="formula">V<sub>H</sub> = V<sub>TH</sub> − V<sub>TL</sub> = (V<sub>OH</sub> − V<sub>OL</sub>) R₁ / (R₁ + R₂)</p>

## Worked example

V<sub>ref</sub> = 2.5 V, R₁ = 10 kΩ, R₂ = 100 kΩ, rail-to-rail output 0–5 V:

- V<sub>TH</sub> = (2.5 × 100k + 5 × 10k) ÷ 110k = **2.7273 V**
- V<sub>TL</sub> = (2.5 × 100k) ÷ 110k = **2.2727 V**
- Hysteresis = **454.5 mV**, centred on 2.500 V

## Notes

- The output goes **low** when the input rises above V<sub>TH</sub> and **high** when it falls below V<sub>TL</sub> (inverting action).
- Positive feedback shifts the band centre above V<sub>ref</sub>; to centre it, lower V<sub>ref</sub> or add a resistor from the + input to ground.

## Design example: choosing R₁ and R₂

Target: **100 mV** of hysteresis with a 0 to 5 V output and V<sub>ref</sub> = 2.5 V.

1. Hysteresis = (V<sub>OH</sub> − V<sub>OL</sub>) × R₁ ÷ (R₁ + R₂), so R₁ ÷ (R₁ + R₂) = 0.1 ÷ 5 = 0.02 and R₂ = 49 R₁.
2. Pick R₁ = 10 kΩ, so R₂ = 490 kΩ. The nearest standard value is **470 kΩ**.
3. Check: V<sub>TH</sub> = (2.5 × 470 + 5 × 10) ÷ 480 = **2.552 V** and V<sub>TL</sub> = 2.5 × 470 ÷ 480 = **2.448 V**, so the hysteresis is about **104 mV**, centred on 2.5 V.

## Why the band moves away from V<sub>ref</sub>

The band is centred on V<sub>ref</sub> only when V<sub>ref</sub> is halfway between the output levels. With the same resistors, a 0 to 5 V output and V<sub>ref</sub> = 1.2 V, the thresholds become 1.279 V and 1.175 V. The centre is 1.227 V, which is 27 mV higher than the reference. To put the band exactly where you want it, lower V<sub>ref</sub> slightly or solve for it here by entering the threshold you need.

## Common mistakes

- **Ignoring the source resistance of V<sub>ref</sub>.** If the reference comes from a resistor divider, its Thevenin resistance adds to R₁ and increases the hysteresis.
- **Assuming the output swings rail to rail.** Under load many comparators stop short of the rails. Use the datasheet V<sub>OH</sub> and V<sub>OL</sub>.
- **Making the hysteresis smaller than the input offset voltage** plus the noise. The circuit can still chatter.
- **Forgetting the pull-up on open-drain outputs.** It sets V<sub>OH</sub> and sits in series with R₂.
