## How to use the calories from macros calculator

Enter grams of **protein**, **carbohydrate**, **fat** and **alcohol**. Total calories and the share from fat are calculated.

## Calories from macros formula

<p class="formula">kcal = 4P + 4C + 9F + 7A</p>

## Worked example with the default values

A meal with **25 g protein**, **40 g carbohydrate**, **15 g fat** and no alcohol:

- Protein: 25 × 4 = 100 kcal
- Carbohydrate: 40 × 4 = 160 kcal
- Fat: 15 × 9 = 135 kcal
- Total = 100 + 160 + 135 = **395 kcal** (1,653 kJ)
- Calories from fat = 135 ÷ 395 = **34.2%**

The same meal gets 25.3% of its energy from protein and 40.5% from carbohydrate. If 8 of the 40 g of carbohydrate is fibre counted at 2 kcal per gram, the total drops to 379 kcal.

## Energy per gram

| Nutrient | kcal per gram | kJ per gram |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | 4 | 17 |
| Protein | 4 | 17 |
| Fat | 9 | 37 |
| Alcohol | 7 | 29 |
| Fibre | about 2 | 8 |

These Atwater factors are the basis of most food labels.

## Reading a nutrition label

If a snack has 10 g protein, 30 g carbohydrate and 8 g fat per serving:

- Protein: 40 kcal
- Carbohydrate: 120 kcal
- Fat: 72 kcal
- **Total: 232 kcal**, with 31% of calories from fat

Labels may differ slightly because of rounding and fibre.

## Why labels and calculators differ

- Labels often round to the nearest 5 or 10 kcal.
- Fibre provides fewer calories than other carbohydrates.
- Some countries list total carbohydrate including fibre; others list available carbohydrate.

## Kilojoules

Australia, New Zealand and parts of Europe use kJ. Multiply kcal by 4.184 to convert: 232 kcal ≈ 971 kJ.

## Grams for a 500 kcal meal at common splits

The split is the share of calories from protein, carbohydrate and fat. Grams = calories × share ÷ kcal per gram:

| Protein / carbs / fat (%) | Protein | Carbohydrate | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 / 40 / 30 | 38 g | 50 g | 17 g |
| 25 / 50 / 25 | 31 g | 62 g | 14 g |
| 40 / 30 / 30 | 50 g | 38 g | 17 g |
| 20 / 30 / 50 | 25 g | 38 g | 28 g |
| 15 / 55 / 30 | 19 g | 69 g | 17 g |

## Alcohol counts too

Alcohol is not a nutrient but it supplies 7 kcal per gram. A drink with 14 g of alcohol (one US standard drink) and 10 g of sugar provides 7 × 14 + 4 × 10 = **138 kcal**, before any mixer. Enter the alcohol grams to see how much of a meal's energy comes from drinks.

## Practical notes

- Use the **grams per serving** from the label and multiply by the number of servings you actually eat.
- In the EU, labels list carbohydrate without fibre and give fibre separately at 2 kcal per gram; US labels include fibre in total carbohydrate. Subtract fibre when comparing.
- Sugar alcohols such as erythritol provide little or no energy, so a low sugar product can show fewer calories than 4 kcal per gram of total carbohydrate suggests.

## Common mistakes

- **Using 9 kcal per gram for all macros.** Only fat is 9; protein and carbohydrate are 4.
- **Adding sugar on top of carbohydrate.** Sugar is part of total carbohydrate on the label, so counting it twice inflates the total.
- **Mixing cooked and raw weights.** Rice, pasta and meat change weight on cooking, so take grams from the same state as the nutrition data.
- **Expecting an exact match with the label.** Rounding to the nearest 5 or 10 kcal and different fibre factors cause small differences.
- **Reading the percentage as grams.** 34% of calories from fat is not 34% of the food's weight.
