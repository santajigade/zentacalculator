## How to use the concrete mix calculator

Enter the **wet volume** and choose the **mix**. The dry volume, cement bags, sand and coarse aggregate are calculated.

This calculator assumes the sand-to-aggregate ratio is 1 : 2, as in all the listed nominal mixes.

## Example — 1 m³ of M20 (1 : 1.5 : 3)

- Dry volume = 1.54 m³
- Cement = 1.54 ÷ 5.5 = 0.28 m³ = **8.1 bags**
- Sand = 0.28 × 1.5 = **0.42 m³**
- Aggregate = 0.28 × 3 = **0.84 m³**

Nominal mixes follow common Indian practice (IS 456). For structural work, always follow your structural engineer's design.

## Nominal mix ratios by grade

| Grade | Mix (cement : sand : aggregate) | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| M10 | 1 : 3 : 6 | Levelling course, PCC |
| M15 | 1 : 2 : 4 | Floors, pathways |
| M20 | 1 : 1.5 : 3 | RCC slabs, beams, columns (small buildings) |
| M25 | 1 : 1 : 2 | Heavier RCC work |

The number after M is the characteristic compressive strength in N/mm² at 28 days. Above M20, design mixes based on lab tests are recommended.

## Materials for 1 m³ by grade

| Grade | Cement bags (50 kg) | Sand (m³) | Aggregate (m³) |
|---|---|---|---|
| M10 | 4.4 | 0.462 | 0.924 |
| M15 | 6.3 | 0.440 | 0.880 |
| M20 | 8.1 | 0.420 | 0.840 |
| M25 | 11.1 | 0.385 | 0.770 |

Values use the 1.54 dry-volume factor and a cement density of 1,440 kg/m³. Add 2–5% for wastage.

## Water–cement ratio

Strength depends heavily on the water–cement ratio. Typical values are 0.45–0.55 for structural concrete. Adding extra water to make concrete easier to place reduces strength and durability. Use a plasticiser instead.

## Curing

Concrete gains about 70% of its 28-day strength in 7 days if kept moist. Cure by ponding, wet hessian or curing compound for at least 7 days (10–14 days in hot, dry weather).

## Worked example: the default 1 m³ of M20

- Dry volume = 1 × 1.54 = **1.54 m³**, split into 5.5 parts of 0.280 m³
- Cement = 0.280 m³ × 1,440 kg/m³ = 403 kg = **8.06 bags**
- Sand = 0.280 × 1.5 = **0.42 m³** (14.8 cft)
- Aggregate = 0.280 × 3 = **0.84 m³** (29.7 cft)
- Water at a water-cement ratio of 0.5 = 403 × 0.5 = about **202 litres**

## Slab example

A 4 m × 3 m roof slab 125 mm thick holds 1.50 m³ of concrete. In M20 it needs 12.10 bags of cement, 0.630 m³ of sand and 1.260 m³ of 20 mm aggregate. With 5% for spillage and uneven formwork, order **13 bags**, 0.66 m³ (23.4 cft) of sand and 1.32 m³ (46.7 cft) of aggregate.

## Batching on site with a measuring box

One 50 kg bag of cement has a loose volume of about 50 ÷ 1,440 = 34.7 litres, so sites use a wooden box of about 35 litres (for example 300 × 300 × 390 mm) to measure sand and aggregate. For M20 that means 1 bag, 1.5 boxes of sand and 3 boxes of aggregate per batch. Measuring by headpans or shovels instead varies by 20% or more and is the main reason site concrete misses its grade.

If the sand is damp it bulks by 20% to 30%, so a box of wet sand holds less actual sand; add roughly a quarter more or check the bulking with a measuring jar test.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping the dry volume factor.** Ordering 1 m³ of materials for 1 m³ of concrete leaves you about a third short.
- **Adding water for workability.** Each extra 5 litres per bag lowers strength noticeably; use a plasticiser instead.
- **Ignoring bulking of sand.** Wet sand measured by volume gives a lean, harsh mix.
- **Using nominal mixes above M25.** IS 456 expects design mixes for higher grades, proportioned from trial batches.
- **Ordering exact quantities.** Allow 3% to 5% wastage and keep a spare bag for the last batch.
