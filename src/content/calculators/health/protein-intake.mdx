## How to use the protein intake calculator

Enter your **body weight** and choose the option that best matches your **activity or goal**. You get a daily protein target and a per-meal amount for four meals.

## Protein intake formula

<p class="formula">Protein (g/day) = body weight (kg) × g/kg factor</p>

## Worked example

A 70 kg person doing regular strength training (1.6 g/kg):

- Daily protein = 70 × 1.6 = **112 g**
- About **28 g** per meal over four meals

The 0.8 g/kg baseline is the recommended dietary allowance from the US National Academies; higher ranges for active people reflect the International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand.

## Who needs more protein

- **Older adults** (65+): 1.0–1.2 g/kg helps slow age-related muscle loss.
- **People doing strength training**: about 1.6 g/kg is enough for most; benefits level off above that.
- **During weight loss**: 1.6–2.2 g/kg helps keep muscle while eating less.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding**: needs rise. Follow your doctor's advice.

People with kidney disease may need to **limit** protein — ask a doctor or dietitian.

## Plant-based protein

Vegetarians and vegans can meet their needs by mixing sources through the day: lentils, chickpeas, beans, soy, tofu, tempeh, seitan, dairy, nuts and seeds. Combining grains and legumes, such as rice with dal, improves the amino acid balance.

## A day at 120 g of protein

- Breakfast: 3 eggs and a glass of milk — about 26 g
- Lunch: 150 g chicken or 200 g paneer with rice — about 40 g
- Snack: Greek yoghurt and a handful of nuts — about 20 g
- Dinner: 150 g fish or 1.5 cups of cooked lentils with vegetables — about 30 g

## Reading the default result

The defaults are a 70 kg adult (154 lb) who lifts weights, so the factor is 1.6 g/kg: 70 × 1.6 = **112 g a day**, or **28 g** in each of four meals. On a 2,000 kcal diet those 112 g supply 448 kcal, about 22% of energy, well inside the 10% to 35% range the US Dietary Reference Intakes give for adults.

## Spreading protein over the day

Research on muscle protein synthesis suggests roughly 0.4 g/kg per meal, about 28 g for a 70 kg person, eaten three to four times a day. A typical Indian vegetarian day of tea with milk, two rotis with dal, rice with sambar and a small curd bowl often lands near 45 g to 55 g, so adding paneer, soy chunks, extra curd or a second dal is the usual way to close the gap.

## Choosing the right factor

Use your current weight unless you carry a lot of body fat; in that case many dietitians base the target on a goal or lean body weight, otherwise the number comes out very high. An 85 kg person cutting fat at 2.0 g/kg would get 170 g, which is hard to eat and rarely necessary. The lean body mass calculator helps estimate that alternative base weight.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting the weight of the food as protein.** 100 g of chicken holds about 31 g of protein, not 100 g.
- **Using raw and cooked values interchangeably.** Cooking drives off water, so 100 g of cooked lentils has far less protein than 100 g dry.
- **Eating most of it at dinner.** A 10 g breakfast and a 70 g dinner reaches the total but spreads it poorly.
- **Picking the highest factor by default.** 1.6 g/kg covers most people who train; more mainly helps during a calorie deficit.
- **Ignoring medical advice.** People with kidney disease, and pregnant or breastfeeding women, should set their target with a doctor or dietitian.
