## How to use the busbar size calculator

Enter the **rated current**, choose **copper** or **aluminium**, set the **design current density**, the **bar thickness** and the number of **bars per phase**. The calculator returns the cross-section required, the width of each bar and the resistive loss per metre of each phase at 20 °C DC. Round up to the next standard bar size.

## Busbar sizing formulas

<p class="formula">A = I ÷ J &nbsp;·&nbsp; w = A ÷ (n × t) &nbsp;·&nbsp; P/L = I² ρ ÷ A</p>

## Example

1,600 A copper busbar at 1.6 A/mm², 10 mm thick bars, one bar per phase:

- A = 1600 ÷ 1.6 = **1,000 mm²**
- w = 1000 ÷ (1 × 10) = **100 mm** → one 100 × 10 mm bar per phase
- Loss = 1600² × 1.724 × 10⁻⁸ ÷ 0.001 = **44.1 W/m** per phase

## Design notes

- **AC effects**: above about 10 mm thickness the [skin effect](/electrical/skin-depth/) means the centre of a copper bar carries little current at 50 Hz, so use several thinner bars with gaps rather than one thick bar.
- **Multiple bars per phase** do not share current perfectly; derate by about 10–20% for 2–4 bars.
- **Painting** bars matt black improves heat dissipation by radiation; enclosure ventilation and ambient temperature matter as much as cross-section.
- Check the short-circuit withstand: copper needs about A ≥ I<sub>sc</sub> × √t ÷ 143 mm² for a 1 s fault from 90 °C to 250 °C (k ≈ 143).

## Worked example: copper against aluminium for the default 1,600 A board

With the default inputs (1,600 A, copper, 1.6 A/mm², 10 mm bars, one bar per phase) the calculator gives A = 1,600 ÷ 1.6 = **1,000 mm²**, a width of **100 mm** and **44.1 W/m** per phase at 20 °C.

The bars do not run at 20 °C. Copper resistance rises by about 0.393% per kelvin, so at a 90 °C working temperature the loss is 44.1 × (1 + 0.00393 × 70) = **56.3 W/m** per phase, or 169 W for every metre of a three phase run. That heat has to leave the enclosure, which is why ventilation matters.

Switching to aluminium at 0.8 A/mm² needs 1,600 ÷ 0.8 = **2,000 mm²**, for example two 100 × 10 mm bars per phase. Because the section doubles while resistivity rises only by 1.64 times, the loss falls to **36.2 W/m** per phase at 20 °C.

## Short circuit check

The thermal withstand of a bare copper bar is roughly A ≥ I<sub>sc</sub> × √t ÷ k with k ≈ 143. For a 50 kA fault cleared in 1 s, A ≥ 50,000 × √1 ÷ 143 = **350 mm²**, so the 1,000 mm² bar passes the thermal test easily. The mechanical force between phases is usually the harder limit: it rises with the square of the peak fault current and falls with phase spacing, so support spacing must be checked against the maker's tested arrangement.

## Common mistakes

- **Using one thick bar for high AC current.** Beyond about 10 mm the centre of a copper bar carries little current at 50 Hz; two 10 mm bars with a 10 mm gap beat one 20 mm bar.
- **Applying small-bar densities to large bars.** A 20 × 5 mm bar can run near 2 A/mm², but a 200 × 10 mm bar has far less surface per mm² and must run lower.
- **Ignoring the neutral.** Harmonic loads (drives, LED lighting, IT equipment) can push neutral current above phase current, so a half size neutral may overheat.
- **Sizing on 20 °C loss only.** Real bars run 30 to 50 K above a 35 to 40 °C panel ambient, which adds roughly a quarter to the loss.
- **Mixing copper and aluminium joints without treatment.** Bare Cu to Al joints corrode; use bimetallic washers or tin plated bars and torque the bolts to the specified value.
