## How to use the LPG gas cylinder duration calculator

Enter the **gas weight** in the cylinder, **burner hours per day** and the burner's **consumption**. Daily use and the number of days are calculated.

## Example

14.2 kg cylinder, 2 burner-hours a day at 150 g/hour: 300 g/day → about **47 days**.

## How long a cylinder lasts

14.2 kg cylinder:

| Daily use | At 150 g/hour | At 200 g/hour |
|---|---|---|
| 1 h/day | 95 days | 71 days |
| 1.5 h/day | 63 days | 47 days |
| 2 h/day | 47 days | 35 days |
| 3 h/day | 32 days | 24 days |
| 4 h/day | 24 days | 18 days |

## Burner consumption

A typical domestic burner uses about 150–250 g of LPG per hour on high flame. Small burners and simmer settings use much less. Rating plates often list consumption in g/h or kW (1 kW ≈ 75–80 g/h of LPG).

## Saving gas

- Use lids on pots — cooking without a lid uses noticeably more gas.
- Match the pan size to the burner so flames do not lick up the sides.
- Use a pressure cooker for rice, dal and pulses.
- Soak beans and grains before cooking.
- Keep burners clean for a blue flame; a yellow flame wastes gas.

## Safety

Check the hose and regulator regularly for cracks, turn off the regulator when not in use and never test for leaks with a flame — use soapy water.

## Worked example with the default values

The defaults are a **14.2 kg** domestic cylinder, **2 burner hours a day** and a burner that uses **150 g per hour**:

- Gas used per day = 2 × 150 ÷ 1,000 = **0.3 kg** (300 g)
- Days the cylinder lasts = 14.2 ÷ 0.3 = 47.33, shown as **47 days**

Most kitchens run more than one burner. If the big burner runs 1.5 hours a day at 200 g/h and the small one 1 hour at 100 g/h, daily use is 400 g and the same cylinder lasts about **35 days**. Enter the combined grams per hour and 1 hour a day, or work out each burner and add them.

## Burner rating and days per cylinder

Stove makers often state heat input in kW rather than grams per hour. LPG releases roughly 46 MJ per kg, so each kW of heat input burns about 78 g per hour. A full 14.2 kg cylinder holds about 653 MJ, or 181 kWh of heat. At 2 hours a day of full flame:

| Burner heat input | Gas per hour | Gas per day | 14.2 kg lasts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 kW | 78 g/h | 157 g | 91 days |
| 1.5 kW | 117 g/h | 235 g | 60 days |
| 2.0 kW | 157 g/h | 313 g | 45 days |
| 2.5 kW | 196 g/h | 391 g | 36 days |
| 3.0 kW | 235 g/h | 470 g | 30 days |

A commercial 19 kg cylinder feeding a 250 g/h burner for 8 hours a day lasts only about **9.5 days**, which is why eateries keep a spare cylinder connected through a changeover valve.

## Checking how much gas is left

The only reliable way is to weigh the cylinder. The tare (empty) weight is stamped on the collar or handle. For example, a cylinder that weighs 27 kg on a bathroom scale with a stamped tare of 15.5 kg holds 11.5 kg of gas, enough for about 38 more days at 300 g a day. Weighing it again after a week gives your real consumption, which is a better input than any rating plate: enter the grams used per day directly and leave the burner fields as they are.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the full rated consumption for simmering.** A burner on low flame may use a third of its rated gas, so rating plate figures overstate use for slow cooking.
- **Counting burner hours once when two burners run together.** Twenty minutes on two burners is forty burner minutes.
- **Mixing up gross and gas weight.** The 14.2 kg figure is the gas only; a full domestic cylinder weighs far more because of the steel shell.
- **Expecting the same days in winter.** Cold weather slows vaporisation, so the flame weakens near the end and some liquid can stay in the cylinder, especially with butane-rich gas.
- **Ignoring a dirty or mis-set burner.** A yellow, lazy flame burns gas without heating the pan well, so cooking takes longer and daily use rises.
