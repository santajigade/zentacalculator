## How to use the CT burden calculator

Choose the **CT secondary rating**, enter the **one-way lead length** and **cable size**, and the **burden of the connected devices** from their datasheets. The calculator gives the lead loop resistance, lead burden and total burden. Select a CT whose rated burden is at least the total — ideally 25–100% of it, since accuracy is specified within that band.

## CT burden formula

<p class="formula">R<sub>leads</sub> = 2 × L × ρ ÷ A &nbsp;·&nbsp; VA<sub>leads</sub> = I<sub>s</sub>² × R<sub>leads</sub></p>

with copper resistivity ρ = 1.72 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m at 20 °C.

## Standard CT burdens

2.5, 5, 10, 15 and 30 VA. Metering CTs are specified as e.g. **0.5 / 5 VA**; protection CTs as e.g. **5P20 / 15 VA**.

## Safety

Never open-circuit a live CT secondary — short it first with the test block or shorting link.

## Worked example with the default values

A 5 A CT feeds a relay and meter with a combined burden of 2 VA through 50 m of 2.5 mm² copper:

- Lead loop resistance = 2 × 50 × 1.72 × 10⁻⁸ ÷ 2.5 × 10⁻⁶ = **0.688 Ω**
- Lead burden = 5² × 0.688 = **17.2 VA**
- Total burden = 17.2 + 2 = **19.2 VA**

The leads use almost 90% of the total. A 15 VA CT is overloaded, and the options are clear when you rerun the numbers:

| Change | Loop resistance | Total burden |
|---|---|---|
| As designed, 5 A, 2.5 mm² | 0.688 Ω | 19.2 VA |
| 5 A, 4 mm² cable | 0.430 Ω | 12.75 VA |
| 5 A, 6 mm² cable | 0.287 Ω | 9.17 VA |
| 1 A secondary, 2.5 mm² | 0.688 Ω | 2.69 VA |

Switching to a 1 A CT cuts the total to **2.69 VA** without touching the cable, which is why remote panels and long switchyard runs nearly always use 1 A secondaries. Check that the relay input is also set or rated for 1 A.

## Effect on a protection CT

For a 5P20 CT the accuracy limit factor holds only at rated burden. With a secondary winding resistance of 0.3 Ω, a 15 VA rating means 0.60 Ω of external burden, while the default circuit is 0.768 Ω. The effective factor falls to 20 × (0.3 + 0.60) ÷ (0.3 + 0.768) ≈ **16.9**, so the CT saturates at about 17 times rated current instead of 20. For a feeder with high fault levels that can delay an overcurrent relay or make a differential scheme unstable.

## Measuring the burden on site

Short and isolate the CT at its test block, then inject rated secondary current into the burden side with a secondary injection set and read the voltage across it. Burden in VA is V × I: for the default circuit you should read about 3.84 V at 5 A. Alternatively measure the loop resistance with a low resistance ohmmeter and add the device burdens from their datasheets. Leads warm up in a hot panel or cable tray; at 75 °C copper is about 21% higher, which takes the default total to 22.9 VA.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the one way length.** Current goes out on one core and back on another, so the loop is twice the route length.
- **Forgetting the earth fault case with star connected CTs.** Under balanced load the common return carries almost nothing, but for a phase to earth fault the full current flows out and back, so size for 2 × L.
- **Adding device VA quoted at 1 A to a 5 A circuit.** A relay burden of 0.1 VA at 1 A is a resistance of 0.1 Ω, which is 2.5 VA at 5 A; convert to ohms first.
- **Leaving out test blocks, terminals and links.** Each joint adds a few milliohms; on 5 A circuits a dozen joints add noticeable VA.
- **Choosing a CT rated far above the burden for metering.** Metering accuracy is specified from 25% to 100% of rated burden, so a 30 VA CT on a 2 VA meter circuit can read outside its class.
