## How to use the parallel plate capacitor calculator

Enter the **dielectric constant** of the material between the plates (1 for air), the **overlapping plate area**, the **separation** and the **applied voltage**. The calculator returns the capacitance, the charge on each plate, the stored energy and the electric field in the gap. To design for a target value, enter the capacitance and clear the area or the gap.

## Parallel plate capacitor formulas

<p class="formula">C = ε<sub>0</sub> ε<sub>r</sub> A ÷ d &nbsp;·&nbsp; Q = C V &nbsp;·&nbsp; W = ½ C V² &nbsp;·&nbsp; E = V ÷ d</p>

ε<sub>0</sub> = 8.8541878128 × 10⁻¹² F/m.

## Example

Two 100 cm² plates 1 mm apart in air, charged to 100 V:

- C = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² × 1 × 0.01 ÷ 0.001 = **88.54 pF**
- Q = 88.54 pF × 100 V = **8.854 nC**
- W = ½ × 88.54 pF × 100² = **0.4427 µJ**
- E = 100 V ÷ 1 mm = **0.1 kV/mm** — well below air's breakdown of about 3 kV/mm

## Capacitance with different dielectrics

Same plates as the example (100 cm², 1 mm gap):

| Dielectric | εr | Capacitance |
|---|---|---|
| Vacuum / air | 1 | 88.5 pF |
| PTFE (Teflon) | 2.1 | 185.9 pF |
| Polypropylene | 2.2 | 194.8 pF |
| Paper (dry) | 3.5 | 309.9 pF |
| FR-4 PCB laminate | 4.4 | 389.6 pF |
| Glass | 6 | 531.3 pF |
| Mica | 6.5 | 575.5 pF |
| Water (20 °C) | 80 | 7,083.4 pF |

Dielectric constants are typical values; they vary with frequency, temperature and grade.

## Applications

- **Capacitive level probes** measure the change in capacitance as a liquid with higher εr replaces air between a probe and the tank wall.
- **Touch sensors and proximity switches** detect the extra capacitance of a finger or object.
- **PCB planes**: a power–ground plane pair 0.2 mm apart in FR-4 gives about 19 pF per cm².

## Designing for a target value

To get 1 nF with a 0.1 mm PTFE film (εr = 2.1), rearrange to A = C d ÷ (ε<sub>0</sub> ε<sub>r</sub>) = 1 × 10⁻⁹ × 0.0001 ÷ (8.854 × 10⁻¹² × 2.1) = **53.8 cm²**, a square about 73 mm on a side. Thinner film or a higher εr shrinks the area in direct proportion.

## Air gap, capacitance and breakdown

For the default 100 cm² plates in dry air, taking a breakdown strength of about 3 kV/mm:

| Gap | Capacitance | Approximate breakdown voltage | Energy at breakdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 mm | 885.4 pF | 0.3 kV | 39.8 µJ |
| 0.5 mm | 177.1 pF | 1.5 kV | 199.2 µJ |
| 1 mm | 88.5 pF | 3 kV | 398.4 µJ |
| 2 mm | 44.3 pF | 6 kV | 796.9 µJ |
| 5 mm | 17.7 pF | 15 kV | 1,992.2 µJ |

Halving the gap doubles C but also halves the voltage the gap can hold, so the maximum stored energy falls. At the default 1 mm gap the plates hold at most about 0.40 mJ before arcing.

## Two dielectrics in one gap

If the 1 mm gap is half glass (εr = 6) and half air, the layers act as two capacitors in series: C = ε<sub>0</sub> A ÷ (d<sub>1</sub>/ε<sub>r1</sub> + d<sub>2</sub>/ε<sub>r2</sub>) = **151.8 pF**, not the average of the two. The air layer then carries most of the voltage, with a field of 0.171 kV/mm at 100 V instead of 0.1 kV/mm, which is why air pockets in insulation are where partial discharge starts.

## How it is used in level sensing

A probe with plates half immersed in oil (εr ≈ 2.2) works like two capacitors in parallel, one in oil and one in air: C = 88.54 pF × (0.5 × 2.2 + 0.5 × 1) = **141.7 pF**. The transmitter measures that change and scales it linearly from empty (88.5 pF) to full (194.8 pF). Conductive liquids such as water need an insulated probe, because the liquid itself becomes one plate.

## Common mistakes

- **Converting area wrongly.** 100 cm² is 0.01 m², not 0.1 m²; an area error is squared compared with a length error.
- **Using total plate area.** Only the overlapping area counts, so offset plates have less capacitance.
- **Ignoring fringing for wide gaps.** When the gap is more than about a tenth of the plate width, the real value is noticeably higher than the formula.
- **Taking εr as constant.** Water, ceramics and many plastics change permittivity with frequency and temperature.
- **Averaging two dielectrics.** Layers stacked across the gap are in series, layers side by side are in parallel.
