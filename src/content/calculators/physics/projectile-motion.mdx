## How to use the projectile motion calculator

Enter the **launch speed** and **angle** to get the range, maximum height and flight time. The chart shows how range changes with angle — note the peak at 45°.

## Formulas (level ground, no air resistance)

<p class="formula">R = v² sin(2θ) ÷ g<br/>H = v² sin²θ ÷ 2g<br/>T = 2v sin θ ÷ g</p>

## Worked example

Launched at the default 20 m/s and 45°, with g = 9.80665 m/s²:

1. Split the velocity: v<sub>x</sub> = 20 × cos 45° = 14.14 m/s and v<sub>y</sub> = 20 × sin 45° = 14.14 m/s
2. Time to the top: 14.14 ÷ 9.80665 = 1.442 s, so the time of flight is **2.884 s**
3. Maximum height: 14.14² ÷ (2 × 9.80665) = **10.20 m**
4. Range: 14.14 m/s × 2.884 s = **40.79 m**, the same as 20² × sin 90° ÷ 9.80665

At x = 30 m the ball is still 7.94 m up, from y = x tan θ − g x² ÷ (2 v² cos² θ). Doubling the launch speed to 40 m/s multiplies the range by four, to 163.2 m, and on the Moon (g = 1.62 m/s²) the same throw would travel 247 m.

## Projectile from a height

If launched from height h₀ above the landing point, the time of flight becomes:

<p class="formula">t = [v sin θ + √((v sin θ)² + 2 g h₀)] ÷ g</p>

and the range is v cos θ × t. From a height, the best angle for maximum range is **less than 45°**.

## Real-world effects

| Effect | Result |
|---|---|
| Air resistance | Shorter range, lower peak, steeper descent |
| Spin (Magnus effect) | Backspin lifts the ball; topspin makes it dip |
| Wind | Changes range and drift |

For a well-hit golf ball or a long throw, real range can be 30–50% less than the formula predicts.

## Sports examples

- **Long jump**: athletes take off at about 18–22°, lower than 45°, because they cannot generate as much speed at steeper angles.
- **Shot put**: the best angle is about 37–40° because the shot is released above the ground.
- **Football goal kicks**: typical angles are 30–45°.

## Horizontal launch

For something thrown or rolled horizontally off a table (θ = 0), time to land is √(2h ÷ g), and range is v × √(2h ÷ g). A ball rolling off a 1 m table at 2 m/s lands 0.90 m away.

Range is shared by complementary angles, but height and time are not: a 75° lob stays in the air 3.7 times longer than a 15° shot that lands at the same spot.

## Measuring a real trajectory

The simplest method is slow motion video against a background with marked distances: step through the frames, note the position every few frames and fit a parabola. Radar guns give launch speed directly, and launch monitors in golf and baseball measure speed, angle and spin together. Comparing those measurements with this calculator shows how much air drag removes.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the level ground formulas from a height.** A ball thrown from a cliff or a shot put released at shoulder height needs the launch height formula above, or the range comes out short.
- **Entering speed in km/h as m/s.** 72 km/h is 20 m/s; typing 72 gives a range almost 13 times too large.
- **Thinking the ball stops at the top.** Only the vertical velocity is zero at the peak; it still moves sideways at 14.14 m/s.
- **Splitting with the wrong trig function.** The horizontal part is v cos θ and the vertical part v sin θ when θ is measured from the ground.
- **Trusting 45° for real balls.** With drag, the best angle for a long throw or a golf drive is well below 45°.
