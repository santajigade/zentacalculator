## How to use the air core inductor calculator

Enter the **coil diameter** measured to the centre of the wire, the **winding length** and the **number of turns**. The calculator returns the inductance and the approximate wire length (ignoring leads). Clear the turns to solve for the turns needed for a target inductance.

## Wheeler's formula

<p class="formula">L (µH) = d² N² ÷ (18 d + 40 l) &nbsp;&nbsp;(d, l in inches)</p>

Equivalent to Wheeler's original L = r² N² ÷ (9 r + 10 l) with radius r. Inductance rises with the square of the turns and roughly with the diameter.

## Example

A coil 20 mm in diameter and 30 mm long with 25 turns:

- d = 20 ÷ 25.4 = 0.787 in, l = 30 ÷ 25.4 = 1.181 in
- L = 0.787² × 25² ÷ (18 × 0.787 + 40 × 1.181) = **6.309 µH**
- Wire length ≈ 25 × π × 20 mm = **1.571 m**

## Inductance of 20 mm long single-layer coils

| Diameter | 10 turns | 20 turns | 40 turns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 mm | 0.402 µH | 1.61 µH | 6.43 µH |
| 15 mm | 0.828 µH | 3.31 µH | 13.2 µH |
| 20 mm | 1.36 µH | 5.43 µH | 21.7 µH |
| 30 mm | 2.64 µH | 10.6 µH | 42.3 µH |
| 50 mm | 5.79 µH | 23.2 µH | 92.6 µH |

Coils shorter than 0.4 × diameter fall outside the formula's 1% range, so treat those rows as estimates.

## Notes

- Spacing the turns lowers inductance slightly but also lowers self-capacitance, raising the self-resonant frequency.
- Check the tuned frequency with the [LC resonant frequency calculator](/electronics/resonant-frequency/).
- For ferrite or powdered-iron cores, use the core's A<sub>L</sub> value in the [inductor A<sub>L</sub> calculator](/electronics/inductor-al-value/).

## Second example: winding for a target of 10 µH

Keep the default 20 mm former and 30 mm winding length and ask for 10 µH. Rearranging Wheeler's formula gives N = √(L × (18d + 40l)) ÷ d:

- 18 × 0.787 + 40 × 1.181 = 61.42
- N = √(10 × 61.42) ÷ 0.787 = **31.47 turns**, so wind **32**
- 32 turns give 10.34 µH and need about 2.01 m of wire

Squeezing 32 turns into 30 mm leaves a pitch of 0.94 mm, so the wire must be thinner than about 0.94 mm including enamel. The default 25 turn coil has a 1.20 mm pitch, which suits 1.0 mm enamelled wire with a small gap.

| 60 mm | 3.00 | 3.57 µH | yes |

Stretching the default coil from 30 mm to 36 mm drops it from 6.31 µH to 5.47 µH, which is the usual way to fine tune a coil after winding.

## Checking a wound coil

Measure inductance with an LCR meter at a frequency well below the self-resonant frequency, or resonate the coil with a known capacitor and read the frequency. With 100 pF across the default 6.31 µH coil, resonance should fall at **6.34 MHz**; a lower reading points to extra capacitance from leads and fixture.

## Common mistakes

- **Entering the outside diameter.** With 1 mm wire on a 20 mm centre diameter, typing 21 mm gives 6.88 µH, 9% too high.
- **Forgetting the leads.** Straight leads add roughly 1 nH per millimetre, which matters for coils below about 0.5 µH.
- **Using the formula for multilayer or pancake coils.** Wheeler's single layer expression does not apply; those shapes have their own formulas.
- **Mounting the coil against metal.** A nearby chassis or ground plane induces eddy currents and lowers both inductance and Q; keep about one coil diameter of clearance.
- **Cutting the wire to the exact length shown.** The figure excludes leads, so add a few centimetres for each end.
