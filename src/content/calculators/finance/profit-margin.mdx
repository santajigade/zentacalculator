## How to use this calculator

Enter the **cost** and **selling price** to see your profit, margin and markup. To set a price, press × on Selling price and type the margin (or markup) you want.

## The formulas

<p class="formula">profit = price − cost   margin = profit ÷ price   markup = profit ÷ cost</p>

## Worked example

You buy a product for 60 and sell it for 100.

- Profit = 100 − 60 = **40**
- Margin = 40 ÷ 100 = **40%**
- Markup = 40 ÷ 60 = **66.7%**

## Margin vs markup table

| Markup | Equals margin |
|---|---|
| 25% | 20% |
| 50% | 33.3% |
| 100% | 50% |
| 200% | 66.7% |

Mixing these up is a common pricing mistake. If you want a 50% margin but add a 50% markup, your actual margin is only 33.3%.

<p class="formula">Price = Cost ÷ (1 − margin)</p>

## Typical margins by industry

| Industry | Typical net margin |
|---|---|
| Grocery retail | 1–3% |
| Restaurants | 3–9% |
| Manufacturing | 5–10% |
| Professional services | 10–20% |
| Software | 15–30% |

Gross margins are much higher than net margins, because net margin also subtracts rent, salaries, marketing and taxes.

## Margin with GST or VAT

Calculate margin on prices **before** tax. Sales tax is collected for the government and is not revenue. If your selling price includes 18% tax, divide it by 1.18 first, or use the [GST calculator](/finance/gst/).

## Common mistakes

- **Pricing with margin but thinking in markup.** A 50% markup is only a 33% margin.
- **Forgetting discounts and returns.** A 10% sale cuts a 30% margin to about 22%.
- **Leaving out shipping and payment fees** from cost, which can quietly erase thin margins.

## Gross vs net margin

This calculator gives **gross** margin — selling price minus the direct cost of the item. **Net** margin also subtracts overheads such as rent, salaries, marketing and tax. To estimate net margin, include those costs in the Cost field.
