## How to use the aquarium volume calculator

Enter the tank's **length**, **width** and **water height**. The volume and weight of the water are calculated.

## Example

A 100 × 40 cm tank filled to 45 cm: 100 × 40 × 45 = 180,000 cm³ = **180 litres** (47.6 US gal), weighing about **180 kg** of water.

## Common tank sizes

| Tank (L × W × H) | Volume | Water weight |
|---|---|---|
| 40 × 25 × 25 cm | 25 L (7 US gal) | 25 kg |
| 60 × 30 × 35 cm | 63 L (17 US gal) | 63 kg |
| 80 × 35 × 40 cm | 112 L (30 US gal) | 112 kg |
| 100 × 40 × 50 cm | 200 L (53 US gal) | 200 kg |
| 120 × 45 × 50 cm | 270 L (71 US gal) | 270 kg |
| 150 × 50 × 60 cm | 450 L (119 US gal) | 450 kg |

## Actual water volume

Substrate, rocks, decorations and the gap at the top reduce the real water volume by about **10–20%**. Use the real volume when dosing medicines, fertilisers or water conditioners.

## Total weight

A filled aquarium weighs roughly: water + glass + substrate + rock. As a rule of thumb, allow about **1.2–1.4 kg per litre** of tank volume. A 200 L tank can weigh 250–280 kg, so make sure the stand and floor can support it.

## Filtration and heating

| Equipment | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| Filter flow | 4–6 times the tank volume per hour |
| Heater | about 1 W per litre (more in cold rooms) |
| Water change | 10–25% weekly |

## Stocking

The old “one inch of fish per gallon” rule is unreliable. Consider adult fish size, activity, waste output and the tank's footprint. Larger tanks are more stable and forgiving for beginners.

## Other shapes

- **Cylinder**: π × r² × height.
- **Bow-front**: approximate with a rectangle using the average depth.

## Worked example: from glass size to real water volume

The default tank measures 100 × 40 cm and is filled to 45 cm: 100 × 40 × 45 ÷ 1,000 = **180 L**, or 47.6 US gallons (39.6 UK gallons). That figure uses outside dimensions. With 10 mm glass the inside is 98 × 38 cm, so the water is 167.6 L. A 5 cm gravel bed occupies 18.6 L, but water fills about 40% of the gaps between grains, so it displaces roughly 11.2 L. The water you actually dose is about **156 L**, around 13% less than the label volume.

## Equipment for the default tank

- Heater at about 1 W per litre: 180 W, so a 200 W heater (or two 100 W units for redundancy).
- Filter turnover of 4 to 6 times per hour: 720 to 1080 L/h rated flow.
- A 20% weekly water change: about 31 L, based on the real water volume.

## Weight and the floor

Allowing 1.3 kg per litre of tank volume for water, glass, substrate and rock, the default tank weighs about 234 kg. Spread over a 1 × 0.4 m stand, that is 585 kg per square metre, far above the roughly 200 kg/m² (2 kN/m²) that residential floors are typically designed to carry spread evenly. Place large tanks across the floor joists or near a load-bearing wall, and use a stand built for the full weight.

## Common mistakes

- **Using outside dimensions.** Glass thickness, the top rim and bracing reduce the water volume, often by 5 to 10% on mid-sized tanks.
- **Using tank height instead of water height.** Most tanks are filled 2 to 3 cm below the rim.
- **Confusing US and UK gallons.** A UK gallon is 4.546 L and a US gallon 3.785 L; dosing labels from different countries differ by 20%.
- **Dosing on the gross volume.** Substrate, rock and a sump change the real volume; medicines are best dosed on the water actually present.
- **Forgetting the sump.** If the tank drains to a sump, add its running water volume to the total for dosing and heating.
