## How to use the heat sink calculator

Enter the **power** the device dissipates, the design **junction temperature**, the **ambient temperature** inside the enclosure, and the device's **Rθjc** and interface **Rθcs**. The result is the highest heat sink thermal resistance you can use.

For more background, read [What Is Thermal Conductivity?](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/what-is-thermal-conductivity/).

## The thermal circuit

<p class="formula">T<sub>j</sub> = T<sub>a</sub> + P × (R<sub>θjc</sub> + R<sub>θcs</sub> + R<sub>θsa</sub>)</p>

<p class="formula">R<sub>θsa</sub> ≤ (T<sub>j</sub> − T<sub>a</sub>) ÷ P − R<sub>θjc</sub> − R<sub>θcs</sub></p>

## Example

10 W in a TO-220 MOSFET (Rθjc 1.5 °C/W), thermal pad 0.5 °C/W, 40 °C ambient, 125 °C target:

- Total allowed = 85 ÷ 10 = 8.5 °C/W
- R<sub>θsa</sub> ≤ 8.5 − 1.5 − 0.5 = **6.5 °C/W**

## Typical package resistances

| Package | Rθjc | Rθja (no heat sink) |
|---|---|---|
| TO-92 | — | 150–200 °C/W |
| SOT-23 | — | 200–350 °C/W |
| D-PAK (on PCB copper) | 1–3 °C/W | 40–80 °C/W |
| TO-220 | 0.5–3 °C/W | 50–65 °C/W |
| TO-247 | 0.3–1 °C/W | 35–45 °C/W |

## Typical heat sinks

| Heat sink | Rθsa (natural convection) |
|---|---|
| Small clip-on TO-220 | 15–25 °C/W |
| Extruded fin, 50 mm | 4–8 °C/W |
| Extruded fin, 100 mm | 1.5–3 °C/W |
| Large finned profile | 0.5–1 °C/W |
| With fan airflow | 2–4 times lower |

Mount fins vertically for natural convection, and remember the enclosure air temperature — not room temperature — is the ambient.

## Checking a chosen heat sink

With the default inputs the sink must be at most 6.5 °C/W. Suppose you pick a profile rated **5 °C/W**. Working back up the thermal circuit at 10 W:

- Heat sink temperature = 40 + 10 × 5 = **90 °C**
- Case temperature = 90 + 10 × 0.5 = **95 °C**
- Junction temperature = 95 + 10 × 1.5 = **110 °C**, leaving 15 °C of margin below the 125 °C target.

If the drain tab must be insulated from the sink with a mica or silicone pad of about 1.2 °C/W instead of 0.5 °C/W, the allowed sink resistance falls to 8.5 − 1.5 − 1.2 = **5.8 °C/W**.

## Required heat sink against power

For the default 125 °C junction target, 40 °C ambient, R<sub>θjc</sub> 1.5 and R<sub>θcs</sub> 0.5 °C/W:

| Power | Total allowed | Max R<sub>θsa</sub> | Junction with an ideal sink |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 W | 42.50 °C/W | 40.50 °C/W | 44 °C |
| 5 W | 17.00 °C/W | 15.00 °C/W | 50 °C |
| 10 W | 8.50 °C/W | 6.50 °C/W | 60 °C |
| 15 W | 5.67 °C/W | 3.67 °C/W | 70 °C |
| 20 W | 4.25 °C/W | 2.25 °C/W | 80 °C |
| 30 W | 2.83 °C/W | 0.83 °C/W | 100 °C |

The last column is the junction temperature with an ideal (zero resistance) heat sink. Once it passes the target, no passive heat sink is enough.

## Second example: a linear regulator

Linear regulators waste the whole voltage drop as heat. A 5 V regulator fed from 12 V and supplying 0.8 A dissipates (12 − 5) × 0.8 = **5.6 W**. With a TO-220 package of R<sub>θjc</sub> 3 °C/W, a 0.5 °C/W pad, 50 °C inside the box and the same 125 °C target:

- Total allowed = (125 − 50) ÷ 5.6 = 13.39 °C/W
- R<sub>θsa</sub> ≤ 13.39 − 3 − 0.5 = **9.89 °C/W**, a medium extruded sink
- With no sink at all (R<sub>θja</sub> about 60 °C/W) the junction would head for 50 + 5.6 × 60 = 386 °C; the bare package can only shed 1.25 W, which limits the current to about 179 mA

Lowering the input to 9 V cuts the loss to 3.2 W and relaxes the sink to 19.94 °C/W, which is often cheaper than a bigger heat sink.

## Several devices on one heat sink

When two of the default 10 W MOSFETs share one sink, the sink carries 20 W while each junction still sits 20 °C above it through its own R<sub>θjc</sub> + R<sub>θcs</sub>. The limit becomes R<sub>θsa</sub> ≤ (125 − 40 − 10 × 2) ÷ 20 = **3.25 °C/W**, half of what a separate sink for each device could be, with the sink running at 105 °C.

## Measuring temperatures in practice

Fix a fine thermocouple to the device tab or to the sink right beside the package with a dab of thermal epoxy, run the circuit at full load until readings stop changing (often 20 to 40 minutes), and measure the air inside the enclosure, not the room. The junction temperature is then T<sub>case</sub> + P × R<sub>θjc</sub>. Measure the power from voltage and current, because datasheet loss estimates often miss switching losses.

## Common mistakes

- **Using room temperature as ambient.** Air inside a closed enclosure is often 10 to 20 °C warmer than the room, which can halve the allowed R<sub>θsa</sub>.
- **Reading R<sub>θja</sub> as the heat sink figure.** R<sub>θja</sub> is for the bare package with no heat sink and is not used once a sink is fitted.
- **Ignoring the insulating pad.** A pad can add more resistance than the device itself; include it in R<sub>θcs</sub>.
- **Trusting the catalogue figure at any power.** Natural convection ratings are quoted at a stated temperature rise and fin orientation; a horizontal sink or a smaller rise performs worse.
- **Forgetting other devices on the same sink.** Add all the power that flows into a shared heat sink before calculating its temperature.
