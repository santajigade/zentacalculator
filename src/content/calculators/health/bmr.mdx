## How to use the BMR & daily calorie calculator

Choose your **sex** and **activity level**, then enter your **age**, **weight** and **height**. Your BMR and daily maintenance calories (TDEE) update instantly. Weight and height accept kg or lb and cm, m, ft or inches.

## The formula (Mifflin–St Jeor, 1990)

<p class="formula">BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + s</p>

- **s** = +5 for men, −161 for women
- **TDEE** = BMR × activity factor

## Worked example

A 30-year-old man, 70 kg, 175 cm, lightly active:

- BMR = 700 + 1,093.75 − 150 + 5 = **1,649 kcal/day**
- TDEE = 1,648.75 × 1.375 = **2,267 kcal/day**

A 30-year-old woman, 60 kg, 165 cm: BMR = **1,320 kcal/day**.

## Activity factors

| Level | Factor |
|---|---|
| Sedentary | 1.2 |
| Lightly active | 1.375 |
| Moderately active | 1.55 |
| Very active | 1.725 |
| Extra active | 1.9 |

Most people overestimate their activity. If unsure, pick one level lower.

## BMR by formula

For a 30-year-old man, 70 kg, 175 cm:

| Formula | BMR (kcal/day) |
|---|---|
| Mifflin–St Jeor (used here) | 1649 |
| Revised Harris–Benedict (1984) | 1696 |
| Katch–McArdle (needs body fat %; 15% shown) | 1655 |

The formulas usually agree within 5%. Katch–McArdle can be better for very lean or muscular people because it uses lean body mass. Find yours with the [lean body mass calculator](/health/lean-body-mass/).

BMR falls by about 50 kcal a day for every 10 years of age, mainly because muscle mass declines.

## What affects your metabolism

- **Muscle mass**: muscle uses more energy at rest than fat. Strength training helps maintain it.
- **Age**: metabolism slows gradually from middle age.
- **Body size**: larger bodies need more energy.
- **Hormones**: thyroid conditions can raise or lower BMR. See a doctor if you suspect a problem.
- **Severe dieting**: very low calorie intake can reduce energy expenditure, making further weight loss harder.

## Using TDEE for goals

- **Maintain weight**: eat about your TDEE.
- **Gradual weight loss**: a modest deficit of 300–500 kcal below TDEE.
- **Gain muscle**: a small surplus of 200–300 kcal with strength training.

Track your weight for 2–3 weeks and adjust, because every formula is an estimate.

## Limitations

This is an estimate for healthy adults. It does not account for body composition, medical conditions, pregnancy or medication. Muscular people often burn more than predicted. Use the result as a starting point and adjust based on real-world changes over a few weeks.
