## How to use the THD calculator

Enter the **fundamental** and **harmonic magnitudes** from a power quality analyser — either in volts, amps or as percentages of the fundamental. You get the THD and the true RMS value. Enter 0 for harmonics you do not have.

For more background, read [Active Harmonic Filter Working and Sizing](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/active-harmonic-filter-working-sizing/) and [Neutral Conductor Sizing with Harmonics](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/neutral-conductor-sizing-harmonics/).

## THD formula

<p class="formula">THD = √(V₂² + V₃² + V₄² + …) ÷ V₁</p>

<p class="formula">True RMS = √(V₁² + V₂² + V₃² + …) = V₁ × √(1 + THD²)</p>

## Example

Fundamental 100; harmonics 2, 4, 3, 1.5 and 1 (3rd, 5th, 7th, 11th, 13th):

THD = √(4 + 16 + 9 + 2.25 + 1) ÷ 100 = **5.7%**

## IEEE 519-2014 voltage limits

| Bus voltage at PCC | Individual harmonic | THD |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 1 kV | 5.0% | 8.0% |
| 1 kV – 69 kV | 3.0% | 5.0% |
| 69 kV – 161 kV | 1.5% | 2.5% |
| > 161 kV | 1.0% | 1.5% |

Current limits (TDD) depend on the ratio of short-circuit current to load current at the point of common coupling.

## Typical current THD

| Load | Current THD |
|---|---|
| 6-pulse VFD, no choke | 80–120% |
| 6-pulse VFD with 3–5% line reactor | 35–45% |
| 12-pulse drive | 10–15% |
| Active front-end drive | under 5% |
| LED lighting, SMPS | 20–150% |

## Problems caused by harmonics

- Overheating of transformers, cables and neutral conductors (triplen harmonics add in the neutral).
- Nuisance tripping of breakers and failure of capacitor banks through resonance.
- Errors in meters that are not true-RMS.

## Worked example with the default values

Square each harmonic: 2² + 4² + 3² + 1.5² + 1² = 4 + 16 + 9 + 2.25 + 1 = **32.25**.

- THD = √32.25 ÷ 100 = 5.679 ÷ 100 = **5.68%**
- True RMS = √(100² + 32.25) = **100.16**

The 5th harmonic alone supplies 50% of the squared sum, so it is the one to target with a filter. Note that 5.7% distortion raises the RMS by only 0.16%: harmonics add in quadrature, so small amounts barely change the heating value.

## THD, RMS and distortion power factor

| THD | RMS ÷ fundamental | Distortion power factor |
|---|---|---|
| 5% | 1.001 | 0.999 |
| 10% | 1.005 | 0.995 |
| 30% | 1.044 | 0.958 |
| 50% | 1.118 | 0.894 |
| 80% | 1.281 | 0.781 |
| 100% | 1.414 | 0.707 |

For a drive drawing current at 40% THD, the RMS current is 1.077 times the fundamental, so cables and transformers carry about 8% more heating current than the kW suggests, and the true power factor is the displacement power factor multiplied by 0.928.

## THD versus TDD

IEEE 519 current limits use total demand distortion, which divides the harmonic current by the maximum demand current I<sub>L</sub> instead of the present fundamental. A feeder running at 50 A with 40% current THD and a 100 A maximum demand has TDD = 40% × 50 ÷ 100 = **20%**. Judging a lightly loaded feeder by THD makes it look much worse than it is.

## How it is measured

Class A power quality analysers (IEC 61000-4-30) compute harmonics with a 10 cycle (50 Hz) or 12 cycle (60 Hz) FFT window up to the 40th or 50th order, and IEEE 519 assessments use the 95th percentile of 10 minute values over a week. Record at the point of common coupling, and log voltage and current together so you can tell whether distortion comes from your loads or from the grid.

## Common mistakes

- **Entering only the harmonics shown on screen.** Leaving out the 17th, 19th and higher orders underestimates THD for 6-pulse drives.
- **Mixing units.** All inputs must be in the same unit, either all volts, all amps or all percent of the fundamental.
- **Comparing current THD with the voltage limit.** The 8% figure applies to voltage at 1 kV or below; current is judged by TDD.
- **Using a meter that is not true RMS.** An average-responding meter can read 10 to 40% low on distorted current.
- **Confusing THD-F and THD-R.** Some analysers divide by the total RMS (THD-R) instead of the fundamental, which gives a lower number at high distortion.
