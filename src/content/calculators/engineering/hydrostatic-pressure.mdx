## How to use this calculator

Enter the **liquid height** and **density** to get the hydrostatic pressure at the bottom. To set up a level transmitter, enter the maximum level to get its upper range value. To read level from a pressure gauge, press × on the level and type the pressure.

## The formula

<p class="formula">P = ρ × g × h</p>

- **ρ** — liquid density (kg/m³)
- **g** — standard gravity, 9.80665 m/s²
- **h** — height of liquid above the measuring point (m)

## Worked examples

**Water tank, 5 m level**

P = 1000 × 9.80665 × 5 = 49,033 Pa = **0.490 bar** (7.11 psi)

**Diesel tank (ρ ≈ 832 kg/m³), 3 m level**

P = 832 × 9.80665 × 3 = 24,477 Pa = **0.245 bar**

The same pressure would show only 2.5 m on a transmitter calibrated for water — always range for the real density.

## Typical densities at about 20 °C

| Liquid | Density (kg/m³) |
|---|---|
| Water | 998 |
| Sea water | 1,025 |
| Diesel | 820 – 845 |
| Petrol | 720 – 775 |
| Ethylene glycol | 1,113 |
| Sulphuric acid (98%) | 1,830 |

## Ranging a level transmitter

1. Calculate the pressure at minimum level (often 0) and maximum level.
2. If the transmitter is mounted below the tank nozzle, add the liquid head between the nozzle and the transmitter (zero elevation).
3. Enter these as the 4 mA and 20 mA values — then use the [4–20 mA scaling calculator](/engineering/4-20ma-scaling/) to check readings.

A useful rule: **1 m of water ≈ 0.098 bar ≈ 1.42 psi ≈ 9.81 kPa**.
