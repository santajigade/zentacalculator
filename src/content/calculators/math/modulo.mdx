## How to use the modulo calculator

Enter the **dividend a** and the **divisor b**. The calculator returns the floored quotient, **a mod b** (the mathematical modulo, which always has the sign of b) and the **truncated remainder** that the % operator gives in C-family languages. For positive numbers the two are the same.

## Modulo formula

<p class="formula">a mod b = a − b × ⌊a ÷ b⌋ &nbsp;·&nbsp; truncated remainder = a − b × trunc(a ÷ b)</p>

## Worked example

17 mod 5:

- 17 ÷ 5 = 3.4, so the quotient is **3**
- 17 − 5 × 3 = **2**, so 17 mod 5 = 2

## Floored vs truncated

| a | b | ⌊a/b⌋ | a mod b (floored) | a % b (truncated) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| -17 | 5 | -4 | 3 | -2 |
| 17 | -5 | -4 | -3 | 2 |
| -17 | -5 | 3 | -2 | -2 |
| 100 | 7 | 14 | 2 | 2 |

| 5.5 | 2 | 2 | 1.5 | 1.5 |

## Uses of modular arithmetic

- **Clock time** — 15:00 + 10 hours = 25 mod 24 = 01:00.
- **Even and odd** — n mod 2 is 0 for even numbers, 1 for odd.
- **Check digits** — ISBN-10 uses mod 11, IBAN uses mod 97.
- **Cyclic counters and ring buffers** — index = (index + 1) mod size.

## Rules of modular arithmetic

Remainders can be taken at every step of a sum or product without changing the final remainder, which keeps numbers small:

<p class="formula">(x + y) mod n = [(x mod n) + (y mod n)] mod n &nbsp;·&nbsp; (x × y) mod n = [(x mod n) × (y mod n)] mod n</p>

For example (17 × 23) mod 5 = (2 × 3) mod 5 = **1**, and 391 ÷ 5 leaves the same remainder.

## Worked examples in daily use

- **Day of the week.** 100 days after a Tuesday: 100 mod 7 = **2**, so it falls 2 days after Tuesday, on a Thursday.
- **Large powers.** 7¹⁰⁰ mod 13 is found by repeated squaring, reducing mod 13 at every step. Because 13 is prime, Fermat's little theorem gives 7¹² ≡ 1, so 7¹⁰⁰ ≡ 7^(100 mod 12) = 7⁴ = 2,401 ≡ **9** (mod 13).
- **ISBN-10 check digit.** For 0-306-40615-?, the weighted sum 10×0 + 9×3 + 8×0 + 7×6 + 6×4 + 5×0 + 4×6 + 3×1 + 2×5 = 130. The check digit is (11 − 130 mod 11) mod 11 = **2**.
- **Luhn check for card numbers.** For the payload 7992739871, doubling every second digit from the right and summing gives 67; the check digit is (10 − 67 mod 10) mod 10 = **3**.

## Modulo in code

- Python: `-17 % 5` gives 3 (floored); `math.fmod(-17, 5)` gives −2.0 (truncated).
- C, Java, JavaScript: `-17 % 5` gives −2. For a non-negative index use `((a % b) + b) % b`.
- Excel and Google Sheets: `=MOD(-17, 5)` gives 3, the floored result.
- For powers of 2, `x % 8` equals `x & 7` for non-negative integers, which compilers use as a fast shortcut.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming % is always positive.** A ring buffer index of `(i − 1) % size` in C or Java goes negative when i = 0 and reads outside the array.
- **Dividing by zero.** a mod 0 is undefined; the calculator returns no result rather than a remainder.
- **Using floating point for big integers.** In JavaScript, numbers above 2⁵³ lose precision, so a large mod gives a wrong answer; use BigInt.
- **Mixing up quotient direction.** For −17 ÷ 5 the floored quotient is −4, not −3; the remainder only works out if the quotient matches the convention used.
- **Reducing too late.** Computing 7¹⁰⁰ first and then taking mod 13 overflows; reduce after every multiplication.
