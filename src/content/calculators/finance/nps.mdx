## How to use the NPS calculator

Enter your **monthly contribution**, your **age**, the **expected return**, the **share of the corpus you will use to buy an annuity** and the **annuity rate**. The calculator shows your corpus at 60, the lump sum you can take and the monthly pension.

## NPS formula

The corpus grows like a monthly SIP:

<p class="formula">Corpus = M × [((1 + i)<sup>n</sup> − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)</p>

with i = annual return ÷ 12 and n = months until 60. Then:

- **Lump sum** = (1 − annuity share) × corpus
- **Monthly pension** = annuity share × corpus × annuity rate ÷ 12

## Worked example

₹5,000 a month from age 30 to 60 at 10%:

- Total contributed = **₹18,00,000**
- Corpus at 60 = **₹1,13,96,627**
- Lump sum (80%) = **₹91,17,301**
- Pension from 20% annuity at 6% = **₹11,397 a month**

## Corpus by starting age

## NPS exit rules at 60 (non-government subscribers)

| Corpus | What you can do |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹8 lakh | Withdraw everything, no annuity needed |
| ₹8–12 lakh | Up to ₹6 lakh lump sum; rest as annuity or systematic withdrawal |
| Above ₹12 lakh | Up to 80% lump sum; at least 20% in an annuity |

You can also defer withdrawal and stay invested up to age 85. Government employees follow different rules (40% minimum annuity).

## Choosing your asset mix

- **Active choice**: pick your own split across equity (up to 75%), corporate bonds and government securities.
- **Auto choice**: equity is reduced automatically as you get older.

Younger investors can usually afford more equity for higher long-term growth.

## Keep in mind

- Annuity rates change with interest rates. Check current quotes from annuity providers before you retire.
- Pension from an annuity does not rise with inflation unless you choose a rising annuity.
- Returns are market-linked and not guaranteed.

## How the return assumption changes the result

The default plan (₹5,000 a month from age 30, 20% annuity at 6%) is very sensitive to the yearly return, because the money compounds for 30 years:

| Return | Corpus at 60 | 80% lump sum | Pension from 20% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8% | ₹75,01,476 | ₹60,01,181 | ₹7,501 |
| 9% | ₹92,22,370 | ₹73,77,896 | ₹9,222 |
| 10% | ₹1,13,96,627 | ₹91,17,301 | ₹11,397 |
| 11% | ₹1,41,51,139 | ₹1,13,20,911 | ₹14,151 |
| 12% | ₹1,76,49,569 | ₹1,41,19,655 | ₹17,650 |

Two percentage points either side of 10% cuts the corpus by about a third or raises it by more than half. A mix with 50% equity has historically earned less than an equity-heavy mix, so pick the return that matches your asset allocation rather than the best past figure.

## Lump sum or bigger pension

The same ₹1,13,96,627 corpus can be split in different ways at exit:

| Annuity share | Lump sum | Annuity purchase | Monthly pension at 6% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | ₹91,17,301 | ₹22,79,325 | ₹11,397 |
| 40% | ₹68,37,976 | ₹45,58,651 | ₹22,793 |
| 60% | ₹45,58,651 | ₹68,37,976 | ₹34,190 |
| 100% | ₹0 | ₹1,13,96,627 | ₹56,983 |

Putting more into the annuity gives a steady income for life but locks the money in; the lump sum can be invested in a [systematic withdrawal plan](/finance/swp/) instead.

## Reading the result in today's money

All figures are in rupees of the year you retire. At 6% inflation, the ₹11,397 monthly pension in the default example buys what about **₹1,984** buys today, and the ₹1,13,96,627 corpus is worth about ₹19,84,268 in today's terms. That is why the contribution usually needs to rise with salary; check a step-up plan with the [retirement corpus calculator](/finance/retirement-corpus/).

## Working back from a target pension

For a ₹50,000 monthly pension with 40% of the corpus in a 6% annuity, the corpus must be ₹50,000 × 12 ÷ (0.40 × 0.06) = **₹2,50,00,000**. Starting at 30 with a 10% return, that needs about **₹10,968 a month**. With only the 20% minimum annuity the corpus must double to ₹5,00,00,000, or ₹21,936 a month.

## Tax benefit in practice

Under the old tax regime, ₹50,000 a year put into NPS under section 80CCD(1B), over and above the 80C limit, saves ₹15,600 for someone in the 30% slab (31.2% with cess). Under the new regime only the employer's contribution under 80CCD(2) is deductible, so ask your employer about routing part of the salary into NPS.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the annuity rate as fixed.** The 6% default is today's level; the rate you get depends on interest rates in the year you retire.
- **Forgetting inflation.** A pension that looks comfortable in future rupees can be modest in today's money.
- **Assuming the whole lump sum is tax-free.** Up to 60% of the corpus is tax-free; check the tax treatment of any amount above that.
- **Using an equity return with Auto choice.** Auto choice cuts equity steadily after age 35, so the average return over the plan is lower than a pure equity figure.
- **Missing the minimum contribution.** A Tier I account needs at least ₹1,000 a year to stay active.
