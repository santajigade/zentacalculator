## How to use the bending stress calculator

Enter the **bending moment**, the section's **elastic section modulus Z** in cm³ and the material's **yield strength**. The calculator returns the maximum bending stress at the outer fibre and the factor of safety against yielding. To size a section, enter the stress you will allow and clear Z.

## Bending stress formula

<p class="formula">σ = M × y ÷ I = M ÷ Z &nbsp;·&nbsp; FoS = S<sub>y</sub> ÷ σ</p>

## Example

An IPE 200 (Z = 194 cm³) carrying a 15 kN·m moment, S235/A36-type steel with 250 MPa yield:

- σ = 15,000 N·m ÷ 194 × 10⁻⁶ m³ = **77.3 MPa**
- Factor of safety = 250 ÷ 77.3 = **3.23**

## Notes

- Slender beams can fail by lateral-torsional buckling before reaching the yield stress; design codes reduce the capacity of unrestrained beams.
- Combine bending with axial or shear stresses (for example with the von Mises criterion) when both act together.

## Worked example: flat bar on two supports

A 50 × 150 mm steel flat bar, standing on edge, spans **3 m** between two simple supports and carries a uniform load of **10 kN/m**:

- Bending moment at mid span = w L² ÷ 8 = 10 × 3² ÷ 8 = **11.25 kN·m**
- Section modulus = b h² ÷ 6 = 50 × 150² ÷ 6 = 187,500 mm³ = **187.5 cm³**
- σ = 11,250 N·m ÷ 187.5 × 10⁻⁶ m³ = **60.0 MPa**
- Factor of safety against 250 MPa yield = 250 ÷ 60.0 = **4.17**

Laid flat, the same bar has Z = 150 × 50² ÷ 6 = 62.5 cm³, so the stress triples to 180 MPa and the bar yields. Orientation matters as much as size.

## How bending stress is measured in practice

On a real structure the stress is checked with a strain gauge bonded to the outer fibre, aligned with the beam axis. In the elastic range σ = E × ε, so the IPE 200 example above at 77.3 MPa on steel (E = 200 GPa) shows about **387 µε**. A quarter bridge picks up this strain, and a half bridge with one gauge on the top face and one on the bottom doubles the signal and cancels temperature and axial effects. Load cells of the bending beam type work on exactly this principle.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing cm³ and mm³.** Steel tables list Z in cm³; 194 cm³ is 194,000 mm³. A factor of 1,000 error is the most common slip.
- **Using I instead of Z.** I (cm⁴) must be divided by the distance to the outer fibre before it gives stress.
- **Taking the wrong axis.** An IPE 200 has Z ≈ 194 cm³ about its strong axis but only about 28.5 cm³ about its weak axis.
- **Leaving out self-weight and point loads.** Add every load to the bending moment at the critical section, not just the main one.
- **Comparing with ultimate strength.** For ductile steel the outer fibre yielding is the usual limit; ultimate strength gives a falsely high factor of safety.
