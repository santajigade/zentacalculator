## How to use the RC filter cutoff frequency calculator

Enter **R** and **C** to get the cutoff frequency and time constant. Add a **test frequency** to see how much a low-pass or high-pass filter attenuates it. To design a filter, enter the target cutoff and one component, then press × on the other.

For more background, read [RC and RL Time Constant Explained](https://www.instrumentationblog.in/rc-and-rl-time-constant-explained/).

## RC filter cutoff frequency formulas

<p class="formula">f<sub>c</sub> = 1 ÷ (2πRC)</p>

<p class="formula">Low-pass gain = −10 log₁₀[1 + (f/f<sub>c</sub>)²] dB &nbsp;·&nbsp; High-pass gain = −10 log₁₀[1 + (f<sub>c</sub>/f)²] dB</p>

## Practical tips

- The source impedance adds to R; the load impedance appears in parallel with C. Keep the load at least ten times R.
- For ADC anti-aliasing, set the cutoff below half the sampling rate.
- Use C0G/NP0 or film capacitors for stable filters; ceramic X7R values change with voltage and temperature.

## Worked example with the default values

R = 10 kΩ and C = 100 nF, with a 1 kHz test signal:

1. τ = RC = 10,000 × 100 × 10⁻⁹ = **1 ms**
2. f<sub>c</sub> = 1 ÷ (2π × 0.001) = **159.15 Hz**
3. f ÷ f<sub>c</sub> = 6.283, so the low-pass gain is −10 log₁₀(1 + 39.48) = **-16.07 dB**
4. The high-pass gain at the same frequency is **-0.11 dB**

As a low-pass filter the circuit passes 15.7% of a 1 kHz signal with a phase lag of 81.0°; as a high-pass filter it passes nearly all of it.

## Step response in the time domain

The same RC sets how fast the output follows a step, which matters for sensor signals and digital inputs:

| Time | Default value | Output reached |
|---|---|---|
| 1 τ | 1 ms | 63.2% |
| 2 τ | 2 ms | 86.5% |
| 3 τ | 3 ms | 95.0% |
| 4 τ | 4 ms | 98.2% |
| 5 τ | 5 ms | 99.3% |

Five time constants, 5 ms here, is the usual rule for "settled".

## Filtering mains hum on a slow signal

A 4 to 20 mA loop signal read across 250 Ω often carries 50 Hz pickup. Putting the cutoff at 5 Hz with a 10 µF film or tantalum capacitor needs R = 1 ÷ (2π × 5 × 10 µF) = **3,183 Ω**, and attenuates 50 Hz by 20.0 dB. The default 159 Hz filter would barely touch it, so the cutoff must sit well below the interference, not near it.

## Common mistakes

- **Dropping the 2π.** 1 ÷ RC gives 1,000 rad/s, not hertz; the cutoff is 159.2 Hz.
- **Ignoring the load.** A 10 kΩ load across the capacitor halves the passband level to 0.50 and moves the cutoff to 318 Hz, because the source sees R in parallel with the load.
- **Expecting two stages to keep the same cutoff.** Two identical buffered sections are −6 dB at f<sub>c</sub> and −3 dB at 0.644 f<sub>c</sub>, 102 Hz for the defaults; without a buffer the second stage loads the first and the result is lower still.
- **Mixing nF and µF.** 100 nF is 0.1 µF; entering 100 µF makes the cutoff a thousand times lower.
- **Using a polarised electrolytic in a high-pass filter** where the DC across it can reverse.
