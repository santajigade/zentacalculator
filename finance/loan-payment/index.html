    A loan's monthly payment depends on three things: the amount borrowed, the interest rate and the number of months. This loan payment calculator gives the equal monthly instalment (EMI) on a reducing balance, the total repaid and the total interest, and can solve for the amount, rate or term instead.

    Borrowers use it to budget before signing for a home, car or personal loan, and to compare offers by their full cost.

    How to use the loan payment calculator

    Enter the loan amount, interest rate and term to see your monthly payment (EMI), the total you will repay and the total interest. You can also work backwards:

    • How much can I borrow? → press × on Loan amount and type the monthly payment you can afford.
    • How long will it take? → press × on Loan term and type your monthly payment.
    • What rate am I being charged? → press × on the rate and type the amount, term and payment from your offer.

    Switch the term to months if your lender quotes it that way.

    Loan payment formula

    EMI = P × i × (1 + i)n ÷ ((1 + i)n − 1)

    • P — loan amount
    • i — monthly interest rate = annual rate ÷ 12
    • n — number of monthly payments = years × 12

    Worked example

    A loan of 200,000 at 9% for 5 years:

    • i = 0.09 ÷ 12 = 0.0075
    • n = 60
    • EMI = 4,151.67 per month
    • Total paid = 4,151.67 × 60 = 249,100.26
    • Total interest = 49,100.26

    How the term changes the cost

    For 200,000 at 9%:

    Term Monthly payment Total interest
    3 years 6,359.95 28,958.08
    5 years 4,151.67 49,100.26
    10 years 2,533.52 104,021.86
    20 years 1,799.45 231,868.46

    A longer term lowers each payment but you pay much more interest overall.

    Amortization: where each payment goes

    200,000 at 9% for 5 years (payment 4,151.67 a month):

    Year Principal repaid Interest paid Balance at year end
    1 33,166 16,654 166,834
    2 36,277 13,543 130,557
    3 39,680 10,140 90,877
    4 43,403 6,418 47,474
    5 47,474 2,346 0

    Early payments are mostly interest. As the balance falls, more of each payment repays the loan. This is why extra payments in the first years save the most.

    Fixed vs floating rate

    Fixed rate Floating (variable) rate
    Payment Same for the whole term Changes with a benchmark rate
    Risk You know the cost upfront Can rise or fall
    Typical use Car and personal loans Home loans, business loans

    With a floating rate, recalculate whenever the rate changes to see your new payment.

    Flat rate vs reducing balance

    Some lenders quote a flat rate, charging interest on the full original amount for the whole term. A 9% flat rate is far more expensive than 9% on a reducing balance — roughly equal to 16% on a reducing balance over 5 years. Always ask for the reducing-balance rate or the APR when comparing offers.

    Tips before you borrow

    • Compare offers by total cost, not just the monthly payment.
    • Check for prepayment penalties if you plan to pay early.
    • Keep total loan payments within a comfortable share of your income.

    Worked example: a prepayment after one year

    Using the default loan (200,000 at 9% for 5 years, EMI 4,151.67), the balance after 12 payments is 166,834.00. A one-time prepayment of 20,000 at that point lowers it to 146,834.00. The lender then offers two choices:

    • Keep the EMI, shorten the term. n = ln(1 ÷ (1 − B × i ÷ EMI)) ÷ ln(1 + i) = 41.25 months, so the loan ends after 54 payments instead of 60 (the last one is 1,047.68). Interest saved: 8,014.02.
    • Keep the term, cut the EMI. The remaining 48 payments fall to 3,653.97. Interest saved: 3,889.64.

    Shortening the term saves more, because the principal is cleared sooner. Cutting the EMI suits someone whose budget is tight.

    How the rate changes the payment

    For 200,000 over 5 years:

    Annual rate Monthly payment Total interest
    7% 3,960.24 37,614.38
    8% 4,055.28 43,316.73
    9% 4,151.67 49,100.26
    10% 4,249.41 54,964.54
    11% 4,348.48 60,909.08
    12% 4,448.89 66,933.37
    14% 4,653.65 79,219.01

    Each extra percentage point adds roughly 95 to 100 a month on this loan, and about 5,700 to 5,800 in interest over the term.

    Fees change the real rate

    A 2% processing fee of 4,000 means you receive 196,000 but still repay 4,151.67 for 60 months. Solving for the rate that makes those payments worth 196,000 gives an effective annual rate of 9.87%, not 9%. Compare loans on this effective rate (the APR), which also captures insurance or other charges deducted at the start.

    Common mistakes

    • Typing a monthly rate into an annual field. 0.75% a month is 9% a year; entering 0.75 as the annual rate gives a payment far too small.
    • Assuming a floating rate rise changes the EMI. Many home lenders keep the EMI and extend the term instead, which can add years of payments; ask which option applies.
    • Ignoring insurance bundled into the loan. A single premium added to the principal earns interest for the whole term.
    • Prepaying late in the term. By year five most of each EMI is already principal, so a prepayment then saves little interest.
    • Comparing loans of different terms by EMI alone. A lower EMI over a longer term can cost more in total; compare total interest and the APR.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is EMI calculated?

    EMI = P × i × (1 + i)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + i)ⁿ − 1), where P is the loan amount, i the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12) and n the number of months.

    How can I reduce my total interest?

    Choose a shorter term, negotiate a lower rate, or make prepayments. The chart shows how the monthly payment changes with term.

    How much can I borrow for a given monthly payment?

    Press × on Loan amount, then type the monthly payment you can afford, the rate and the term.

    Does this include fees and insurance?

    No. Processing fees, insurance and taxes are extra. Add them separately to compare real costs.

    What is an amortization schedule?

    A table showing each payment split into interest and principal, and the balance left after it. The table above summarises it by year.

    Is it better to take a shorter or longer loan?

    A shorter loan has higher payments but much lower total interest. Choose the shortest term whose payment you can comfortably afford.

    Is it better to reduce the EMI or the tenure after a prepayment?

    Reducing the tenure saves more interest. On a 200,000 loan at 9% over 5 years, prepaying 20,000 after a year saves about 8,014 by shortening the term, against about 3,890 by lowering the EMI.

    How does a processing fee affect the interest rate?

    The fee reduces the money you receive while the payments stay the same, so the effective rate rises. A 2% fee on a 9% five year loan gives an effective rate of about 9.87%.
