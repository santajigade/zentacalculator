## US vs UK gallons

They are different. A **US gallon** is 3.785 litres; a **UK (imperial) gallon** is 4.546 litres — about 20% larger. Fuel-economy figures in miles per gallon are therefore not directly comparable between the two countries.

## Worked example with the default value

The converter opens with **1 litre**. Because 1 L is exactly 0.001 m³, every other field follows from one division:

- Millilitres: 0.001 ÷ 10⁻⁶ = **1,000 mL**
- Cubic metres: **0.001 m³**
- US gallons: 0.001 ÷ 0.003785411784 = **0.264172 US gal**
- UK gallons: 0.001 ÷ 0.00454609 = **0.219969 UK gal**
- Cubic feet: 0.001 ÷ 0.0283168 = **0.0353147 ft³**
- Cubic inches: 0.001 ÷ 0.000016387 = **61.0237 in³**

## Sizing a water tank

A rectangular tank measuring 2 m × 1.5 m × 1.2 m holds 2 × 1.5 × 1.2 = **3.60 m³**. Typing 3.6 into the cubic metre field gives **3,600 L**, **951 US gallons**, **792 UK gallons** or **127.1 ft³**. Filled from a garden hose delivering 15 L/min, it takes 3,600 ÷ 15 = 240 minutes, about **4.0 hours**. Measure the inside dimensions, not the outside: a 50 mm wall on a plastic tank of this size cuts the usable volume by several percent.

## Where the definitions come from

The litre is defined as exactly one cubic decimetre. The US gallon is defined as exactly 231 cubic inches, and the inch as exactly 25.4 mm, which fixes the US gallon at 3.785411784 L. The UK gallon is defined directly as exactly 4.54609 L. Fluid ounces also differ: a US fluid ounce is 29.574 mL (1/128 US gal), while a UK fluid ounce is 28.413 mL (1/160 UK gal), so the US fluid ounce is the larger of the two even though the UK gallon is bigger.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up US and UK gallons.** Product labels in the UK, Canada (older documents) and some Gulf countries may use imperial gallons; US data sheets always mean US gallons. The error is about 20%.
- **Converting cubic metres as if they were linear.** 1 m is 100 cm, but 1 m³ is 1,000,000 cm³. Cube the length factor whenever you convert a volume.
- **Treating litres of any liquid as kilograms.** Only water near 4 °C is close to 1 kg/L. Diesel is about 0.83 kg/L and honey about 1.4 kg/L, so convert by mass with the density.
- **Using nominal tank capacity.** Tanks are sold by brim or nominal volume; the working volume below the overflow is often 5% to 10% less.
- **Confusing gas volumes at different conditions.** A cubic metre of gas at 10 bar holds ten times the gas of a cubic metre at 1 bar. Normal or standard cubic metres need the pressure and temperature stated.
