## How to use the solution preparation calculator

Enter the **concentration** you want, the **final volume** of solution, the **molar mass** of the solid you are weighing and its **purity** from the label or certificate of analysis. The calculator returns the moles needed and the mass to weigh. Clear the mass and enter a mass you have to find the concentration it makes.

## Solution preparation formula

<p class="formula">m = c × V × M ÷ purity</p>

with c in mol/L, V in litres, M in g/mol and purity as a fraction.

## Worked example

500 mL of 0.1 M NaCl (58.44 g/mol) from 99.5% pure salt:

- Moles = 0.1 × 0.500 = **0.050 mol**
- Mass = 0.05 × 58.44 ÷ 0.995 = **2.9367 g**

## Good practice

- Use an analytical balance and a Class A volumetric flask for standard solutions.
- Hygroscopic solids such as NaOH absorb water while weighing — standardise those solutions by titration.
- To prepare a weaker solution from a stock instead, use the [dilution calculator](/chemistry/dilution/).

## Percent and ppm solutions

Not every recipe is given in mol/L:

- **% w/v** means grams per 100 mL. 0.9% saline is 0.9 g per 100 mL, or **9 g NaCl per litre**.
- **ppm** in dilute water solutions is mg per litre. A 1,000 ppm standard holds **1 g per litre** of the element or ion. If the salt also contains other atoms, scale by the mass fraction. For example, a 1,000 ppm sodium standard needs 1 × 58.44 ÷ 22.99 = **2.542 g NaCl** per litre.

## Making a solution from a liquid stock

For concentrated acids and bases, first work out the molarity of the stock:

<p class="formula">C<sub>stock</sub> = density (g/mL) × 1,000 × mass fraction ÷ molar mass</p>

37% HCl at 1.19 g/mL is 1.19 × 1,000 × 0.37 ÷ 36.46 = **12.1 M**. To make 1 L of 1 M HCl you need 1 ÷ 12.1 L = **82.8 mL** of concentrated acid. Then use the [dilution calculator](/chemistry/dilution/) for any other strength.

## Common mistakes

- **Entering mL as litres.** It gives an answer 1,000 times too large. This calculator converts the volume for you.
- **Using the anhydrous molar mass for a hydrate.** Use the molar mass of the exact solid on the bottle label.
- **Dissolving the solid in the full volume of water.** The solid adds volume. Dissolve it in less water, then make up to the mark.
- **Filling the flask while the solution is warm.** Volumetric flasks are calibrated at 20 °C. Let heat from dissolving NaOH or acids dissipate first.
- **Trusting the weighed mass of hygroscopic solids.** NaOH and KOH pick up water as you weigh them, so standardise these solutions.
