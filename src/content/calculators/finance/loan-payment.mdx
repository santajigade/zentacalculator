## How to use the loan payment calculator

Enter the **loan amount**, **interest rate** and **term** to see your monthly payment (EMI), the total you will repay and the total interest. You can also work backwards:

- *How much can I borrow?* → press × on Loan amount and type the monthly payment you can afford.
- *How long will it take?* → press × on Loan term and type your monthly payment.
- *What rate am I being charged?* → press × on the rate and type the amount, term and payment from your offer.

Switch the term to months if your lender quotes it that way.

## Loan payment formula

<p class="formula">EMI = P × i × (1 + i)<sup>n</sup> ÷ ((1 + i)<sup>n</sup> − 1)</p>

- **P** — loan amount
- **i** — monthly interest rate = annual rate ÷ 12
- **n** — number of monthly payments = years × 12

## Worked example

A loan of 200,000 at 9% for 5 years:

- i = 0.09 ÷ 12 = 0.0075
- n = 60
- EMI = **4,151.67** per month
- Total paid = 4,151.67 × 60 = **249,100.26**
- Total interest = **49,100.26**

## How the term changes the cost

For 200,000 at 9%:

| Term | Monthly payment | Total interest |
|---|---|---|
| 3 years | 6,359.95 | 28,958.08 |
| 5 years | 4,151.67 | 49,100.26 |
| 10 years | 2,533.52 | 104,021.86 |
| 20 years | 1,799.45 | 231,868.46 |

A longer term lowers each payment but you pay much more interest overall.

## Amortization: where each payment goes

200,000 at 9% for 5 years (payment 4,151.67 a month):

| Year | Principal repaid | Interest paid | Balance at year end |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 33,166 | 16,654 | 166,834 |
| 2 | 36,277 | 13,543 | 130,557 |
| 3 | 39,680 | 10,140 | 90,877 |
| 4 | 43,403 | 6,418 | 47,474 |
| 5 | 47,474 | 2,346 | 0 |

Early payments are mostly interest. As the balance falls, more of each payment repays the loan. This is why extra payments in the first years save the most.

## Fixed vs floating rate

| | Fixed rate | Floating (variable) rate |
|---|---|---|
| Payment | Same for the whole term | Changes with a benchmark rate |
| Risk | You know the cost upfront | Can rise or fall |
| Typical use | Car and personal loans | Home loans, business loans |

With a floating rate, recalculate whenever the rate changes to see your new payment.

## Flat rate vs reducing balance

Some lenders quote a **flat rate**, charging interest on the full original amount for the whole term. A 9% flat rate is far more expensive than 9% on a reducing balance — roughly equal to 16% on a reducing balance over 5 years. Always ask for the reducing-balance rate or the APR when comparing offers.

## Tips before you borrow

- Compare offers by **total cost**, not just the monthly payment.
- Check for prepayment penalties if you plan to pay early.
- Keep total loan payments within a comfortable share of your income.
