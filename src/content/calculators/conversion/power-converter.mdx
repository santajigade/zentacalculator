## How to use the power converter

Type a power in **any** unit and the others update instantly. Useful for motors, engines, air conditioners and heaters.

## Power formula

<p class="formula">1 kW = 1.341 hp = 1.360 PS = 3,412 BTU/h   1 TR = 3.517 kW = 12,000 BTU/h</p>

**Example:** an 11 kW motor is **14.75 hp**. A 1.5-ton air conditioner is 1.5 × 3.517 = **5.28 kW** of cooling (18,000 BTU/h).

## Motor sizes

Electric motors are often sold in standard kW ratings with an hp equivalent: 0.75 kW (1 hp), 1.5 kW (2 hp), 2.2 kW (3 hp), 3.7 kW (5 hp), 7.5 kW (10 hp), 11 kW (15 hp). The power on the nameplate is the **output** at the shaft; the electrical input is higher because of losses.

## Car engine power

Car brochures may list kW, hp or PS. 100 kW = 134 hp = 136 PS. In the US, “hp” usually means mechanical horsepower; in Europe and Asia, “hp” on a brochure often means PS.

## Electrical power

For electrical loads, power in watts = volts × amps (× power factor for AC). See the [Ohm's law calculator](/physics/ohms-law/) for related calculations.

## hp vs PS

Mechanical (imperial) horsepower is 745.7 W. Metric horsepower (PS, CV, pk) is 735.5 W — about 1.4% smaller. Car brochures in Europe and Asia often quote PS.

## Air conditioner sizes

AC capacity is often given in tons or BTU/h of **cooling**. The electrical power drawn is much lower — divide the cooling kW by the unit's efficiency (EER/COP, often 3–4).

## Worked example with the default value

The calculator starts at **1 kW**, which is 1000 W:

- 1000 ÷ 745.6999 = **1.3410 hp**
- 1000 ÷ 735.4987 = **1.3596 PS**
- 1000 ÷ 0.29307107 = **3,412.1 BTU/h**
- 1000 ÷ 3516.85 = **0.28435 TR**, and 0.001 MW

## Example: a small chiller, from tons to kW and back

A cold store uses a **7.5 TR** chiller. Its cooling output is 7.5 × 3.5169 = **26.38 kW**, or 90,000 BTU/h. With a coefficient of performance (COP) of 3.2, the compressor draws about 26.38 ÷ 3.2 = **8.24 kW** of electricity, roughly 11.1 hp, so the motor and cable are sized for about a third of the cooling figure.

To check what the chiller actually delivers, measure the chilled water flow and temperature drop. With 4.5 m³/h and a 5 K difference, cooling = 4.5 ÷ 3600 × 1000 × 4.186 × 5 = **26.16 kW**, which is 7.44 TR. That is close to the rating, so the unit is working near full capacity. A 2,000 W fan heater in the same room adds 6,824 BTU/h of load.

Boiler horsepower is a steam rating, about 13 times a mechanical horsepower, so never mix the two.

## How power is measured

Electrical input is read with a power analyser or a clamp wattmeter that measures voltage, current and power factor together. Shaft power is measured with a torque transducer or dynamometer and the speed, using P = 2π × N × T. Heating and cooling capacity is worked out from mass flow, specific heat and temperature change, as in the chiller check above, which is why air conditioner ratings are given in BTU/h or TR rather than watts of electricity.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing BTU when you mean BTU/h.** BTU is energy; BTU/h is power. A "12,000 BTU" air conditioner is 12,000 BTU/h.
- **Treating cooling capacity as electrical load.** A 1.5 TR unit gives 5.28 kW of cooling but, at a COP of 3.5, draws only about 1.51 kW.
- **Using kVA as kW.** Generator and UPS ratings in kVA must be multiplied by the power factor before comparing with kW loads.
- **Mixing hp and PS.** Metric PS is 1.4% smaller than mechanical hp, so 100 PS is 98.6 hp.
- **Confusing boiler horsepower with engine horsepower.** One boiler hp is 9.81 kW, not 0.746 kW.
