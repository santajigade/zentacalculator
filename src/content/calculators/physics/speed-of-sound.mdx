## How to use the speed of sound calculator

Enter the gas's **heat capacity ratio γ**, its **molar mass** and the **temperature**. The calculator returns the speed of sound and, for the frequency you enter, the wavelength. Values for common gases are in the table below; for mixtures use the mole-weighted molar mass.

## Speed of sound formula

<p class="formula">v = √(γ R T ÷ M) &nbsp;·&nbsp; λ = v ÷ f</p>

R = 8.314 J/(mol·K), T is the absolute temperature in kelvin and M the molar mass in kg/mol.

## Example

Dry air (γ = 1.4, M = 28.965 g/mol) at 20 °C:

- v = √(1.4 × 8.314 × 293.15 ÷ 0.028965) = **343.2 m/s**
- Wavelength of a 1 kHz tone = 343.2 ÷ 1000 = **0.3432 m**

## Practical notes

- An ultrasonic level gauge that assumes 343 m/s but works in 60 °C air reads the distance about 6.6% short — the reason these instruments carry a temperature sensor. See the [ultrasonic level calculator](/engineering/ultrasonic-level/).
- Sound travels much faster in liquids and solids (water ≈ 1,480 m/s, steel ≈ 5,900 m/s); this formula applies to gases only.

## Time of flight measurement

An ultrasonic level transmitter times an echo from the liquid surface and halves the round trip: distance = v × t ÷ 2. In 20 °C air at 343.2 m/s, a surface 2 m below the sensor returns its echo after 11.654 ms. If the tank headspace is really at 60 °C, sound travels at 365.9 m/s, so the same 11.654 ms echo means the surface is 2.132 m away. A gauge that ignored the temperature would report 2 m, an error of 132 mm. Vapour that is not air, such as solvent or CO₂ over a fermenter, changes γ and M too, which a single temperature sensor cannot correct.

## Gas mixtures

For a mixture, average the molar mass by mole fraction and work out γ from the mole-weighted molar heat capacity, not by averaging γ directly. Biogas of 60% methane and 40% CO₂ has M = 27.23 g/mol and c<sub>p</sub> = 36.27 J/(mol·K), so γ = 36.27 ÷ (36.27 − 8.314) = 1.297. At 35 °C sound travels at **349 m/s**, which is why ultrasonic gas meters and biogas analysers can infer methane content from sound speed.

## Common mistakes

- **Entering molar mass in kg/mol into a g/mol field.** 0.028965 instead of 28.965 gives a speed about 32 times too high.
- **Using Celsius in the formula by hand.** T must be in kelvin; the calculator converts it, but a spreadsheet will not.
- **Averaging γ by mole fraction.** It is the heat capacities that average, as in the biogas example above.
- **Expecting frequency to change the speed.** In ordinary gases sound is non-dispersive; frequency changes only the wavelength.
- **Using the gas formula for liquids or solids.** Those depend on bulk or elastic modulus and density instead.
