## How to use the pond liner size & volume calculator

Enter the pond's **length**, **width**, **maximum depth** and the **overlap** you want around the edge. The liner size and approximate volume are calculated.

## Example

A 3 × 2 m pond, 0.6 m deep, 0.3 m overlap:

- Liner = **4.8 × 3.8 m**
- Volume ≈ **2,160 litres**

## Liner sizes for common ponds

| Pond (L × W × D) | Liner size | Water volume (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 × 1.5 × 0.6 m | 3.8 × 3.3 m | 1,080 L |
| 3 × 2 × 0.6 m | 4.8 × 3.8 m | 2,160 L |
| 3 × 2 × 0.8 m | 5.2 × 4.2 m | 2,880 L |
| 4 × 3 × 1.0 m | 6.6 × 5.6 m | 7,200 L |
| 5 × 4 × 1.2 m | 8.0 × 7.0 m | 14,400 L |

## Liner materials

| Material | Lifespan | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| PVC | 10–20 years | Cheaper, less UV-resistant |
| EPDM rubber | 20+ years | Flexible, fish-safe, popular |
| Butyl rubber | 20–30+ years | Very durable, more expensive |
| HDPE | 20+ years | Stiff, used for large ponds and lagoons |

Use an underlay (geotextile or old carpet) to protect the liner from stones and roots.

## Depth for fish

For fish that overwinter outdoors, many keepers aim for at least **0.8–1 m** in one area so the water does not freeze solid and stays cooler in summer.

## Pump size

Circulate the full pond volume at least once every 1–2 hours for ponds with fish. A 3,000 L pond needs a pump delivering about 1,500–3,000 L/h at the required head.

## Worked example: ordering for a 3 × 2 m pond

With the default inputs (length 3 m, width 2 m, maximum depth 0.6 m, overlap 0.3 m):

- Liner length = 3 + 2 × 0.6 + 2 × 0.3 = **4.8 m**
- Liner width = 2 + 2 × 0.6 + 2 × 0.3 = **3.8 m**
- Liner area = 4.8 × 3.8 = **18.24 m²**, about 3.0 times the 6 m² water surface
- Water volume ≈ 3 × 2 × 0.6 × 0.6 = **2,160 litres**

A 1 mm EPDM sheet of that size weighs roughly 21 kg, so two people are needed to lay it. Order the same size of underlay. Filling from a garden hose at about 15 L/min takes around 144 minutes.

## Measuring an irregular pond

Most ponds are kidney or free form rather than rectangles. Dig the hole first, then measure the liner rather than the plan:

1. Run a tape or string across the **longest** part of the hole, pushing it down so it follows the floor and both walls, and add twice the overlap. That is the liner length.
2. Repeat across the **widest** part at right angles for the liner width.
3. Use the deepest point for depth even if most of the pond is shallower; shelves do not change the liner size, because the sheet simply folds onto them.

For the volume of a free form pond, a more reliable figure comes from a water meter reading taken before and after filling.

## Volume factor by pond shape

The calculator uses 0.6 of the box volume. The right factor depends on how steep the sides are; for the default 3 × 2 × 0.6 m hole:

| Factor | Pond shape | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | Saucer shape, gently sloping all round | 1,800 L |
| 0.6 | Typical garden pond with one shelf | 2,160 L |
| 0.7 | Steep sides, small marginal shelf | 2,520 L |
| 0.85 | Near vertical walls (formal pond) | 3,060 L |

Use the measured or best estimated volume when dosing dechlorinator, salt or medicines, since an overdose based on the box volume can harm fish.

Liners are sold from standard roll widths, so round both dimensions up to the next size the supplier stocks rather than joining strips.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding the depth only once.** The sheet climbs both walls, so each dimension needs 2 × depth.
- **Skimping on overlap.** Less than about 0.3 m leaves too little to bury or trap under slabs, and the edge can slip in once the water weight pulls it down.
- **Measuring the flat plan of a sloped hole.** A tape laid across the top underestimates the length the liner has to follow; measure along the contour.
- **Filling before trimming.** Partly fill the pond first so the liner settles into folds, then trim the excess edge.
- **Leaving the liner exposed above water.** UV degrades PVC quickly; cover the top band with stones, soil or edging.
