## How to use this converter

Type a power in **any** unit and the others update instantly. Useful for motors, engines, air conditioners and heaters.

## Formula

<p class="formula">1 kW = 1.341 hp = 1.360 PS = 3,412 BTU/h   1 TR = 3.517 kW = 12,000 BTU/h</p>

**Example:** an 11 kW motor is **14.75 hp**. A 1.5-ton air conditioner is 1.5 × 3.517 = **5.28 kW** of cooling (18,000 BTU/h).

## Motor sizes

Electric motors are often sold in standard kW ratings with an hp equivalent: 0.75 kW (1 hp), 1.5 kW (2 hp), 2.2 kW (3 hp), 3.7 kW (5 hp), 7.5 kW (10 hp), 11 kW (15 hp). The power on the nameplate is the **output** at the shaft; the electrical input is higher because of losses.

## Car engine power

Car brochures may list kW, hp or PS. 100 kW = 134 hp = 136 PS. In the US, “hp” usually means mechanical horsepower; in Europe and Asia, “hp” on a brochure often means PS.

## Electrical power

For electrical loads, power in watts = volts × amps (× power factor for AC). See the [Ohm's law calculator](/physics/ohms-law/) for related calculations.

## hp vs PS

Mechanical (imperial) horsepower is 745.7 W. Metric horsepower (PS, CV, pk) is 735.5 W — about 1.4% smaller. Car brochures in Europe and Asia often quote PS.

## Air conditioner sizes

AC capacity is often given in tons or BTU/h of **cooling**. The electrical power drawn is much lower — divide the cooling kW by the unit's efficiency (EER/COP, often 3–4).
