## How to use the calibration error calculator

Enter the **output range** of the instrument (4 and 20 for a 4–20 mA transmitter), the **expected value** from your reference standard, the **measured value** and the **tolerance** as ± % of span. The calculator gives the error, the error as a percentage of span and of reading, and the share of the tolerance used: **over 100% means the point fails**.

## Calibration error formulas

<p class="formula">Error = Measured − Expected</p>

<p class="formula">% of span = Error ÷ (URV − LRV) × 100 &nbsp;·&nbsp; % of reading = Error ÷ Expected × 100</p>

<p class="formula">Tolerance used = |% of span| ÷ Tolerance × 100</p>

## Example

A 4–20 mA pressure transmitter at 50% input should read 12.000 mA; it reads 12.050 mA. Tolerance ±0.25% of span:

- Error = **+0.050 mA**
- % of span = 0.05 ÷ 16 = **0.3125%**
- % of reading = 0.05 ÷ 12 = **0.417%**
- Tolerance used = 0.3125 ÷ 0.25 = **125%** → as-found **fail**, adjust and record as-left

## Tolerance in mA for a 4–20 mA loop

| Tolerance (% of span) | ± mA | ± µA |
|---|---|---|
| ±0.1% | 0.016 mA | 16 µA |
| ±0.25% | 0.040 mA | 40 µA |
| ±0.5% | 0.080 mA | 80 µA |
| ±1.0% | 0.160 mA | 160 µA |
| ±2.0% | 0.320 mA | 320 µA |

## Good calibration practice

- Use a reference at least **4 times more accurate** than the tolerance (test uncertainty ratio 4:1).
- Record **as-found** data before touching zero or span — it is what tells you whether the process was measured correctly since the last calibration.
- Test up and down the scale to reveal **hysteresis**; the [5-point calibration calculator](/engineering/five-point-calibration/) lists the test points.

## Converting the error to process units

If the transmitter in the example is ranged 0 to 10 bar, the 0.050 mA error is 0.050 ÷ 16 × 10 = **0.03125 bar** (31.25 mbar). Percent of span is the same whichever unit you use, so 0.3125% of span is also 0.3125% of the 0 to 10 bar range. The tolerance of ±0.25% of span is ±0.040 mA, so the reference used to test it should be accurate to about 0.040 ÷ 4 = **±0.010 mA** or better.

For a 4 to 20 mA, 0 to 10 bar transmitter with a ±0.25% of span tolerance:

## Second example: a temperature transmitter

The calculator works in any unit. A temperature transmitter ranged 0 to 200 °C is checked in a dry block at 150 °C and reads 150.6 °C, with a tolerance of ±0.5% of span:

- Error = 150.6 − 150 = **+0.6 °C**
- % of span = 0.6 ÷ 200 = **0.30%**, % of reading = 0.6 ÷ 150 = 0.40%
- Tolerance used = 0.30 ÷ 0.5 = **60%**, so this point passes as found

## Reading a five-point run: zero error or span error?

A single point cannot tell you what to adjust. This as-found run on a 4 to 20 mA transmitter with ±0.25% of span tolerance shows an error that grows up the scale:

| Input | Expected | Measured | Error | % of span | Tolerance used |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 4.000 mA | 4.008 mA | +0.008 mA | 0.0500% | 20% |
| 25% | 8.000 mA | 8.017 mA | +0.017 mA | 0.1062% | 42% |
| 50% | 12.000 mA | 12.027 mA | +0.027 mA | 0.1687% | 67% |
| 75% | 16.000 mA | 16.036 mA | +0.036 mA | 0.2250% | 90% |
| 100% | 20.000 mA | 20.046 mA | +0.046 mA | 0.2875% | 115% |

A straight-line fit through the five points gives a zero error of **+0.0078 mA** at 4 mA and a span error of **+0.0380 mA** over the 16 mA span (0.238% of span). 1 point fails, and the pattern says the span adjustment is the main fix, with a small zero trim. An error that is the same at every point would be a pure zero shift.

## Is the calibrator good enough?

A typical loop calibrator specified at ±(0.01% of reading + 2 µA) has an uncertainty of 0.0001 × 12 + 0.002 = **3.2 µA** at 12 mA. Against the ±40 µA tolerance that is a test uncertainty ratio of 40 ÷ 3.2 = **12.5:1**, comfortably above the 4:1 rule.

## Calibration in practice

Let the instrument and reference stabilise at the test temperature, exercise a pressure transmitter to full range once or twice, and then record the as-found readings at each point before adjusting anything. If any point fails, adjust zero and span, repeat the full run and record the as-left results. Many sites apply a **guard band**, for example adjusting whenever the error is above 80% of tolerance (here ±0.032 mA), so the instrument is unlikely to drift out before the next calibration.

## Common mistakes

- **Dividing by the reading instead of the span.** Percent of reading makes the same error look several times larger near the bottom of the scale; most tolerances are in percent of span.
- **Losing the sign.** A positive error means the instrument reads high. Keep the sign on the record so drift trends can be seen over several calibrations.
- **Adjusting before recording as-found.** Without as-found data there is no way to tell whether the process was measured correctly since the last calibration.
- **Using a reference that is too coarse.** A calibrator with uncertainty close to the tolerance cannot prove a pass or a fail.
- **Mixing units across inputs.** Range, expected and measured values must all be in mA, or all in bar; mixing them gives a meaningless result.
