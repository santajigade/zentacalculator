## How to use the thin lens equation calculator

Enter the **focal length** and **object distance**. The calculator finds the image distance, magnification and lens power. Use the sign convention below. Any two of f, dₒ and dᵢ determine the third.

## The equations

<p class="formula">1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ &nbsp;·&nbsp; m = −dᵢ ÷ dₒ &nbsp;·&nbsp; P = 1 ÷ f</p>

## Sign convention (real-is-positive)

| Quantity | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| f | Converging lens, concave mirror | Diverging lens, convex mirror |
| dᵢ | Real image | Virtual image |
| m | Upright image | Inverted image |

## Converging lens, f = 10 cm

| Object distance | Image distance | Magnification | Image |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 cm | 12.5 cm | -0.25 | real, inverted, reduced |
| 30 cm | 15.0 cm | -0.50 | real, inverted, reduced |
| 20 cm | 20.0 cm | -1.00 | real, inverted, same size |
| 15 cm | 30.0 cm | -2.00 | real, inverted, enlarged |
| 12 cm | 60.0 cm | -5.00 | real, inverted, enlarged |
| 8 cm | -40.0 cm | 5.00 | virtual, upright, enlarged |
| 5 cm | -10.0 cm | 2.00 | virtual, upright, enlarged |

- Object beyond 2f: real, inverted, smaller image (cameras, eyes).
- Object at 2f: same-size inverted image.
- Object between f and 2f: enlarged inverted image (projectors).
- Object inside f: virtual, upright, enlarged (magnifying glass).

## Lenses in contact

Powers add: P = P₁ + P₂. Two +5 D lenses together make a +10 D lens with a 10 cm focal length.

## Spectacles

Short-sighted (myopic) people wear diverging lenses with negative dioptres; long-sighted people and reading glasses use positive lenses.

## Worked example with the default values

A converging lens with f = 10 cm and an object 30 cm away:

1. 1/dᵢ = 1/f − 1/dₒ = 1/10 − 1/30 = 3/30 − 1/30 = 2/30, so **dᵢ = 15.0 cm** (positive, so a real image on the far side of the lens)
2. m = −dᵢ ÷ dₒ = −15 ÷ 30 = **-0.500**, so the image is inverted and half size
3. P = 1 ÷ f = 1 ÷ 0.10 m = **10.00 dioptres** (the focal length must be in metres for dioptres)

A 2 cm tall object therefore gives an image -1.0 cm tall, the minus sign showing it is upside down.

## Diverging lens, f = −10 cm

A diverging lens always gives a virtual, upright, reduced image, whatever the object distance:

| Object distance | Image distance | Magnification |
|---|---|---|
| 50 cm | -8.33 cm | 0.167 |
| 30 cm | -7.50 cm | 0.250 |
| 20 cm | -6.67 cm | 0.333 |
| 10 cm | -5.00 cm | 0.500 |
| 5 cm | -3.33 cm | 0.667 |

## Measuring the focal length of a real lens

The quickest method is to focus a distant window or tree onto a wall: the object is effectively at infinity, so the lens to wall distance is the focal length. For a better result use the displacement (Bessel) method. Fix an object and a screen D = 50 cm apart; a 10 cm lens gives a sharp image at two positions, dₒ = 36.18 cm and dₒ = 13.82 cm, which are 22.36 cm apart. Then f = (D² − s²) ÷ 4D = (2,500 − 500) ÷ 200 = **10.0 cm**. It only works when D is more than 4f, and it avoids measuring from the centre of a thick lens.

The same equation explains camera focusing. A 50 mm lens focused at 3 m needs dᵢ = 50.85 mm, so the lens moves only 0.85 mm forward from its infinity position.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing sign conventions.** This calculator uses real is positive. Textbooks using the Cartesian convention write 1/f = 1/v − 1/u with u negative, so do not copy signs between the two.
- **Using centimetres for dioptres.** P = 1 ÷ f only gives dioptres with f in metres: 10 cm is 0.1 m and +10 D, not +0.1 D.
- **Putting the object exactly at the focal point.** With dₒ = f the rays leave parallel and the image distance is infinite, so the calculator cannot return a finite value.
- **Forgetting that a convex mirror is diverging.** Enter a negative focal length for car wing mirrors and shop security mirrors.
- **Applying it to thick lenses.** For thick lenses and multi element camera lenses, distances are measured from the principal planes, not from the glass surface.
