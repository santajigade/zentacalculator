## How to use the typing speed calculator

Enter the number of **characters typed**, any **uncorrected errors** and the **time** in minutes. Gross WPM, net WPM and characters per minute are calculated.

## Typing speed formulas

<p class="formula">Gross WPM = (characters ÷ 5) ÷ minutes<br/>Net WPM = gross WPM − errors ÷ minutes</p>

## Worked example

A 5 minute test produces 1,250 characters (spaces included) with 5 uncorrected errors:

- Standard words = 1,250 ÷ 5 = 250
- Gross WPM = 250 ÷ 5 = **50.0**
- Net WPM = 50 - 5 ÷ 5 = **49.0**
- Characters per minute = 1,250 ÷ 5 = **250**
- Accuracy = (1,250 - 5) ÷ 1,250 = **99.6%**

### A one minute test

330 characters in 1 minute with 8 errors gives a gross speed of 66 WPM but a net speed of only **58 WPM**, at 97.6% accuracy. On a short test every error costs a full word per minute, so accuracy matters more than raw speed.

Many data-entry and transcription jobs ask for 50–60 WPM with high accuracy.

## Accuracy

<p class="formula">Accuracy = (characters − errors) ÷ characters × 100</p>

1,250 characters with 5 errors is 99.6% accuracy. Aim for 95% or more before trying to type faster.

## Words per minute vs keystrokes per hour

Data entry tests often use **keystrokes per hour (KPH)**: KPH = characters per minute × 60. The example's 250 characters per minute is **15,000 KPH**. Numeric keypad tests are scored in KPH because WPM makes little sense for strings of digits.

## Practice plan

- Learn proper finger placement on the home row (ASDF JKL;).
- Practise 10–15 minutes a day rather than one long session.
- Focus on accuracy first; speed follows.
- Use real text that matches your work, such as emails or code.

## How typing tests are scored

Most online and employment tests show a passage, time the typing for 1, 3 or 5 minutes and compare the result with the original text. Gross WPM uses every character typed; net WPM takes off one word per minute for each error left uncorrected. Errors you fix with backspace do not count against net WPM, but the time spent fixing them lowers the gross figure. Results from 1 minute tests usually run a few WPM higher than 5 minute tests, so a job advert asking for 50 WPM normally means a longer, accuracy checked test.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting real words.** Typing tests count every 5 characters as a word, so a passage of long technical words scores the same as one of short words.
- **Leaving out spaces.** Spaces and punctuation are keystrokes; dropping them cuts the result by about a sixth for normal English text.
- **Subtracting the total errors.** Net WPM subtracts errors per minute: 5 errors in 5 minutes costs 1 WPM, not 5.
- **Comparing tests of different lengths.** A 1 minute burst is not comparable with a 10 minute transcription test.
- **Chasing speed with low accuracy.** Below about 95% accuracy the corrections usually cost more time than the extra speed saves.
