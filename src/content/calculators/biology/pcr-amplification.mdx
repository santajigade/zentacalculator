## How to use the PCR amplification calculator

Enter the **starting copies**, **number of cycles** and **efficiency**. The theoretical number of copies and fold amplification are calculated.

## PCR amplification formula

<p class="formula">N = N₀ × (1 + E)<sup>n</sup></p>

## Example

1,000 copies, 30 cycles at 90% efficiency:

- Fold = 1.9³⁰ = **2.3e+08**
- Copies = **2.3e+11**

At 100% efficiency the same reaction would reach 1.07e+12 copies, which shows how much efficiency matters.

## Fold amplification by efficiency

| Cycles | 100% efficiency | 90% | 80% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 1.02e+03 | 613 | 357 |
| 20 | 1.05e+06 | 3.76e+05 | 1.27e+05 |
| 25 | 3.36e+07 | 9.31e+06 | 2.41e+06 |
| 30 | 1.07e+09 | 2.3e+08 | 4.55e+07 |
| 35 | 3.44e+10 | 5.71e+09 | 8.6e+08 |

A small drop in efficiency makes a big difference after 30 cycles.

## Plateau phase

In practice, PCR slows and stops (plateau) as primers and nucleotides run out and the polymerase loses activity. Most reactions plateau somewhere around 10¹¹ to 10¹² copies, regardless of the theoretical number.

## qPCR efficiency from a standard curve

Run a 10-fold dilution series and plot Ct against log₁₀(copies). The slope gives efficiency:

<p class="formula">E = 10<sup>−1/slope</sup> − 1</p>

A slope of −3.32 means 100% efficiency. Acceptable assays are usually 90% to 110% (slope −3.6 to −3.1).

## Ct and starting amount

Each cycle difference in Ct means about a 2-fold difference in starting template (at 100% efficiency). A sample with a Ct 3.3 cycles lower has about 10 times more template.

## Worked example with the default values

1,000 starting copies, 30 cycles at 90% efficiency:

- Fold amplification = (1 + 0.9)<sup>30</sup> = 1.9<sup>30</sup> = **2.305 × 10<sup>8</sup>**
- Copies after PCR = 1,000 × 2.305 × 10<sup>8</sup> = **2.305 × 10<sup>11</sup> copies**

At 100% efficiency the same reaction would give 2<sup>30</sup> = 1.074 × 10<sup>9</sup> fold and 1.074 × 10<sup>12</sup> copies, 4.7 times more. In a real 25 to 50 µL reaction the default result is already near the plateau, so the true yield will be lower than the theoretical figure.

## How many cycles to reach a target?

Rearranging the formula gives n = log(N ÷ N₀) ÷ log(1 + E). To reach 10¹⁰ copies from 1,000 starting copies takes log(10⁷) ÷ log(1.9) = **25.1 cycles** at 90% efficiency, or 23.3 cycles at 100%.

## Practical notes

- Use the fewest cycles that give enough product. Extra cycles past the plateau mostly add primer dimers and non-specific bands.
- For cloning or sequencing, start from more template rather than adding cycles, which also lowers the chance of copying polymerase errors.
- Count starting copies from DNA mass and length with a copy number calculation before planning a reaction.
