  - q: What is the difference between mean and median?
    a: The mean is the sum divided by the count. The median is the middle value when the numbers are sorted. The median is less affected by unusually large or small values.
  - q: How is the median found with an even number of values?
    a: Take the average of the two middle values. For 3, 5, 8, 10 the median is (5 + 8) ÷ 2 = 6.5.
  - q: What is the average of 10, 20 and 30?
    a: (10 + 20 + 30) ÷ 3 = 20.

    a: Yes. The mean of 1 and 2 is 1.5, which is not one of the values. The median of an even number of values can also fall between two values.

## How to use this calculator

Type or paste your numbers into the box — separated by commas, spaces or new lines. You can paste a column straight from a spreadsheet. All results update as you type.

## Formulas

<p class="formula">mean = (x₁ + x₂ + … + xₙ) ÷ n<br/>range = maximum − minimum</p>

The **median** is the middle value of the sorted list (or the average of the two middle values when n is even).

## Worked example

Numbers: 12, 15, 9, 21, 15, 18, 10

- Sum = 100, count = 7
- Mean = 100 ÷ 7 = **14.29**
- Sorted: 9, 10, 12, **15**, 15, 18, 21 → median = **15**
- Range = 21 − 9 = **12**
- Mode (most frequent value) = 15

## Mean or median?

| Situation | Better measure |
|---|---|
| Values evenly spread | Mean |
| A few very large or small values (salaries, house prices) | Median |
| Most common choice (shoe size, rating) | Mode |

Example: incomes of 30, 32, 35, 38 and 400 (thousand) have a mean of 107 but a median of 35. The median describes the typical person far better.

## Mean, median and mode compared

For the data 2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 10, 40:

| Measure | Value | How |
|---|---|---|
| Mean | 10 | Sum 70 ÷ 7 values |
| Median | 5 | Middle value when sorted |
| Mode | 3 | Most frequent value |
| Range | 38 | 40 − 2 |

The single large value (40) pulls the mean up to 10, while the median stays at 5. That is why median income and median house prices are often reported instead of the mean.

## Other kinds of average

| Average | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Arithmetic mean | Most everyday data | Test scores |
| Weighted mean | Values with different importance | Course grades with credits |
| Geometric mean | Growth rates and ratios | Average yearly investment return |
| Harmonic mean | Rates over the same distance | Average speed for a round trip |

Example: driving 60 km at 60 km/h and back at 40 km/h is **48 km/h** on average (harmonic mean), not 50.

## Finding a missing value for a target average

To reach an average of 80 over 5 tests when the first four average 76, you need 80 × 5 − 76 × 4 = **96** on the last test. The [grade needed calculator](/everyday-life/grade-needed/) does this for exams.

## Weighted averages

If some values count more than others (such as exam scores worth different credits), multiply each value by its weight, add them up, and divide by the total weight.
