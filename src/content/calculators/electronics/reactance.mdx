## How to use the reactance and impedance calculator

Enter the **frequency**, **inductance**, **capacitance** and series **resistance**. You get both reactances, the total impedance of the series RLC circuit and the phase angle. Set L or C to zero to analyse an RC or RL circuit (for an RL circuit, set C very large).

## Reactance and impedance formulas

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Inductive reactance | XL = 2πfL |
| Capacitive reactance | XC = 1 ÷ (2πfC) |
| Series impedance | Z = √(R² + (XL − XC)²) |
| Phase angle | φ = atan((XL − XC) ÷ R) |

## Current in an AC circuit

Ohm's law works with impedance: I = V ÷ Z. With 230 V across the default circuit, the current is 230 ÷ Z.

## Power factor

For a series circuit, power factor = cos φ = R ÷ Z. Inductive loads such as motors have lagging power factor; capacitors are added to correct it — see the [kVA to kW converter](/electrical/kva-to-kw/).

## Applications

- **Filters**: reactance changes with frequency, letting RC and LC circuits pass or block certain frequencies.
- **Motors and transformers**: their inductance limits current and sets power factor.
- **Speaker crossovers**: inductors send bass to woofers; capacitors send treble to tweeters.

## Worked example: the default circuit on 230 V

A 100 mH inductor, a 100 µF capacitor and a 20 Ω resistor in series at 50 Hz:

- X<sub>L</sub> = 2π × 50 × 0.1 = **31.42 Ω**
- X<sub>C</sub> = 1 ÷ (2π × 50 × 100 × 10⁻⁶) = **31.83 Ω**
- Z = √(20² + (31.42 − 31.83)²) = **20.004 Ω**
- φ = atan(-0.415 ÷ 20) = **-1.19°**, slightly capacitive

The two reactances almost cancel because the circuit's resonant frequency, 1 ÷ (2π√(LC)) = **50.33 Hz**, is close to 50 Hz. On 230 V the current is 230 ÷ 20.004 = **11.50 A** and the resistor dissipates **2,644 W**. The voltage across the inductor is 11.50 × 31.42 = **361 V** and across the capacitor **366 V**, far above the supply, because the two are in antiphase and their voltages mostly cancel.

## Measuring reactance in practice

An LCR meter applies a small AC test signal at a chosen frequency, commonly 100 Hz, 1 kHz or 10 kHz, and reports inductance or capacitance together with the series resistance (ESR). Measure at a frequency near the one the part will see, because iron cored inductors and electrolytic capacitors change value with frequency and level. In a working mains circuit, reactance can be found from a clamp meter current and a voltage reading as X = V ÷ I, provided the resistance is small or is subtracted.

## Common mistakes

- **Entering inductance in henries.** This calculator expects millihenries, so 0.1 means 0.1 mH and gives a reactance a thousand times too small.
- **Adding the reactances.** In a series circuit X<sub>L</sub> and X<sub>C</sub> subtract, because their voltages are 180° apart.
- **Using the series formula for a parallel circuit.** For L and C in parallel at 50 Hz the combined reactance is X<sub>L</sub>X<sub>C</sub> ÷ |X<sub>C</sub> − X<sub>L</sub>| = **2,409 Ω**, close to an open circuit, not a short.
- **Rating parts for the supply voltage only.** Near resonance the component voltages can be many times the supply, as the 361 V in the example shows.
- **Ignoring harmonics.** At the 5th harmonic (250 Hz) X<sub>L</sub> is five times larger and X<sub>C</sub> five times smaller, so a circuit tuned at 50 Hz behaves very differently for distorted waveforms.
