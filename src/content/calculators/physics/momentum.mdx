## How to use the momentum calculator

Enter the **mass** and the **velocity** to get the momentum in kg·m/s and the kinetic energy. To find a speed, clear the velocity and enter the momentum; to find a mass, clear the mass. Both mass and velocity have unit selectors (kg, g, lb; km/h, m/s, mph and more), and the calculation is always done in SI units. Enter a negative velocity for motion in the opposite direction.

## Momentum formula

<p class="formula">p = m × v &nbsp;·&nbsp; KE = ½ m v² = p² ÷ 2m</p>

p is in kg·m/s when m is in kg and v in m/s. The change in momentum equals the impulse, Δp = F × Δt.

## Worked example

A 160 g cricket ball bowled at 140 km/h:

- Speed in m/s = 140 ÷ 3.6 = 38.89 m/s
- Momentum p = 0.16 × 38.89 = **6.22 kg·m/s**
- Kinetic energy = ½ × 0.16 × 38.89² = **121.0 J**

A 1,200 kg car has the same momentum when crawling at only 0.019 km/h (0.52 cm/s) — yet at that speed its kinetic energy is just 0.016 J, because the energy depends on v².

## Conservation of momentum in a collision

In a collision with no outside forces, the total momentum before equals the total after:

<p class="formula">m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂</p>

If the objects stick together (a perfectly inelastic collision) the common final speed is v = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂) ÷ (m₁ + m₂). A 1,000 kg car at 20 m/s hitting a stationary car:

| Stationary car | Final speed | KE before | KE after | KE lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 500 kg | 13.33 m/s | 200 kJ | 133.3 kJ | 33% |
| 1,000 kg | 10.00 m/s | 200 kJ | 100.0 kJ | 50% |
| 1,500 kg | 8.00 m/s | 200 kJ | 80.0 kJ | 60% |
| 2,000 kg | 6.67 m/s | 200 kJ | 66.7 kJ | 67% |
| 5,000 kg | 3.33 m/s | 200 kJ | 33.3 kJ | 83% |

Momentum is conserved in every case, but most of the kinetic energy turns into deformation, heat and sound. For bouncing (elastic) collisions use the [elastic collision calculator](/physics/elastic-collision/).

## Stopping the ball: why catching time matters

The cricket ball's 6.22 kg·m/s must all be removed to stop it. Stopped against a rigid surface in 0.01 s the average force is 6.22 ÷ 0.01 = **622 N**; a fielder who draws the hands back so the catch takes 0.1 s feels only **62 N**. Same momentum change, one tenth of the force.

## Equal energy is not equal momentum

For the same 100 J of kinetic energy, the heavier object carries much more momentum, because p = √(2 m KE):

| Object | Mass | Speed for 100 J | Momentum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Golf ball | 0.0459 kg | 66.0 m/s | 3.03 kg·m/s |
| Cricket ball | 0.16 kg | 35.4 m/s | 5.66 kg·m/s |
| Football | 0.43 kg | 21.6 m/s | 9.27 kg·m/s |
| Shot put | 7.26 kg | 5.2 m/s | 38.11 kg·m/s |

This is why a slow, heavy object is harder to stop by hand than a fast, light one with the same energy.

## How momentum is measured

Momentum is not read directly; mass and velocity are measured separately. Balls are weighed and timed with a radar gun, light gates or high speed video. In school labs two light gates on an air track give the velocities before and after a collision. For bullets the classic method is the ballistic pendulum: an 8 g bullet that lodges in a 2 kg block and lifts it 10 cm gives the block 1.400 m/s, so the bullet was moving at about **352 m/s**. Momentum is conserved in the impact, then energy in the swing.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving speed in km/h.** 6.22 kg·m/s needs 38.89 m/s; using 140 directly gives a momentum 3.6 times too big.
- **Entering grams as kilograms.** A 160 g ball is 0.16 kg.
- **Adding momenta without signs.** Two equal cars meeting head on at the same speed have a total momentum of zero, not double.
- **Using kinetic energy conservation for a crash.** Only momentum is conserved when objects stick or crumple.
- **Adding 2D momenta as plain numbers.** Resolve into x and y components first, then combine.

## Practical notes

- **Direction matters:** momentum is a vector. In a head-on collision, give one velocity a negative sign before adding momenta.
- **Stopping force:** the average force needed to stop an object is its momentum divided by the stopping time, F = p ÷ t — see the [impulse calculator](/physics/impulse/). Air bags, crumple zones and catching a ball with "soft hands" all work by making t longer.
- **Recoil:** when a gun fires or a rocket burns, the forward momentum of the bullet or exhaust equals the backward momentum of the gun or rocket. A heavier gun recoils more slowly for the same bullet.
- At speeds approaching the speed of light, use relativistic momentum p = γmv instead.
