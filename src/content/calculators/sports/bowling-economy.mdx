## How to use this calculator

Enter **runs conceded**, **wickets** and **balls bowled**. Economy, average and strike rate are calculated.

## Worked example with the default values

A bowler has conceded **480 runs**, taken **20 wickets** and bowled **432 balls** in a season:

- Overs bowled = 432 ÷ 6 = **72 overs**
- Economy rate = 480 ÷ 72 = **6.67** runs per over
- Bowling average = 480 ÷ 20 = **24.00** runs per wicket
- Strike rate = 432 ÷ 20 = **21.6** balls per wicket

The three numbers are tied together: economy = average ÷ strike rate × 6, here 24 ÷ 21.6 × 6 = 6.67. Knowing any two gives the third.

## One spell from a scorecard

Scorecards write bowling figures as overs, maidens, runs and wickets. A 10 over ODI spell of **10-1-42-3** means 60 balls, 42 runs and 3 wickets: economy **4.20**, average **14** and strike rate **20**. Maidens do not enter any of the formulas; they are already counted in the balls and the runs.

## Benchmarks by format

| Format | Good economy | Good average | Good strike rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test cricket | under 3.0 | under 30 | under 60 |
| ODI | under 5.0 | under 30 | under 35 |
| T20 | under 7.5 | under 25 | under 20 |

Rough guides; conditions and eras vary.

## Which stat matters most?

- **Economy rate** matters most in limited-overs cricket, where stopping runs wins games.
- **Strike rate** shows how often a bowler takes wickets, key in Test cricket.
- **Average** combines both: runs per wicket.

## Converting overs notation

Overs are written as completed overs plus balls: 8.3 means 8 overs and 3 balls (51 balls), not 8.3 overs. Enter balls bowled for exact results.

## T20 spells compared

Four overs is the maximum a bowler can bowl in a T20 innings, so a full spell is 24 balls:

| Figures | Economy | Average | Strike rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4-0-24-1 | 6.00 | 24.0 | 24.0 |
| 4-0-28-2 | 7.00 | 14.0 | 12.0 |

| 4-0-36-3 | 9.00 | 12.0 | 8.0 |
| 4-0-45-1 | 11.25 | 45.0 | 24.0 |

A wicketless spell has no average or strike rate, because both divide by wickets. In the calculator, entering 0 wickets is not allowed for that reason.

## Practical notes

- **Wides and no balls count against the bowler** in runs conceded, but they are not legal deliveries, so they do not add to the balls bowled.
- **Byes and leg byes are not charged to the bowler.** Use the bowler's runs column on the scorecard, not the runs scored while he was bowling.
- **Compare like with like.** A T20 economy of 7.5 is good, while the same figure in a Test match would be expensive. Use the benchmarks for the format being played.
- **Small samples mislead.** One spell of 3 wickets for 12 runs gives a superb average, but career averages only settle after a few hundred overs.

## Common mistakes

- **Dividing runs by the overs notation.** 8.3 overs is 51 balls, or 8.5 overs as a decimal, not 8.3.
- **Including byes and leg byes** in runs conceded, which makes the economy and average look worse than the official figures.
- **Counting wides and no balls as balls bowled,** which inflates the balls and flatters the economy and strike rate.
- **Judging a bowler on economy alone in Tests.** A miserly bowler who rarely takes wickets can have a fine economy and a poor strike rate.
- **Averaging averages across seasons.** Add up all runs and all wickets first, then divide, or a short season counts as much as a long one.
