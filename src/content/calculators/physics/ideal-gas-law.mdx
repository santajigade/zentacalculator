## How to use the ideal gas law calculator

Enter **any three** of pressure, volume, moles and temperature to find the fourth. Pressure must be **absolute** (not gauge); temperature can be in °C, °F or K.

## Ideal gas law formula

<p class="formula">P V = n R T   (R = 8.314 J/mol·K)</p>

## Worked example

1 mol of gas at 0 °C (273.15 K) and 101.325 kPa:

V = nRT ÷ P = 1 × 8.314 × 273.15 ÷ 101,325 = **22.41 L**

At 25 °C it expands to 24.47 L.

## Combined gas law

For a fixed amount of gas, P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂. A tyre at 220 kPa absolute and 20 °C warms to 50 °C: P₂ = 220 × 323.15 ÷ 293.15 ≈ **243 kPa**.

## Molar volume at different conditions

The volume of 1 mole of an ideal gas:

| Conditions | Temperature | Pressure | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| STP (IUPAC) | 0 °C | 100 kPa | 22.71 L |
| Older STP | 0 °C | 101.325 kPa | 22.41 L |
| Room conditions | 25 °C | 101.325 kPa | 24.47 L |

Always check which standard a textbook or datasheet uses.

## The gas constant R

| Units | Value of R |
|---|---|
| J/(mol·K) or kPa·L/(mol·K) | 8.314 |
| L·atm/(mol·K) | 0.08206 |
| L·bar/(mol·K) | 0.08314 |

## Using mass instead of moles

n = mass ÷ molar mass. For 64 g of oxygen (O₂, 32 g/mol), n = 2 mol. Common molar masses: N₂ 28, O₂ 32, CO₂ 44, air about 28.97 g/mol.

## Density of a gas

Rearranging gives gas density: ρ = P × M ÷ (R × T). Air at 20 °C and 101.325 kPa has a density of about **1.204 kg/m³**.

## Always use absolute values

Temperature must be in kelvin (or entered in °C so the calculator converts it) and pressure must be absolute, not gauge. A tyre at 2.2 bar gauge is about 3.2 bar absolute.

## When it is less accurate

Real gases deviate at very high pressures and low temperatures, close to condensing. For air and most gases at everyday conditions, the error is well under 1%.
