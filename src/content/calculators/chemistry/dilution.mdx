## How to use the dilution calculator

Enter the **stock concentration**, the **final concentration** and **final volume**. You get the volume of stock to use and how much solvent to add. Any concentration unit works as long as both are the same.

## Dilution formula

<p class="formula">C₁ V₁ = C₂ V₂</p>

## Worked example

Make 200 mL of 0.5 M solution from a 10 M stock:

- V₁ = 0.5 × 200 ÷ 10 = **10 mL** of stock
- Add **190 mL** of water

## Safety tip

When diluting strong acids, always add **acid to water**, slowly and with stirring — never water to acid.

## Dilution factor

<p class="formula">Dilution factor = V₂ ÷ V₁ = C₁ ÷ C₂</p>

A 1 : 10 dilution has a factor of 10. Note that “1 : 10” usually means 1 part in 10 total, but some fields use it to mean 1 part plus 10 parts. Check the convention in your protocol.

## Percent solutions

The C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ rule works for any concentration unit — mol/L, % (w/v), mg/mL or ppm — as long as C₁ and C₂ use the same one. To make 500 mL of 70% ethanol from 95% stock: V₁ = 70 × 500 ÷ 95 = **368 mL** of stock, made up to 500 mL with water.

## Mixing volumes are not always additive

Mixing ethanol and water gives slightly less volume than the sum of the parts. For accurate work, add solvent **up to** the final volume mark in a volumetric flask rather than adding a calculated volume of solvent.

## Serial dilutions

For very large dilutions, dilute in steps (for example 1:10 four times for 1:10,000). Each step is more accurate than one huge dilution.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding V₂ of diluent instead of making up to V₂.** C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ gives the final volume. The diluent you add is V₂ − V₁.
- **Mixing concentration units.** If C₁ is in mol/L and C₂ in mmol/L, convert first. 1 M = 1,000 mM.
- **Confusing 1:10 with 1+10.** A 1:10 dilution is 1 part in 10 total. A 1+10 mix is 1 part plus 10 parts, which is 1:11.
- **Making very large single-step dilutions.** Pipetting 1 µL into 1 L is inaccurate. Use two or three serial steps instead.
- **Forgetting that errors add up in serial dilutions.** A 1% pipetting error in each of 6 steps can add up to about 6% in the final tube.

## Choosing pipette volumes

Pipettes are most accurate between about 35% and 100% of their maximum volume. For a 1:20 dilution to 10 mL, pipette 500 µL with a 1,000 µL pipette rather than a 5 mL one, and add the stock to the diluent, not the other way round.

## Checking the default example step by step

With the default inputs (C₁ = 10, C₂ = 0.5, V₂ = 200 mL) the calculator returns V₁ = 10 mL and 190 mL of solvent. The dilution factor is 10 ÷ 0.5 = **20**, so the stock volume is always the final volume divided by 20: 200 ÷ 20 = 10 mL. In practice, pipette the 10 mL of stock into a 200 mL volumetric flask that already holds some water, then fill to the mark rather than measuring 190 mL separately.

## Diluting concentrated acids and bases

Bottles of concentrated reagent are labelled in % by mass, not mol/L, so first convert the label to molarity:

<p class="formula">C (mol/L) = w × ρ (g/mL) × 1000 ÷ M</p>

For 37% hydrochloric acid with a density of 1.19 g/mL and M = 36.46 g/mol: C = 0.37 × 1.19 × 1000 ÷ 36.46 = **12.08 M**. To make 1 L of 1 M HCl, V₁ = 1 × 1000 ÷ 12.08 = **82.8 mL** of the concentrated acid, added slowly to about 800 mL of water and then made up to 1 L.

## Serial dilution from the 10 M stock

Four 1:10 steps take the default 10 M stock down by 10,000 times with ordinary pipettes:

| Step | Transfer | Concentration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 mL into 9 mL | 1 M |
| 2 | 1 mL into 9 mL | 0.1 M |
| 3 | 1 mL into 9 mL | 0.01 M |
| 4 | 1 mL into 9 mL | 0.001 M |

Mix each tube fully before taking the next aliquot, and use a fresh tip each time so stock carried on the outside of the tip does not raise the next concentration.
